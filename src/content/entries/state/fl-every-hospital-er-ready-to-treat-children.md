If your child needs emergency care in Florida, the hospital ER now has to be ready for kids, not just adults. Every hospital with an emergency department must have written policies for treating children. They cover sorting patients by urgency, taking vital signs, weighing kids and recording the weight in kilograms, working out medicine doses, and using child-sized equipment. Hospitals must conduct training on these policies at least once a year, including hands-on drills.

Each ER must also name a pediatric emergency care coordinator, a doctor, physician assistant, nurse or paramedic who makes sure the plan is followed. Hospitals must take a national pediatric readiness assessment on the national schedule. The state's Agency for Health Care Administration must set minimum standards for children's emergency care, require hospital disaster plans to cover children and newborns, and publish each ER's readiness score. Families don't need to do anything: the rules apply to hospitals.

Rep. Vanessa Oliver sponsored the House bill, and Sen. Gayle Harrell carried the Senate version. They had carried the proposal in 2025, when it passed the House but died in the Senate. Reps. Johanna López and Susan Valdés co-sponsored the 2026 House bill. It passed both chambers without a single no vote, 109-0 in the House and 36-0 in the Senate. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on May 22, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
