An unpaid hospital or ambulance bill can't drag down your credit in New York. Under the Fair Medical Debt Reporting Act, hospitals, health care professionals, and ambulance services can't report medical debt to credit bureaus, and they have to bar the collection agencies they hire from doing it too. Credit reporting companies can't include medical debt in New Yorkers' reports, no matter when the debt was incurred. If medical debt is reported anyway, the debt becomes void.

Lenders and landlords look at credit reports, so a single emergency room bill used to be able to follow someone into a car loan or an apartment application. Now it can't. You don't need to do anything to be protected. Ordinary credit card charges don't count as medical debt under the law, unless the card was issued specifically to pay for medical care.

Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assemblymember Amy Paulin sponsored the bill, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 13, 2023. It took effect immediately, making New York the second state, after Colorado, to keep medical debt off credit reports. It had support from both parties: it passed the Senate 50–8, with 10 Republicans joining every Democrat present.
