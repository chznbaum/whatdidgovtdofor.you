New York lets local governments cut property taxes for homeowners 65 and older, and for homeowners with disabilities and limited incomes, by taxing as little as half their home's assessed value. Only people under an income limit qualify. Before this law, the most a locality outside New York City could set was $29,000. Now a county, city, town, village or school district can set its limit as high as $50,000 for the full 50% break. With the sliding-scale options, smaller breaks of 20%, 10% and 5% reach incomes under $58,400.

It's a local option, so how much you save depends on what your own governments adopted. Nassau County's legislature voted unanimously in September 2022 to raise its cap for the full break from $20,000 to $50,000. To apply outside New York City, file with your local assessor, usually by March 1, and include a copy of your federal tax return if you filed one.

Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Thomas Abinanti carried it in the Assembly. Her memo said the income limit hadn't been raised since 2009 while property taxes kept rising on seniors with fixed incomes. Republican Sen. Pamela Helming cosponsored it, and the Senate passed it 62–0. The sponsor's memo said the law would cost the state nothing but might reduce revenue for local governments that opt in. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on August 8, 2022.
