Scammers often tell people to feed cash into a crypto ATM, and once the money is sent it is almost impossible to get back. Starting January 1, 2027, Florida kiosk operators must show a fraud warning before a transaction begins. They must also cap how much a person can send: $2,000 a day for someone who has used that company for fewer than seven days, and $10,000 a day after that. You can choose a paper or electronic receipt showing the fees and any applicable exchange rate.

If you are scammed on your first kiosk transaction, the operator must refund the full amount within 72 hours if you notify both the company and law enforcement or another government agency within 60 days and provide proof, such as a police report or notarized affidavit. Businesses that are not already licensed as money transmitters must also register with the state Office of Financial Regulation. Existing businesses must apply by January 31, 2027; the registration requirement starts March 1.

Rep. Michael Owen (R) and Rep. Dan Daley (D) sponsored the House bill. Sen. Darryl Rouson (D) sponsored the matching Senate bill, co-introduced by Sen. Kristen Arrington (D). Flagler County Sheriff Rick Staly (R) pushed the proposal after his office traced more than $2.75 million in local kiosk scams, mostly against retirees, and AARP Florida's advocacy helped drive the bill to passage. It passed without a no vote in either chamber, and Gov. Ron DeSantis (R) signed it on June 26, 2026.
