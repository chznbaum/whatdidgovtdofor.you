Crypto kiosks look like ATMs and often sit in gas stations and convenience stores. They turn cash into cryptocurrency almost instantly, and once the money is sent, it's nearly impossible to get back. Scammers pretend to be police, a government office, or a relative in trouble, then pressure people to feed cash into these machines. Minnesota's Commerce Department counted nearly $1 million in reported losses from 2023 to 2025, including more than $540,000 from 70 cases in 2025 alone. Nationally, people 60 and older made up 86% of reported losses in these scams when the victim's age was known.

Since Aug. 1, 2026, it has been illegal to run or offer one of these machines in Minnesota, and operators have until Dec. 31 to take them out of public places. Kiosk-only operators must also pay out money or crypto they hold for customers by Dec. 31 unless those customers can access or sell it another lawful way. Customers can choose dollars or a transfer to their crypto wallet. You can still buy crypto through regulated online platforms. To report a kiosk that's still running, contact the Commerce Department.

Sen. Amanda Hemmingsen-Jaeger and Rep. Erin Koegel, both Democrats, carried the ban. It followed Minnesota's 2024 law that first regulated the machines, which hadn't stopped the scams. AARP Minnesota pushed for the ban, and Republican Rep. Bernie Perryman joined Koegel in presenting it to the House commerce committee. The bill passed with support from both parties, 127–7 in the House and 53–13 in the Senate, and Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 5, 2026.
