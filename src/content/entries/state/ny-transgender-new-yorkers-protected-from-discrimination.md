New York's Human Rights Law now names gender identity or expression as a protected trait. That means an employer, landlord, real estate broker, school, lender, or business open to the public can't treat you worse because you're transgender, or because of how you look, act, or dress compared with what people expect of your sex at birth. The law also covers apprenticeship programs, unions, job agencies, and harassment of domestic workers by the people who employ them.

You don't have to sign up for anything. If you face this kind of bias, you can file a complaint with the state Division of Human Rights or go to court. The law also made specified crimes committed in whole or substantial part because of someone's gender identity or expression count as hate crimes. That part took effect on November 1, 2019; the rest began on February 24, 2019.

The fight took years. Assembly Member Richard Gottfried got the bill through the Assembly 11 times, but for years the Senate's Republican leadership refused it a floor vote. After Democrats won control of the Senate, Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal brought it to the floor in the first week of the 2019 session. Three Republicans joined 39 Democrats to pass it 42–19 on January 15. The Assembly passed it the same day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on January 25, 2019.
