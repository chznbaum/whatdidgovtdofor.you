When New York's state overdose prevention program hands out naloxone, the medicine that reverses an opioid overdose, the handout must now include fentanyl test strips. The strips let someone check whether drugs contain fentanyl before using them. The required information card also has to explain what the strips are and how to use them. The card already covered how to spot an overdose, when to call 911, and New York's Good Samaritan protections for people who call for help.

The law does not create a separate application for the strips. If a registered program gives you naloxone, it must include them. When the law was signed, the state counted more than 1,300 registered programs at 5,800 sites. The state also said it had already given out more than 20 million fentanyl and xylazine test strips and nearly 700,000 naloxone kits for free.

Sen. Monica Martinez sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Alicia Hyndman carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it unanimously in 2023, but it died in the Assembly. The Senate passed it again 61–0 in March 2024, and the Assembly passed it 143–0 that April. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 17, 2024, and it took effect 90 days later.
