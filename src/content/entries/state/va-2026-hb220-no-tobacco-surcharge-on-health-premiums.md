If you smoke or use other tobacco and buy your own health insurance, or get it through a small employer, Virginia insurers can charge you up to 1.5 times what a nonsmoker pays. This law ends that. Plans that start, change, or renew on or after January 1, 2027 can't set your premium based on tobacco use. You don't have to apply or do anything. The ban is built into how plans set rates.

Virginia has banned the surcharge before. A 2023 law did it for plans starting in 2024, but it was set to expire on January 1, 2026. The state's insurance regulator reported that in 2024, the ban cut tobacco users' premiums by $25 to $123 a month, depending on age. To make up the difference, insurers raised rates for individual-market members by about $3.80 a month, which was less than 1%.

Del. Patrick Hope tried in 2025 to keep the ban from expiring, but Gov. Glenn Youngkin vetoed his bill. Hope brought it back in 2026, and Sen. Jennifer Carroll Foy carried an identical Senate bill. Hope's bill passed the House 81–16 and the Senate 40–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on March 31, 2026.
