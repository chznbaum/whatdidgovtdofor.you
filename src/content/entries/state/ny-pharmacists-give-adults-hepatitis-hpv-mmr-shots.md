If you're 18 or older in New York, you can get vaccinated against hepatitis A, hepatitis B, HPV, measles, mumps, rubella and chickenpox at a pharmacy. Before this law, pharmacists couldn't give those shots, so adults had to get them from a doctor's office or clinic. The law also lets pharmacists give other adult vaccines if the state health commissioner, in consultation with the education commissioner, approves them.

You don't apply for anything. Certified pharmacists can give the shots under either a prescription for you or a standing order from a doctor or nurse practitioner. With a standing order, you don't need your own prescription. Pharmacists must report each shot they give to the state health department.

New York had allowed pharmacists to give some adult vaccines since 2008, but that authority came with expiration dates that kept needing renewal. This law removed those dates and made it permanent. State Sen. Toby Ann Stavisky and Assembly Member Alicia Hyndman sponsored it. It passed the Senate 61–2 and the Assembly 147–0 on June 1, 2021, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 2, 2021. It took effect 90 days later.

In May 2026, a follow-up law sponsored by Stavisky and Assembly Member Amy Paulin removed references to the CDC's vaccine advisers from the pharmacy provisions of New York law. The health commissioner, consulting the education commissioner, can still authorize other adult vaccines. The law also lets pharmacists give COVID-19 shots to children as young as 2.
