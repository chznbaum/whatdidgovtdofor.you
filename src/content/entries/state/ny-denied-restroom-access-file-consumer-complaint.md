Under New York's Crohn's and Colitis Fairness Act, a business open to the public that has a restroom for its workers must let you use it if you have Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, irritable bowel syndrome, another condition that needs urgent toilet access, or an ostomy device. A few conditions apply: at least two employees have to be working, no public restroom can be close by, and your using that restroom can't pose an obvious health, safety or security risk. The business can ask for proof, such as a form signed by your health care provider or an ID card from a patient nonprofit.

This 2020 law says what happens when a business refuses you anyway. The state's Division of Consumer Protection and any county, city or town consumer protection office must take your complaint, try to settle it, and send it to the right agency if needed. The bill's sponsor also points people to the attorney general's office. You don't need a lawyer to file.

The 2017 law didn't say who handles complaints. The governor's approval message said people could file with consumer offices, but the law itself didn't, and the sponsors said some offices were not enforcing it. Assemblymember Amy Paulin and Sen. Simcha Felder carried the fix that wrote that duty into law. The Senate passed it 60–0 in July 2020, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 7, 2020. It took effect right away.
