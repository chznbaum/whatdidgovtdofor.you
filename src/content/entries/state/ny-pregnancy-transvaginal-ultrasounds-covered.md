If you're pregnant and have a private health plan that New York regulates, the plan will have to cover a transvaginal ultrasound when you need one medically and national clinical guidelines recommend it. This scan can find a short or weakening cervix early. Doctors can treat that problem if they catch it in time, and if they miss it, it can lead to a very early birth. The rule covers plans issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2027. You don't have to apply. The scan becomes part of the pregnancy care your plan already has to cover.

The law is named for Eli Parker Levitt, who was born at 23 weeks in 2020 and died soon after. His mother, Carolyn Spiro Levitt, was told at a routine visit at 20 weeks that her cervix had already opened, after earlier assurances that the pregnancy was going well. She hadn't been tested for the problem sooner. The bill's sponsors say a weak cervix is behind about 5% of extremely early births.

Assembly Member Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn first introduced the Assembly bill in 2021, and then-Sen. Diane Savino sponsored the first Senate companion. Sen. Jessica Scarcella-Spanton took over the Senate bill in 2023 and carried it through passage in 2025. After three legislative sessions, the Assembly passed the final bill 145–0 and the Senate 59–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 16, 2025.
