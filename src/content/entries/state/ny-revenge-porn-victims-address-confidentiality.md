If someone illegally shared or posted intimate images of you without your consent, and you moved because of it, you can now ask New York to keep your new home address secret if you fear for your or your children's safety. The new law adds these victims to the state's Address Confidentiality Program. Victims of domestic violence, sexual offenses, stalking and human trafficking could already use it.

People in the program get a substitute address from the Department of State. They can give it out in place of their home, school or work address. State and local agencies generally have to accept it on public records, and the department forwards your mail and legal papers to you for free. To join, you file an application with the Secretary of State and sign statements that you are a victim, left your residence because of the offense, and fear for your or your children's safety. Trained state, local and nonprofit agencies can help you apply. Certification normally lasts four years and can be renewed.

Sponsors said the change protects victims from being found while their case moves through the legal system. Assembly Member Latrice Walker sponsored the bill, and Sen. Luis Sepúlveda carried the Senate version. The Assembly passed it 139–0 in January 2026, and the Senate followed 59–0 that March. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 26, 2026, and it took effect the same day.
