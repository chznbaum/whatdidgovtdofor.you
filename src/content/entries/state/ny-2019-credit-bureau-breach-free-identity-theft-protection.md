Credit bureaus hold the most sensitive facts about your finances, and you never chose to give them that information. Under this law, if a credit reporting agency is breached and Social Security numbers are exposed, it generally must offer free identity theft prevention services to every New Yorker whose information was taken or is reasonably believed to have been taken. The services can last up to five years. Where it applies, the agency must also offer help fixing damage from identity theft. It must tell you how to enroll and how to freeze your credit.

You don't pay anything, but you do have to sign up using the information the agency sends you. The agency can skip offering the services only if, after a proper investigation, it reasonably concludes the breach is unlikely to harm the people affected.

The law grew out of the 2017 Equifax breach, which exposed the personal information of more than 8.5 million New Yorkers. Assembly Member Jeffrey Dinowitz first introduced the measure in 2017 and carried it through two legislative sessions. Sen. David Carlucci sponsored the 2017–18 Senate version, and Sen. Leroy Comrie carried it in 2019. The Senate passed the bill 62–0 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 25, 2019, the same day he signed the SHIELD Act, and it took effect that September. It also covered breaches from the three years before it took effect.
