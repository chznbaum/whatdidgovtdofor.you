When COVID-19 shut down much of New York in 2020, many people lost work and fell behind on bills. This law barred utilities and municipal systems from shutting off residential electricity, gas, water or landline phone service for an unpaid bill during the state's COVID emergency. Service disconnected during the emergency for nonpayment had to be restored within 48 hours.

The general ban applied without an application, and providers had to notify customers. The law said customers whose finances were hurt by COVID would keep protection for 180 days after the emergency if they self-certified that change to their provider. They could enter or revise a payment plan with no down payment, late fees or penalties. They still owed the bills, and providers could disconnect service for safety.

State Sen. Kevin Parker and Assembly Member Walter Mosley, both Brooklyn Democrats, carried the bill. It passed the Senate 58–4 and the Assembly 111–32 in late May. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 17, 2020. The Public Utility Law Project called Cuomo the first governor in any state to sign a bill like it. A chapter amendment set a March 31, 2021 sunset. Chapter 106 restored and expanded the moratorium on May 11; after the emergency ended June 23, qualifying customers remained protected through December 21, 2021.
