If your rent check bounces in New York, your landlord can charge a fee for it only if your lease says so. Even then, the fee can't be more than $20 or what the bounce actually cost the landlord, whichever is greater. If the landlord asks for more than $20, you can ask for proof of those costs, and the landlord has to give it to you.

You don't have to apply for anything. The limit applies to residential tenants across the state, including people who rent from a co-op shareholder. Before this law, only rent-stabilized tenants had a cap, through guidance from the state housing agency. The new law put the limit into state law for every tenant.

Assembly Member Harvey Epstein first introduced the bill in 2022, and Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal carried it in the Senate starting the next session. It passed the Senate 37–22 in June 2025, with every Republican present and one Democrat voting no. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Oct. 16, 2025, along with seven other housing bills, and it took effect that day.
