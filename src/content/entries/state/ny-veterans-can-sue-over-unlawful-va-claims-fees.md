Some people who aren't accredited by the VA offer veterans paid "help" with disability or pension claims, then charge fees the rules don't allow. Some also take veterans' personal information and use it to drain bank accounts. New York already barred being paid for this kind of help. This law gives the ban real teeth. Since May 4, 2026, a person harmed by an unlawful fee can sue and recover their actual losses or $2,500, whichever is more. A court may also award costs and reasonable attorney's fees. It can impose a civil penalty of up to $10,000 per violation. Certain deceptive conduct targeting people seeking or using veterans-benefit services can draw a separate civil penalty of up to $10,000. Knowingly and willfully seeking or taking unlawful fees is a misdemeanor punishable by up to a year in jail.

You don't need to apply for anything. The state veterans' services agency must run a hotline and an email address for complaints. It must also explain how to check whether someone is VA-accredited and when charging a fee is legal. Penalty money goes into a new fund that gives grants to certified veterans' service organizations, which help veterans file claims.

Sen. Siela Bynoe and Assemblymember Steve Stern, who chairs the Assembly's Veterans' Affairs Committee, sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 62–0 in June 2025 and passed the Assembly the same week. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 5, 2025.
