If you're 13 to 17 in New York, a website, app, online service or connected device that knows you're a minor generally may process your personal data only when it is strictly necessary for one of the law's listed purposes or you give informed consent. The law also treats users of services primarily directed to minors as covered users. For children 12 and under, the federal COPPA standard governs when data can be collected or used and when verifiable parental consent is required. Covered operators cannot buy or sell minors' personal data.

You do not have to enroll for the protection. A covered service's consent request must stand on its own, explain that declining will not block continued use, and present “no” as the most prominent choice. Revoking consent must be as easy as giving it. If you decline or revoke consent for a kind of processing, the service cannot ask again for that processing during the following calendar year. The state attorney general enforces the law and can seek up to $5,000 per violation.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Nily Rozic sponsored the bill, which Attorney General Letitia James's office co-authored and helped advance. Gov. Kathy Hochul also proposed the act in her FY 2025 Executive Budget. It passed without a single no vote—61–0 in the Senate and 144–0 in the Assembly—before Hochul signed the final bill on June 20, 2024. The protections took effect one year later and remain in effect.
