Faulty e-bike and scooter batteries can overheat and start fires that spread fast. This law bars uncertified batteries from the New York market. A lithium-ion battery sold either on its own or inside an e-bike, e-scooter, moped or similar device must be certified by an accredited testing lab to a recognized safety standard, such as UL 2849 or UL 2271. The certification or the lab's mark must appear directly on the product and also on its packaging or sale paperwork.

You don't have to apply for this protection. When you shop for a battery or battery-powered device, look for the certification or lab mark on the battery. Local prosecutors and city and county lawyers can take violators to court. A violator faces a fine of up to $500 for a first violation and up to $1,000 for the same offense again within two years. Every uncertified battery counts as a separate violation.

Sen. Liz Krueger introduced the bill in early 2023 and Assembly Member Jeffrey Dinowitz carried it in the Assembly. Krueger's memo cited more than 264 lithium-ion battery fires in New York City in 2023. Dinowitz called these batteries the city's number one cause of fire deaths. The Senate passed the final version 61–0 in May 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on July 11, 2024, as part of a package of e-bike safety laws. It took effect on October 9, 2024.
