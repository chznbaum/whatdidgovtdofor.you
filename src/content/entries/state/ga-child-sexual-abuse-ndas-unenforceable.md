If you were sexually abused as a child, a confidentiality clause can no longer automatically keep you quiet about the abuse in Georgia. Trey's Law makes a provision in a settlement, employment contract, or other agreement void and unenforceable to the extent it bars anyone from disclosing an act of childhood sexual abuse or related facts. It applies to agreements made before, on, or after May 11, 2026. Other settlement terms, including the amount and payment terms, can still stay private.

You don't have to apply. A person who wants to enforce a covered gag clause must first obtain a final court judgment, no longer subject to appeal, declaring the agreement or provision enforceable.

The law is named for Trey Carlock, who settled a lawsuit over abuse at a Missouri summer camp but was barred by a nondisclosure agreement from talking about it. His sister, Elizabeth Carlock Phillips, founded Trey's Law and has pushed it across the country. Gov. Brian Kemp called it a priority of First Lady Marty Kemp and the GRACE Commission in his January 2026 State of the State address. Rep. Soo Hong (R-Lawrenceville) sponsored the bill, and Sen. Kay Kirkpatrick (R-Marietta) carried it in the Senate. The House passed it 174–0, and the Senate passed it 50–0. Kemp signed it, and it took effect May 11, 2026.
