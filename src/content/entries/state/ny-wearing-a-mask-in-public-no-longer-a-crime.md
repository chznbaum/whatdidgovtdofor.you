For 175 years, New York law made it a crime to wear a mask or disguise while gathering in public with other masked people. Anyone charged faced up to 15 days in jail. The law dates to 1845, when it was written to stop Hudson Valley tenants who disguised themselves during the anti-rent riots. In spring 2020, the state was telling everyone to cover their faces against COVID-19, so following health advice could technically break the law. This repeal ended that conflict. You don't need to do anything to be covered.

Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assembly Member Dan Quart carried the repeal. Attorney General Letitia James had urged a fix in a May 2020 letter, and Bailey credited Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and James with ensuring passage. Quart said the old law left men of color open to police harassment. The Senate passed it 35–27 on May 27, 2020, with Republicans and five Democrats voting no, and the Assembly passed it the same day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 13, 2020, and it took effect right away.

The 2020 repeal still stands. Since June 2025, state law has separately made it a Class B misdemeanor to hide your identity while committing or immediately fleeing a felony or Class A misdemeanor. Masking or gathering by itself is not a state crime. But pending Assembly Bill A4773 would restore a loitering violation for masked public gatherings.
