Some cities and nonprofits run pilots that give people regular cash payments, with no strings attached, and study what happens. Before this law, joining one could put your public assistance at risk, because the extra money could count as income. Now New York's cash assistance rules ignore money and resources you get from an OTDA-approved pilot that studies direct cash payments, for as long as you're in the pilot, up to 60 months.

You don't need to apply separately for the exemption. The pilot's state or local sponsor requests approval, and a county or state agency must participate. Once the pilot is approved, your local social services office applies the exemption when it figures out whether you qualify and how much you get. SNAP is a federal program, so the follow-up law tells the state to seek any federal waiver needed to let SNAP ignore the pilot money too.

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal and Sen. Jabari Brisport carried the bill. Rosenthal's memo pointed to a New York City pilot giving monthly cash to young people experiencing homelessness, and said people in such studies shouldn't lose lifesaving aid for taking part. The Assembly passed it 107–41 in March 2022 and the Senate 43–19 that May. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 23, 2022, on the condition that lawmakers pass agreed changes. Rosenthal and Brisport sponsored those follow-up bills, which Hochul signed on March 24, 2023. The exemption took effect that spring and is set to expire on March 23, 2029.
