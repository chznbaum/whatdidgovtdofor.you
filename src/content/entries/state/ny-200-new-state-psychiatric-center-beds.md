New York is adding 200 inpatient beds at psychiatric hospitals the state runs itself. State psychiatric centers provide longer-term treatment than acute-care hospital units, often lasting months. How patients enter depends on the unit: Rockland Children's accepts inpatient referrals from hospitals, emergency rooms or courts, while forensic beds serve people sent through the criminal courts or correction system.

The first 125 beds opened between December 2024 and March 2025, spread across centers in Binghamton, Queens, Albany, Orangeburg, Manhattan and Rochester. Fifteen are at Rockland Children's Psychiatric Center for children and teens. Sixty are civil beds for adults, and 50 are forensic beds for people whose cases go through the courts. The last 75 beds will be three Transition to Home units at Creedmoor Psychiatric Center in Queens. That program gives chronically homeless New Yorkers with mental illness treatment aimed at getting them into stable housing. In the state's April 2026 update, the Creedmoor units were still being developed.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the $55 million for the beds in her 2024–25 budget, the Legislature passed it, and she signed the State Operations budget bill on May 1, 2024. The state Office of Mental Health, led by Commissioner Ann Sullivan, is opening the units.
