Federal rules generally require a nursing home resident to be seen every 30 days for the first 90 days and every 60 days after that. Virginia now requires every licensed nursing home to notify the resident, their family or legal representative, and the Virginia Department of Health when a required physician visit is missed. You do not have to request the notice.

The law also requires a complete assessment within 14 days of admission, promptly after a significant health change, and at least every 366 days. A registered nurse coordinates it. Each assessment must be reviewed at least every 92 days. The home must write a comprehensive care plan within seven days after completing a comprehensive assessment, and the resident and their family or legal representative must get a meaningful chance to participate. The department must develop a plan to fill open inspector jobs, create a training manager and onboarding plan, and report yearly on nursing-home oversight.

Sen. Glen Sturtevant wrote SB 555 after a CBS 6 investigation found no evidence in a woman's records that her doctor visited during a 20-day rehabilitation stay. Nursing-home industry lobbyists opposed most of his proposal; senators removed its monthly-visit rule, fines and public scorecard but kept the notice and assessment provisions. A House committee folded it into SB 429, whose chief patron was Sen. Chris Head and co-patron was Sen. Tammy Brankley Mulchi. The final bill passed the Senate 40–0 and House 95–1. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it April 13, 2026.
