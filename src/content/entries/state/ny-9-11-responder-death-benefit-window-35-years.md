Many police officers, firefighters, EMTs and other public workers who worked at Ground Zero later got sick. Under New York law, if one of them retires and later dies of a qualifying World Trade Center illness, the death is presumed to have come from their work at the site. An eligible survivor can then apply to convert ordinary retirement survivor benefits to an accidental death benefit. Until now, that presumption covered only deaths within 25 years of retirement. For people who retired soon after 9/11, that window was starting to close.

The new law extends the window to 35 years. It covers the state and New York City pension systems, including the city's police, fire, teachers' and employees' systems. To claim it, a beneficiary applies to the retirement system within the normal deadline for an accidental death benefit. The retiree must already have met the WTC requirements, such as filing proof that they took part in the rescue, recovery or clean-up.

State Sen. Robert Jackson sponsored the bill, with Republican Sen. Stephen Chan as a cosponsor, and Assemblymember Stacey Pheffer Amato carried it in the Assembly. It passed the Senate 61–0 on June 2, 2026, and the Assembly passed it on June 5. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 10, 2026. The added cost falls on public employers. For the city systems, the actuary said the increase would be allocated almost entirely to New York City and estimated $786.6 million in fiscal 2027 if 25% of deaths between 25 and 35 years after retirement are reclassified.
