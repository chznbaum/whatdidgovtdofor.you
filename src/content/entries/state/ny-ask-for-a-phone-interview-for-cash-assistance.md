To get cash assistance in New York, you have to be interviewed by your local social services office, and in the past that usually meant going there in person. This law lets the office do the interview by phone or another digital method, such as video, if you ask. The same goes for the substance use and domestic violence screenings that can be part of applying. It covers both new applications and renewals.

You don't need to show a hardship to ask for a phone or digital interview. Tell your caseworker or local office that you want one. The law leaves the final decision about offering it with the local office.

Phone interviews for SNAP had been allowed since 2011. For cash assistance, they were allowed only under temporary waivers during COVID-19. Under those waivers, New York City interviewed about 166,000 applicants by phone as applications roughly doubled. Sen. Roxanne Persaud and Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal carried the first bill, which let offices offer phone interviews if they chose to. Then they passed this follow-up, which ties the option to the applicant's request. It passed the Senate 45–18, with two Republicans joining Democrats, and the Assembly 104–44. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed both bills on June 11, 2021, and the change took effect that October.
