If a job injury or occupational disease causes lost wages or reduced earning capacity, New York workers' comp may pay weekly disability benefits. For an injury or disablement dated July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027, the minimum is $384.45. Workers whose weekly wage was lower receive that wage instead. The minimum is part of a qualifying claim; workers still must file the claim itself.

The law raised the minimum to $275 for 2024 dates and $325 for dates from January 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026. For new dates each July 1 beginning in 2026, the minimum is one-fifth of the state average weekly wage. A worker's rate is fixed by the injury or disablement date, so later annual increases do not raise an older claim. Employers fund workers' compensation coverage, and workers cannot be charged for it.

Sen. James Sanders Jr. first introduced the indexing plan in 2018. He sponsored the 2023 Senate bill, and Assemblymember Latoya Joyner carried the Assembly version after they carried a version that passed both chambers and was vetoed by Gov. Kathy Hochul in 2022. The Senate approved the new bill 49–12: all 40 voting Democrats and nine Republicans supported it, while 12 Republicans opposed it. The Assembly passed it 98–40 on June 10, 2023, and Hochul signed it on September 6.
