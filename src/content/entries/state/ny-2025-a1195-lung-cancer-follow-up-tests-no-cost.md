A lung cancer screening scan is often just the first step. If it finds something, you may need more scans or tests to learn what it is. This law makes New York-regulated individual, group and HMO plans cover follow-up screening and diagnostic services when your provider recommends them and they match nationally recognized clinical guidelines. The plans cannot charge a copay or coinsurance and generally cannot apply a deductible.

You don't need to apply. Your plan can use prior authorization to check that the services match the guidelines. If you have a high-deductible plan tied to a health savings account, the tests may still count toward your annual deductible unless the service has an A or B rating from the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force. The law starts with policies issued, renewed, modified, altered or amended on or after Jan. 1, 2027.

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer deaths in New York. About 13,900 New Yorkers are diagnosed with it each year, and about 7,400 die from it. Assembly Majority Leader Crystal Peoples-Stokes and Sen. Joseph Addabbo Jr. introduced earlier versions in 2024 and carried them again in 2025. The Assembly passed the bill 144–0 on May 21, and the Senate passed it 56–0 on May 29, with support from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 4, 2025.
