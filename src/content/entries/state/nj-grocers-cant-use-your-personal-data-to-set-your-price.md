Starting Aug. 1, 2027, New Jersey bans sellers from using personal data—such as your location, browsing history or past purchases—to set your price for food, drinks, paper goods, cleaning supplies, health and beauty products, and pet supplies. The ban applies in stores and on delivery apps. Businesses that mainly prepare food or drinks for immediate consumption, including takeout, are not covered.

You don't need to do anything to be protected. Stores may still offer sales, publicly disclosed discounts for groups such as teachers or veterans, and voluntary loyalty programs, provided they follow the law's disclosure and uniform-terms rules. Starting Feb. 1, 2027, the state also pauses new use of electronic shelf labels for one year while it studies them; existing labels may be repaired or replaced.

As enacted, a violation is also a violation of the state's Consumer Fraud Act. The attorney general can sue for actual damages from negligent or worse violations and other court-ordered relief. A5738, introduced Oct. 29, would remove Consumer Fraud Act treatment and private lawsuits and require a 45-day chance to cure before the attorney general could sue.

Assembly members Chigozie Onyema, Annette Quijano, William Sampson, Shanique Speight, David Bailey and Rosy Bagolie carried the Assembly bills. Sens. Joe Cryan and Joe Lagana carried the Senate versions. Both chambers passed the measure June 30, 2026, mostly along party lines. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it July 23, 2026.
