If your brother or sister gets seriously ill, New York's Paid Family Leave lets you take time off to care for them. You keep your job, and you're paid 67% of your average weekly wage, up to $1,228.53 a week in 2026. You can take up to 12 weeks, all at once or a day at a time. The law counts biological and adopted siblings, half-siblings and stepsiblings.

To use it, get the family care forms from your employer, its insurance carrier or the state's website. Your sibling's health care provider has to certify the serious health condition. You must send the completed request to your employer's carrier within 30 days after your leave starts, or you can lose benefits.

When the state created Paid Family Leave in 2016, the first plan included siblings, but they were dropped from the final deal. Until this law, you could care for a sibling only if one of you had raised the other. Sen. Joseph Addabbo and Assemblymember Sandy Galef sponsored the bill to add siblings, and an earlier version had been filed in the 2019–2020 session. It passed the Senate 43–20 and the Assembly 113–35. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 1, 2021, and it took effect Jan. 1, 2023. Paid Family Leave is paid for by worker payroll contributions, and the sponsor's memo said the cost to state and local governments was expected to be minimal.
