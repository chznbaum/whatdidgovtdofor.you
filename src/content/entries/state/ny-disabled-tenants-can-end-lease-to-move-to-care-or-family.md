If you rent in New York and have a disability, you can end your lease early and stop owing rent when you need to move somewhere that fits your needs. That covers two situations: a doctor certifies you can no longer live on your own and you're moving in with a family member, or you've been offered a place in an adult care facility, a nursing home, subsidized housing, or cheaper housing built for seniors or people with disabilities. Your spouse or dependents living with you are covered too.

To use it, send your landlord written notice with a physician's certification. If you're moving to a facility or housing project, also include an executed lease or contract showing your admission or pending admission. Unlike seniors using the same law, tenants with disabilities do not need a notarized statement from the family member they are moving in with. The lease ends no sooner than 30 days after your next rent due date. A landlord who holds onto your belongings to collect later rent commits a misdemeanor, and no lease can waive this right. It applies to leases or rental agreements entered into on or after Oct. 3, 2019.

New York already gave this right to tenants 62 and older. Assembly Member Pamela Hunter, who had pushed an earlier version in 2017–18, and Sen. Luis Sepúlveda sponsored the bill to extend it to people with disabilities, who can be left paying rent on a home they can no longer use after an accident or illness. It passed the Assembly 144–0 and the Senate 60–0, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Oct. 3, 2019.
