If you live in one of 17 New Jersey counties and just welcomed a baby, a registered nurse or advanced practice nurse will come to your home for free, usually within two weeks. The nurse checks on you and the baby, helps with feeding, sleep and newborn care, and connects you to local help if you need it. Families who adopt or take in a foster or kinship child qualify, as do parents after a stillbirth or newborn loss. The visit is voluntary. You book it through Family Connects NJ, and saying no doesn't affect any other services you get.

The program started in January 2024. Atlantic, Burlington, Camden, Cape May, Monmouth and Salem counties joined in January 2026. In 2025, nurses referred 18% of the families they visited for medical attention.

State Sens. Teresa Ruiz and Joseph Vitale and Assembly members Shanique Speight, Valerie Vainieri Huttle and John Armato sponsored the 2021 law that created the program. It generally requires state-regulated health plans to cover the visits without copays or deductibles; high-deductible and catastrophic plans must use the lowest cost-sharing federal law allows. The law started the program with $2.75 million. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on July 29, 2021. Gov. Mikie Sherrill proposed almost $49 million for the program in the FY2027 budget. The enacted budget includes $48.63 million to bring the program to Union, Hunterdon, Morris and Warren counties in January 2027.
