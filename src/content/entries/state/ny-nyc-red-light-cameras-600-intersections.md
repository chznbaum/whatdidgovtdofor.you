Running a red light is a factor in many deadly crashes at city intersections. From 2023 to 2025, it was a factor in 22% of fatal intersection crashes in New York City. For years, state law allowed the city to put red light cameras at only 150 of its intersections. This law raised the limit to 600. A driver who runs a red light at a camera gets a ticket in the mail.

The city put the cameras in during 2026 and reached all 600 intersections in October. According to the city's Department of Transportation, red light running drops by more than 80% where cameras are installed. Injuries from T-bone crashes at those intersections fall about 70%, and injuries from rear-end crashes fall nearly 60%. You don't need to do anything to benefit; the cameras protect everyone crossing or driving through.

New York City needs Albany's permission to run its own traffic cameras. State Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assembly Member Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the bill. It passed the Senate 41–20 and the Assembly 100–40 in June 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that October. The program's authorization runs out on Dec. 1, 2027, unless the Legislature renews it.
