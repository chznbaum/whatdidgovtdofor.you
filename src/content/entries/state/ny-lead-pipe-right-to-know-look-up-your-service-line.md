The pipe that carries water from the street main into your home can be made of lead, and lead in drinking water is especially dangerous for children. Under this law, New York's covered public water systems must list every service line they serve, with its address and what it's made of: lead, galvanized requiring replacement, non-lead, or unknown. Every system with at least 15 year-round connections or 25 year-round residents is covered, as are certain systems that regularly serve at least 25 of the same people. Systems that still have lead, galvanized lines requiring replacement, or unknown lines must update their lists every year and report which lines they replaced.

You don't have to apply for anything. The state Department of Health has to post each inventory on its website within 120 days of getting it, and county health departments must link to those lists. For systems serving more than 10,000 and fewer than 2 million people, the state had to provide an address-searchable map—or link to the system's own—by Oct. 16, 2026. The Health Department's map was last updated on Oct. 6, 2026. New York City has its own service line map. A state audit found that a third of systems missed the first deadline in Oct. 2024, and many reported lots of unknown lines, so check again as systems update their lists.

Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assemblymember Amy Paulin carried the bill. The Senate passed it 57–4, and the Assembly passed it 105–35. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19, 2023. A follow-up law in 2024 matched it to federal rules and set the map deadline.
