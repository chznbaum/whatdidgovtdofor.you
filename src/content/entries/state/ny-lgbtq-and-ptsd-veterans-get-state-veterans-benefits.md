For decades the military pushed out gay, lesbian, bisexual and transgender troops with "bad paper" discharges. Other veterans got such discharges after behavior caused by PTSD, a brain injury or sexual assault in the service. Many New York benefits required an honorable discharge, so these veterans were shut out. A 2015 study by Sen. Brad Hoylman's office found at least 53 state veterans' benefits that depended on discharge status.

This law changes the discharge-status rule for listed state benefits. If you have other-than-honorable, general-under-honorable-conditions or uncharacterized service—not a bad-conduct or dishonorable discharge—and it stemmed from your sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, PTSD, traumatic brain injury or military sexual trauma, New York can treat your service as honorable for those benefits. You still must meet each benefit's other rules. The law reaches civil service exam fee waivers, tuition awards and the state veterans burial fund. You must apply with a complete military personnel file and a signed statement. Claims based on PTSD, brain injury or military sexual trauma also require medical evidence connecting the condition to your service and discharge. Your federal discharge papers do not change.

Hoylman first introduced the bill in 2015, and Assembly Member David Buchwald pushed earlier versions for years. Hoylman and Assembly Member Didi Barrett carried the final bill, which passed the Senate 61–0 and the Assembly 146–0 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on November 12, 2019. The state began taking applications in June 2020, and the law took effect that November.
