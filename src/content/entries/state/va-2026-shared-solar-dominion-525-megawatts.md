If you get your power from Dominion Energy, you can buy a subscription to a shared solar farm and get a credit on your monthly bill. You don't need to own a home or put panels on your roof. The first round of Virginia's shared solar program was capped at 200 megawatts and filled up. This law requires Dominion to open 525 more megawatts. Of that, 75 megawatts may go to projects that allocate more than 51% of their capacity to low-income customers.

You sign up through a company that runs a shared solar farm, not through Dominion. Residential applicants can't be turned away over a credit check. Low-income subscribers don't pay the program's minimum monthly bill and can't be charged early termination fees. Backers say subscribers usually save 10% or more on their bills. The law also tells state regulators to minimize costs shifted onto customers who don't subscribe.

Del. Rip Sullivan and Senate Majority Leader Scott Surovell carried identical bills, and Sullivan's office says they passed with support from both parties. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed HB 807 on April 13, 2026. Dominion awarded capacity to 27 new projects on July 1, but none is yet listed as in service.
