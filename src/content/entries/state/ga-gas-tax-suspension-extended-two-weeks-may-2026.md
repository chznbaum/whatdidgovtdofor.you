From May 20 through June 2, 2026, Georgia didn't collect its state excise tax on motor fuel. That's 33.3 cents on every gallon of gasoline and 37.3 cents on every gallon of diesel. The pause also covered propane, ethanol, natural gas and other fuels the tax applies to. Local sales taxes still applied. Drivers didn't have to apply for anything. Fuel distributors simply didn't send the state tax on fuel sold during those two weeks.

The order picked up where an earlier pause left off. Lawmakers had suspended the tax for 60 days in HB 1199, and that suspension ran out at 11:59 p.m. on May 19. On May 15, Gov. Brian Kemp declared a state of emergency over high prices and extended the pause for two more weeks, through Memorial Day travel. His office said regular gas was averaging $4.02 a gallon in Georgia at the time.

Most of Georgia's road money comes from the gas tax. To keep road funding whole, the state used surplus money to make up the taxes it didn't collect, according to the Georgia Budget and Policy Institute. Kemp suspended the tax again in late September, and later orders extended that pause through November 30.
