If a government officer violates your rights under the U.S. Constitution in New York, you can now sue them in state court. That includes federal agents, like ICE and Border Patrol officers, as well as state and local officials. You can ask for damages, including for emotional distress, and a court can order the officer to stop. You can also win punitive damages when the violation was malicious or reckless, and you can recover your lawyer's fees if you win.

This matters most for federal agents. Lawsuits against state and local officers were already possible in federal court, but a 2022 Supreme Court decision, Egbert v. Boule, largely closed the door on damage suits against federal immigration agents. The new law covers violations back to Jan. 1, 2025. For violations that happened before the law took effect, you can recover your actual losses but not punitive damages.

State Sen. Zellnor Myrie and Assemblymember Gabriella Romero introduced the New York Civil Rights Act in September 2025. Gov. Kathy Hochul put a version of it in her public protection budget bill. The Senate passed the bill 39–22 and the Assembly 93–47 on May 21, 2026, and Hochul signed it on May 27. The law took effect immediately.
