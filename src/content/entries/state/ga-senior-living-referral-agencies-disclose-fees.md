When a family looks online for an assisted living community or a personal care home for a parent, they often land on a referral service that feels like an independent adviser. Many of these agencies show only homes that pay to be in their network, and the home pays the agency a fee when your relative moves in. Families often never find out.

Since July 1, 2026, Georgia law requires these referral agencies to tell you about their services and their financial ties to the homes they recommend, including that a home pays them for the referral. They have to get you or your relative's representative to acknowledge that you received this disclosure. Without that acknowledgment, they can't collect a fee from the home. Agencies must also tell you that their list may not include every option and that you can end their services and use of your personal information. You don't need to apply; the disclosure rules apply whenever an agency makes a paid referral.

Sen. Shawn Still (R) sponsored the bill, and Rep. John LaHood (R) carried it in the House. Sens. Russ Goodman, Max Burns and Drew Echols, all Republicans, co-sponsored it. The Georgia Senior Living Association, which represents senior living providers, supported it, and the law limits how long an agency can claim a fee to 24 months. Both parties supported it: the Senate passed it 53–0 and the House 168–0. Gov. Brian Kemp (R) signed it on May 11, 2026.
