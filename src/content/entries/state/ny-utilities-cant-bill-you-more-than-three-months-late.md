If your New York utility waits more than three months after a billing period ends to send the first bill, it can’t charge you for that service. For homes, this covers electricity, gas, steam and qualifying water service. It covers small businesses’ gas and electricity if they meet the law’s size limits. Seasonal and short-term customers are excluded. A utility may still collect when the delay wasn’t its fault or the customer caused it. A residential customer who still owes must be offered a payment plan; the down payment is capped at half the debt or three months’ average bills, whichever is less.

Utility corporations with more than 15,000 customers must put 13 months of usage at your address on every residential bill, including prior residents’ usage. If you ask, they have 30 days to send billing amounts for the 13 months before the billing period. The three-month limit and usage history are automatic; only the billing-amount history requires a request.

Sen. Michelle Hinchey and Assemblymember Jonathan Jacobson sponsored the law after Central Hudson customers went months without accurate bills, then received large charges. The Senate passed the original bill 48–13 in June 2023, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. Under an agreement with Hochul, lawmakers passed a chapter amendment changing the cutoff from two months to three. The Senate approved it 44–14, and Hochul signed it on Feb. 7, 2024. Both votes drew support from both parties. The protections took effect June 19, 2024.
