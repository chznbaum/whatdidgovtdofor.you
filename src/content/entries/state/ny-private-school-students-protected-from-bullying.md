For 15 years, New York's Dignity for All Students Act protected only public school students from bullying. Kids at private and religious schools had no such guarantee. This law closes that gap. Students at those schools can't be bullied or harassed by other students at school, on the bus, or at school events. That includes online bullying and off-campus bullying that's likely to disrupt school.

Families don't need to apply. Every private school must adopt a plain-language anti-bullying policy, post it online or on its parent portal, and share it with staff, parents and students every year. A staff member who witnesses bullying or receives an oral or written report must tell the principal within one school day and file a written report within two more. The principal must investigate promptly, tell the student who was targeted what was found, and take steps to stop the bullying and protect anyone who reported it.

The law is named for Jack Reid, a 17-year-old who died by suicide in 2022 after a year of bullying at his boarding school. His parents, Elizabeth and Bill Reid, pushed for the bill. Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Michaelle Solages carried it. It passed the Senate 56–3 and then the Assembly in June 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that October.
