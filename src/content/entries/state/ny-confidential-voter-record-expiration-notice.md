In New York, domestic violence survivors and people who have survived a sex crime can ask their county board of elections to keep their voter registration record private. The public can't see or copy it, so an abuser can't look up the survivor's address that way. That protection lasts four years. Before this law, nothing required anyone to warn domestic violence survivors when it was about to end. Once it lapsed, their current address could become public again.

Now the board of elections must mail domestic violence survivors a notice at least six months before their confidentiality expires. The notice gives the expiration date and explains how to apply again. They don't have to sign up for the notice; it goes automatically if their record is confidential. The new notice rule does not cover other sex-crime survivors unless they also qualify as domestic violence victims. To renew, a survivor sends a new signed statement to the county board, in person or by mail.

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal sponsored the bill, and Sen. Michelle Hinchey carried it in the Senate. On June 1, 2026, both chambers passed it with no votes against: 60–0 in the Senate and 138–0 in the Assembly. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 27, 2026, and it took effect right away.
