Street drugs in Florida can contain dangerous substances, including fentanyl and xylazine, an animal sedative found in overdose deaths. Simple test products can show whether either is present. Before this law, using or possessing with intent to use a xylazine test could be charged as a first-degree misdemeanor, punishable by up to a year in jail and a $1,000 fine. Florida had exempted fentanyl-testing products in 2023, but not xylazine tests.

Since July 1, 2026, narcotic-drug-testing products used only to determine whether a controlled substance contains xylazine no longer count as drug paraphernalia. Fentanyl and fentanyl-analogue tests remain exempt under the earlier law. People can possess or use qualifying xylazine tests, and clinics, health groups and families can distribute them without a drug-paraphernalia charge. No application is required. The exemption does not cover products that measure quantity, weight or potency. The change only concerns the test product; it does not legalize possessing the controlled substance being tested.

Rep. Michelle Salzman, a Pensacola Republican, carried HB 477 in the House. Sen. Don Gaetz, a Niceville Republican, sponsored the identical SB 646 and moved the House bill through the Senate. Both chambers passed it without a single no vote, 116–0 in the House and 34–0 in the Senate. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on June 16, 2026.
