In New York, a licensed mental health professional who tries to change the sexual orientation of a patient under 18 is committing professional misconduct and can face discipline from the state. The law doesn't cover counseling for someone going through a gender transition. It also doesn't cover therapy that helps a young person explore their identity without trying to change their sexual orientation. Families don't need to do anything to be protected. A parent or teen can report a provider to the state's licensing authorities.

Assemblymember Deborah Glick and Sen. Brad Hoylman carried the ban. The Assembly first passed it in 2014, but for years the Senate never brought it to a vote. In January 2019, after Democrats took control of the Senate, it passed both chambers on the same day: 57–4 in the Senate and 141–7 in the Assembly. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on January 25, 2019.

The ban's future is now uncertain. On March 31, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 8–1 that Colorado's similar ban has to meet the strictest First Amendment test when it is applied to talk therapy. The Court sent that case back to the lower courts. New York's law is still on the books, but it could face the same kind of challenge.
