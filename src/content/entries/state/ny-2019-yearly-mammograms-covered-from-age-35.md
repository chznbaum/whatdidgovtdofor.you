If you're 35 to 39 and on a large group health plan regulated by New York, your plan must cover a mammogram every year when your doctor recommends one and the insurer determines it is medically necessary. Before this law, these plans had to cover one baseline mammogram between 35 and 39; the general annual requirement began at 40. Covered mammograms can't be charged against your deductible or carry coinsurance. You don't file a separate application: ask your doctor, and your insurer makes the medical-necessity decision.

The law is named for Shannon Saturno, a teacher from Babylon on Long Island. She was diagnosed with an aggressive breast cancer at 29, while she was pregnant, and she died young. Her family fought for the bill for more than two years. Donna Cioffi and Linda Bonanno of First Company Pink worked with Jean-Pierre and Martinez to build support.

Assemblywoman Kimberly Jean-Pierre first carried the bill in 2017. It passed the Assembly that year and again in 2018 but stalled in the Senate. In 2019 Sen. Monica Martinez carried it in the Senate. Both final votes were unanimous: 62–0 in the Senate on June 13 and 146–0 in the Assembly five days later. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 2, 2019. It applies to plans issued or renewed starting 30 days after he signed it.
