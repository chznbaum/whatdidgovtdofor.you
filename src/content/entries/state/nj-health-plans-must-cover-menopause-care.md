If you're a woman diagnosed with perimenopause or menopause in New Jersey, your health plan now has to cover the treatment you medically need. That includes hormone therapy (including bioidentical hormones), non-hormonal medicines for symptoms, mental health care, pelvic floor physical therapy, bone health screenings and medicines, recommended preventive care for conditions like osteoporosis, and counseling on managing menopause. Plans have to cover this care the same way they cover any other medical condition, and they have to tell you clearly what's covered.

You don't need to apply. The rule covers insurance regulated by the state, the health plans for state and school employees, and Medicaid. It reaches covered policies and contracts as they're delivered, issued, executed, or renewed after April 9, 2026. Individual Health Coverage Program plans follow on January 1, 2027. Many large employers pay their workers' claims directly ("self-funded" plans), and New Jersey insurance mandates generally don't reach those plans. If you're not sure which kind you have, ask your plan.

Assemblywoman Heather Simmons sponsored A5278 with Assembly members Roy Freiman and Lisa Swain. Sens. John Burzichelli and Angela McKnight sponsored its Senate companion, S4148. The enacted bill passed the Assembly 79–0 in June 2025 and the Senate 37–0 that December, with no votes against it in either chamber. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 9, 2026.
