If you're at higher risk of an overdose and your prescriber gives you an opioid painkiller, New York law says the first opioid prescription you get each year must come with a prescription for an opioid antagonist, a drug such as naloxone that can reverse an overdose. Three things trigger the rule: a history of substance use disorder, a daily dose of 90 morphine milligram equivalents or more, or taking opioids together with benzodiazepines or other sedative sleep drugs.

You don't have to ask or apply. The rule does not apply to prescriptions written in general hospitals, nursing homes, facilities licensed under Article 31 of the Mental Hygiene Law, or for patients under hospice care. The law requires the prescription to be written, but you still have to fill it. In 2023, KFF Health News reported that a state program covered naloxone copays of up to $40 for people with insurance.

Assemblymember Edward Braunstein first introduced the bill in the 2019–2020 session, and Sen. Pete Harckham, who chaired the Senate's substance abuse committee, carried it in the Senate. It passed the Assembly 144–3 in May 2021 and the Senate 51–12 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 30, 2021, and it took effect June 28, 2022.
