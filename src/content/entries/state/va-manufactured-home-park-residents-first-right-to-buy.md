If you rent a lot in a Virginia manufactured home park and the owner agrees to sell it to an outside buyer, you and your neighbors get the first chance to buy it. Sales to the owner's family and existing partial owners are exempt. Within five business days of signing a sales contract, the owner must notify tenants with valid lot rental agreements and the local government, giving the price and the main terms.

To use the right, residents form a group that documents that it represents more than half of the park's tenant households with valid lot rental agreements. The group has 15 days to say it intends to buy. It then has 60 days to offer the same price on substantially equivalent terms, and usually another 60 days to get financing and close. It may assign its right to the local government or a qualifying nonprofit. If residents pass, the local government gets 15 more days to step in. Whoever buys this way must keep the land a manufactured home park for at least 15 years, unless a majority of tenants with valid lot rental agreements consent in writing to conversion to another form of affordable housing. A tenant with a valid lot rental agreement can sue an owner who breaks the law. Damages include actual damages and statutory damages equal to 12 times the monthly lot rent—or a reasonable amount set by the court—plus reasonable lawyer's fees.

Del. Paul Krizek (D) carried the bill. It passed the House 71–27 and the Senate 21–19, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger (D) approved it on April 13, 2026. It took effect July 1, 2026.
