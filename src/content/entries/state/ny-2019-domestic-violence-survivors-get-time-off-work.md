If you're an employee in New York and a victim of domestic violence, your employer must give you reasonable time off. That includes medical care for injuries (yours or your child's), services from a shelter, program or rape crisis center, counseling, safety planning (including moving), and legal help, assisting a prosecution, or going to court. An employer can refuse only by showing that the time off would cause undue hardship to the business.

You should give advance notice when you can. If you can't, your employer may ask for proof afterward, such as a police report, a court order, or a note from a doctor or advocate. Your employer can have you use paid leave you already have; if you have none, the time can be unpaid. Your health insurance continues while you're out, and your employer must keep your situation confidential as far as the law allows.

Since 2009, New York's Human Rights Law has barred covered employers from discriminating against domestic violence victims. This 2019 law restated those protections in the law's employment section and added the duty to give time off. Versions of the time-off measure had been introduced every session since 2009; a 2010 version passed both chambers but was vetoed. Assemblymember Helene Weinstein sponsored the Assembly version, and Sen. Roxanne Persaud sponsored it in the Senate. It passed the Senate 59–0 and the Assembly 140–1 on May 14, 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 20, 2019, and it took effect that November 18.
