If you buy a ticket to a concert, game or show in New York, the seller has to show you the total price, including every fee you must pay, in the listing before you choose a seat. Fees have to be named and their dollar amounts shown, and they can't be displayed more prominently than the total. The price can't go up as you check out. You don't have to do anything: the rule applies to box offices, ticket sites and resale marketplaces.

The law also bans delivery fees for tickets sent electronically or printed at home. A seller can charge only the real, reasonable cost of mailing or shipping a paper ticket you chose. Resale sites must show the ticket's original price, and tickets initially offered to the public free, with no purchase required, can't be resold. Fines for using bots to scoop up tickets rose to $1,000 to $2,500 per violation, and to as much as $10,000 for a repeat offender.

Sen. James Skoufis sponsored the bill after his committee investigated ticketing practices in 2021, and Assemblymember Daniel O'Donnell carried it in the Assembly. It was a narrower version of Skoufis's earlier package. Both chambers passed it with no votes against, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 30, 2022. Lawmakers have to renew the state's ticket law periodically. The latest renewal keeps it in effect through June 30, 2027.
