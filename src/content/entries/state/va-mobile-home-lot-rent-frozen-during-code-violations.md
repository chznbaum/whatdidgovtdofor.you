If you rent a lot for your manufactured home in Virginia and the locality has cited your community for breaking zoning, building, or fire codes, or has found habitability problems, the owner can't raise your lot rent when your lease renews until the problem is resolved. A violation counts as resolved only when the locality says it was fixed or confirms that real progress is being made. Violations tied to homes or additions the owner doesn't control don't count.

If the owner raises your rent anyway, you can get back every extra dollar you paid while the violation was open. If you have to sue to recover it, the court can make the owner pay your attorney fees too. You don't need to apply. The protection is part of state law.

The law also requires the first page of a written lot lease to begin listing the security deposit, rent for each payment period, and one-time charges due before the lease starts or with the first rent payment. A park owner cannot collect another charge, fee, or deposit unless it is listed there or in a separate contract you and the owner sign.

Del. Paul Krizek was the bill's chief patron, joined by Dels. Dan Helmer, Rozia Henson, and Cia Price. It passed the House 61–35, passed the Senate 21–19 with changes, and the House agreed to those changes 65–34. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
