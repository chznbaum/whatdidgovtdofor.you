If an immediate family member, housemate or intimate partner is at risk of harming themselves or others and has access to guns, you can now ask a Virginia judge or magistrate for an emergency order that temporarily bars that person from buying, having or carrying guns. Virginia's “red flag” law used to let only prosecutors and police file. Since July 1, 2026, immediate family and household members, intimate partners, physicians, specified licensed mental health professionals and certain school officials can file too.

An emergency order ordinarily runs through the 14th day after it is issued, although state law allows limited extensions in some circumstances. At a hearing within 14 days, a court can issue a full order lasting up to 180 days. A prosecutor or police officer may ask the court to extend it for another period of up to 180 days, and the law does not limit the number of extensions. The judge must weigh evidence such as recent violence or threats, protective-order violations, substance abuse and recent attempts to buy guns. Cases can now be heard in general district and juvenile and domestic relations courts, not just circuit court, and petitions involving minors go to juvenile court. Guns belonging to someone else can be returned to their lawful owner if the owner shows they will be stored safely and kept from the person under the order. Knowingly making a materially false statement during the petitioning process is a Class 1 misdemeanor.

Del. Rip Sullivan carried the bill in the House, and Sen. Creigh Deeds carried an identical Senate bill. The House passed it 62–34 and the Senate passed it 21–19. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
