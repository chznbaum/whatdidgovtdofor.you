New York's Human Rights Law makes it illegal for a school to deny a qualified student access to its facilities, or to permit harassment, because of race, religion, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin and other protected traits. This 2019 law states plainly that the protection covers public schools, school districts, BOCES, and public colleges and universities, not just private schools.

That matters because of a 2012 ruling by the state's top court. It held that public schools weren't covered, and the state Division of Human Rights had to close more than 70 open complaints against them. After that, public school families could file a grievance with the state education commissioner, but they had no forum like the one private school students had to win compensation for harm. Now a student or parent can file a complaint with the Division of Human Rights, which can investigate alleged discrimination and harassment tied to a protected trait at public schools.

Assemblymember Erik Dilan and Sen. Shelley Mayer carried the bill. Versions of it had been introduced in Albany since the 2009–2010 session. It passed the Assembly 142–0 in February 2019 and the Senate 61–0 that April. Gov. Andrew Cuomo had proposed the same change in his 2014 State of the State agenda; he signed it on July 25, 2019, and it took effect right away.
