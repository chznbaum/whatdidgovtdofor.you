If your health plan is regulated by New York and covers physician services, it has to include outpatient benefits for care—within each provider's legal scope—from a licensed mental health counselor, marriage and family therapist, or psychoanalyst. The law also broadened coverage for licensed clinical social workers by eliminating a separate three-year post-license experience requirement. The rule applies to individual and group insurance policies and to nonprofit health-plan contracts. It took effect for policies or contracts issued, renewed, modified, altered, or amended starting January 1, 2023. You don't have to apply. Use a licensed provider your plan covers.

The sponsors argued that because state law didn't name these licensed professionals, insurers could refuse to pay for their care. That hit hardest in rural areas with few psychiatrists or psychologists. In 2019, 40 of New York's 62 counties were mental health shortage areas, all of them outside New York City and Long Island.

Assemblymember Harry Bronson carried the bill, and versions of it go back to the 2011–2012 session. Gov. Andrew Cuomo vetoed it in 2019. Sen. Timothy Kennedy sponsored it in the Senate, which passed it 61–0 in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 30, 2022. As part of a deal she made when signing, a 2023 follow-up law took creative arts therapists off the list.
