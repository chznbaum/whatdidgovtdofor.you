If you own and live in your Minnesota home, your homestead credit refund on 2026 property taxes is 14.88% bigger than the usual formula gives. This is the 2025 Homestead Credit Refund, which is based on your 2025 income. The Department of Revenue says the average refund rises by $213, to about $1,641. About 588,000 homeowners are forecast to claim the refund for 2026.

To qualify, you must be a full- or part-year Minnesota resident with 2025 household income under $142,490, and you must have owned and lived in the home on January 2, 2026. The home must be classified as your homestead, and you can't owe back property taxes. If you already filed, you don't need to do anything, and the state adds the increase for you. If you haven't filed yet, you can still claim the refund until August 16, 2027, including for free on the department's website. Only the regular refund goes up, not the special refund.

Property taxes rose nearly 7% statewide, and in April 2026 Sen. Grant Hauschild (DFL) proposed a one-time increase in the refund. It became part of the omnibus tax law carried by Sen. Ann Rest (DFL) and Rep. Greg Davids (R). Both parties backed it: the bill passed the House 126–8 and the Senate 52–15. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 27, 2026. The roughly $125 million cost comes from the state's general fund, and the income limit means the money goes to low- and middle-income homeowners.
