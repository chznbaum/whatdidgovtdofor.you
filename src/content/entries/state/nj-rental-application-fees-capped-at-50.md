If you're applying to rent an apartment in a New Jersey building with at least three units, the landlord can't charge you more than $50 to apply. The cap covers the application fee and any similar fee, so a landlord can't get around it by adding separate charges for credit checks, background checks or processing. State officials have also warned that charging fees for units that aren't available, or taking far more applications than a landlord needs just to collect fees, may break the state's consumer fraud law.

You don't need to do anything to get the protection. It covers most rentals, but not homes in one- or two-family houses, and it doesn't cover real estate agents unless the agent is the landlord. Starting in 2027, the state can raise the cap each year to keep up with prices in the New York area, and the Division of Consumer Affairs posts the current limit. If you were overcharged, you can file a complaint with the Division of Consumer Affairs. Fines run up to $500 for a first offense, $750 for a second and $1,000 after that, and the extra money is taken out of the fine and paid back to you.

Assembly members Linda Carter and Yvonne Lopez sponsored the bill in the Assembly, and Sens. Brian Stack and Raj Mukherji carried it in the Senate. The Senate passed it 25–14 and the Assembly gave final approval 49–25 on Jan. 12, 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, 2026, and it took effect May 1, 2026.
