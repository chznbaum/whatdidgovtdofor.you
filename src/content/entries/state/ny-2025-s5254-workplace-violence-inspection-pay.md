If you work for a public employer in New York, such as a state agency, a city, county or town, a school district, or a public authority, your employer can't cut your pay for helping keep your workplace safe from violence. That covers three things: walking along when the state Labor Department inspects your workplace, taking part in a workplace violence risk evaluation, and joining the yearly review of your employer's violence prevention program.

New York has required public employers to plan for preventing workplace violence since 2006, and workers were understood to take part in these checks on paid time. According to the bill's sponsor, some employers had started making workers use their own leave for this, which discouraged them from speaking up about unsafe conditions. The new law spells out that taking part can't reduce your wages. It applies automatically, with no form to file.

Sen. Chris Ryan sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Harry Bronson, who chairs the Assembly Labor Committee, carried it in the Assembly. Bronson said he worked with Ryan and the Civil Service Employees Association to pass it. It passed the Senate 61–0 in March 2025 and the Assembly 146–0 that June, with a Republican, Sen. Dean Murray, among the cosponsors. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 5, 2025, as part of a Labor Day package of worker protections, and it took effect immediately.
