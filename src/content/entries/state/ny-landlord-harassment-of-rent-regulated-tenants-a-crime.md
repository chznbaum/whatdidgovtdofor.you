If you live in a rent-regulated New York apartment, your landlord commits a crime by deliberately trying to force you out through conduct that makes the home unlivable, risks your health or safety, or disturbs your peace. Examples include cutting heat or hot water, exposing you to hazardous materials, or using construction to make the building unbearable. The crime is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 364 days in jail. Under the 2019 law, systematically targeting two or more apartments, or repeating the misdemeanor within five years, is a class E felony punishable by up to four years. A good-faith eviction case does not count as harassment.

No application is needed. The protection also covers family members and others who lawfully succeed to a rent-regulated tenancy.

Attorney General Eric Schneiderman drafted and proposed the bill in 2017. Sen. Liz Krueger and Assemblymember Joseph Lentol carried it through two legislative sessions. After Letitia James became attorney general, her office continued it as a program bill. The Attorney General's office found that no landlord had been convicted under the old law in nearly two decades. The Assembly passed it 108–39, the Senate 42–17, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on December 3, 2019. It took effect in May 2020. A 2026 budget law later added a class D aggravated crime, punishable by up to seven years, for targeting at least three tenants across at least two buildings or repeating the first-degree crime within five years.
