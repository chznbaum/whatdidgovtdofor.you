If you're approved to visit someone in a Virginia state correctional facility, your in-person visit now has to last at least two hours. Staff can cut it short only if you or the person you're visiting asks, or if there's an active security problem. If your primary residence is at least 150 driving miles from the facility, the Department of Corrections must give you longer or extra visits when space and security allow.

The law also limits when the prison can take visits away. Visits can be suspended only for something that happens during a visit and poses a direct, serious threat to people's safety or the prison's security. They can't be suspended just because the person inside was disciplined for something unrelated. If visits are suspended, there's a set timeline and process to appeal. These protections apply to approved visitors and eligible incarcerated people at state correctional facilities.

Families had complained of short visits cut shorter by dress-code disputes, entry denials, and long bans. Sen. Angelia Williams Graves and Del. Bonita Anthony carried matching bills to protect family visits, which advocates say make prisons safer. The two chambers settled their differences in a conference committee. The law also directed the department to convene a work group to recommend further changes by October 1, 2026. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both bills on April 13, 2026, and they took effect July 1.
