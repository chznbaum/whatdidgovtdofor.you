If a fire or other casualty damages your Virginia rental badly enough that you have to move out, your landlord cannot simply end your lease on 14 days' notice after January 1, 2027. Before giving notice, the landlord must meet with you, or make a reasonable effort to, and discuss the damage and possible alternatives. The landlord must check whether a substantially similar unit in the same complex will be available within a reasonable time and, if so, offer it under the terms of your current lease. The notice period grows from 14 days to 21. These steps do not apply if the landlord determines that the damage resulted from your violation of the law's tenant-maintenance duties.

If you receive a termination notice, you have seven days to ask in writing for the landlord to reevaluate the damage and whether the home is habitable. The landlord must involve you in that review. If you move out and end the lease yourself, you also have 21 days, up from 14, to send written notice. If the lease ends, security deposits and prepaid rent are handled under existing rules, and rent is prorated to the date of the damage.

Del. Adele McClure carried the bill after a similar 2025 measure was vetoed by Gov. Glenn Youngkin. The Senate passed it 21–19, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican no. The House accepted the Senate amendment 66–32, with three Republicans joining Democrats. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed the law on May 14, 2026.
