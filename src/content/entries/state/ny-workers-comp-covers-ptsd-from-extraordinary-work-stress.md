If you are covered by New York's workers' compensation system and a distinct event or events at work cause PTSD, acute stress disorder or major depressive disorder, the Board cannot reject your claim just because that stress is normal for your occupation. A physical injury is not required for this protection. You still need medical evidence based on the applicable DSM criteria and must show that extraordinary work-related stress from the event caused the disorder.

Before June 4, 2025, non-first-responders could already file and win mental-stress claims, but they generally had to show stress greater than similarly situated workers experienced in a normal work environment. The new rule removes that comparison for these three diagnoses when its other requirements are met. It applies to claims still pending before the Board on June 4 regardless of the accident date, but not to Board decisions already final before then. File an Employee Claim (Form C-3), as with any other work injury or illness.

Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assemblymember Karines Reyes sponsored the 2024 law and 2025 amendment. Versions had been introduced since 2017, and Gov. Kathy Hochul vetoed one in 2022. Hochul signed the 2024 bill subject to an agreed chapter amendment specifying the covered diagnoses. The follow-up passed the Senate 52–11 with votes from both parties, and Hochul signed it Feb. 14, 2025.
