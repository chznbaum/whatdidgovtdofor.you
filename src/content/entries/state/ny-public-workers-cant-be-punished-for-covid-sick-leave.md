If you work for a public employer in New York, such as the state, a city, a county or a school district, your employer can't fire you, discipline you, or take other action against you because you used sick leave or comp time for COVID-19. That covers time spent in quarantine, recovering, getting medical care, or dealing with anything else tied to a COVID diagnosis or exposure. It also bars your employer from labeling you "chronically absent" for those days. You don't need to apply. The law applies on its own and reaches back to absences on or after March 1, 2020.

The law grew out of New York City's jails. Sen. Diane Savino said 893 correction officers who tested positive and had to use sick time under Health Department rules were then marked chronically absent. She said the label can hurt a worker's pay, promotions and hours, and can make layoffs more likely.

Savino and Assemblymember Peter Abbate Jr. carried the bill. It passed the Senate 63–0 in March 2021 and the Assembly 149–1 that April. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 1, 2021.
