New York's paid family leave gives eligible workers up to 12 weeks of paid, job-protected time off to bond with a new child, care for a family member with a serious health condition, or help when a family member is deployed abroad on active military service. Under current rules, most full-time employees must work 26 consecutive weeks for one employer, while most part-time employees qualify after 175 days. Construction workers who move among contractors can repeatedly lose their progress toward eligibility.

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, construction employees who work for multiple employers under a collective bargaining agreement can count qualifying weeks across covered signatory employers. A week counts only when you worked that employer's normal workweek. Once 26 of the previous 39 weeks qualify, you are eligible while working for a covered employer, and the employer immediately before your leave is responsible for the claim. If you are laid off and receive unemployment benefits, you can qualify upon returning to covered work if you still meet the test. Existing union health plan or fund benefits stay in force during leave as though you kept working.

Assemblymember Harry Bronson and Sen. Leroy Comrie sponsored the original bill and the 2026 chapter amendment in their chambers. The original passed the Assembly 138–10 in April 2025 and the Senate 56–3 in June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19 with an agreement to revise its implementation. The follow-up amendment passed the Assembly 139–0 and the Senate 55–1 in January 2026; Hochul signed it on Feb. 13.
