If a work injury leaves you permanently and totally disabled in Minnesota, your employer's workers' comp has to pay for nursing services from someone in your household. Before this law, it had to pay a family member who provided those services. Now it also has to pay someone who lives with you but isn't legally family, like a partner you aren't married to. The law also keeps a late filing from wiping out that caregiver's claim to be paid.

For injuries on or after Oct. 1, 2025, up to $10 million of a workers' comp claim and any later award is protected from being seized to pay debts. The old limit was $1 million. The law also makes it harder for construction employers to falsely report that they have no employees and underpay insurance premiums. Since Jan. 1, 2026, a builder who buys a policy reporting no employees has to sign a statement saying so, report any employees it hires within 60 days, and give a copy of the policy to businesses it contracts with.

These changes came from the state's Workers' Compensation Advisory Council, which includes both labor and business members. Rep. Dave Baker, a Republican, and Sen. Jennifer McEwen, a Democrat, carried the bill. The final version passed the House 134–0 and the Senate 53–12, and Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 22, 2025.
