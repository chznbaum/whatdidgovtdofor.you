If you're hurt while held in a New York prison or local jail, you keep the normal deadline to sue and also get at least two years after your release to file. This covers custody periods ending on or after Dec. 19, 2025. Claims whose deadline had already passed by then are not revived.

At state prisons and local jails, officials must make a good-faith effort to give the attorney general all relevant video and audio within 72 hours after a death. Recordings found later must follow within 24 hours. For deaths in state prison, families must be told the cause from the final autopsy, and DOCCS must post the death online within 48 hours after telling them. Copies of autopsy reports sent to state oversight officials must include photographs and X-rays. DOCCS must plan and begin installing fixed cameras across its prisons and transport vehicles, as funding allows, and keep footage at least 60 days. The Correctional Association, an independent watchdog, can visit on 24 hours' notice, and its hotline calls can't be monitored. The State Commission of Correction grows from three to five members, one of whom must have been incarcerated in New York.

Sen. Julia Salazar and Assemblymember Erik Dilan carried the bill after prison staff killed Robert Brooks and Messiah Nantwi. It passed in June 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. As part of the deal, lawmakers passed changes in early 2026 that dropped some of their original plans, including a larger, more independent commission.
