New York law barred unauthorized deductions from pay, but some courts read “deduction” narrowly. They allowed a claim when an employer held back part of a paycheck, but not when it paid nothing at all. That left a worker whose boss kept everything without the same Labor Law claim.

The law closed that gap for nonpayment after August 19, 2021. It says there is no exception to civil liability for an employer’s unauthorized failure to pay earned wages, benefits or wage supplements. A worker who wins can recover the unpaid amount, reasonable lawyer’s fees and interest, plus extra damages equal to 100% of unpaid wages unless the employer proves it acted in good faith. No application is needed.

Article 6 generally has a six-year filing period. Courts disagree about older conduct: the First Department says this amendment is not retroactive, while the Fourth Department says it is. How far back this specific claim reaches can therefore depend on where the case is heard; the rule is clear for nonpayment after the effective date.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Jo Anne Simon carried it in the Assembly. An earlier version passed the Senate in 2020 but died in the Assembly. In 2021 the Senate passed it 51–12, with every Democrat and eight Republicans voting yes. The Assembly passed it 138–10. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 19, 2021, and it took effect immediately.
