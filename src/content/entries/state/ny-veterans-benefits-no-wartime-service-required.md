Some New York veterans' benefits used to go only to people who served during an official period of war, such as the Persian Gulf conflict. If your service fell outside those periods, you did not count for these programs. This law removed that wartime-service bar for several benefits, so peacetime service no longer shuts you out on that ground.

Three things changed. Veteran assistance, a safety-net program for veterans and certain family members who meet its financial and other eligibility rules, now uses a definition that does not require wartime service. The law also includes veterans with a qualifying condition or a discharge under the old anti-LGBT policies, as long as the discharge was not bad conduct or dishonorable. Severely disabled veterans can apply for special disability license plates without proving wartime service. And state and local veterans' service agencies must help veterans and their families without limiting that help to wartime veterans. Local agencies can also help with a discharge-upgrade application.

The law did not enroll anyone automatically or remove other qualifications. You still have to apply through the agency that handles each benefit. If you were turned away solely because of when you served, you can apply again; a state or local veterans' service agency can help.

Assemblymember Kimberly Jean-Pierre and Sen. James Skoufis sponsored the bill. Assemblymember Stacey Pheffer Amato and Republican Sens. Jacob Ashby and Pamela Helming cosponsored it. The Senate passed it 60–0 in June 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Veterans Day, Nov. 11, 2024. It took effect right away.
