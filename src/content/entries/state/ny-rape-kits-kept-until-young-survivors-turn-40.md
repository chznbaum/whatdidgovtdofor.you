After a sexual assault, a hospital can collect evidence in a kit even if the survivor isn't ready to go to the police. New York currently requires that evidence to be stored for 20 years. Starting Dec. 2, 2027, the default storage period will last for 20 years or until the survivor's 40th birthday, whichever is later. That gives people whose kits were collected as children or teens more time to decide whether to have the evidence tested.

Starting then, the Office of Victim Services cannot destroy a kit at the direction of a parent, guardian, conservator, or other party when the survivor is a minor, a vulnerable elderly person, or a person deemed incompetent or physically disabled under state law. A minor who had capacity to consent to the exam independently can request destruction or decide whether the kit is reported. If the survivor was under 18 at the time of the assault, the office must try to contact them when they turn 18. Hospitals must explain the storage period when evidence is collected, and the office must try to reach survivors at least 90 days before the storage deadline. The storage protections do not require an application.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the changes in her 2026 State of the State and executive budget. Assembly Member Amy Paulin and Sen. Andrew Gounardes carried the bills; Assembly Member Andrew Hevesi and Sen. Rob Rolison cosponsored their chamber's versions. The measure passed the Assembly 141–0 and the Senate 54–0. Hochul signed it on June 5, 2026.
