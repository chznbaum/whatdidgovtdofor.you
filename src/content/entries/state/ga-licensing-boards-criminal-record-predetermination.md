If you have a criminal record and want a job that needs a covered Georgia license, you'll be able to find out early whether your record will get in the way. Starting July 1, 2027, you can ask the licensing board to decide before you spend time and money on classes or training. You can ask even while you're still incarcerated. If the board finds you eligible, that answer is binding as long as you meet the other licensing requirements and your submitted criminal record was accurate and remains unchanged.

The law covers more than 40 licensing boards. A covered board can turn you down based on your record only for a conviction directly related to the work, and it has to show that link. It can no longer rely on vague “moral turpitude” standards. The rules generally bar boards from relying on sealed or pardoned convictions, completed first-offender cases, or misdemeanors more than five years old, with exceptions for specified offenses. Before denying you over your record, a board must offer a hearing where you can present evidence of rehabilitation, including work history or treatment. It must also put its reasons in writing.

Sen. Brian Strickland, a Republican, sponsored the bill and called it a several-year effort. House Majority Leader Chuck Efstration presented the final House changes, while Rep. Tyler Paul Smith served as the House sponsor and chaired the committee that advanced it. Democratic and Republican senators cosponsored the measure. It passed the House 160–3 and the Senate 50–0. Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 12, 2026.
