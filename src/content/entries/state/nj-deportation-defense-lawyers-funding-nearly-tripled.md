If you live in New Jersey, are held by immigration agents or are facing deportation, and can't afford a lawyer, the state may pay for one. The Detention and Deportation Defense Initiative has offered free lawyers since 2018. The state budget for the year that began July 1, 2026, nearly tripled its funding, from $8.2 million to $20.2 million. The state says the money covers every low-income detainee at Newark's Delaney Hall who doesn't already have a lawyer.

You or a family member can ask for help through a referral form from the state's Office of New Americans. Unaccompanied children can call 201-305-9217. A referral doesn't guarantee you'll get a lawyer. In November the state allocated the money among 10 providers, including Legal Services of New Jersey, the state Office of the Public Defender and Church World Service. The new groups are still hiring attorneys, and advocates say some requests have waited weeks for an answer.

Gov. Mikie Sherrill announced the $12 million increase on June 4, 2026, with Senate President Nick Scutari and Assembly Speaker Craig Coughlin, after ICE detentions in New Jersey surged, including at Delaney Hall. Lawmakers included the expansion in the budget Sherrill signed on June 30. The Department of Human Services, led by Commissioner Stephen Cha, runs the program through the Office of New Americans.
