When a lawsuit or criminal charge targets abortion or gender-affirming care, New York law creates a way to respond. If the case is based on your legally protected health activity, you can bring a claim in New York State Supreme Court within six years against the person or entity that started it.

Protected activity includes receiving or trying to receive abortion or gender-affirming care, wherever the patient is, as long as the activity does not violate New York law. It also includes providing that care as a New York-licensed professional who is physically in the state, offering coverage through a New York-based insurer, or helping someone while physically in New York. A friend who drives a loved one to a clinic while in New York can be covered. You do not sign up in advance.

If you prove unlawful interference, you can recover compensatory damages plus costs and attorneys’ and expert-witness fees. If the case against you was started or continued to harass, intimidate, punish, or otherwise maliciously inhibit rights protected in New York, the court can award additional damages of up to three times your compensatory damages.

Sen. Alessandra Biaggi and Assemblymember Chris Burdick sponsored the 2022 law; the Senate passed it 41–22 and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it June 13. Sen. Shelley Mayer sponsored the 2023 damages amendment. Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Harry Bronson sponsored the 2025 update that supplied today’s definition of protected activity; Hochul signed it Dec. 19, 2025.
