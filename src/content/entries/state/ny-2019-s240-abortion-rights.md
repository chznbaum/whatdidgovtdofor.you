New York's Reproductive Health Act puts the right to an abortion into state law. You can get an abortion for any reason through the 24th week of pregnancy. After that, it is still allowed if the fetus isn't viable or if the patient's life or health is at risk. The law also protects your right to choose or refuse birth control and sterilization. It moved abortion out of the state's criminal code and into its public health law, and it lets any licensed health professional provide abortions if that falls within the scope of their license.

You don't need to apply for anything. The state Attorney General's office says the right covers people of all ages. It has stayed in force since the U.S. Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade in 2022.

Sen. Liz Krueger and Assemblymember Deborah Glick sponsored the bill. The Assembly passed it several times over the years. Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie said that for 12 years, the Republicans who led the state Senate kept it from coming to a floor vote. After Democrats took over the Senate under Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins, both chambers passed it on Jan. 22, 2019, the 46th anniversary of Roe. The Senate vote was 38–24. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it the same day.
