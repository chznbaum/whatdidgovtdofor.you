Georgia's First Offender Act lets a judge give some first-time defendants probation or a fine instead of a conviction. Until this year, their court records usually stayed public until they finished the sentence, often for years. Private background check companies could copy those records and keep them in their own files, which made it harder to get a job or rent a home. Starting July 1, 2026, the court restricts and seals the records at sentencing. Private background check companies can't get them.

Sealing is automatic for people sentenced on or after July 1, 2026. People sentenced earlier whose first offender status hasn't been revoked can ask the court to seal their records. The Georgia Justice Project has petition forms. Judges, prosecutors, public defenders, and police can still see sealed records, and so can gun license and gun purchase checks. If you don't finish your probation, a judge can unseal the records and enter a conviction. Some charges, such as DUI and serious violent felonies, don't qualify for first offender treatment at all.

Rep. Leesa Hagan (R-Lyons) wrote the bill, and the House passed it 172–0 in 2025. Sen. Bill Cowsert carried it in the Senate, which passed a revised version 47–0 in March 2026. The House then approved that version 166–0. The Georgia Justice Project pushed for the change. Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 5, 2026.
