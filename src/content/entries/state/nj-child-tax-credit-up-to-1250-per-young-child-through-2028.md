New Jersey gives families a tax credit for each child under 6 if their taxable income is $80,000 or less. For the 2026, 2027 and 2028 tax years, this law raises that credit by 25% in every income bracket. A family with taxable income of $30,000 or less now gets $1,250 per young child instead of $1,000. In the top bracket, from $60,000 to $80,000, the credit rises from $200 to $250.

You claim it on your New Jersey income tax return. The credit is refundable, so if it's more than the tax you owe, the state pays you the difference. You can claim it with a Social Security number or an ITIN, and it doesn't count as income for state benefit programs. Married taxpayers generally must file jointly, except those filing federally as head of household or surviving spouse. The first higher credits come on 2026 returns filed in 2027, and the amounts drop back after 2028.

Senate Majority Leader Teresa Ruiz and Sens. Angela McKnight and Paul Sarlo carried the bill in the Senate. Assembly members Verlina Reynolds-Jackson, Eliana Pintor Marin, Marisa Sweeney, Maureen Rowan, Margie Donlon and Michael Venezia sponsored the identical Assembly bill. It passed 39–1 in the Senate and 79–0 in the Assembly on June 30, 2026, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it that day with the state budget. The Legislature's budget office expects it to cost about $52 million in its first year. The broader budget package raised about $750 million through limits on corporate and high-income business loss deductions and a fee on certain large employers, helping preserve school and transit funding.
