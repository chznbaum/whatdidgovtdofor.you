Virginia is replacing every SNAP EBT card with a chip card you can tap or insert instead of swipe. Skimmers hidden in readers can copy a magnetic stripe and drain the account. A chip creates a new encrypted code for each purchase, making copied data far harder to use. Federal reimbursement for stolen SNAP benefits ended in December 2024, so Virginia usually cannot replace the money.

You do not have to apply. The card comes by mail to the address your local social services office has on file. Activate it and set a new PIN. Your old card stops working then or 60 days after mailing. Benefits and payment dates do not change. The first 145,300 cards went in September to high-theft localities, including Norfolk, Portsmouth, Richmond and Chesterfield. Remaining customers are due cards by Dec. 31.

State Sen. Barbara Favola requested funding for chip cards in 2025. Gov. Glenn Youngkin then proposed $3 million, which lawmakers approved. Commissioner James Williams said his department would buy the cards and roll them out in 2026; Commissioner Duke Storen’s department carried out the launch. The $8.25 million project also uses existing card-contract and payment-accuracy funds. In Lynchburg, reported October theft was just under $20,000 as of Oct. 29, down from nearly $63,000 in August; all October reports involved old magnetic-stripe cards.
