New upholstered furniture and mattresses sold in New York can't contain added flame retardants from four classes: halogenated, organophosphorus, organonitrogen and nanoscale chemicals. The plastic case or stand of a TV, computer monitor or other screen can't contain added organohalogen flame retardants. New York was the first state to ban these chemicals in electronics cases, which Toxic-Free Future found in every TV it tested in 2019. The sponsors pointed to links between these chemicals and cancer, infertility and harm to children's development, and to the toxic smoke they can add when a home burns.

You don't have to do anything. The rules apply to sellers of new products, and used furniture isn't covered. Since Dec. 1, 2025, upholsterers can't use these chemicals when they repair or recover your couch. Stores that sell banned items can be fined up to $1,000 a day, and up to $2,500 a day for a repeat violation. The state let mattresses using specified modacrylic or aramid fibers stay on sale until June 15, 2026.

Sen. Todd Kaminsky and Assemblymember Steve Englebright carried the Family and Fire Fighter Protection Act, which passed the Senate 63–0 with Republican cosponsors. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 31, 2021, after negotiating changes. Kaminsky and Englebright then passed those changes in January 2022. They pushed the start date back to Dec. 1, 2024, and created a compliance-certificate process that sellers can use to avoid liability when they rely in good faith on a manufacturer's assurance.
