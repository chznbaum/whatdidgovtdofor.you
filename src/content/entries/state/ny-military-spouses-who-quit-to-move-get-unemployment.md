Military families move when the service sends them, and the spouse at home often has to leave a job to go along. Until this law, New York's unemployment rules didn't name that situation as a good reason to quit. Now the law does: if your spouse is in the U.S. armed forces and a military transfer takes the family somewhere you can't commute from, leaving your job doesn't disqualify you from unemployment benefits.

You apply for unemployment the usual way through the state Department of Labor and still have to meet the normal rules, like having enough past earnings and looking for work. The benefits come from the state's shared unemployment fund instead of being charged to your old employer, unless that employer is one that reimburses the fund directly.

Sen. Robert Ortt (R) sponsored the bill in the Senate, where similar versions had been introduced every session since 2013. Assemblymember Mathylde Frontus (D) carried it in the Assembly and called it a fix for "an oversight in our unemployment system." It passed the Senate 63–0 in May 2021 and passed the Assembly that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul (D) signed it on Veterans Day, Nov. 11, 2021, and it took effect right away. The Defense Department says that made New York the 48th state to offer unemployment pay to military spouses.
