If you get SNAP or cash assistance in New York, your benefits come on an EBT card. Thieves have stolen benefits by copying cards at store card readers, a trick called skimming. This law requires the automated phone line for EBT cards to let you permanently cancel your card, lock it, or unlock it. That way, someone without the app can still shut off a card they think has been stolen or copied.

You don't have to apply; the requirement applies automatically to the EBT support line. The state separately launched app-based card locking in February 2024, and locking moved to the ebtEDGE app and portal when New York changed EBT vendors that May. Before then, the state had paid back more than $20 million in stolen benefits through a federal replacement program whose authority later ended for thefts after December 20, 2024.

Assembly Member Sam Berger of Queens sponsored the Assembly bill, and Sen. Simcha Felder carried the Senate version. It passed the Assembly 144–0 in April 2024 and the Senate 61–0 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 22, 2024. Berger and Felder then sponsored matching chapter-amendment bills. The enacted Senate version dropped a plan to lock cards only for out-of-state or online purchases and gave the state a full year to set up the phone option. The requirement took effect in November 2025.
