If you are an incoming or prospective undergraduate applying for financial aid at a New York college, the aid letter you get back must now show your estimated net cost: what attendance will cost after subtracting the grants and scholarships you never have to repay. It also has to list the loan options you could take, kept separate from the free money, so loans can't make an offer look bigger than it is.

You don't have to ask for it. When a New York college, vocational school, or other school with a state-approved program responds to an incoming or prospective undergraduate's aid application, it must provide New York's standard award letter, which the Department of Financial Services designs. The letter already had to show costs for each year of the degree, every grant and loan, and the school's graduation, borrowing, and loan default rates.

State Sen. Andrew Gounardes argued that the standard letter, created in 2016, didn't require a net price, even though more than half of New York students graduate with significant debt. The state's form already had a "What you will pay" section, and the law now makes it a requirement. His first version passed the Senate in 2024 but died in the Assembly. In 2025 he and Assembly Member Steven Raga brought it back, with Assembly Members George Alvarez and Nader Sayegh as cosponsors. It passed both chambers without a single no vote. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 5, 2025, and it took effect 30 days later.
