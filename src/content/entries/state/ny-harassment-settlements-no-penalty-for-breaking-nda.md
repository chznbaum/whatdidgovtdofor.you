If you settle an employment claim involving unlawful discrimination, discriminatory harassment or retaliation in New York, your release is unenforceable if the deal sets damages in advance or makes you return settlement money for breaking a claim-related nondisclosure or nondisparagement clause. It also cannot make you say you were never subjected to the conduct. An employer may still seek money for actual harm it proves from a breach.

These protections cover employees, job applicants and independent contractors and apply to agreements signed on or after Nov. 17, 2023. If claim confidentiality is your preference, you generally have up to 21 days to consider it, may sign sooner, and have at least 7 days after signing to revoke it. But a claim already filed in court or an administrative proceeding carries a separate, full 21-day waiting period. Confidentiality cannot block you from a government investigation or from giving facts needed for unemployment, Medicaid or other public benefits. Separately, contracts restricting disclosure of future discrimination claims must say you may speak with listed authorities, including the Attorney General.

Sen. Nathalia Fernandez sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assembly Member Nily Rozic sponsored the companion bill. Earlier versions had been introduced since 2015. The Senate passed it 58–3 in March 2023 with votes from both parties, and the Assembly passed it that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 17, 2023.
