More than a decade after Superstorm Sandy, some New Jersey families are still living with the damage. This law requires the state Department of Community Affairs to reopen its Reconstruction, Rehabilitation, Elevation and Mitigation (RREM) grant program. It pays for unmet rebuilding needs with program money that hasn't been committed yet. In response, the department created a new track, RREM Pathway D, which is now taking applications.

The standard Pathway D caps are $100,000 to repair remaining Sandy damage, $500,000 for eligible reconstruction, and $100,000 to raise a home. To qualify, the house must have been damaged by Sandy and be a single-family detached home, including a manufactured home. You must own it and live there as your primary residence, and your household adjusted gross income must be under $250,000. Rentals and second homes don't qualify. You apply online with a licensed contractor's estimate, photos, and proof of damage. Households under 80% of area median income that never applied to RREM, or that bought their home after the storm, come first.

Sen. Carmen Amato, who was mayor of Berkeley Township when Sandy hit, sponsored the bill with Sen. Brian Stack. Assembly members Brian Rumpf and Gregory Myhre carried it in the Assembly; Sen. Angela McKnight and Assemblyman Paul Kanitra were cosponsors. The sponsors included Republicans and Democrats. It passed both houses without a single no vote on Jan. 12, 2026, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, 2026.
