New York already requires the local prevailing wage for workers on public construction sites. More of that work now happens in shops and factories. This law covers seven listed kinds of custom fabrication: wall panels, woodwork, electrical work, plumbing, heating, cooling, ventilation or exhaust duct systems, rebar cages, and mechanical insulation, when made solely and specifically for a public project. Covered workers would earn the prevailing wage for the New York county where the project is located even if their shop is out of state, and their employers would have to keep certified payrolls.

Workers would not need to apply. The rule would be written into public contracts bid on or after June 18, 2026. Stocked or off-the-shelf parts are not covered. The 2026 follow-up also excludes projects where federal funding triggers federal wage rules; highways and structures, vehicles, or watercraft used to transport people; and certain exempt housing projects.

Sen. Robert Jackson carried versions starting in the 2021–22 session, and Assembly Member Harry Bronson sponsored matching Assembly bills. Cosponsors from both parties backed the 2025 Senate bill, which passed 57–2. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 20, 2025, and signed the follow-up amendment on February 13, 2026.

The law was set to start June 18, 2026. Construction industry groups sued, and on June 8 Judge Anne M. Nardacci entered a consent preliminary injunction suspending the effective date and barring enforcement. The injunction remained in place when checked November 13, 2026.
