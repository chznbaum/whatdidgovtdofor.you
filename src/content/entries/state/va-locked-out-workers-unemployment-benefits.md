Virginia's unemployment law has long denied benefits to people who are out of work because of a labor dispute. This law says that an employer's lockout doesn't count as a labor dispute. If your employer shuts you out of your job during a contract fight and you otherwise qualify, you can collect unemployment while the lockout lasts.

Benefits can still be denied in three narrow situations: your union refuses to meet with the employer under reasonable conditions to talk about the issues behind the lockout; a final ruling under the National Labor Relations Act finds your union refused to bargain in good faith; or the lockout was a direct result of your union breaking an existing contract. You apply through the Virginia Employment Commission, the same way as for any other unemployment claim. The change took effect July 1, 2026.

Sen. Lamont Bagby carried the bill. It passed the Senate 21–19 on a party-line vote, with every Democrat voting for it and every Republican against, and it passed the House of Delegates 64–34. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
