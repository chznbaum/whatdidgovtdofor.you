If a flood, fire, hurricane, other natural disaster or terrorist attack leads to a qualifying emergency declaration, New York law sets deadlines for insurance claims. After the insurer receives a properly executed proof of loss and all requested paperwork, the initial deadline to accept or reject a claim is 15 business days. A noncommercial claim may get a written 15-business-day extension; suspected arson starts with 30 business days, and inaccessible property can add time with notices every 15 business days. Commercial claims may be extended by 30 business days and then further, with updates every 30 business days. Settlement payment is due within 4 business days after the insurer receives the agreement and the claimant meets any conditions.

You do not have to enroll; the protections apply when you file a covered property or liability claim. You may make urgent health and safety repairs and prove the loss with photos, video, inventories or receipts. A rejection must provide applicable policy provisions and the deadline to sue. If personal property is involved, it must explain how to file a state complaint.

The effort began after Superstorm Sandy. James Skoufis first introduced a version in 2013; Steve Stern carried the 2023 Assembly bill and its 2024 amendment. Senators of both parties passed each measure 62–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed both on January 26, 2024. The amendment raised the payment period from 3 to 4 business days and set separate rules for commercial and noncommercial extensions. The law took effect February 25, 2024.
