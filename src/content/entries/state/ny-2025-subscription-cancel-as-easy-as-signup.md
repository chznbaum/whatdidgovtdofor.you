If a covered subscription renews on its own, New York law says canceling it must be as easy as signing up and available through the same medium. Businesses must also offer cancellation through every medium they use to take consent. If you signed up in person, they must at least let you cancel online or by phone. They may make a save offer, but cannot obstruct, ignore or unreasonably delay your request.

Before you agree, the business must spell out the price, charge frequency, cancellation deadline and how to cancel. For a price increase, it must get your approval first or let you cancel for at least 14 days after the higher charge and refund the unused part of the term. Trials lasting more than a month require a warning 3 to 21 days before the cancellation deadline for the first paid period. You do not have to apply. Exemptions include some locally franchised services, regulated financial businesses, alarm operators and service-contract companies.

Sen. Rachel May, chair of the Senate Consumer Protection Committee, said she would make sure Gov. Kathy Hochul's proposal covered companies its FCC exemption left out. After Senate Democrats raised concerns, the Senate removed that exemption, and its version won the budget negotiations. Hochul had proposed the broader cancellation rules in her FY 2026 budget. The final bill passed the Assembly 103–47 and the Senate 40–22 on May 7, 2025. Hochul signed it May 9, and the rules took effect Nov. 5, 2025.
