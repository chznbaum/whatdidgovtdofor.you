If you grew up in New York without legal immigration status, you can apply for the same state college aid as your classmates, including the Tuition Assistance Program (TAP) and the Excelsior Scholarship. The law also covers students with DACA, Temporary Protected Status, or U or T visas. Before 2019, state law limited this aid to citizens and certain legal residents.

For undergraduate aid, you generally need to have attended a New York high school for at least two years and graduated, or earned a New York high school equivalency diploma, and then enroll in college within five years. Graduate students generally have ten years. You apply through the state's DREAM Act application. It doesn't ask students without legal status for a home address or financial records. The application opened on July 3, 2019. The law also lets people with a taxpayer ID number open a New York 529 college savings account, and it set up a privately funded scholarship fund.

The Assembly passed the DREAM Act year after year, but each time it died in the Republican-led Senate. After Democrats took the Senate in 2019, Sen. Luis Sepúlveda and Assembly Member Carmen De La Rosa carried it, and it passed both chambers on Jan. 23. It's named for Sen. José Peralta, who sponsored it for years and died in 2018. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on April 12, 2019. Lawmakers estimated it would cost about $27 million.
