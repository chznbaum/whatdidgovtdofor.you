Long-term care insurance helps pay for a nursing home or home care later in life. Many New Yorkers who bought it years ago have seen their premiums double or even triple. This law makes those increases harder to slip past you. When an insurer asks to raise rates, the Department of Financial Services must post a public notice within 14 days and take comments for 30 days. When it decides, it must post the decision and explain why. If an increase is approved, your insurer must tell you at least 90 days before it takes effect.

If you're shopping for a policy, the insurer has to show you its rate increases over the past 10 years before you sign or pay. It also has to say whether your rate is locked for a while, what could make it go up, and what you can do if it does, such as cutting benefits or canceling. You don't need to apply for any of this. It applies to rate requests filed on or after January 1, 2024.

Sen. Shelley Mayer first introduced the bill in the 2021–22 session. She carried it with Assemblymember Chris Burdick, and Assembly Insurance Committee chair David Weprin cosponsored it. The final version passed the Senate 53–9 and the Assembly in June 2023. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 17, 2023.
