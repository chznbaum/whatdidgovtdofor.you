If you vote by absentee ballot in New York and make certain mistakes on the envelope, the board of elections can't just set your vote aside. It has to tell you what went wrong and how to fix it. Mistakes you can fix include forgetting to sign, a signature that doesn't match the one on your registration, a missing witness when you sign with a mark, and mailing the ballot back without its inner envelope.

The board must mail you a notice within one day of finding the problem. If it has your email address or phone number, it must also email or call you. To fix the problem, you send back a signed form called a cure affirmation, in person, by mail, or by email. The board has to receive it within seven business days after it mails the notice, or by 5 p.m. on the seventh day after the election, whichever is later. If the form fixes the problem, your ballot is counted.

The law passed in July 2020, weeks after New York City rejected about 80,000 mail ballots in the June primary, mostly over signature problems. The pandemic was pushing far more people to vote absentee. Sen. Zellnor Myrie, who chaired the Senate Elections Committee, sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Charles Lavine carried the Assembly version. It passed the Senate 40–20, with every Democrat voting for it and every Republican against. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 21, 2020. Later laws rewrote this part of the election law and added more mistakes you can fix, such as a ballot that arrives a few days after the election without a postmark.
