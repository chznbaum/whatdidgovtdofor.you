If you're treated at a New York general hospital and staff know or suspect that someone is abusing you at home, the hospital has to tell you that domestic violence and victim help services are available. If you want an advocate with you, the hospital must call the local domestic violence or victim assistance group and ask for one. You don't need to apply or know who to call. The hospital makes the call.

The law also sets up how hospitals handle these cases. Each one needs written policies for spotting, assessing, treating, and referring abuse victims. Nurses, doctors, social workers, other clinical staff, and security staff must keep getting training on those policies. Each hospital also names one staff member to work with the local victim services group that the state Health Department picks for its area. The Health Department wrote a model policy that hospitals can follow.

Assembly Member Charles Lavine had sponsored versions of the bill since 2011. Sen. Julia Salazar carried its Senate companion in 2019. The measure passed the Senate 59–0 that May, with support from both parties, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it in December. In 2020, Lavine and Salazar sponsored a follow-up that limited required training to specified clinical and security staff and moved the start date to Dec. 23, 2020.
