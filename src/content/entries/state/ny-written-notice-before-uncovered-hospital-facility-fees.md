A facility fee covers hospital overhead, separate from what the doctor charges. It can surprise you when a doctor's office belongs to a hospital. In New York, a hospital, health system or provider can't bill you for a facility fee your insurance won't cover unless you got written notice. For visits booked at least a week ahead, the notice must come seven days before service. If you booked less than a week out, it must come on the visit date.

The notice must say how much the fee is, what it's for, and whether your plan will pay it. If you're uninsured, it must explain how to apply for financial help. It has to be in plain language, in bold type, and available in the six most common languages in the hospital's area. When a practice joins a hospital system and its patients start owing facility fees, the practice must warn them. No facility fee can be charged for preventive services defined by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force. You don't need to do anything to get these protections.

Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assembly Member Richard Gottfried first introduced facility-fee protections in a broader medical-debt bill in 2019. They reintroduced them in 2021 and carried the narrower notice measure through both houses in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 23, 2022, and it took effect June 21, 2023. Rivera has since proposed going further, with a bill that would bar facility fees insurance won't cover.
