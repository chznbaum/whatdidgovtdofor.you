In New York, no one under 18 can get married. A marriage is barred if either person is a minor, and no parent or judge can sign off on an exception. Clerks can't issue a marriage license to anyone under 18, and the rule has applied to licenses issued since Aug. 21, 2021.

New York had already raised its marriage age once. A 2017 law moved the minimum from 14 to 17 but still let 17-year-olds marry with a parent's and a judge's consent. Most minors who marry are teenage girls, often wed to adult men, and advocates said the consent exception left them open to forced marriages. The 2021 law closed it, making New York one of the first states to ban marriage under 18 outright.

Sen. Julia Salazar and Assemblymember Phil Ramos carried the bill. Survivor-advocate Naila Amin led the campaign to close the remaining exception, and Ramos named the measure Naila's Law after her. Fraidy Reiss, who survived a forced marriage in Brooklyn and founded the group Unchained At Last, had pushed for an age-18 floor since 2015. The Senate passed the bill 61–2, with members of both parties in favor, and the Assembly passed it 147–0. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 22, 2021.
