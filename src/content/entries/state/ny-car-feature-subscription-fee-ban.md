Some carmakers have started charging monthly or yearly fees to switch on features your car already has, such as heated seats or remote start. Starting in February 2027, New York bans that. Manufacturers, dealers and their agents can't sell you a subscription for a feature that uses hardware installed when you bought or leased the car and keeps working without any ongoing cost or support from them.

You don't need to do anything to be covered. Some features are left out: navigation updates, infotainment, satellite radio, in-car Wi-Fi, telematics, roadside assistance, driver-assist and self-driving software, and other services that need a cellular or data connection. Regular car loan and lease payments don't count as subscriptions. Companies that break the law face a civil penalty of up to $250 per point of sale for each violation, and the Department of State can write rules to carry it out.

Assemblymember William Magnarelli and Sen. James Skoufis have pushed the ban since 2024. Magnarelli's first version passed the Assembly that year but stalled in the Senate. Both chambers passed a second version in 2025, but Gov. Kathy Hochul vetoed it that December. Their 2026 bill passed the Assembly 138–3 and the Senate 57–3, and Hochul signed it on November 23, 2026.
