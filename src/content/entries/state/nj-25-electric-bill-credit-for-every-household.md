If your home electric account is with PSE&G, Atlantic City Electric, Jersey Central Power & Light, or Rockland Electric, a one-time $25 credit was automatically applied in August 2026. You did not have to apply. The Board of Public Utilities says eligible customers who did not receive the credit should contact their utility.

The credit was meant to soften high electricity supply costs in 2026. Gov. Mikie Sherrill issued Executive Order No. 1 on January 20, 2026, her first day in office. It declared an electricity affordability emergency and directed the Board of Public Utilities to issue residential bill credits or an initial set of credits by July 1. The order also told the board to consider delaying utility rate-increase cases and reducing some charges. The board approved the $25 August credit on June 30.

The credit was funded from money already inside the electric-bill system, not state tax revenue. The board used $75.8 million in solar alternative compliance payments first; state law requires those payments to be refunded directly to ratepayers. Any remaining cost came from the Clean Energy Program budget, which is funded mainly by charges collected on utility bills. The 2026 credit was smaller than in 2025, when the same four utilities issued two $50 credits to more than 3.6 million customers.
