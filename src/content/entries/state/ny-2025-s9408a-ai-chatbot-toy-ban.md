Starting Feb. 22, 2027, no one can make, exchange, distribute, sell, or stock for sale in New York a toy designed for children age 12 or younger if it has an AI companion built into it. That covers products like talking stuffed animals, dolls, and robots that hold human-like conversations with kids, remember what they say, and ask them about their feelings. The ban lasts five years.

Parents don't have to do anything. The state attorney general enforces the law and can go to court to stop sales. Companies face fines of up to $15,000 a day for each violation, and the state doesn't have to show that a child was harmed.

The governor's office said a consumer watchdog had tested these toys and found sexually explicit conversations, few or no parental controls, safety limits that weakened in longer chats, and toys that could record voices or scan faces. State Sen. Andrew Gounardes introduced the bill in March 2026, and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 57–3 on June 1, 2026, with votes from both parties, including Senate Republican Leader Rob Ortt. The Assembly passed it the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 24, 2026.
