If a New York clinician prescribes you mifepristone or misoprostol, the two medicines used in a medication abortion, they can ask that the label on your pills show their practice's name or address instead of their own name. The pharmacy has to honor that request, and the prescriber has to tell you when they use it. You don't need to do anything.

The point is to protect the people who provide abortion care, and with them the care itself. In early 2025, a Louisiana grand jury indicted a New York doctor for prescribing abortion pills online to a patient in that state. Keeping the prescriber's name off the bottle makes it harder for other states to find and charge them. The law builds on New York's 2023 shield law for doctors who treat out-of-state patients by telehealth.

State Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assembly Member Karines Reyes, a nurse, wrote both rounds of the law. The first, signed by Gov. Kathy Hochul on Feb. 3, 2025, took effect right away and let labels show the practice's name. A follow-up bill added the address option, required pharmacies to comply, and required prescribers to tell patients. It passed the Senate 40–21, and Hochul signed it on March 20, 2025.
