If you're surviving domestic violence in New York and share a phone plan, cable or satellite account, or utility contract with someone else, you can get off it. Send the provider a written statement that you no longer want to be on the contract because you are a victim of domestic violence. The provider can't ask for private details about the abuse, and it must release you within seven days. It must dispose of the information you submitted within 30 days in a way that protects its confidentiality.

Leaving costs nothing. Companies can't charge a fee or penalty, require the primary account holder's approval, hold you back over unpaid bills on the account, or block you from keeping your phone number. Providers must post these options on their websites and apps; phone, cable and satellite companies must also make them available in stores. Before this law, survivors had to submit records such as a police report, an order of protection or a professional's affidavit to leave many of these contracts.

Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal and Sen. Kevin Parker sponsored the law. Rosenthal said abusers used shared contracts to control their victims. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in October 2022. Early in 2023, the two lawmakers passed a follow-up bill that clarified how the process works and set the start date at June 30, 2023. The Senate passed it 63–0, and Hochul signed it on March 3, 2023.
