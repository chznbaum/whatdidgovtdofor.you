Virginia law prohibits discrimination because of sexual orientation or gender identity in covered employment, housing, credit, and public accommodations. That includes firing or refusing to hire someone, denying housing or credit, or refusing covered services for either reason. Some employers and private or religious accommodations are exempt under the relevant statutes.

You don't have to apply for these protections. The rules apply directly to covered employers, housing providers, creditors, and public accommodations. As of 2026, the state employment law generally covers employers with at least five employees and anyone employing a domestic worker.

Sen. Adam Ebbin sponsored the 2020 bill, which combined several similar proposals. It cleared the Senate with Republican support: on the final vote, six Republican senators joined all 21 Democrats. The House of Delegates passed it 54–46. Gov. Ralph Northam signed it on April 11, 2020, and it took effect that July. It made Virginia the first Southern state with broad statewide LGBTQ nondiscrimination protections.
