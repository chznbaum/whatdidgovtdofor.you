If Minnesota's break law covers your job, your employer must allow a rest break of at least 15 minutes within each four consecutive hours you work. If using the nearest convenient restroom takes longer, the break must be longer. Breaks under 20 minutes are paid. Before 2026, state law only promised enough time to use the restroom.

If you work six or more consecutive hours, your employer must allow at least 30 minutes to eat. The old rule began at eight and set no minimum. A meal break can be unpaid only if you are fully off duty. You don't need to apply. If your employer denies a required break, it owes the missed break time at your regular rate plus the same amount again. You can sue, or the state Department of Labor and Industry can enforce it. A union contract can set different breaks. The law excludes some workers, including certain farmworkers, executives and professionals, and some seasonal camp staff.

The changes were part of the 2025 jobs and labor budget law. Rep. Dave Pinto and Senate President Bobby Joe Champion were its chief authors. It passed in one legislative day with votes from both parties: 40–27 in the Senate and 96–37 in the House. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on June 14, 2025, and the break rules took effect January 1, 2026. The same law also let employers require reasonable documentation after more than two consecutive scheduled workdays of earned sick and safe time, down from more than three.
