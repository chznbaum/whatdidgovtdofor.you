Lead can build up in your bones over the years and get released into your blood during pregnancy, where it can harm you and your baby. Under this New Jersey law, a licensed physician or physician assistant, or a certified advanced practice nurse or midwife, who provides prenatal care must check you for lead-exposure risks using the latest guidance from the federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. When that guidance calls for it, you get a blood lead screening.

You don't need to apply. The check is part of your prenatal care, and you can decline the test in writing. If your provider can't do the test, they may refer you in writing to someone who can. Labs have five business days to send results to your provider and to state and local health officials. If your lead level is elevated, your provider must tell you in writing, explain in plain language what lead poisoning means, and help make sure children under 6 in your home have been tested.

Assembly member Shanique Speight and Sen. James Beach were the legislative champions named by Isles. Assembly members Reginald Atkins and Luanne Peterpaul and Sen. Teresa Ruiz were also prime sponsors. Isles led the coalition that pushed for the law. It passed both houses with no votes against, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 7, 2026. It took effect April 1, 2026.
