New Jersey-regulated health plans must cover vaccines the state Health Department recommends without a copay, coinsurance or deductible. This includes private plans, NJ FamilyCare, and state and school employee plans. For a network plan, the $0 protection generally applies in network; if the plan has no provider who can give the vaccine, it must cover an out-of-network provider at no cost. Private policies must have been issued or renewed on or after January 18, 2026. A newly recommended vaccine is added for plan years starting at least one year after the recommendation, so coverage is not always immediate. You do not apply separately.

The law makes the state Health Department the source of vaccine guidance in New Jersey law. The department must weigh guidance from four national doctors' groups—pediatricians, family doctors, OB-GYNs and internists—along with the federal advisers. In September 2026, it adopted the American Academy of Pediatrics' 2026 schedule for children and the American Academy of Family Physicians' 2026 schedule for adults.

Sens. Joseph Vitale and Andrew Zwicker and Assemblywoman Carol Murphy, who chairs the Assembly Health Committee, sponsored the bill. The Senate passed it 25–12 in December 2025 and the Assembly 51–24 in January. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 18, 2026, and it took effect immediately. The law remains in force, but A4433 and S3965, introduced in February and March 2026, would repeal it; both remain pending in committee.
