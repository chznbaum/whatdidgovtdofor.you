In New York, a large freight railroad can't run a train or locomotive carrying hazardous materials with just one person aboard. The law covers Class I and Class II railroads, which are the big national and regional carriers. It counts as hazardous anything the U.S. Department of Transportation designates as hazardous. Switching work in rail yards is exempt. A railroad that breaks the rule can be fined $250 to $1,000 the first time, and up to $10,000 for a third violation within three years. Workers and neighbors don't have to do anything to be covered.

Sen. Timothy Kennedy and Assemblymember William Magnarelli carried the crew-size bill for years. An earlier version died in committee. Gov. Kathy Hochul vetoed another in 2022, saying federal law might override it. After the February 2023 derailment and chemical fire in East Palestine, Ohio, the Legislature passed it again. The Senate vote was 55–6, with Democrats and most Republicans voting yes. Hochul signed it on December 8, 2023, writing that federal action was still pending and state rules were needed.

The law was then narrowed from all freight trains to trains carrying hazardous materials. Kennedy and Magnarelli passed that change in early 2024, the Senate voting 60–1. Since June 2024, a federal rule has also required two-person crews on most trains nationwide. A federal appeals court upheld that rule in August 2026.
