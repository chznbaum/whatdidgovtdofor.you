If you file for bankruptcy and are eligible to use Georgia's exemptions, you can now shield up to $50,000 of equity in a residence that you or a dependent uses. Before July 1, 2026, the limit was $21,500 and had not changed in 11 years. For qualifying married households—including joint filers and a debtor who solely owns the home their spouse also uses as a primary residence—the limit can reach $100,000. The exemption does not guarantee that you can keep the home: in Chapter 7, a trustee may sell it when there is nonexempt equity; in Chapter 13, nonexempt equity can increase what you must repay.

The exemption is not automatic. You claim it on Schedule C as part of your bankruptcy case. Special federal rules decide which state's exemptions apply if you recently moved. Starting July 1, 2031, Georgia will adjust the amounts annually based on the prior year's inflation rate.

The National Association of Consumer Bankruptcy Attorneys began pushing for stronger Georgia protections in 2022 and worked with Rep. Soo Hong starting in 2024. Hong carried HB 1024 in the House with sponsors Matt Reeves and Rob Leverett, and Sen. Marty Harbin carried it in the Senate. Every vote cast was yes: 170–0 in the House and 51–0 in the Senate. Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 11, 2026.
