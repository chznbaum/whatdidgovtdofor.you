When officials declare a local, state or federal emergency because of a natural disaster or terrorist attack, New York sets deadlines for insurance claims caused by that event. After the insurer receives a properly completed proof of loss and all requested materials, its first decision period is 15 business days. For a non-commercial claim, the insurer may take one extra 15-business-day period if it gives written reasons. If it is barred from entering the property, more time is possible; it must update you every 15 business days and decide within 15 days after access returns. For suspected arson, the first period is 30 business days. Commercial claims can receive a 30-business-day extension and further time with notices every 30 business days.

You do not apply separately; the protections apply when you file your claim. Photos, videos, inventories and receipts can serve as proof of loss. You may make urgent repairs to windows, walls, doors, roofs, heating, water and electrical systems when needed for health and safety. A rejection must identify the relevant policy terms and deadline to sue. Once you and the insurer agree on payment, it must pay within 4 business days after receiving the agreement or after you meet its conditions, whichever is later. Certain fire-insurance proceeds subject to tax-district liens are excepted.

James Skoufis first introduced an Assembly version after Superstorm Sandy in 2013. He later sponsored the Senate bill, while Steve Stern carried it in the Assembly. The original bills passed both chambers in 2023, and they also sponsored the negotiated amendment. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed both measures on January 26, 2024; the amendment passed the Senate 62–0.
