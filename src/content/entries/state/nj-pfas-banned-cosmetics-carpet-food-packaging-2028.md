PFAS, often called "forever chemicals," resist breaking down and accumulate in the environment. Starting Jan. 12, 2028, New Jersey bars sales of cosmetics, carpets and rugs, and fabric treatments such as stain-repellent sprays if PFAS was deliberately added. The same ban covers disposable food packaging made mostly from paper or plant fibers, including takeout containers, wrappers, plates and straws. Unavoidable traces do not count, and sales of used carpets and fabric treatments are exempt.

Cookware is not banned. Pots, pans and other cookware with added PFAS on a food-contact surface or handle generally must say "This product contains PFAS" in English and Spanish on the label and online. Labels required by another state or federal law also qualify, and small items with no wrapper or tag are exempt. The rules apply to sellers and manufacturers. The consumer affairs division can fine violators up to $20,000 per violation; a court can impose up to $25,000 per day of a violation.

The law gives the Department of Environmental Protection $4.5 million for PFAS source reduction, research, monitoring and environmental testing. It must establish the source-reduction program by Jan. 12, 2027, and report its research findings and policy recommendations. Sens. Linda Greenstein and Bob Smith and Assembly members Shama Haider, Lisa Swain and Alixon Collazos-Gill carried the bill. It passed the Senate 37–0 and the Assembly 58–2 on Dec. 22, 2025, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 12, 2026.
