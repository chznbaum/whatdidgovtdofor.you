Fentanyl is now mixed into many street drugs, often without the buyer knowing, and as little as 2 milligrams can kill. Test strips can show whether a pill or powder contains it. Matthew's Law lets any pharmacist or health care provider who can prescribe in New York give these testing kits to anyone. You don't need a prescription, and you don't have to explain why you want one. Before the law, the strips came mostly from treatment programs and harm reduction groups, and only a few places had them.

The law took effect in December 2023. In early 2024, a follow-up law also sponsored by McDonald and signed by Gov. Kathy Hochul (D) removed the original limit of five tests per visit and the strict storage rules. It also said no license is needed to hand out the kits. Since January 2025, the law has covered tests for xylazine, an animal sedative that has also turned up in street drugs.

The law is named for Matthew Horan, a 30-year-old from Westchester who died of a fentanyl overdose in November 2020. His sister, Kailey Horan, asked Sen. Pete Harckham to make testing kits easier to get. Harckham and Assemblymember John McDonald, a pharmacist, carried the bill. It passed the Senate 61–0 and the Assembly 144–1, and Hochul signed it on November 17, 2023, three years after Matthew died.
