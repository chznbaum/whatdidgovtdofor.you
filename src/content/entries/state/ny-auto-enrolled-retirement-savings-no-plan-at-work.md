If your job in New York has no 401(k) or other retirement plan, your employer may now have to sign you up for New York Secure Choice. It's a state-run Roth IRA, and the money comes out of your paycheck. The rule covers private employers with 10 or more workers in the state that have been in business for at least two years. The account belongs to you and stays with you when you change jobs. Employers pay no program fee and aren't allowed to add matching money.

You don't have to apply. Unless you pick another amount, 3% of your pay goes in. You can lower or raise what you save, choose to increase your rate automatically each year, or opt out completely. Your account costs $28 a year plus an annual fee of 0.22% to 0.31% of its balance. The initial registration deadlines were March 18, 2026 for employers with 30 or more workers, May 15 for those with 15 to 29, and July 15 for those with 10 to 14.

New York created Secure Choice in its 2018 budget, but employer participation was voluntary. Assemblymember Robert Rodriguez and Sen. Diane Savino sponsored the 2021 law that required covered employers to enroll workers automatically unless they opt out. It passed the Assembly 125–22 and the Senate 44–19, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 21, 2021. The program opened statewide in October 2025.
