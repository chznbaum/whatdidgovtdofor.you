Home solar deals can be hard to compare. A sales pitch may promise big savings, while the loan or lease behind it runs for decades. Starting January 1, 2027, Virginia requires every sale, lease, or power purchase agreement for a home solar system to come with a written contract. It must name the installer and its license number, list the payment schedule and every fee, and describe the system's size, expected output, and any production guarantees.

Before you sign, you also get a standard state disclosure form. It says who pays for maintenance, what the savings estimates assume about future power rates, and whether the deal could affect selling your home. Leases and power purchase deals must show the total of all payments over the full term. Under the state's rule, you can cancel without penalty within five business days. You don't need to do anything to get these protections. Companies that willfully break the rules face civil penalties of up to $2,500 per violation.

Sen. Lamont Bagby carried the bill, and Del. Irene Shin carried an identical House version. The final version passed the Senate 39–0 and the House 92–3. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026. The state Board for Contractors finished the detailed rules in the fall of 2026.
