New York's wage-payment law sets basic rules for how private-sector employees get paid. Your employer must pay you at least twice a month. It needs your written OK before it puts your pay in direct deposit. And if it fails to pay your benefits or wage supplements within 30 days of when they're due, it can face a misdemeanor charge. Private-sector employees in executive, administrative or professional roles used to lose these protections once they earned more than $900 a week. This law raised that line to $1,300 a week, about $67,600 a year.

You don't have to apply. If you work in the private sector, your job is classified as executive, administrative or professional, and you earn $1,300 a week or less, the protections have covered you since March 13, 2024. The change doesn't affect who is exempt from minimum wage or overtime, which is set separately. The bill's sponsor also said the state Department of Labor had turned away unpaid-wage claims from workers above the old $900 line, and that raising it would let more of them get the department's help.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Kenny Burgos carried it in the Assembly. Gounardes argued that the $900 cutoff, set in 2007, no longer matched today's pay: adjusted for inflation, the same amount would be about $1,300. The Senate passed it 62–0 in May 2023 and the Assembly followed in June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on September 15, 2023.
