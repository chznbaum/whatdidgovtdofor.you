New York's minimum wage went up three times under a 2023 budget agreement between Gov. Kathy Hochul and the Legislature. In New York City, Long Island, and Westchester County it climbed from $15 an hour to $16 in 2024, $16.50 in 2025, and $17 on January 1, 2026. In the rest of the state it rose from $14.20 to $15, then $15.50, then $16. Home care aides got additional increases on top of those rates.

Workers don't have to do anything to get the raise. Employers must pay at least the rate for their region, and fast food workers follow the same regional rates.

The law also ties future raises to inflation. Starting in 2027, the wage is supposed to rise each year based on a regional consumer price index, so paychecks keep pace with prices. But the law includes off-ramps that pause a raise when certain economic conditions are met. The state says one of them was triggered for 2027, so the minimum wage stays at $17 and $16 on January 1, 2027. Hochul has proposed changing the off-ramp so the wage can be adjusted starting in March 2027; that needs the Legislature's approval.

Hochul signed the increase into law as part of the state budget in May 2023.
