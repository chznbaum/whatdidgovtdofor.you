New Jersey has long had Code Blue alerts that open warming shelters on freezing nights. Until this law, the state had no matching rule for dangerous heat. The law creates a three-year Code Red pilot. When the National Weather Service issues a heat advisory, or the air quality index reaches 151 or higher, a participating county must declare a Code Red. Its plan then opens cooling centers, sends outreach workers to at-risk people, and arranges transportation to the centers.

The law first offers participation to the five counties with the highest documented homeless populations in the latest Point-in-Time count. If any decline, eligibility moves down the list. The Department of Community Affairs currently lists Camden, Essex, Hudson, Mercer and Monmouth as participating. Each gets a $500,000 state grant, out of $2.5 million total. Their cooling-center listings include libraries, drop-in centers and overnight sites. On a Code Red day, check DCA's list or dial 2-1-1 for local options; some overnight sites ask people to call ahead.

Assemblywoman Yvonne Lopez had sponsored Code Red legislation since 2018. She and Assemblywoman Tennille McCoy were the prime Assembly sponsors of the enacted bill, while Sens. Shirley Turner and Angela McKnight carried it in the Senate. It passed both houses without a no vote on Jan. 12, 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it in his final act in office on Jan. 20. The law took effect immediately and expires three years later.
