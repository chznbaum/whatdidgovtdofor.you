New Jersey state agencies, counties, towns and public colleges can no longer ask for five identifiers—your immigration or citizenship status, birthplace, Social Security number or taxpayer ID number—unless needed for a public service, benefit, program or license, or required by state or federal law, a judicial order or warrant. Information collected for those services isn't a public record and generally can't be disclosed except under a legal requirement, judicial order or warrant, or with your written consent. After a compelled disclosure, the agency must notify you within 90 days unless notice would compromise a criminal investigation.

The law also limits license plate reader data and bars the Motor Vehicle Commission from giving records to law enforcement for federal immigration-law purposes without consent or a judicial order or warrant, unless federal law requires otherwise. Starting April 1, 2027, health care facilities face similar rules for the five identifiers, with exceptions for care, billing, legal requirements and public programs. You do not need to apply.

Assembly members Annette Quijano, Ellen Park and Louis Greenwald and Senators Teresa Ruiz, Andrew Zwicker and John McKeon sponsored it. AFSC says it and New Jersey Alliance for Immigrant Justice partners spent six years organizing and drafting proposals; NJPP says it long championed the privacy approach. An earlier version passed January but Gov. Phil Murphy let it expire unsigned, citing a drafting problem. The revised bill passed March 23, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it March 25. Advocates say plate-reader data can still reach federal agencies.
