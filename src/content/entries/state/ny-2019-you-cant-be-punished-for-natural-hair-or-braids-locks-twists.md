Your hair is protected under New York's civil rights law. The law defines race to include traits historically linked to race, such as hair texture and protective hairstyles like braids, locks and twists. If an employer, landlord, business or public school treats you worse because of those traits, that counts as race discrimination. A "neutral" grooming rule that bans natural hair can break the law too.

The protection covers everything the state Human Rights Law covers, including jobs, housing and public places. A matching change to the Dignity for All Students Act covers public school students. You don't need to sign up for anything. If you're treated unfairly over your hair, you can file a complaint with the New York State Division of Human Rights.

State Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assemblymember Tremaine Wright sponsored the bill. The Senate passed it 46–16 in June 2019 with every Democrat and six Republicans voting yes, and the Assembly passed it three days later. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 12, 2019, and it took effect that day. New York became the second state with this protection, after California. New York City's human rights commission had issued similar guidance for the city earlier that year.
