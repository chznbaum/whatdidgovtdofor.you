If you have lived through domestic violence, New York law protects you in nearly all housing and when you apply for credit, attend a covered educational institution, or use a store, restaurant or another business open to the public. Most landlords—and real estate agents, creditors, covered schools and public accommodations—cannot treat you worse because of your status as a domestic violence victim. Some small, owner-occupied rentals and room-sharing arrangements are exempt from parts of the housing law.

You don't need to apply for anything. The protection is automatic. If you face this kind of discrimination, you can file a complaint with the New York State Division of Human Rights, which the law opened to survivors for housing, education and public accommodations.

Before 2022, the Human Rights Law covered domestic violence survivors only on the job. Gov. Kathy Hochul included broader protection in her 2022 State of the State agenda. Sen. Roxanne Persaud carried the bill in the Senate, where it passed 62–0 in April 2022, and Assemblymember Nikki Lucas carried it in the Assembly. The Assembly passed it on May 4, Hochul signed it on May 13, 2022, and it took effect right away.
