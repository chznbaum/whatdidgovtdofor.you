If you get medication for opioid use disorder at an in-network opioid treatment program, your covered New York-regulated plan can't charge you a copay or coinsurance for treatment there. The rule applies to individual and group policies regulated by the state that cover this care. It took effect for policies issued, renewed, or changed on or after January 1, 2023. You don't have to apply.

Copays added up because treatment can involve frequent visits. When the bill passed, state officials said some patients might have to come in up to six times a week for 90 days. Federal rules effective in October 2024 now let clinicians authorize take-home doses sooner based on each patient's circumstances. New York says the copay and coinsurance ban covers medication the program gives you to take home. Deductibles can still apply, and medication filled at a retail pharmacy can carry the plan's usual cost-sharing.

The bill became law nearly five years after Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal first introduced the measure in January 2018. Rosenthal sponsored the final Assembly bill, and Sen. Pete Harckham carried its Senate version. The Assembly passed it 143–1 in January 2022, and the Senate passed it 57–4 that May. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 23, 2022.
