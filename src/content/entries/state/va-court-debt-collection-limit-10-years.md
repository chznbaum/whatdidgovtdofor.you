In Virginia, unpaid court fines and costs could be collected for decades. A circuit court had up to 60 years from the date of the offense or delinquency, and a general district court had up to 30. Starting January 1, 2027, both kinds of court generally get 10 years from the date of judgment. For covered debt tied to charges that result in active incarceration, however, the 10-year clock starts on the deferred plan's due date, which must be at least 180 days after scheduled release. Once the time runs out, no action can be brought to collect the debt, and a partial payment won't restart the clock.

The law also gives you more breathing room. Unpaid debt generally is not sent to collections until 180 days after judgment, up from 90. If a court sentences you to active jail or prison time and orders covered fines or costs tied to that charge or another charge sentenced the same day, it must put you on a deferred payment plan automatically. Collection can't start while you're actively incarcerated and covered by that plan. Payments can still be required if you participate in work release, home or electronic incarceration, or a nonconsecutive-days program.

Del. Cia Price carried HB 17 in the House, and Sen. Angelia Williams Graves carried the identical Senate bill, SB 180. The Commonwealth Institute led advocacy for the 10-year limit. The House first passed Price's bill 66–32 in February 2026. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both bills on April 8, 2026.
