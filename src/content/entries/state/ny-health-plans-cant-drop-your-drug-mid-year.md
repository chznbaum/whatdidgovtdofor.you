If your health plan is regulated by New York and covers the essential benefits the Affordable Care Act requires, it can't take a drug off its list of covered drugs once your plan year starts. It also can't move the drug to a tier with a bigger copay, deductible or coinsurance, or add new hurdles like prior approval. Those changes can only happen when you enroll or renew. You don't have to do anything: the rule has applied to plans issued or renewed since Jan. 1, 2023.

There are a few exceptions. A plan can drop a drug the FDA says should come off the market. It can move a drug to a costlier tier if it adds a generic or interchangeable version at the same time. It can raise a drug's copay tier mid-year, but not for anyone already taking the drug or diagnosed with a condition it treats before the year began. Before each new plan year, plans must give you 90 days' notice if a drug is being dropped or will cost you more, and post that notice online. The law also does not override a collective bargaining agreement or a union's right to bargain formulary changes.

Sen. Neil Breslin and Assembly Majority Leader Crystal Peoples-Stokes carried the bill. They argued that people pick plans based on whether their medicine is covered, and that losing it mid-year can disrupt treatment. It passed in 2021, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. A follow-up law the same sponsors passed, which Hochul signed on Feb. 24, 2022, lengthened the notice to 90 days, covered plans that don't start in January, and set the start date. Lawmakers from both parties voted for both versions; the Assembly passed the original 147-0 and the follow-up 143-0.
