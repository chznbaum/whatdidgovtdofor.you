If you're on Medicaid in New York and have been exposed to community violence or have a personal history of injury from it, this law lets Medicaid pay for violence prevention services after a licensed provider finds that you're at elevated risk of violent injury or retaliation and refers you. These services can include peer support, mentoring, conflict mediation, crisis intervention, education, and help connecting to other services. New York's current policy excludes people in institutional settings and some people receiving care coordination through other Medicaid programs.

The community health workers who provide violence prevention services need at least six months of full-time-equivalent experience in violence prevention or high-risk or justice-involved youth development during the prior three years. They must complete state-approved training, receive approval from the Health Department's Office of Gun Violence Prevention, and take at least four hours of continuing education each year. The law gave the Health Department 30 days to seek federal approval. Coverage began January 1, 2024, and the state now publishes billing codes and payment rates for the service.

Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Jessica González-Rojas sponsored it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 57–4 on May 30, 2023, and the Assembly passed it on June 10. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 17, 2023.
