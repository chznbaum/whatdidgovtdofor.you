If you volunteer with a fire company or an ambulance corps in New York, your state income tax credit for that service is going up from $200 to $800 a year. If you and your spouse both serve and file jointly, you get $1,600 instead of $400. The bigger credit starts with tax year 2027, so you'll first claim it on the return you file in 2028.

To qualify, you must be an active volunteer for the whole year. You claim the credit on your state tax return. Some towns and other local governments offer volunteers a property tax exemption instead. Under the new law you get one or the other, the state credit or a qualifying local exemption, but not both.

The $200 credit had stayed the same since it was created in 2007. Over the past 20 years, the number of volunteer firefighters in New York has dropped by about a third. Sen. Monica Martinez carried the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Karen McMahon carried it in the Assembly. It passed both houses without a single no vote, including 60–0 in the Senate. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in October 2026.
