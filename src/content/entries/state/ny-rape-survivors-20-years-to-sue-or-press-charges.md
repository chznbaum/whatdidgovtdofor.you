If you were raped in New York, or survived one of the other sex crimes this law covers, you generally have 20 years to sue the person who did it. You can also sue anyone whose actions or neglect led to the assault, such as an employer or an institution. Before this law, most covered claims had a five-year deadline. You don't need a criminal charge to file a lawsuit.

The law also gives prosecutors more time. For second-degree rape, charges must be brought within 20 years of the assault or 10 years after it is first reported to law enforcement, whichever comes first. Third-degree rape charges have a 10-year limit instead of five years. Charges for first-degree incest can now be brought at any time, as they already could for first-degree rape. The new limits apply to assaults on or after Sept. 18, 2019, and to older cases whose deadline hadn't passed yet. Cases that were already too old stayed closed.

Gov. Andrew Cuomo proposed the change in his 2019 Women's Justice Agenda. After it was left out of that year's budget, Sen. Alessandra Biaggi and Assembly Member Aravella Simotas carried it through the Legislature in the last days of the session. In Simotas's bill memo, she argued that a short deadline works against survivors, because trauma often keeps them from reporting right away. The Senate passed it 59–3 and the Assembly 135–13, with members of both parties voting yes. Cuomo signed it on Sept. 18, 2019.
