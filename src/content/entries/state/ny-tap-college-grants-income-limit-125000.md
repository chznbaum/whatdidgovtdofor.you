New York's Tuition Assistance Program (TAP) is a state grant for college that you don't pay back. Since the 2024–25 school year, more families qualify. A student who depends on their parents qualifies if the family's New York net taxable income is $125,000 or less, up from $80,000. Independent students qualify at up to $60,000 if they're married and up to $30,000 if they're single, up from $40,000 and $10,000. The smallest grant rose from $500 to $1,000. The largest is $5,665 a year.

To get TAP, you complete a TAP application each year. Most New York residents also file the FAFSA; students using the New York State DREAM Act follow its application path. Eligible students may attend an approved New York college full time or part time, and some non-degree workforce programs also qualify. The state estimated about 93,000 students would benefit, including 48,000 who were newly eligible. By October 2024, more than 40,000 newly eligible students had applied.

The $80,000 limit hadn't changed since 2000. Assembly Higher Education Chair Patricia Fahy made raising it her top goal in 2024 with a "Turn on the TAP" campaign. Senate Higher Education Chair Toby Ann Stavisky joined her, and both houses put the change in their budget plans. Republican Sen. Dean Murray, who sat on the Senate committee, backed it too. The final budget added $55.7 million for TAP, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on April 20, 2024.
