If you're on Medicaid in New York, you have more choices for therapy. Licensed mental health counselors and licensed marriage and family therapists used to be unable to bill Medicaid for their own services. Under this law, they can. Since March 1, 2023, fee-for-service Medicaid has paid them for covered care in private practice, and Medicaid managed care plans began covering their services on April 1, 2023.

There is no separate application for this coverage. For fee-for-service Medicaid, the therapist must be licensed and registered with the state Education Department and enrolled with New York Medicaid as a billing provider. If you are in managed care, ask whether the therapist accepts your specific Medicaid plan before your first visit.

Sen. Diane Savino and Assemblymember Harry Bronson carried the bill. Their 2019 version passed both houses, with only one no vote in the Senate, but Gov. Andrew Cuomo vetoed it. They passed it again in 2021, and the Senate approved it 63–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul agreed with the Legislature to narrow coverage to mental health counselors and marriage and family therapists, then signed the original law on Dec. 29, 2021 and the follow-up amendment on Feb. 24, 2022. The Health Department then set the rules and opened enrollment to these therapists in early 2023.
