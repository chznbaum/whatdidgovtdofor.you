If you owe money on a consumer debt in New York, the company you owe and any collector working for it can't use social media to try to collect. The ban covers direct messages as well as posts, on any platform where users build a profile, connect with others and share posts, like Facebook or Instagram. Email and direct-message services alone don't count as social media, so collectors can still reach you those ways.

You don't need to do anything to be protected. The rule is part of the state's list of banned debt collection practices, and it took effect the day it was signed.

Sen. Kevin Parker first introduced a Senate version in 2015. He and Assemblymember Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn carried the 2024 bills. Bichotte Hermelyn warned that a collector messaging someone online could reach the wrong person, since many people don't use their real names, and could open the door to scams and harassment. The bill passed the Assembly in June 2024 and passed the Senate 58–1 a day later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 21, 2024, as part of a package of online privacy bills.
