New York law already made health plans cover treatment for drug and alcohol addiction, but it didn't clearly require insurers to cover gambling addiction unless the person also had a substance use disorder. Starting in 2027, that gap closes. The state budget changed "substance use disorder" in the insurance law to "substance-related and addictive disorder," the psychiatric category that includes gambling disorder. Gambling treatment now falls under the same coverage, parity, and review rules as other addiction care.

You don't need to apply. If your health insurance is regulated by New York, the coverage arrives when your plan is issued, renewed or modified on or after Jan. 1, 2027. Plans must cover both inpatient and outpatient treatment, and they can limit it to programs licensed or certified by the state Office of Addiction Services and Supports. The state Department of Financial Services must also report on how well insurers cover gambling treatment.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the coverage in January 2026, as sports betting grew and three new New York City casinos were approved. Assembly Member Phil Steck had pushed a narrower bill covering outpatient gambling treatment, which passed the Assembly in March; Sen. Joseph Addabbo carried the Senate version. The coverage became law in the budget Hochul signed on May 28, 2026.
