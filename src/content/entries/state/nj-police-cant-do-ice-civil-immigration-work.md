New Jersey police, sheriffs, and jails can't stop, question, arrest, search, or hold you solely over immigration status or a suspected civil immigration violation. Officers can ask about your status only when relevant to an investigation of an indictable offense they believe you committed. Local agencies generally can't help ICE operations focused solely on civil immigration enforcement or share nonpublic records or database access for that purpose. Exceptions include court orders, emergencies, and certain existing detention agreements. Police and sheriffs also can't sign 287(g) agreements, which deputize them as immigration agents. Jails can hold someone for ICE past release only in limited cases, such as a serious criminal record or a federal judge's final removal order, and never past 11:59 p.m. that day.

These policing limits apply automatically. If you're held and ICE asks to interview you or asks the jail to keep you, the jail must tell you in writing, in a language you understand. Crime or trafficking victims who ask a law enforcement agency for a U or T visa certification must have the request processed within 120 days.

In 2018, Attorney General Gurbir Grewal put similar rules in a directive that a later administration could have dropped. Gov. Phil Murphy vetoed a broader codification bill in January 2026. Assembly members Ellen Park, Verlina Reynolds-Jackson, Gabriel Rodriguez and Annette Quijano, with Sens. Britnee Timberlake, Gordon Johnson and Raj Mukherji, carried the narrower replacement. Both houses passed it March 23, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it March 25.
