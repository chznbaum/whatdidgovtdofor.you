In Virginia, your employer can't use your immigration status, or your family's, to get away with breaking wage laws. Threatening to call immigration authorities so you'll accept less than the minimum wage, or so you won't ask for pay you're owed, is now illegal. This covers private businesses and also the state, its agencies, and local governments.

If it happens to you, you can file a complaint with the state Commissioner of Labor and Industry within 180 days. Someone else can also report it, and the commissioner can investigate without a complaint. Your name stays confidential unless you agree to share it. If the commissioner confirms the threat, the employer can be fined up to $5,000 for a first violation, $9,000 for a second, and $12,000 for each one after that. Each threat against each worker counts separately. The fines go to the state, not to you, but you keep every other right you have to recover your wages.

Del. Michelle Maldonado carried the bill. It passed the Senate 21–19 and the House 64–35. Gov. Abigail Spanberger proposed removing the references to immigration status, but in April lawmakers declined to take up her change. She signed the bill as passed on May 14, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
