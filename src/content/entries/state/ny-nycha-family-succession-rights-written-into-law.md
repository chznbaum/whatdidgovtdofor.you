If you live in a New York City public housing apartment that isn't in your name, this law protects your path to stay there. When the tenant on the lease moves out for good or dies, NYCHA must keep a process for household members to claim the lease, and state law now says that process has to continue.

NYCHA has to put its succession rules in writing and follow federal, state and city law. The written rules must say who qualifies, whether you might have to move to a different-sized apartment, what you pay while your claim is pending, and how to file a grievance if NYCHA says no (subject to limits in NYCHA's own rules). Before NYCHA makes a substantive change to these rules, it must give written notice at least 30 days ahead, take written comments, and consider them. You still have to apply through NYCHA and meet its requirements.

Assembly Member Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn and State Sen. Brian Kavanagh sponsored the bill. In his sponsor's memo, Kavanagh wrote that many long-time household members had no clear, written right to stay, which left seniors, people with disabilities and other family members open to eviction. The Senate passed it 45–14 in June 2025 with votes from both parties, and the Assembly passed it four days later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 16, 2025, and it took effect immediately.
