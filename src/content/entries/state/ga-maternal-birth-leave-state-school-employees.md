If you work full time for the State of Georgia or a local public school system and you give birth, you can now take up to 120 hours of paid leave in the three weeks right after delivery to recover. That's about three weeks for a full-time worker. The law has been in effect since July 1, 2026, and it covers single and multiple births.

To qualify, you need six straight months on the job, or 700 hours worked in the six months before the birth if you're paid hourly. You ask through your employer's HR office. The birth leave must be used first, within those three weeks; unused hours don't carry over. After it ends, you can still use the up to 240 hours a year of paid parental leave Georgia already gives its eligible workers. The law also bars employers from punishing workers for taking the leave.

Rep. Sandy Donatucci, a Gwinnett County Republican, wrote the bill. House Speaker Pro Tempore Jan Jones and Rep. Sharon Cooper were among the co-authors, and Sen. Brian Strickland carried it in the Senate. It passed the House 170–0 and the Senate 50–0, and Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 5, 2026.
