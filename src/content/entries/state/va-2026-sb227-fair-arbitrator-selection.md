Many job offers and customer contracts make you settle disputes in private arbitration instead of court. Virginia's new law sets fairness rules for providers that handle more than 100 arbitrations a year arising from pre-dispute agreements tied to Virginia. They can't force you to accept a particular arbitrator. You and the other side first get a real chance to agree on one. If you can't agree, the firm has to use a neutral method, such as taking turns striking names from a list or drawing from a pool both sides approved. A proposed arbitrator must tell you about anything that could raise doubts about their fairness. A firm also can't take a case if either party or either party's law firm has or recently had a financial interest in the firm.

When a contract, law, or provider's rules require the business that drafted the arbitration clause to pay fees and costs needed to begin the case, the business has 30 days after the due date to pay. If it still hasn't paid, it loses the right to force arbitration, and you can take your claim to court. You don't have to apply for these protections. They cover agreements entered into on or after July 1, 2026, involving disputes connected to Virginia. If the arbitrator-selection rules are broken, you can ask a court to stop the process or to throw out an award. The State Corporation Commission can also fine a high-volume provider up to $10,000 per violation.

Senate Majority Leader Scott Surovell carried the bill. It passed both chambers without a single no vote, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026.
