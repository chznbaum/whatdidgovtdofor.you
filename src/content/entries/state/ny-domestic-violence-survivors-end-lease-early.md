If you rent in New York and you, or someone you live with, is a victim of domestic violence, you can end your lease early when staying puts you at risk of more abuse. Send your landlord written notice. The lease ends on a date you pick, at least 30 days later. Within 25 days of the notice, give proof: an order of protection, a police report, a medical record, or a signed statement from a qualified third party such as a counselor. You pay rent only up to the end date, and the landlord must refund any rent you paid in advance for the time after that.

Your landlord can't keep your security deposit just because you left early, and can't tell future landlords that you broke a lease. Landlords who knowingly break the law can owe up to $1,000 plus your actual damages and legal fees.

New York first let survivors end a lease in 2007, but only with a court order, only if they were current on rent, and only after notifying co-tenants, who could include the abuser. Assembly Member Andrew Hevesi and Sen. Alessandra Biaggi carried the bill that dropped those requirements. The Senate passed it 62–0, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on December 20, 2019.
