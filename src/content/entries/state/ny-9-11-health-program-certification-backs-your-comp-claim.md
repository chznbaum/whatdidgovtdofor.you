If you performed eligible World Trade Center rescue, recovery or clean-up work and the federal World Trade Center Health Program certified your illness, New York's Workers' Compensation Board must treat that certification as presumptive evidence that the illness was caused by your service. That gives your claim a legal head start, but it does not automatically award benefits: the insurer or employer can dispute other parts of the claim, and the board still decides it. An eligible dependent can use the presumption in a death claim tied to a qualifying condition. Before this law, the board did not have to give the federal certification this weight; the sponsor memo said that led to many valid claims being denied.

To use Article 8-A, you must be covered by New York's workers' compensation system and register your service with the board on Form WTC-12. It covers eligible paid workers and volunteers who served from Sept. 11, 2001 through Sept. 12, 2002 in Lower Manhattan south of Canal Street, at Fresh Kills, on barges or piers, or at morgues. The current registration deadline is Sept. 11, 2030. You then file a workers' comp claim. The law also gave claimants whose claims were denied before Sept. 9, 2022 two years to refile despite the ordinary deadlines. That window closed Sept. 9, 2024.

Assemblymember Peter Abbate and Sen. Robert Jackson sponsored the paired bills. Assemblymembers Stacey Pheffer Amato, Judy Griffin and Michael Lawler and Sens. Alessandra Biaggi, Anna Kaplan and Jessica Ramos cosponsored them. The Assembly passed A9922A 149–0 on May 31, 2022; the Senate passed it 63–0 the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 9, 2022.
