When a prescription-drug recall poses a reasonable probability of serious harm or death, the FDA classifies it as Class I, its most serious kind. In New York, your pharmacy must then make a reasonable attempt to tell you if you're currently taking that drug and got it there. It has to try to reach you by phone or mail within three days of learning about the recall, whether it heard from the FDA, the drugmaker, a wholesaler, or anyone else.

You don't have to sign up. The duty falls on the pharmacy, so it helps to keep your phone number and address current there. Big chains can send the notice from a central office instead of from each store. Before this law, New York pharmacies had no duty to tell patients about recalls.

Assemblymember Daniel Rosenthal and Sen. Leroy Comrie of Queens sponsored the bill. Lawmakers amended it twice in 2019, cutting the deadline from seven days to three. It passed the Assembly on May 30, 2019, and the Senate on June 11 by a vote of 61–0, with Democrats and Republicans both voting yes. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 18, 2019, and it took effect right away.
