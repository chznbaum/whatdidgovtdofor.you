If your child has diabetes and goes to a Virginia public school, the school must let them carry and use a phone or smart device to monitor their glucose or contact you, the school, or their doctor about their care. Your consent and written approval from their prescriber are required. State law already let students carry their diabetes supplies and check their glucose on the bus, at school, and at school events; the 2026 law added the explicit phone and smart-device protection.

A school nurse or other authorized employee trained in insulin and glucagon can now help your child put an insulin pump back in, following American Diabetes Association standards. School boards can't forbid trained staff from giving this care, though no employee is required to do it.

Starting with the 2028–29 school year, every school division must have a written diabetes care plan. The plan covers staff training and how to ensure each school attended by at least one student with diabetes has enough trained employees: at least two when the instructional and administrative staff is 10 or more, and at least one when it is smaller. It also covers parents' input and dispute resolution. Schools will give parents state information on diabetes at the beginning of each school year.

Del. Josh Thomas carried HB 1301. Del. Mike Cherry carried similar HB 624, which was folded into Thomas's bill, and later served as a House conferee. Sen. Jeremy McPike carried the identical Senate version. Parents of children with diabetes, including a Chesapeake mother whose son's school wouldn't let him check his blood sugar in class, pushed for the change. The final HB 1301 conference report passed without a single no vote, 96–0 in the House and 39–0 in the Senate. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026.
