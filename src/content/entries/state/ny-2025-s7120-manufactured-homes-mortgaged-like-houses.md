In New York, manufactured homes from the 1995 model year onward generally have been titled through the DMV even when permanently attached to land. That made common agency-backed 30-year mortgage financing harder to obtain; many manufactured homes are instead financed as personal property, which can carry higher costs. Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA-backed programs generally require a manufactured home to be legally treated as real estate. Until this law, New York alone lacked a formal statutory process for surrendering the title and making that conversion.

Starting December 12, 2026, you can choose to convert if your home is anchored to a permanent foundation and connected to residential utilities. You must own the land, or hold a recordable lease and get the landowner’s written consent. The law sets a $200 county recording fee and no transfer tax. You then file the recorded affidavit and required conversion paperwork with the DMV. Any liens and security interests must be released as part of the process. Once the application is accepted, the home is treated as real property and can be sold by deed and mortgaged like a house. Conversion is optional.

Sen. Sean Ryan and Assembly Member Billy Jones carried the enacted bill. Mortgage banker James Bopp described the effort as a 15-year political battle. The Senate passed it 61–0 and the Assembly 146–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in December 2025.
