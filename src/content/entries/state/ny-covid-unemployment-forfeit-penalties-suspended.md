If New York's Labor Department found that you had made a false statement on a past unemployment claim, it could penalize you with forfeit days. During those days you can't collect benefits you would otherwise be owed. When COVID-19 shut down much of the state in 2020, these old penalties kept some laid-off workers from getting any help, including the extra $600 a week Washington was paying. Many told reporters they had been penalized over technicalities or misunderstandings.

This law switched the penalties off for as long as the state's COVID disaster emergency lasted, backdated to May 11, 2020. People who were otherwise eligible could collect without having to apply for anything new. The law expired when the emergency ended after June 24, 2021, and the Labor Department resumed the penalties on July 4.

Sen. Monica Martinez of Long Island wrote to state officials and proposed the bill in April 2020, and Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal of Manhattan carried it in the Assembly. Labor Commissioner Roberta Reardon announced department relief on May 13, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo issued Executive Order 202.31 suspending the penalties the next day. The law codified the suspension and tied it to the emergency. The Senate passed it 48–14, with nine Republicans voting with Democrats. The Assembly passed it 97–46 the next day, and Cuomo signed it on June 13, 2020.
