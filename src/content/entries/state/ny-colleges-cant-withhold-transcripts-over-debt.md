If you owe money to a New York college or a licensed private career school, the school can't use your transcript to make you pay. It can't hold the transcript back, can't make releasing it depend on paying the debt, and can't charge you a higher fee or handle your request worse than anyone else's. Schools can still charge their usual fee for a transcript, as long as everyone pays the same.

You don't need to apply for this; it's how schools have to treat every request. If a school breaks the rule, the state's Superintendent of Financial Services can order it to stop and fine it $500 for each violation after a hearing. You can also sue the school yourself to make it stop, and a court can order the school to pay your lawyer's fees if you win.

Transcripts are often what stands between a student and a new job or a transfer to another school. Assemblymember Harvey Epstein and Sen. Kevin Thomas introduced the bill in 2021, and Assembly Higher Education Committee Chair Deborah Glick worked with them to get it passed. The Senate passed it that year, but the bill stalled in the Assembly. In January 2022, Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed a statewide ban, and the SUNY and CUNY boards voted to stop the practice at their campuses. The bill passed the Assembly 106–41 and the Senate 44–19 that March, Hochul signed it on May 4, 2022, and it took effect 30 days later.
