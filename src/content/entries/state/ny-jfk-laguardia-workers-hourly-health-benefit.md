If your private employer has at least 11 employees and you spend at least half your workweek at JFK, LaGuardia or a covered airline-catering location, your employer must provide an hourly health benefit on top of the applicable minimum wage. Cleaners, baggage handlers, wheelchair attendants and passenger-service staff can qualify; executive, administrative and professional employees and government workers do not. The benefit began at $4.54 in July 2021. New York's labor department lists it at $5.92 as of Aug. 10, 2026, for the first 40 hours each week. It can be paid in cash, toward health insurance or as another irrevocable benefit. You do not have to join your employer's plan or apply.

Sen. Alessandra Biaggi and Assembly Member Alicia Hyndman wrote the Healthy Terminals Act, and 32BJ SEIU and UNITE HERE Local 100 campaigned for it. 32BJ said airport workers often earned too much for Medicaid and ACA subsidies but too little to afford employer insurance. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed the act on Dec. 31, 2020. Biaggi and Hyndman also carried this follow-up law, which rewrote it into the form that took effect. The Senate passed it 45–18 and the Assembly 148–1. Cuomo signed it on March 25, 2021.

A 2025 state budget signed by Gov. Kathy Hochul expanded the law. Since January 2026, it has covered employees who spend at least half their workweek at a covered location, bringing in more part-time workers. It also added paid holidays and vacation and exempted employers with 10 or fewer workers.
