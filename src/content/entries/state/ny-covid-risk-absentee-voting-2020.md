New York used to give out absentee ballots only for a short list of reasons, such as being out of town or sick. This law widened the meaning of "illness." If going to your polling place risked catching or spreading a disease that could make you or others sick, you qualified. During the pandemic, that meant almost any voter could request a ballot and vote by mail instead of standing in line.

You still had to apply for the ballot, but you didn't need a doctor's note or any proof. The law took effect right away, in time for the November 2020 election. That fall New Yorkers cast more than 1.7 million absentee ballots.

Sen. Alessandra Biaggi introduced the bill in March 2020, before late and missing ballots marred the June primary. Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried it in the Assembly. It passed the Senate 42–18 and the Assembly 118–23, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 20, 2020. It was always temporary. Biaggi and Dinowitz won an extension that Gov. Kathy Hochul signed in January 2022, and the rule expired at the end of that year.
