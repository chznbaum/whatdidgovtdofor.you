If your health plan is regulated by New York State and your doctor, midwife, or other licensed provider prescribes prenatal vitamins, your plan must cover them. You don't owe a deductible or coinsurance. The rule covers individual and group plans that offer comprehensive medical coverage, even plans with no other prescription drug benefit. It started with policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2025. You don't need to apply. Just get a prescription and fill it.

Some employer plans are self-funded and regulated by federal law rather than the state, so the rule may not reach every job-based plan. Your plan can tell you which kind it has.

Sen. Joseph Addabbo Jr. introduced the coverage bill in 2023, and Assemblymember Aileen Gunther carried it in the Assembly. Both chambers passed it without opposition in 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that October. That version still let plans charge deductibles and coinsurance. As part of the deal to sign it, Addabbo and Assemblymember Paula Kay passed a follow-up law in early 2025. It extended the rule to plans without drug coverage and dropped the cost-sharing, noting that federal health guidelines treat prenatal vitamins as preventive care. The Senate passed it 59–0, and Hochul signed it on February 14, 2025.
