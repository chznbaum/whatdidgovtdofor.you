Before this law, moving from one New York county to another, or into or out of New York City, could cost you your vote in your new neighborhood. Even after living there for 30 days, if you missed the registration deadline, your registration stayed at your old address and you couldn't vote at your new polling place.

Now, when your county board of elections learns that you've moved anywhere in the state, it has to transfer your registration and party enrollment to your new address. You don't need to fill out a new form. If you've lived at your new address for at least 30 days but haven't reported the move by Election Day, you can go to the polling place for your new address and sign an affidavit listing your old and new addresses. Poll workers must offer you one. You then vote by affidavit ballot, and that ballot also updates your registration.

Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the bill in the Assembly and Sen. David Carlucci carried it in the Senate. It was among the first bills the Legislature passed in January 2019, alongside early voting. The Senate approved it 49–12, with 9 Republicans joining all 40 Democrats. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on January 24, 2019, and it took effect 60 days later.
