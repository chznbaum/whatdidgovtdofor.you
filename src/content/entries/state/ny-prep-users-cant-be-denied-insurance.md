PrEP is medication, taken as pills or shots, that helps prevent HIV. In 2018, state regulators reported that some insurers might be denying applications because applicants used it. New York law now expressly forbids a life, accident or health insurer from refusing or ending coverage, limiting the coverage offered, or charging a different rate for the same coverage solely because someone was prescribed PrEP.

You don't have to apply for this protection. It applies when an insurer covered by the law considers your application, whether to continue your coverage, its limits or its price. The protection took effect the day the law was signed.

The state Department of Financial Services told insurers in 2018 that adverse decisions based only on PrEP use already conflicted with state insurance law. This law writes that guidance into the statute. GMHC's public-policy team worked with legal experts to draft the bill after several staff members had been denied life or disability coverage because of their HIV status. Assembly Member David Weprin and Sen. Neil Breslin carried it. An earlier version also protected people living with HIV, but that part was dropped after insurers pushed back. The final bill passed the Assembly 137–0 and the Senate 61–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 28, 2024.
