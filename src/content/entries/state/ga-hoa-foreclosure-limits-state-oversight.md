If your Georgia community is governed by the Property Owners’ Association Act, this law raises the threshold before the association can ask a court to foreclose. Starting Jan. 1, the qualifying lien balance must reach the lesser of $4,000 or 12 months of regular dues, but never less than $2,000. Fines and fees do not count. The association must give 60 days’ notice; paying the amount in the notice within that time ends its right to foreclose. Those foreclosure rules do not cover condominiums or HOAs outside the POA Act.

Starting Jan. 1, associations must register annually with the Secretary of State for $100 to collect fines or fees, record liens or foreclose. They must accept partial payments and apply regular dues first. Residents can file a complaint within 180 days of an action or inaction that harmed them. A hearing officer investigates; disputed fines or fees pause, and the losing party at a hearing owes a $100 administrative fee. For actions filed since July 1, 2026, associations under the POA Act must give written notice, 30 days to pay and an itemized bill before collecting or being awarded attorney’s fees.

Sen. Matt Brass, a Newnan Republican, spent about four years on the bill. Sen. Donzella James, an Atlanta Democrat who had pushed for HOA oversight for years, was a cosponsor, and Rep. Rey Martinez carried it in the House. It passed the Senate 51–0 and the House 155–10, and Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 12, 2026.
