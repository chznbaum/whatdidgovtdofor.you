Before this law, a child in New Jersey usually had to meet the criteria for a psychiatric diagnosis before a counselor or therapist could bill insurance. That could delay help for kids showing early trouble, such as stress at home or a hard life change. The law requires plans to cover screening, prevention and treatment—including individual, group and family therapy—for at-risk children 18 and under without a formal diagnosis. Coverage is provided to the same extent as other services, so normal plan terms may still apply.

It reaches NJ FamilyCare, state-regulated insurers and HMOs, and health plans for state and school employees. A provider submits claims using an “at-risk diagnosis” code based on circumstances affecting a child's behavioral health and development. The law took effect April 20, 2026. Commercial and public employee plans phase it in as policies or contracts are delivered, issued or renewed. On October 29, the state Medicaid agency said it was still developing NJ FamilyCare's covered-code list and would issue provider and managed-care guidance.

Assembly members Andrea Katz, Linda Carter and Rosaura Bagolie carried the bill in the Assembly. Sen. Joseph Vitale and Senate President Nicholas Scutari carried it in the Senate. Pediatric and early-childhood organizations publicly supported the change. The Assembly passed it 60–5 with votes from members of both parties, and the Senate passed it 39–0. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 20, 2026.
