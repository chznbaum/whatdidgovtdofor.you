If you rent in Minnesota, your landlord can't name your minor children as defendants in an eviction case unless a child is the unit's sole renter. A lease can't waive this protection. A tenant whose child is named can recover actual damages or $300, whichever is greater, helping keep an eviction case off the child's record.

If a landlord requires or allows rent through an app or online portal, they must offer a free backup when it breaks. They can't charge a late fee or evict you if both methods fail. If they do, the case must be dismissed, and you are entitled to reasonable lawyer's fees. Cash payments require an immediate receipt in person or one within three business days otherwise.

The law also lets landlords estimate a departing tenant's final shared-meter utility bill. It adds assault on a landlord, employee, or contractor as grounds for a fast-track eviction and raises the maximum civil penalty for abusing that process from $500 to $750.

Rep. Spencer Igo (R) and Sen. Lindsey Port (D) carried the bill. Rep. Samakab Hussein (D) added the protection for children, while Rep. Jeff Dotseth (R) added the assault provision. Rep. Michael Howard (D) and Sens. Eric Lucero (R) and Doron Clark (D) were coauthors. Tenant advocacy group HOME Line led advocacy for the child protection. The House passed it 134–0 and the Senate 63–3. Gov. Tim Walz (D) signed it May 12, and it took effect Aug. 1, 2026.
