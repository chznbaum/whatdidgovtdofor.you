Discrimination at work isn't always on purpose. A background check, hiring test, scheduling rule or computer screening tool can look neutral and still weed out far more people of one race, sex, age or other protected group. Under this law, a practice like that can break New York's Human Rights Law even if you can't prove anyone meant to discriminate.

To bring a claim, a worker or applicant shows that a specific practice actually or predictably falls harder on a protected group. The employer then has to prove, with evidence, that the practice is job-related and a business necessity. Even then, the worker can still win by showing a fairer option would do the job. That defense never excuses discrimination someone intended. The rule applies to job practices from Dec. 19, 2025, on, and you file under the same law and process as any other discrimination complaint.

Courts had used this approach since the U.S. Supreme Court's 1971 Griggs decision, but the federal government pulled back from it in 2025. Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assembly Member Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn sponsored the bill to write the standard into state law. The Senate passed it 59–3 in June 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19, 2025, the same day she signed a separate law doing the same for housing.
