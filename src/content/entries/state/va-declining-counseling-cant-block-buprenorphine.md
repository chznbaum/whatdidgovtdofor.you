Buprenorphine is a medicine used to treat opioid addiction, and many patients get it through a prescriber's office. Virginia's rules required those prescribers to provide counseling or refer patients to it. A 2026 law makes counseling an offer, not a condition. Providers must offer counseling or a referral when it's clinically indicated. If you say no, that alone can't be used to deny you the medicine.

You don't need to apply for this. The law took effect July 1, 2026, and the Virginia Board of Medicine's updated rule took effect Sept. 23, 2026. If counseling is clinically indicated, your prescriber still has to offer it or provide a referral and document that in your medical record.

The change came out of the Joint Commission on Health Care's study of ways to respond to the fentanyl crisis. In 2025, the Virginia Society of Addiction Medicine asked the Board of Medicine to drop the counseling requirement, and the board voted that August to take no action. Sen. Todd Pillion carried SB 641, and Del. Otto Wachsmann carried the identical House bill, HB 712. Neither bill drew a single no vote in either chamber. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both on April 6, 2026, and held a ceremonial signing in Abingdon that May.
