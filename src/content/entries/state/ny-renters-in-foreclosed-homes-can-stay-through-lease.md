If you rented a New York home or apartment after a foreclosure case had already begun, the eventual new owner usually must let you stay. After the owner serves written notice, you usually get at least 90 days or the rest of a good-faith lease, whichever is longer, with the protected lease term capped at three years. You must keep paying rent, but you don't need to apply. The notice must identify the new owner and give an address.

New York law had already protected tenants who lived there when the foreclosure began. The 2020 law extended the definition of tenant to people who rented during the case, and the 2021 follow-up set the cap and conditions. The tenant cannot be the property owner, and rent cannot be substantially below market unless the unit is subsidized. A new owner who will live in one unit can limit that tenant to 90 days unless the unit is subsidized. Rent-controlled and rent-stabilized tenants have separate protections. The change applies to foreclosure cases begun on or after January 1, 2021.

Sen. Leroy Comrie and Assemblymember Jaime Williams sponsored the parallel bills. Versions date to 2015–16. The Assembly passed the original bill 97–45 and the Senate 43–17 on July 21, 2020; Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it December 23. A follow-up chapter amendment, requested when Cuomo approved the first bill, added the three-year cap and lease conditions. The Senate passed it 48–15 and the Assembly 99–50 before Cuomo signed it March 25, 2021.
