If you drive a truck that brings concrete or asphalt to a public construction site in New York City or in Nassau, Suffolk, Westchester or Putnam County, or hauls it away, you must now be paid the prevailing wage when the hauling is within 50 miles of the worksite. That's the local wage and benefits rate set for public works jobs. The law covers the time you spend loading and unloading. It also covers return trips, whether your truck is full or empty.

You don't have to apply. Employers have to pay the rate, and covered time must be included in certified payroll records. The law took effect on December 12, 2025, the day it was signed, and applies to contracts signed on or after that date.

New York already required prevailing wages for drivers hauling sand, gravel, stone, dirt and fill to public jobs, under a 2021 change to state labor law. This law extends that protection to concrete and asphalt haulers in the downstate region. An earlier version of the bill stalled in committee in 2023–2024. Assembly Member Steven Otis sponsored this one, and Sen. Shelley Mayer carried the Senate version. The Assembly passed it on June 5, 2025. The Senate followed on June 9 by a 56–6 vote, with Democrats and most Republicans in favor. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 12, 2025, even though some contractor groups had urged a veto.
