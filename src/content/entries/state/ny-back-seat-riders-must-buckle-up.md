In 1984, New York became the first state to require seat belts, but the rule covered only drivers and front-seat passengers. Since Nov. 1, 2020, adults in the back seat of most passenger vehicles have had to buckle up too. Separate laws that took effect the same day applied the rule to taxis and liveries; together, the laws also cover ride-hail cars like Uber and Lyft throughout the state. In other passenger vehicles, kids under 16 already had to be belted or in a car seat.

The reason is simple: a seat belt keeps you in place in a crash. According to AAA Northeast data cited by the bill's sponsors, an unbelted back-seat rider is twice as likely to be killed and eight times as likely to be seriously hurt. An unbelted rider can also be thrown into the people up front and raise their risk of dying. Over a decade, 289 people were killed and 25,596 hurt in New York while riding unbelted in the back seat.

You don't sign up for anything; you just buckle up. A rider who doesn't can be fined up to $50. Police can stop a car just because a back-seat passenger isn't buckled.

Assemblymember Walter Mosley and Sen. David Carlucci sponsored the bill. It passed the Assembly 105–39 in February 2020 and the Senate 54–8 that March. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Aug. 11, 2020.
