If you live in Minnesota and want a COVID-19 vaccine, you can get one from a pharmacist, even if you're under 65 and healthy. In August 2025 the FDA limited the shots mainly to older adults and people with underlying health conditions. Minnesota uses a state standing order to give pharmacists the authority to vaccinate anyone 3 or older who asks.

You don't need to apply or bring a prescription; just go to a pharmacy that has the vaccine. Calling ahead is a good idea for young children, and so is asking your insurer whether the shot is covered. The state protocol doesn't guarantee coverage. People without insurance, or whose insurance falls short, may qualify for state programs that offer vaccines at low or no cost.

Gov. Tim Walz (D) issued Executive Order 25-09 on Sept. 8, 2025, after federal health officials narrowed their vaccine recommendations. The order told the Minnesota Department of Health to issue a COVID-19 standing order. It also told the health and commerce departments to meet with insurers and providers to remove barriers to vaccines. The department issued the standing order on Sept. 22, 2025, and posted new protocols for the 2026–27 season. In 2026, the Legislature dropped a provision that would have required insurers to cover recommended vaccines at no cost, so coverage still depends on your plan.
