Lead poisoning can permanently harm a young child's development, and it often has no symptoms. The 2022 law put a checkup rule into statute: your child's primary care provider must use a state questionnaire to assess lead-exposure risk at every routine well-child visit from 6 months until age 6. If your child misses routine checkups, the assessment is required at least annually. Providers must also give parents prevention guidance and contact information for their county's lead prevention program.

You don't need to apply. The law added pre-K and kindergarten to a proof-of-screening rule that already covered child care and nursery schools. Before or within three months after enrolling a child under 6, these programs must obtain proof of a lead screening. Without proof, they must give parents lead information and refer them to a primary care provider or local health authority. Schools that apply for and receive registry access can use state records to confirm a screening.

The law is named for Dakota, who lived in a Bronx public housing apartment. She was tested at ages 1 and 2. After she changed doctors at 4, another screening found her lead level had risen from 5 to 45 micrograms per deciliter, leaving permanent developmental challenges. Her mother, Tiesha Jones, helped Sen. Gustavo Rivera write the proposal. Rivera first introduced Dakota's Law in 2018; Assembly Majority Leader Crystal Peoples-Stokes carried the Assembly version. It passed the Senate 63–0 and the Assembly 146–1 before Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on August 17, 2022.
