Before 2019, New York law let someone charged with intentional murder seek to have the charge reduced by proving they acted under "extreme emotional disturbance." This law closes off one basis for that defense. Learning or being told the victim's sexual orientation, sex, gender, gender identity, gender expression or sex assigned at birth can no longer count as a reasonable excuse.

The rule applies statewide to intentional second-degree murder, first-degree murder and aggravated murder charges for which extreme emotional disturbance is an affirmative defense. No one has to apply for anything. Advocates pointed to the 2013 killing of Islan Nettles, a transgender woman beaten to death in Harlem, whose attacker said he did not want to be "fooled" after learning she was transgender.

Sen. Brad Hoylman, then the only openly LGBTQ member of the State Senate, sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Daniel O'Donnell carried it in the Assembly. O'Donnell credited former Assemblymember Rubén Díaz Jr. as the measure's first Assembly author. Gov. Andrew Cuomo had asked for the change as one of his program bills. The Senate passed it 62–0 on June 19, 2019, and the Assembly passed it the same day. Cuomo signed it on June 30, 2019, during WorldPride.
