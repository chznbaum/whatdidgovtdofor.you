If you're in a Virginia public high school, you don't have to give up a Saturday or find a ride to a far-off test center to take the SAT. Every school board now has to join the College Board's SAT School Day program, or a similar one. Each student gets at least one chance to take the test at their own high school, during regular school hours, in junior or senior year. Testing windows for this school year run in October 2026 and from March to April 2027.

You don't have to take it. If your school offers the fall date, it should line up with the PSAT where it can, so students can take one test or the other on the same day. The law doesn't waive the test fee or say who pays it. Some divisions, including Fairfax and Prince William counties, offer the school-day SAT to students at no cost. Ask your school whether it is free and, if not, what it charges.

The idea came from Saniya Yamin, a Loudoun County student who spent a year pressing for it. Del. David Reid carried the bill, HB 410. It passed the Senate 40–0, and the House approved the final version 85–12. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
