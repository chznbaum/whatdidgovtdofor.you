Manufactured homes are built in a factory and cost far less than a house built on site. One study using 2020 data estimated that building and installing a single-section manufactured home cost about $57,000, 35% as much as a comparable site-built home, before land and certain site costs. Until this law, most Virginia localities only allowed them on land zoned for farming.

Since July 1, 2026, any Virginia city, county or town must allow a manufactured home in every zoning district where site-built homes are allowed. The home must sit on its own lot, be made part of the real estate, and get its occupancy permit within five years of the date it was built. Localities can't treat it differently or more strictly than a single-family house in the same district. You go through the same local permits as for a site-built house, and the owner must convert the manufactured home to real property. Historic-district protections still apply, and so do neighborhood covenants, such as HOA rules, that restrict manufactured homes.

Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg and Del. Michelle Maldonado carried identical Senate and House bills, with Sens. Jennifer Carroll Foy and Todd Pillion joining the Senate bill as joint patrons. Both bills passed the General Assembly almost unanimously, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed them on March 31, 2026. "The need for affordable and attainable housing is not a Democratic or Republican issue," Maldonado said.
