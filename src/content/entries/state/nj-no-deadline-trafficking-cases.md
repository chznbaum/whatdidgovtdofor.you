If you survived human trafficking in New Jersey, there's no longer a deadline to sue the people who trafficked you. You can also sue people who profited from the trafficking if they knew, or recklessly ignored, where the money came from. Prosecutors can now bring trafficking charges at any time, along with charges for aggravated criminal sexual contact. Before, trafficking charges and lawsuits generally had to be filed within five years.

You don't have to apply for anything. The law took effect the day it was signed. It covers any case whose deadline hadn't already passed by then, so a case that was still open on Nov. 20, 2026 can now be filed at any time. Fourth-degree criminal sexual contact against a child keeps its current limit: five years after the victim turns 18, or two years after the abuse comes to light, whichever is later.

Sen. Linda Greenstein introduced S1270 in January 2026 with Sen. Renee Burgess. Sens. Vin Gopal and Troy Singleton introduced S2566 to remove the deadline for trafficking prosecutions; lawmakers combined it with S1270 in June. Assembly members Sterley Stanley, Dan Hutchison and Shanique Speight carried the measure in the Assembly. Gopal said survivors shouldn't lose their chance at justice because they didn't come forward within five years. The bill passed with no votes against: 40–0 in the Senate and 79–0 in the Assembly. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on Nov. 20, 2026.
