If you work in New York and are not a manager or supervisor, your employer can't fire, discipline or otherwise discriminate against you for refusing to attend a meeting, or watch or listen to a message, when its main purpose is to share the company's opinions on politics or religion. "Political" covers elections, parties, laws and rules, and your decision whether to join or support a union. These sessions are often called "captive audience" meetings, and employers have long used them during union drives.

You don't have to apply for this. The law took effect the day it was signed, and employers must post a notice telling workers about it. Optional casual conversations and information you need to do your job, or that the law requires your employer to share, are still allowed. A narrow exemption lets certain religious organizations require connected employees to hear religious speech; it does not cover political or anti-union messages. If an employer breaks the law, the Attorney General can go to court to stop it, a judge can fine the employer $300 for a first violation and $500 for each one after that, and workers can sue for damages.

State Sen. Jessica Ramos, who chairs the Senate Labor Committee, carried the bill in the Senate, where it passed 53–9 in June 2023. Assembly Member Karines Reyes sponsored it in the Assembly, which passed it two days later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on September 6, 2023. Business groups have argued that federal labor law overrides state bans like this one. As of November 18, 2026, New York's law remained in force.
