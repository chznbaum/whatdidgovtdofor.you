If your child attends a traditional New York City public school, the city is phasing in target class sizes of 20 students in kindergarten through 3rd grade, 23 in grades 4 through 8 and 25 in high school. Physical education and performing groups have a 40-student target. These are not yet absolute caps for every class: 70% of counted classes had to comply in 2026-27, and approved exemptions are allowed. Families do not apply.

The city reported 73.4% of counted classes within the targets in fall 2026. It counted elementary art, gym, music and other “cluster” classes for the first time; without those classes, the chancellor said 69.5% met the targets. Full compliance is due by September 2030, subject to exemptions.

Sen. John Liu sponsored the 2022 bill. Its Assembly version was introduced at Assemblymember Manny De Los Santos's request, and the UFT named Liu, Assembly Education Chair Michael Benedetto and De Los Santos as lead champions. It passed the Senate 59-4 and Assembly 147-2. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 8, 2022. A 2023 amendment sponsored by Liu and De Los Santos delayed the phase-in start to September 2023.

In 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani lobbied for more time amid a budget deficit. Liu sponsored, and Assemblymember George Alvarez requested, a law that lowered that year's target from 80% to 70% and moved the final deadline from 2028 to 2030. Hochul signed it June 26.
