Leaving an abusive home can mean paying a security deposit and first month's rent at once. Florida offers a one-time payment to help survivors get out. This law raised the cap from $1,500 to $2,500 per claim and the lifetime limit from $3,000 to $5,000. To qualify, there must be proof of a domestic violence offense and a report to the proper authorities. You must apply through a certified Florida domestic violence center, which must confirm your need and safety plan.

The law also adds court protections. Judges can now weigh threats to injure or kill a family pet and whether a military protective order exists when deciding on a domestic violence injunction. Dating- and sexual-violence orders go into the statewide database police use to verify orders, and penalties rise for repeat domestic violence offenses.

Through June 2028, Pinellas judges can order monitoring during probation in qualifying misdemeanor cases; judges across Pinellas and Pasco can do so in qualifying felony cases. It applies after a guilty finding, withheld adjudication, or no-contest plea, when probation includes a no-contact order. Monitoring is mandatory if clear and convincing evidence shows a threat of violence or physical harm to the victim.

Democratic Rep. Debra Tendrich, who has publicly discussed surviving domestic violence, sponsored the bill with Republican Rep. Danny Nix. Republican Sen. Alexis Calatayud carried the Senate version, co-introduced by Democratic Sen. Lori Berman. Both chambers passed it without a no vote, and Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it May 21, 2026.
