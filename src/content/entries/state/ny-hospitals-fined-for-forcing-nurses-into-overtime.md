Since 2009, New York law has barred hospitals, nursing homes and other licensed health care employers from making nurses work past their scheduled hours, except during disasters, declared emergencies, unplanned emergencies, or a procedure already underway. The rule came with no fines, so employers faced little cost for breaking it. This law adds them. The state Labor Department can now fine an employer up to $1,000 for a first violation, $2,000 for a second within a year, and $3,000 for each one after that.

It covers registered nurses and licensed practical nurses who provide direct patient care. Employers now have to tell the state each time they use one of the exceptions. They file a detailed report if they use mandatory overtime on 15 or more days in a month, and they must explain the overtime and say when it will end if they use it on 45 or more days in three months. A nurse, a group of nurses, or their union can file a complaint with the Labor Department online or by phone.

Assembly Member Aileen Gunther and Sen. Robert Jackson carried the bill, and the Senate passed it 55–8 in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 30, 2022, on condition that it be changed. The follow-up law, also sponsored by Gunther and Jackson, added the reporting rules. It also dropped a provision that would have paid nurses an extra 15% of their overtime pay for each violation. The changes took effect June 28, 2023.
