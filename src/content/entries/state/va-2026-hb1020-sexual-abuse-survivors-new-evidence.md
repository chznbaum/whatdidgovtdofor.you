In Virginia, survivors of sexual abuse suffered as a child or while incapacitated generally have 20 years to sue once the legal clock starts. Before this law, the clock started when the survivor turned 18 or regained capacity, or when a doctor or psychologist first linked their injuries to the abuse, whichever came later. Under this law, the clock can also start when the survivor finds corroborating evidence, or when they reasonably should have found it.

Corroborating evidence means independent proof that backs up the claim. It includes physical evidence like biological samples, records, recordings or photos. It also includes witness statements, such as testimony that the abuser did something similar to someone else, and a confession or admission by the abuser. Survivors don't need to apply for anything. The new start date applies when they file a lawsuit. For claims against an organization rather than the abuser, the new start date only covers abuse that happens on or after July 1, 2026.

RAINN helped draft the legislation. Del. Atoosa Reaser carried the bill in the House, and Sen. Barbara Favola carried an identical Senate bill, SB 37. Both parties backed it: the House passed it 98–0 and the Senate 38–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
