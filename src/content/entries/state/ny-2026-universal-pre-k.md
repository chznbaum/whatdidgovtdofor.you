New York is making pre-K free for every 4-year-old. Under the budget Gov. Kathy Hochul signed on May 28, 2026, every 4-year-old in the state is supposed to have a free pre-K seat in their school district by the start of the 2028–29 school year. Seats are offered through local school districts, and they're for all 4-year-olds, not just those from low-income families.

The budget added $563 million a year in state pre-K funding for the 2026–27 school year, as part of a $1.7 billion increase in state spending on child care and early education. The money is already showing up: more than 400 school districts, over half the districts in the state, added free pre-K seats this fall, and the state expects tens of thousands more seats over the next few years.

The same budget also expanded help with child care costs for younger children, capping costs at $15 a week for many families in the state's Child Care Assistance Program, and raised the state's child and dependent care tax credit, which the state says will help about 230,000 families. Those changes are rolling out on their own schedules. For families with 4-year-olds, universal pre-K is the most concrete piece: a free seat in their own school district within two school years.
