Starting December 19, 2026, period products sold or distributed in New York can't contain certain harmful chemicals as an intentionally added ingredient, at any level. The law covers tampons, pads, liners and menstrual cups, both disposable and reusable. The list includes lead, mercury, formaldehyde, triclosan, toluene, talc, two phthalates, four parabens, a fragrance chemical called butylphenyl methylpropional, and PFAS, the "forever chemicals."

You don't have to do anything. The rule applies to what manufacturers put in products, so what's on store shelves in New York has to comply. These products sit against some of the body's most absorbent skin for hours at a time, which is why advocates pushed for the ban. Other states, including California, Colorado and Maine, already restrict PFAS in period products. New York's law also covers parabens and heavy metals.

Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal and Sen. Nathalia Fernandez carried the bill, which passed the Senate 59-0 and the Assembly 148-1 with support from both parties in 2025. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 19, 2025, after agreeing with lawmakers on changes. Rosenthal and Fernandez passed those changes in early 2026, and Hochul signed them on February 13, 2026. The changes kept the ban on added chemicals and wrote the full list into the law. They also took out a plan for the Department of Health to set limits on trace amounts of these chemicals that end up in products by accident, as well as the health commissioner's power to add chemicals to the list.
