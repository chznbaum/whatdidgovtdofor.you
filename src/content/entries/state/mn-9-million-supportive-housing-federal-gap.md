Since 1994, the federal Continuum of Care program has helped pay for housing and services for people leaving homelessness, including permanent supportive housing for people with serious health needs or addiction, rapid rehousing and transitional housing. Changes to how HUD administered those grants left providers facing potential funding gaps in 2026. Chapter 43 created a state backstop to help keep housing and services going while providers waited for federal money.

Chapter 43 redirected $9 million from an existing $10 million supportive housing appropriation that had not been spent. In May, Chapter 100 added $4 million from Minnesota Housing investment earnings, bringing the program to $13 million. It also expanded eligibility to fiscal-year 2024 or 2025 federal grantees whose contracts expired or will expire before the end of 2027 and who face a funding gap. Tenants do not apply. Minnesota Housing awards money directly to providers, may prioritize need in coordination with local Continuums of Care, and may award grants without a competition. The state grants must be repaid if a provider's federal renewal funding restarts. Providers report every 90 days after the money is obligated on spending and people served.

Rep. Michael Howard and Sen. Lindsey Port, both Democrats, carried the bill. Republican Rep. Spencer Igo joined as a coauthor and backed it in committee. The House passed it 103–31 and the Senate 39–28. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on March 27, 2026, and it took effect the next day.
