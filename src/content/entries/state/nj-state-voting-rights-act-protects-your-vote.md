New Jersey now has its own voting rights law. It bans election rules that keep voters of color from voting or leave them less able to vote than other voters. It also bans election setups, like at-large seats, that water down their votes. Threatening, tricking, or blocking voters or election workers is illegal, including blocking the way to a polling place, ballot drop box, or elections office. It also bars voting barriers based on disability, sex, sexual orientation, or gender identity unless the state shows a compelling reason.

You don't have to apply for anything. If a rule shuts you out, you, a voters' group, or the state Attorney General can sue in state court. Starting Oct. 1, 2027, places with a record of discrimination will need the Attorney General's OK before changing things like polling sites or voting maps. A language will also get ballot materials when more than 4,000 eligible voters who speak it have limited English, or when they make up more than 2% of voters and number at least 300.

Assemblywoman Verlina Reynolds-Jackson and Sens. Shirley Turner, Joe Cryan, and Nick Scutari carried the bill. Assembly Speaker Craig Coughlin also championed it, the New Jersey Institute for Social Justice led a coalition of more than 60 groups that worked for four years, and then-Lt. Gov. Dale Caldwell helped lead the process. First introduced in 2022, it won passage after the U.S. Supreme Court further weakened the federal Voting Rights Act. On June 30, 2026, the Senate passed it 25–14 and the Assembly approved the final version 57–21. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on July 2, 2026.
