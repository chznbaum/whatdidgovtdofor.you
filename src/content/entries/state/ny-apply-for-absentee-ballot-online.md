If you qualify for an absentee ballot in New York, you can ask for one online. You don't have to print, sign, and mail a form. You fill out the state's online application, swear that it's true, and agree to let your elections board use the signature the DMV or the board already has on file. If you'd rather, you can upload your signature instead. The law counts your request as filed the day you send it.

The online application must be offered in every language the federal Voting Rights Act requires, and it must work with the tools voters with disabilities use. If the board can't find your signature, it must ask you for one. If you still do not provide one, the board must send your ballot with a paper application for you to sign and return with the ballot. Mail and in-person requests still work too. New York City's board takes online requests at requestballot.vote.nyc. For the November 2026 election, online and mailed requests had to arrive by Oct. 24.

The state first opened an online request site in 2020, during the pandemic. This law made it permanent. Assembly Member Latrice Walker and Sen. Zellnor Myrie sponsored it. It passed the Senate 43–20, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican voting no. A Republican co-chair of the state Board of Elections warned that someone could request a ballot in another voter's name. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 22, 2021. A later chapter amendment delayed its effective date from Jan. 1 to April 1, 2022.
