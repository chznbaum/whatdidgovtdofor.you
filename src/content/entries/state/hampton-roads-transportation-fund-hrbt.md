A 2013 state law created a pot of road money that only Hampton Roads can spend. Since July 2013, shoppers in 14 Hampton Roads cities and counties have paid an extra 0.7% sales tax, and fuel sold in the region carries an added tax, now a set amount per gallon. That money goes into the Hampton Roads Transportation Fund, run since 2014 by a commission of the region's mayors, county leaders and state lawmakers.

The biggest project it pays for is the HRBT expansion. The $3.9 billion job adds two new two-lane tunnels under the harbor between Hampton and Norfolk and widens nearly 10 miles of Interstate 64, taking the crossing from four lanes to eight. Some of the new lanes will be express lanes, free for carpools and buses and tolled for solo drivers. About 92% of the cost comes from regional funds. The fund has also paid to widen I-64 in Newport News and replace Chesapeake's High Rise Bridge.

The regional fund was part of a statewide transportation funding law carried by Republican House Speaker Bill Howell. Republican Gov. Bob McDonnell amended the bill, and the General Assembly approved his version on April 3, 2013.
