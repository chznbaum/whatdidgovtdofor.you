For one year, from Thanksgiving 2022 to Thanksgiving 2023, New York let people who were sexually abused as adults go to court no matter how long ago it happened. Normally a civil suit has to be filed within a set number of years, so many survivors had been shut out for good. The law brought those expired claims back for the year and told the courts to set rules for handling them quickly.

Survivors didn't apply for anything. They filed a lawsuit before the window closed. More than 3,000 suits were filed, and many named prisons, jails and hospitals where survivors said abuse was ignored or hidden. The window has now closed. In 2026, about 1,500 of these cases were still in the courts.

The law was modeled on the 2019 Child Victims Act. That same year, New York gave adult survivors 20 years to sue over certain sex crimes, but the change didn't help people whose deadlines had already passed. Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal carried the Adult Survivors Act. Safe Horizon and a survivor-led coalition pushed for it for years. The Senate passed it without opposition in 2021, but it died in the Assembly. In 2022 the Senate passed it 62–0 again, and this time the Assembly followed, 140–3. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on May 24, 2022.
