Virginia law has long limited how much of your paycheck a creditor can garnish. Debts owed to the state for taxes were an exception. Starting July 1, 2027, those limits apply to state tax debt too. Each week, Virginia can take no more than the smaller of two amounts: 25% of your disposable pay—the earnings left after deductions required by law—or whatever you earn above 40 times the higher of the federal or Virginia minimum wage. If your weekly disposable pay is at or below that floor, none of it can be garnished for state taxes.

The law also helps people the IRS has marked "Currently Not Collectible," a status for taxpayers who can't afford to pay. Virginia Tax must recognize that status and offer a matching state status with similar protection from collection. The department says it has already been doing this on its own. The law makes it a requirement, and you don't have to apply for the cap. Federal tax levies aren't covered.

A 2025 bill to close the tax-debt exception stalled in the state Senate, and lawmakers asked Virginia Tax to study the issue instead. In 2026, Del. Phil Hernandez carried HB 488. It passed the House 98–0 and the Senate 40–0, with no votes against it in either chamber. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026.
