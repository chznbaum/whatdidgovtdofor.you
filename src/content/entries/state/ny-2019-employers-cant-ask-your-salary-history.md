When you apply for a job in New York, the employer can't ask what you earned before, whether on an application, in an interview, or by calling your old boss. It can't base the decision to hire you, or the pay it offers, on your past pay. If you already work there, your employer can't ask what you're paid at other jobs, and it can't punish you for refusing to answer or for filing a complaint.

You can still share your past pay if you choose to, for example to negotiate a higher offer. If an employer breaks the rule, you can file a complaint with the state Department of Labor or sue for damages and lawyer's fees. The law covers private employers, government agencies, and recruiters.

The ban had been years in the making. Assemblyman Marcos Crespo sponsored the Assembly bill after carrying versions of the measure for five years. Sen. David Carlucci sponsored the Senate bill that became law. Gov. Andrew Cuomo also proposed a statewide ban and signed the bill on July 10, 2019. It passed the Senate 43–19, with four Republicans joining Democrats, and passed the Assembly 126–20. It took effect January 6, 2020.
