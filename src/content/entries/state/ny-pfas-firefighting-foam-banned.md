Some foams used on fuel fires contain PFAS, "forever chemicals" linked to cancer, thyroid disease and other health problems. Foam spills have fouled drinking water from Newburgh to Long Island. Under this law, no one in New York, whether a state agency, a fire department or a company, may use Class B foam with added PFAS for training. Since March 22, 2022, manufacturers generally can't make, sell or ship it for use in the state. For prohibited foam, they must recall what they sold, pay to dispose of it safely, and refund the buyers.

Firefighters don't have to do anything to be covered. The state fire office found a safe PFAS-free alternative and says it won't grant exemptions. The law also leaves an exception if federal rules require PFAS. Companies that sell firefighters' protective gear must say in writing whether it contains PFAS. Fines run up to $5,000 for a first violation and $10,000 for each repeat violation.

Sen. Brad Hoylman and Assembly Member Phil Steck sponsored the bill. The Senate passed it 62–0, and the Assembly passed it 142–1. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Dec. 23, 2019, after lawmakers agreed to a follow-up bill, also sponsored by Hoylman and Steck. Cuomo signed that amendment in April 2020. The law has since been expanded to ban intentionally added PFAS, and PFAS above a state-set limit, in firefighters' protective gear starting in 2028.
