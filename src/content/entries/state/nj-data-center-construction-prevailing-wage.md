New Jersey's prevailing-wage law used to cover mainly public construction. Now it also covers private data centers designed to use 5 megawatts of power or more a year. If you work on one of these projects, whether you're building it, tearing down, altering, fabricating custom parts, or making repairs, you must be paid at least the going rate the state Labor Department sets for your trade. If a subcontractor shorts you, the general contractor is on the hook too.

You don't have to apply. The rule covers projects that started on or after April 1, 2026. Contractors must be registered with the state, file certified payrolls, carry insurance and bonds, and make sure workers get OSHA safety training. They can only bring in temp workers who are enrolled in a registered apprenticeship. Projects of 25 megawatts or more can also use a project labor agreement with the building-trades unions.

The push began after a data center developer in Vineland brought in outside workers through labor brokers. The Carpenters union then worked with Sen. Paul Sarlo and Assembly Majority Leader Lou Greenwald to craft the bill. Sarlo and Sen. Patrick Diegnan were its Senate prime sponsors; Greenwald and Assemblymember Heather Simmons were the Assembly version's prime sponsors. The Legislature passed it in its January 2026 lame-duck session: 55–15 in the Assembly and 28–10 in the Senate. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 20, 2026, one of his last acts in office. In August 2026, Gov. Mikie Sherrill's administration sent towns guidance on these wage rules.
