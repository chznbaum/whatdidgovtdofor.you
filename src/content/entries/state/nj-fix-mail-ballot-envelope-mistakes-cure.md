If you vote by mail in New Jersey, small envelope mistakes can get your ballot thrown out. Leaving out the inner envelope, skipping the certificate, or not sealing the envelope have all been grounds for rejection, and until now only signature problems could be fixed. This law changes that. If your county can identify you from the pre-printed information and there's still time, it must send you a new mail-in ballot. Ballots can't be rejected for problems you didn't cause, like a torn envelope or weak glue.

You don't have to apply for this cure. If the county can identify your name and address from official pre-printed information and has time to issue another mail ballot, it must send a cure letter and a replacement ballot. You return the new ballot under the usual mail-ballot deadlines. If you receive a cure letter before Election Day, you can instead vote a provisional ballot during early voting or on Election Day.

Sen. Paul Sarlo introduced the bill in November 2025, after a Bergen County judge threw out more than 400 mail-in ballots, most of them over envelope problems. Assembly members Clinton Calabrese, Ellen Park and Joe Danielsen carried it in the Assembly. Sarlo also worked with then-Lt. Gov. and Secretary of State Tahesha Way as the legislation was developed. Both chambers passed it on Jan. 12, 2026, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, his last day in office. The state's voter system has to be updated first, and election officials say the changes will start in 2027, not in time for the November 2026 election.
