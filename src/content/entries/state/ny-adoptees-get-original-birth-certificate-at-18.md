If you were adopted and born in New York, you can get a certified copy of your original birth certificate once you turn 18. That's the certificate issued before the adoption, and it shows the birth-parent information recorded when you were born. You get it on the same terms as anyone else gets theirs. New York had sealed these records since 1936. Before this law, adoptees had to go to court, which cost money and often ended in a no.

You apply to the state Health Department if you were born outside New York City, or to the city Health Department if you were born in the city. Adoptees can order online. If an adoptee has died, their children, grandchildren or great-grandchildren, or a lawyer for them, can apply by mail. The law also stopped adoption agencies from withholding non-identifying information, such as family medical history.

Adult adoptees pushed for the change for more than 20 years. Assemblyman David Weprin championed it for a decade, and Sen. Velmanette Montgomery carried it in the Senate. It passed the Senate 56–6 and the Assembly 140–6 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on November 14, 2019. Requests opened on January 15, 2020, and more than 3,600 adoptees born outside the city applied in the first 48 hours.
