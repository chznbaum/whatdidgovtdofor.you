People housed in New York's general confinement prisons work jobs that can include making license plates, and the wages are very low. Until late 2022, state law also allowed the prison system to collect up to $1 a week from that compensation as an "incarceration fee" to help cover incarceration costs. The fee provision was repealed, so the state no longer has that authority.

The change happened automatically. Nobody had to apply, and it took effect the day it was signed. A dollar a week sounds small, but it could take a real share of prison pay. At the maximum rate, 52 weeks of the fee added up to $52. The repeal did not change other deductions from prison pay.

Assemblymember Demond Meeks of Rochester sponsored the bill, and Sen. Zellnor Myrie of Brooklyn carried the Senate version. Sen. Rachel May co-sponsored Myrie's bill. The Assembly passed it 95–50 on May 16, 2022. The Senate passed it 41–20 eight days later; all 41 yes votes came from Democrats, and all 20 no votes came from Republicans. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 23, 2022.
