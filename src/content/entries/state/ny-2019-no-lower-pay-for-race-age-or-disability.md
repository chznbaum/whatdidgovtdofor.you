In New York, your employer can't pay you less than a coworker because of your age, race, creed, color, national origin, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, military status, sex, disability, genetic traits, family or marital status, or because you've survived domestic violence. Before this law, the state's equal pay rule covered only pay gaps between men and women.

The law also widened what counts as comparable work. You no longer have to find someone doing the exact same job. It's enough that the work is substantially similar in skill, effort and responsibility and done under similar conditions. Employers can still pay differently based on seniority, merit, how much or how well you produce, or real job-related factors like education, training or experience.

You don't have to apply. If you think you're underpaid, you can complain to the state Labor Department or the Attorney General's office, or bring your own lawsuit.

Sen. Alessandra Biaggi, then in her first year in the Senate, sponsored the bill, and Assembly Member Karen McMahon carried it in the Assembly. The same expansion had first appeared in Gov. Andrew Cuomo's 2019 executive budget. It passed the Senate 61–1 and the Assembly 138–9 in June 2019. Cuomo signed it on July 10, 2019, the day of New York City's parade for the World Cup-winning U.S. women's soccer team. It took effect that October.
