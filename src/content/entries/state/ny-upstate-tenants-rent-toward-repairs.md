If your landlord won't fix a dangerous condition, you and your neighbors can go to court. No heat, running water, electricity or working sewage generally must last five days; a rodent infestation also qualifies. A judge can order rents paid to a court-appointed administrator, who uses the money for repairs. Any surplus goes back to the owner. The owner may ask to make the repairs instead, but must show the court they can do the work and post security.

In a building with several units, at least a third of the tenants must sign on. In a single-family rental, one tenant can file. The local code enforcement office can also start a case. Cases are heard in county, district or city court. The law applies everywhere except New York City and Nassau, Suffolk, Westchester and Rockland counties. Those places already had a similar process under Article 7-A.

Assemblymember John McDonald first introduced the idea in the 2019–20 session. The Assembly passed his bill in 2021 and again in 2022, and Sen. Sean Ryan carried it in the Senate. The Senate passed it 43–20 in June 2022, with one Republican voting yes and one Democrat voting no. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 15, 2022. A 2023 chapter amendment delayed its start until June 13, 2023, giving courts time to prepare.
