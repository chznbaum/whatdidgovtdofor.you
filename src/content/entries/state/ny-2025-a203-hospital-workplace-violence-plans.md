Health care workers are hurt by workplace violence more often than workers in many other fields. This law requires every general hospital and nursing home in New York to run a workplace violence prevention program. Starting in 2027, general hospitals must assess threats each year. They must consider incident reports, entrances, alarms, training and local conditions, then write a plan to address the risks. The plan can include more staff, better lighting, alarms and barriers.

Hospital employees and their unions must be actively involved in the assessment and plan; hospitals may use existing safety or labor-management committees. Hospitals must give staff a written summary, explain how to report an incident, and share redacted summaries of incident logs and trends with the safety committee. Emergency departments also get security. In a city or county with at least 1 million people, an off-duty officer or trained security worker must be in the ER at all times, except when an emergency requires moving personnel. Elsewhere, one must be on site and assigned near the ER. Critical access, sole community and rural emergency hospitals are exempt unless their violence rate rises enough to trigger the requirement.

Assembly Member Catalina Cruz and Sen. Luis Sepúlveda carried the bill. Earlier versions had been introduced since 2018, and the Workplace Violence Prevention Alliance advocated for it for nearly two years. Republicans co-sponsored the Senate bill. The Assembly passed it 146–0 and the Senate 58–1 on June 11, 2025. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 12, 2025.
