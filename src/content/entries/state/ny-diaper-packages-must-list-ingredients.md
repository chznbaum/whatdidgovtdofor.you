A baby can wear diapers around the clock for years, but until recently the package didn't have to say what they were made of. Now every package or box of diapers sold in New York must print, or attach, a clear list of every ingredient the maker added on purpose. The list runs from the largest amount to the smallest. The rule covers disposable and reusable diapers.

You don't have to do anything: the list should be on the package wherever you shop in the state. Makers had 12 months after the law was signed to comply. That deadline was December 2025. A maker that sells diapers without the list faces a fine of up to $1,000 per package. The law covers only ingredients added on purpose, not contaminants that get in during manufacturing.

Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal and Sen. Cordell Cleare carried the bill. A version was first introduced in the 2019–20 session. The sponsors pointed to research that found chemicals such as toluene and xylene in disposable diapers. The Senate first passed it in 2023, but it died in the Assembly. The Assembly passed it in March 2024, and the Senate passed it again that June, 54–7, with all seven no votes from Republicans. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 11, 2024. Reporting at the time called New York the first state to require ingredient lists on diaper packages.
