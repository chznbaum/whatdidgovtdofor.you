If you volunteer with a fire department or rescue squad in Virginia, your employer can't fire, discipline, threaten or otherwise penalize you for missing work to answer an emergency alarm or to serve during a declared state of emergency. That includes cutting your pay, changing your schedule or moving you to another location as punishment.

To be covered, you must be an active member in good standing of a volunteer fire or EMS agency, or one of its auxiliary units, that your city or county recognizes. Tell your employer at least one hour before your shift that you're responding, and give notice again each day if the call runs longer. When you return, bring a copy of the incident report and a note from the officer in charge confirming you were needed and when you were released. Your employer doesn't have to pay you for the missed time, but you can use sick leave or other paid leave you've earned. Workers who are deemed essential by law or by contract aren't covered. If your employer retaliates, you have one year to sue for your job back, lost wages and benefits, and attorney fees.

Sen. Bill Stanley, a Republican, introduced the bill as part of a package for first responders. It passed the Senate without opposition and the House 93–2, with both parties voting for it. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
