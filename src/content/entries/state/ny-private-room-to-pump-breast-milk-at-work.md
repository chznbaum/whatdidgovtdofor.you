If you work in New York and need to pump breast milk, your employer must provide a private room or other location after you ask. It cannot be a restroom or toilet stall. Usually, it must be near your work area, well lit, private and free from interruptions, with a chair, a pump surface, nearby clean running water and an outlet if the workplace has power. You may use a workplace fridge to store your milk. If those requirements would cause significant difficulty or expense, your employer must still provide a nearby private place outside a restroom and meet as many requirements as possible. The law covers every employer, regardless of size.

You must give reasonable advance notice and make your request in writing; an email, text, letter or workplace chat message can count. Your employer must give you a written policy when you're hired, annually and when you return after giving birth, and answer your request within five business days. The law bars retaliation. These protections last for three years after your child is born. Since June 19, 2024, a separate state budget amendment has required paid 30-minute breaks whenever you reasonably need to pump.

Sen. Alessandra Biaggi sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Amy Paulin carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 62–1 in both 2021 and 2022; the first effort died in the Assembly, which passed the bill in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 9, 2022.
