If you live in New York and are 16 or older, you can apply for a standard driver's license or learner permit no matter what your immigration status is. You don't need a Social Security number: if you've never had one, you sign a form saying so. You can prove who you are with documents like a foreign passport or consular ID. You still pass the same written and road tests and pay the same fees as everyone else. The license looks like any other standard New York license and is marked "not for federal purposes," so you can't use it to board a domestic flight.

The law also protects your information. The DMV can't give your records to agencies whose main job is enforcing immigration law unless they bring a court order or a judge's warrant. If one of those agencies asks for your records, the DMV has three days to tell you.

Assembly Member Marcos Crespo and Sen. Luis Sepúlveda carried the bill. It passed the Assembly 87–61 and the Senate 33–29. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 17, 2019, the same day the Senate passed it, and the DMV began taking applications that December. A federal judge threw out a Justice Department lawsuit against the law in December 2025, but the department has appealed.
