PFAS firefighting foam has been linked to kidney cancer, testicular cancer and other diseases. Firefighters are among the workers most at risk. Since July 1, 2026, Florida has banned using the foam for nonemergency training, testing or instruction. Training and testing had routinely released it at fire facilities. Anyone who keeps the foam must report its inventory to the state.

Starting July 1, 2027, the foam cannot be sold, bought or distributed, and leftover stock requires a disposal plan. Starting July 1, 2029, keeping or using it is banned. Airports, military uses with no alternative, emergencies where no other foam is available, and approved retrofit projects are exempt from the 2029 ban. The state may help fire departments and airports pay to switch. Violators can be fined up to $10,000 per violation, per day. Public wastewater systems designed for at least 25,000 gallons a day must also test biosolids and treated water for PFAS quarterly. Until EPA sets water-quality standards and Florida adopts them, those results are informational only.

Reps. Bill Conerly and Omar Blanco sponsored the House bill; Blanco is a firefighter. Sen. Gayle Harrell sponsored the Senate companion, which Sens. Keith Truenow and Lori Berman co-introduced. The measure followed years of PFAS advocacy by the late Rep. Joe Casello and Rep. Rob Long. Named for Casello, a former firefighter who died in 2025, it passed both chambers without a no vote. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on June 16, 2026.
