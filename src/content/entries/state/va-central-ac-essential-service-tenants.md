Virginia's landlord-tenant law treats a short list of things as "essential services": heat, running water, hot water, electricity and gas. Starting July 1, 2026, central air conditioning is on that list too, as long as your landlord supplies it and it was working, or the landlord said it was working, when your lease started. Landlords who rent a unit without central AC don't have to add it.

Being on the list matters because it gives you stronger tools. If your landlord willfully or carelessly fails to keep the AC running, you can send written notice. If it still isn't fixed in a reasonable time, you can recover damages for the lower value of your home or move to reasonable substitute housing and skip rent for that period. You can also recover attorney fees. If a landlord deliberately shuts off your AC without a court order, a judge can order it turned back on. You can also recover your actual damages, plus $5,000 or four months' rent, whichever is more.

You don't need to apply for anything. The protection is part of the law for every covered lease.

Del. Marty Martinez carried the bill. It passed the House of Delegates 63–35 in February 2026 and the Senate 21–19 in March. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
