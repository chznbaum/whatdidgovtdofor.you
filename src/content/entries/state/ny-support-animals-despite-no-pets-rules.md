If you have a disability and an animal alleviates its symptoms or effects, New York's Human Rights Law says the animal can be a reasonable accommodation for housing. A covered landlord, seller, co-op, or other housing provider cannot enforce a no-pets policy when the accommodation is necessary for you to use and enjoy the home.

You request the accommodation from the housing provider. When necessary, the provider may ask for medical information showing that you have a covered disability and that the animal addresses a disability-related need. The law uses the broad word “animal”; it does not restrict this housing accommodation to trained service dogs.

The Division of Human Rights, then led by Commissioner Helen Diane Foster, first asked lawmakers for the change in 2016 after courts overturned some of its accommodation rulings. In Kennedy Street Quad v. Nathanson, a 2009 appeals court ruled that evidence showing a dog eased two residents’ depression did not establish that the dog was necessary for them to use and enjoy their home. The 2020 law added animal accommodations directly to the statute.

Assembly Member Erik Dilan carried versions of the bill for three legislative sessions; the first two passed the Assembly but stalled in the Senate. Sen. Monica Martinez sponsored the 2019 Senate companion. The final Assembly bill passed that chamber in January 2020 and the Senate 51–9 in February. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 11, 2020.
