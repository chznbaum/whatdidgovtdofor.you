A 2022 Florida law gave students with epilepsy or seizure disorders the right to a written seizure action plan at school. The plan says what the child's seizures look like, when to call for help, and how to give rescue medicine. This 2026 law makes those rules cover charter schools as well as regular public schools.

To get a plan in place, a parent gives the school a plan signed by the parent and the child's doctor or other medical professional. The doctor can now write care orders in whatever form they choose. School district and charter school workers who regularly deal with the student must take seizure-care training. The law now says plainly that this includes the people who teach the student and the people who drive them to and from school or school activities. The training counts for five years. Every public school must also post a state poster showing the basic steps for helping someone who is having a seizure.

Reps. Jim Mooney and Omar Blanco sponsored the House bill. Sen. Ileana Garcia sponsored the Senate version, with Sen. Shevrin Jones as a co-sponsor. Both chambers passed it with no votes against: 112-0 in the House and 37-0 in the Senate. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on May 11, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
