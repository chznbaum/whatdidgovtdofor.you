Many costly medicines come with a coupon or a charity grant that pays part of your share. Some insurers and pharmacy benefit managers took that money but didn't count it toward your deductible or out-of-pocket limit. When the coupon ran out partway through the year, patients found they still owed their full deductible at the pharmacy. New Jersey's law ends that. Insurers, plan administrators, and pharmacy benefit managers must now credit any cost-sharing paid by you or by someone else on your behalf.

You don't need to apply. The rule covers health plans that insurers sell or renew in New Jersey on or after April 9, 2026, so some people get it at their plan's next renewal. It doesn't reach self-insured employer plans, the state and school employee health programs, or Medicaid. For high-deductible plans paired with a health savings account, the credit starts once you meet the federal minimum deductible, though it applies to preventive care right away. State analysts said shifting costs from patients to insurers could push premiums up, but they could not estimate by how much; they also said better health outcomes could produce savings.

Assembly Majority Leader Lou Greenwald and Assemblymen Roy Freiman and Sterley Stanley carried the bill in the Assembly. Senate President Nick Scutari and Republican Sen. Jon Bramnick carried it in the Senate. Patient groups for people with HIV, hepatitis, and cancer pushed for it, and it passed both houses without a single no vote. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 9, 2026.
