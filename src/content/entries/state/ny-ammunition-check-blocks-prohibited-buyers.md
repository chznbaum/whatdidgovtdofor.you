New York already requires a background check every time someone buys ammunition. But according to the bill's sponsor, those checks screened buyers only against federal rules. People the state itself bars from having guns could slip through, including people under an extreme risk protection order (often called a red flag order) and people convicted of certain serious crimes under New York law.

This law closes that gap. A store must now deny an ammunition sale if the buyer can't legally have a rifle or shotgun under New York law or federal law, or if a temporary or final red flag order covers them. Families who go to court to get a red flag order against a relative in crisis can count on it blocking ammunition sales as well as gun sales. Buyers who are allowed to have guns don't need to do anything new. The ammunition check uses the same standard as the check for owning a gun.

Sen. Michael Gianaris sponsored the bill in the Senate, where an earlier version passed in 2024. Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 48–11 on May 19, 2026, with all 11 no votes coming from Republicans, and the Assembly passed it on May 28. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Oct. 21, 2026, and it took effect that day. Federal courts have so far upheld New York's ammunition background checks against challenges from gun-rights groups.
