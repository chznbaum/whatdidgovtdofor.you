If you freelance in New York and a covered client hires you for $800 or more of work, alone or added up over 120 days, the deal has to be in writing. The contract must spell out the work, the pay and when you'll be paid, and the client has to keep it for six years. If no payment date is set, you must be paid within 30 days of finishing. A client can't make you accept less money in exchange for paying you on time, and can't punish you for using these rights. You can't sign these rights away.

You don't have to apply. If a client stiffs you, you can sue for damages, including double the unpaid amount plus lawyer's fees, or file a complaint with the state Attorney General, who can investigate and seek back pay and penalties. Lawyers practicing law under the contract, licensed medical professionals, qualifying sales representatives and construction contractors are left out. Federal, New York state and local, and foreign government clients are also excluded.

New York City has protected freelancers this way since 2017. Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assembly Member Harry Bronson, who chairs the Assembly Labor Committee, carried the bill to take those protections statewide. It passed in June 2023 and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that November. This follow-up bill, which passed the Senate 46–13 and the Assembly 144–2, moved the rules into the state's business law and put the Attorney General in charge of enforcing them. Hochul signed it on March 1, 2024, and the protections started Aug. 28, 2024.
