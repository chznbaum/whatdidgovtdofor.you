Starting January 1, 2027, AI chatbot companies can't give minors in New York features the law calls unsafe. A chatbot can't claim to be a real person or to have feelings. It can't pose as a child's friend, partner, or authority figure, flatter them, or tell them what they want to hear. It can't use personal or health details from earlier chats, push kids to keep secrets or avoid asking adults for help, encourage suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, or drug use, or produce sexual content.

Families don't need to apply. Companies can offer these features only after confirming that a user is an adult. They must offer more than one way to check age, including one that either doesn't rely on a government ID or lets the user remain anonymous to the company. They generally must delete age-check data immediately, unless another law requires keeping it. Narrow exemptions cover tools limited to customer service; efficiency, research, or technical help; and internal business use. For the first two, the chatbot can't answer outside that purpose. The state attorney general can seek up to $25,000 per violation and must run a public complaint website.

State Sen. Kristen Gonzalez introduced the bill in January 2026, and Assemblymember Alex Bores carried it in the Assembly. Attorney General Letitia James helped shape it. The Senate passed it 60–0 and the Assembly 137–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 28, 2026.
