If you rent a home in New York, your landlord or management company can't add legal fees to your bill unless a court has ordered you to pay them. That covers court fees, lawyer and attorney fees, notary charges, and administrative fees tied to running the building or taking someone to court. The rule applies to rent-stabilized and rent-controlled apartments and to market-rate rentals. A lease clause or charge that says otherwise is void, so you don't owe it.

You don't have to apply for anything. If a fee like this shows up on your rent statement and no judge ordered it, the law says the landlord had no right to charge it. Before the law, the sponsors said, many tenants paid these fees out of fear of being evicted. A survey cited in the sponsor's memo found that 56% of rent-stabilized tenants had been charged a legal fee in the past year, and the fees averaged more than $1,000.

State Sen. Robert Jackson and Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the bill. It passed the Senate 43–20 in May 2021, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican voting no. The Assembly passed it 101–46 that June with support from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 21, 2021, and it took effect right away. A 2022 amendment exempted co-op shareholders, so co-op boards can bill their own owners for legal work again. People who rent from a co-op owner are still covered.
