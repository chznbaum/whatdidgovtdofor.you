If your household gets heating help through HEAP or public assistance in New York, you don't have to apply for your utility's bill discount. The state's Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance compares its list of people in those programs with utility customer accounts. It sends the matches to utilities at least twice a year, and each utility has 60 days to put eligible customers into its discount program and send them a letter saying so. The law covers investor-owned utilities, municipal utilities, rural electric co-ops and state power authorities. Utilities can use the data only to confirm eligibility and send that letter, and they can't share it with anyone else.

The discount is part of the Energy Affordability Program. It aims to keep a typical bill below 6% of household income and can be worth up to $500 a year. If you get SNAP, Medicaid or SSI but not HEAP or cash aid, privacy rules mean you still have to sign up yourself at ny.gov/EAP.

Assemblymember Michaelle Solages and Sen. Kevin Parker wrote the law. It passed the Senate 62–0 in June 2023, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. A follow-up bill from the same two sponsors clarified OTDA's role in the data matching, made utilities responsible for secure data connections and narrowed the matching to programs that OTDA runs. That bill passed the Senate 61–0, and Hochul signed it on February 13, 2024.
