Pasteurized donor human milk can provide needed nutrition when maternal milk is unavailable to premature or medically fragile babies. Before this law, New York-regulated commercial health plans had to cover it only for inpatient use. The law removed that limit, so qualifying babies can keep coverage for donor milk after leaving the hospital.

A baby qualifies with a licensed medical practitioner's order if the baby cannot receive maternal milk or breastfeed, or if the mother cannot breastfeed or produce enough milk despite optimal lactation support. The baby must also have weighed under 1,500 grams (about 3.3 pounds) at birth or have a condition that puts them at high risk of necrotizing enterocolitis. The rule applies to individual, small-group, and large-group plans regulated by New York, starting with policies issued or renewed on or after Jan. 1, 2025. Self-funded employer plans, which the state doesn't regulate, aren't covered.

Assemblymember Michaelle Solages and Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal sponsored the bills in their chambers. Assemblymembers Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn and David Weprin cosponsored the Assembly bill. Their memo said insurers rarely paid for donor milk after discharge and that demand at donor milk banks rose during the 2022 formula shortage. Both chambers passed the bill unanimously—the Assembly 137–0 and the Senate 58–0—and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in October 2024.
