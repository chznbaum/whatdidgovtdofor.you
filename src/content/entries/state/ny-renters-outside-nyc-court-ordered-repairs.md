If your landlord won't fix a dangerous problem, like no heat, a leaking roof, or mold, you can ask a judge to order repairs. Before this law, renters outside New York City lacked a simple way to do that. Now you can file a "7-D" petition in a local court where your home is, without waiting for a code inspector.

You can file after living in your home for 30 days in a row. You can name the owner, a public housing authority, or anyone legally responsible for upkeep. Tenant legal-aid guidance says to notify the landlord first and keep proof. The state courts publish fill-in forms. Filing costs $20 in town and village courts and $45 in city courts; fee waivers are available. The judge can order repairs and other just relief, including money or lower future rent until violations are fixed. The law excludes New York City, Nassau and Suffolk, which have different procedures.

Assemblymember Bill Magnarelli and Sen. Rachel May carried the bill after tenant advocates Mary Traynor and Palmer Harvey brought the idea to Magnarelli; Traynor and Legal Services director Dennis Kaufman helped draft it. The Assembly passed it 98–51 and the Senate 44–19 in June 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it Dec. 30, 2022, with a one-year delay for court training. Magnarelli carried a 2023 amendment that set the final jurisdiction and service rules before the law took effect Dec. 30, 2023.
