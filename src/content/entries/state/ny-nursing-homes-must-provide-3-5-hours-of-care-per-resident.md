If you or someone you love lives in a New York nursing home, the home must average 3.5 hours of aide and nurse staffing per resident each day. At least 2.2 hours must come from certified nurse aides and at least 1.1 from licensed nurses. This is a facility-wide average, not a guarantee that each resident personally receives 3.5 hours. Homes must post their staffing where residents, families and workers can see it. Residents do not apply.

The state reviews each home quarterly using payroll data reported to the federal government. A noncompliant home can be fined up to $2,000 for each day it falls below the standard. Penalties may be reduced if the state accepts a regional acute labor shortage or another mitigating factor; ordinary sick days and other routine staffing needs are not excuses. Penalties began arriving in 2026: by July, the state had issued more than $8 million in fines to 45 homes.

Assembly Member Aileen Gunther, a registered nurse, and Sen. Gustavo Rivera sponsored the final bill. NYSNA also credited Assembly Member Richard Gottfried, Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie with leadership. NYSNA, 1199SEIU and CWA District 1 advocated for the staffing laws after a fight lasting more than a decade. The push gained momentum after more than 13,000 New York nursing home residents died of COVID-19. Sponsors lowered the proposed standard from 4.1 hours to 3.5 during negotiations. The bill passed with votes from both parties, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 18, 2021. It was scheduled to start that January, but the Hochul administration paused it through March; it took effect April 1, 2022.
