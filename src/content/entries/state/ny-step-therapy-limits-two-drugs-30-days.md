Some plans use "step therapy": they make you try other drugs before covering your clinician's choice. This law limits that. A plan cannot require more than two other drugs for the same condition or keep you on a required drug beyond 30 days unless medical guidelines support longer. It also cannot require a drug without FDA approval and guideline support. If you switch plans, you do not repeat step therapy for the same drug if you or your provider show you completed it under your old plan within the past year. A current plan generally cannot restart step therapy after a formulary or utilization-management change while an approved override remains in force, unless it identifies a safety concern and an alternative.

Your plan must accept the prescriber's written or electronic statement that a required drug failed. Breaking these rules is grounds for an override unless fraud is involved. The law applies automatically, but you or your provider may need to submit records or request an override. It covers New York-regulated policies issued, renewed, or changed on or after Jan. 1, 2026. Self-funded employer plans, Medicare, and Medicaid fee-for-service are not covered.

Sen. Neil Breslin and Assemblymember John McDonald sponsored the original bill. After three Senate passages and Assembly approval, Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it Dec. 21, 2024, following an agreement on changes. McDonald sponsored the 2025 amendment, and Sen. Jamaal Bailey sponsored its Senate companion. Hochul signed the amendment Feb. 14, setting the Jan. 1, 2026 start date.
