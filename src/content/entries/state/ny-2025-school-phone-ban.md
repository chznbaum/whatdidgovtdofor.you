In New York's public schools, students can't use smartphones, smartwatches, tablets, or other personal internet-connected devices during the school day. The rule runs from the first bell to the last, so it covers lunch and free periods too. Basic cell phones that can't go online, and laptops or tablets the school provides for class, are still allowed.

Each district writes its own policy after hearing from teachers, parents, and students, and decides how phones get stored. Every policy has to give parents a way to reach their child during the day. There are exceptions for students who need a device to manage a medical condition, for those whose special education plan calls for one, and for translation, family caregiving, and emergencies.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the restriction and signed it into law as part of the state budget on May 9, 2025, making New York the largest state with a bell-to-bell policy. The budget included $13.5 million to help schools buy pouches, lockers, or other storage. About 1,080 districts, charter schools, and BOCES put their policies in place for the start of the 2025–26 school year. The budget bill that carried it passed the Senate 44–18, with four Republicans voting yes.
