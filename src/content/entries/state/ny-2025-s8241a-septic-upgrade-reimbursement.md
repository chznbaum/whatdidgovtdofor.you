Failing cesspools and septic systems can release sewage pollution into groundwater and nearby waters. This law created a higher reimbursement for enhanced systems, which either reduce total nitrogen to 19 milligrams per liter or significantly reduce environmental or public-health impacts associated with phosphorus or PFAS. Eligible owners can receive up to 75% of eligible costs, capped at $25,000. Conventional projects remain eligible for up to 50%, capped at $10,000. The law did not add state spending to the program; it changed how much an individual enhanced project can receive.

You may qualify if your property is in a designated priority area within a participating county and the county approves your application. The state identifies these areas based on factors such as vulnerability to groundwater contamination and known water-quality problems. You apply through your county, which decides the grant amount. Reimbursement generally comes after the work is completed, although an owner can authorize payment directly to the contractor. Counties may also set graduated reimbursement rates.

Sen. Pete Harckham and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay carried the bill, which was introduced at the request of the Environmental Facilities Corporation, led by President and CEO Maureen A. Coleman. Sen. Robert Jackson cosponsored it. It passed 59–0 in the Senate on June 12, 2025, and 136–0 in the Assembly the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on July 23, 2025.
