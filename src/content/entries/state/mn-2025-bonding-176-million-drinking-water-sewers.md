Minnesota is borrowing money through state bonds to pay for public works, and the biggest share goes to water. The law sends $176 million to the state's Public Facilities Authority. That covers $87 million in grants split evenly between drinking water and wastewater projects, $39 million to unlock federal clean water and drinking water loan money, $32 million for sewage plant upgrades that meet pollution limits, and $18 million for drinking water projects addressing emerging contaminants above health advisory levels.

You don't apply for this yourself. Cities and towns get the grants for projects on the state's ranked priority lists, which helps them fix aging systems without putting the whole cost on local water and sewer bills. The same law also gives $26 million to rehabilitate public housing, $3 million for infrastructure in resident-owned manufactured home parks, $47 million for local roads and $31 million to replace local bridges.

Rep. Mary Franson, a Republican, was the chief House author; Sen. Sandy Pappas, a DFLer, was the Senate author; and Rep. Fue Lee, a DFLer and the other House Capital Investment co-chair, was the second House author. The bill passed during the June 9, 2025 special session by 116–15 in the House and 57–10 in the Senate. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on June 14. The state repays the bonds over time from the general fund, while cancellations of older, unspent capital-project appropriations lowered the package's net cost.
