If a business uses an algorithm that draws on your personal data to set the price it shows you, New York law says it has to tell you. The notice must read "THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA." It has to be clear and appear next to the price, at the same time as the price. Personal data is broadly defined: anything that identifies you or your device, or could reasonably be linked to you. Without the label, two shoppers could see different prices for the same item and never know why.

You don't have to do anything to get the notice. Insurers and financial institutions covered by federal privacy law are exempt. So are lower prices that a subscription service offers its existing customers. Attorney General Letitia James enforces the law. Her office must first send a cease-and-desist letter and give the business time to fix the problem. After that, it can seek up to $1,000 per violation. Her office has urged shoppers to report prices they think break the rule.

Gov. Kathy Hochul put the requirement in the budget bill she sent lawmakers in January 2025. That bill passed the Senate 40–22 and the Assembly 103–47 on May 7, and Hochul signed it two days later. The National Retail Federation sued to block the label, but Judge Jed Rakoff dismissed the case, finding the statement factual. The law took effect Nov. 10, 2025. The retailers appealed, and the federal appeals court heard arguments on Oct. 19, 2026.
