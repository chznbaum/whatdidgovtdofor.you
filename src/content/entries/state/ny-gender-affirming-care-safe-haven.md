If you or your child gets gender-affirming care in New York, the state won't help another state punish you for it. Police can't arrest anyone for providing or helping provide lawful care here. State and local law enforcement agencies can't share information about that care with out-of-state agencies. New York courts and county clerks can't issue out-of-state subpoenas meant to punish lawful gender-affirming care or remove children over it.

The law also protects families. In custody cases, New York courts can't apply another state's law that allows a child to be taken from a parent for letting them get gender-affirming care. It also protects providers: their licenses and malpractice coverage can't be held against them for lawful care. You don't need to apply for any of this, because the protections apply automatically.

Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Harry Bronson sponsored the law in response to a wave of new state bans. By the June 25 signing, Hoylman-Sigal said 20 states had banned or restricted this care for young people. The Senate passed it 44–18 on June 6, 2023, and the Assembly followed 97–47 on June 10. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 25, 2023, just before the NYC Pride March. In December 2025, a second shield law from Hoylman-Sigal expanded these protections.
