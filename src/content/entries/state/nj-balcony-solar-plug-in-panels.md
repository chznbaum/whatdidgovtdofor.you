Starting March 1, 2027, you can make some of your own electricity in New Jersey with a small solar setup that plugs into a regular wall outlet. These panels often hang from a balcony railing or sit on a patio. A home can use up to 1,200 watts of them. The device must be listed or certified to a qualifying safety standard and must include a feature that prevents it from exporting power to the grid during an outage. Your utility can't make you get its approval, charge you a fee, or make you add extra equipment.

Renters who pay their own electric bill can use one on their balcony or patio. You give your landlord written notice at least 14 days ahead, with proof the device meets the rules and where it will go. Landlords, condo boards, and HOAs can set reasonable limits on size and placement, but they can't ban the devices outright. Towns can't ban them either, and they can't require a permit for one. You are responsible for any damage the device causes to your leased premises.

Sens. John McKeon and Bob Smith carried the bill in the Senate. Assembly members Clinton Calabrese, Ellen Park, and Robert Karabinchak carried it in the Assembly. The Assembly passed it 79-0 with one abstention, and the Senate concurred 40-0, both on June 30, 2026. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on Sept. 1. News coverage said New Jersey was the ninth state to pass a plug-in solar law.
