Starting Sept. 1, 2027, covered social platforms must give New Jersey users they know are under 18 their highest privacy settings by default. The law covers public, account-based services centered on user content and interaction if they are likely to be used by minors and either have over $25 million in revenue or process data for at least 25,000 consumers or households. On platforms with recommendation systems, adults other than parents cannot see minors’ accounts or posts or message them by default, though minors can choose otherwise. Location and interaction counts are off unless the minor turns them on.

Platforms must minimize minors’ data, avoid dark patterns, and take reasonable steps to stop features such as endless scrolling and autoplay from causing compulsive use. Notifications are barred 10 p.m.–6 a.m. year-round and 8 a.m.–4 p.m. on weekdays from Labor Day through Memorial Day. Minors and parents can report harm, and covered minors can request account deletion within 45 days. No application is required. For a negligent or worse violation, a court may award a prevailing minor—or a parent suing on the minor’s behalf—$5,000 per violation or triple damages, whichever is greater.

Assemblymember Andrea Katz and Sen. Raj Mukherji led the bill, with prime sponsors Assemblymembers Marisa Sweeney and Luanne Peterpaul and Sen. Linda Greenstein. Survivor parent Erin Popolo also championed it. On June 30, 2026, it passed the Assembly 73–5 and Senate 36–4 with bipartisan support. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it Aug. 11.
