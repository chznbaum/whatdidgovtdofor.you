If your city, county or town has enough voters who belong to your language-minority group and struggle with English, you get election materials in that language. That covers registration forms, ballots, sample ballots, voting instructions, and notices about polling place changes. Under Virginia's Voting Rights Act, a locality is covered once more than 3% of its voting-age citizens, or more than 5,000 of them, belong to one language-minority group and cannot use English well enough to take part in elections. This law cut those triggers from 5% and 10,000, so localities that fell between the old and new cutoffs can now qualify. As of the last official list, from December 2021, only four did: Fairfax County, Prince William County, Manassas, and Manassas Park.

You don't need to apply. The translated materials must be as good as the English ones and come out at the same time, and machine translation alone is presumed not good enough. The law required the Department of Elections to post the updated list of covered localities and languages by Sept. 1, 2026. If a locality falls short, the attorney general, an affected voter, or a group that serves those voters can sue to make it comply.

The law also spells out how courts judge claims that a local rule suppresses or dilutes votes. It lets organizations sue on behalf of their members or their mission.

Del. Cia Price carried the bill. It passed the House 63–35 and the Senate 21–18, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026. It took effect July 1, 2026.
