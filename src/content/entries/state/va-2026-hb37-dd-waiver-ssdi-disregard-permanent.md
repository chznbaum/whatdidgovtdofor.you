Virginia's developmental disability waivers pay for the support that lets many people with intellectual and developmental disabilities live and work in their communities. To qualify, a person's income has to stay under a Medicaid limit. For people whose disability check comes from Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI), a raise in that check could push them over the line and cost them their services.

Since 2024, Virginia's Medicaid agency has ignored any SSDI income above the federal maximum monthly SSI payment when deciding who qualifies for the Family and Individual Support, Community Living, and Building Independence waivers. That income still counts toward what a person pays toward their own care. There's nothing to apply for: the agency applies the rule when it checks eligibility for people in the waivers or offered a slot.

The 2024 rule was set to expire on July 1, 2026. In public comments for a January hearing, parents said their adult children's SSDI goes up when a parent dies or retires, which could cost them their waiver through no fault of their own. Others said people were cutting back work to stay under the limit. Del. Irene Shin carried HB 37 to drop the end date, with Dels. Laura Jane Cohen and Kathy Tran as chief co-patrons. It passed the House 98–0 and the Senate 40–0, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026.
