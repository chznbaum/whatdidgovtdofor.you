If you're admitted to a hospital or freestanding birthing center in New York to give birth, or for the inpatient stay after, you can name a doula to be with you. The facility can't deny that doula access to you. A doula is a support person who helps you through labor and recovery but doesn't provide medical care. A 2025 follow-up law lets a facility limit access during an emergency, or when it would put you or your care team at risk. A second follow-up requires the state health department to make sure facilities post notices telling patients about this right.

You don't have to apply. Just tell the facility who your doula is. Before this law, doulas reported being turned away at hospitals, asked for certifications New York doesn't require, or blocked from seeing clients after the birth. Supporters say steady doula support is linked to healthier births and can help narrow racial gaps in mothers' health.

State Sen. Roxanne Persaud and Assemblymember Michaelle Solages sponsored the bill. It first passed the Senate in May 2023. It passed again 61–0 in January 2024, and the Assembly passed it the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 11, 2024. Persaud and Solages sponsored the follow-up changes, which were signed in February 2025. The law took effect March 11, 2025.
