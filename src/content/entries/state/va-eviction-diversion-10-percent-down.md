If your landlord sues you in Virginia for unpaid rent, the Eviction Diversion Program can stop the eviction. You go on a court-ordered payment plan instead: you pay off the rest in three monthly payments of 30% each while keeping up with your regular rent. If you make every payment, the judge dismisses the case.

Before this law, few people could use the program. You had to pay 25% of what you owed up front. You were shut out if you had paid rent late more than twice in six months or three times in a year, or if you had recently paid off a past eviction case to stay in your home. From July 2020 to June 2024, only 27 tenants used the pilots in Richmond, Petersburg, Hampton and Danville, which saw about 35,000 eviction filings in 2024 alone.

Now you pay at least 10% at your first court date. You must show up, explain under oath why you fell behind, and show you can afford the payments. You can't qualify if you owe on, or defaulted on, a rent payment plan in the past year, or if you used the program in the past year. Courts that run the program must send information about it, including who qualifies, with the eviction summons.

Del. Adele McClure carried identical House bill HB 837, with Del. Jackie Glass as a co-patron. Sen. Mamie Locke carried SB 273. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed HB 837 on April 6, 2026, and SB 273 on April 13, 2026.
