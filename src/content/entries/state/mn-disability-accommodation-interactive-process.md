If you have a disability and ask for a change that would let you do your job, use your home, attend school, enter a business or use a public service, Minnesota law now says the employer, housing provider, school, business or agency may be discriminating if it refuses to take part in a process to see whether a reasonable accommodation exists. The change took effect Aug. 1, 2026, and covers employment, housing and real property, public accommodations, public services and education.

The law does not guarantee the accommodation you request. Because the new provision says that a failure to engage “may” be discrimination, rather than that it always is, courts will still have to decide how it applies in individual cases. You can report possible discrimination to the Minnesota Department of Human Rights or bring a civil action under the Human Rights Act.

Sen. Erin Maye Quade and Rep. Kim Hicks were the bill's chief authors. Rep. Danny Nadeau, a House coauthor, offered the successful amendment specifying that the process concerns whether a reasonable accommodation exists. The House passed the bill 88–42 and the Senate passed it 42–22 before Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 18.
