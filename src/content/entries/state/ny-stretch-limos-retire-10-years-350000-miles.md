Starting April 20, 2026, a New York-registered stretch limousine used for hire can't be operated on a public road once it is more than 10 years old or has more than 350,000 miles on it, whichever comes first. If the odometer can't be trusted, the state estimates the mileage from recent inspections. Limo companies must also equip each vehicle with a window-breaking tool, a working fire extinguisher and an emergency way out, and drivers must give passengers a safety briefing before each trip. Riders don't have to do anything: the rules apply to the companies.

The law grew out of two crashes. In 2015 a limo crash in Cutchogue on Long Island killed four young women. In 2018 a limo crash in Schoharie killed 20 people. A state task force created after the crashes recommended changes, and Sen. Michelle Hinchey sponsored a 2023 bill to retire old limos at the same 10-year and 350,000-mile limits. It passed the Senate.

Gov. Kathy Hochul put the remaining task force recommendations into her 2024–25 budget proposal, and they became law when she signed the budget on April 20, 2024. The equipment rules took effect that October, and the age and mileage limit took effect two years after the budget was signed.
