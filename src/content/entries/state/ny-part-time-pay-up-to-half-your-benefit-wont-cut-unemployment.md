If you're on unemployment in New York and pick up part-time work, your benefit now depends on how much you earn that week, not how many days or hours you work. You can earn up to half your weekly benefit rate, or $100 if that's more, and still get your full benefit. Above that, each dollar you earn lowers your benefit by a dollar. You don't apply for anything separate: you report your gross weekly earnings when you certify, and the system does the math.

Here's an example. If your weekly benefit rate is $400, you can earn $200 with no cut. If you earn $300, you get $300 in benefits on top of your pay. Under the old rule, any work on a given day cost you a quarter of that week's benefit. Lawmakers said that rule shortchanged part-time workers and pushed people away from taking three or more days of work.

Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assemblymember Al Stirpe sponsored the law. It passed the Senate 62–1 in January 2021, with only one Republican voting no, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it that July. Its start date was tied to the Labor Department getting a computer system that could handle the change, and lawmakers pushed that date back several times. In the meantime, a companion law set up a temporary rule that counted hours instead of days. The department replaced its old unemployment system in November 2026. Its guidance now says only your earnings count, not your hours.
