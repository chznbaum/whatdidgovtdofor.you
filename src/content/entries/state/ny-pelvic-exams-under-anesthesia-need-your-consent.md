In New York, no one may perform or supervise a pelvic exam on you while you're under anesthesia or unconscious unless one of three things is true. You, or someone authorized to speak for you, agreed to that specific exam ahead of time, out loud or in writing. Or the exam is part of a surgery or test you already consented to. Or it's an emergency: you're unconscious, the exam is medically needed to diagnose or treat you, and waiting for consent would raise the risk to your life or health.

You don't have to do anything to be covered. A provider who breaks the rule can be charged with professional misconduct, which can put their license at risk. The law adds to the state's other consent rules; it doesn't weaken any of them.

Sen. Roxanne Persaud and Assemblywoman Michaelle Solages sponsored the bill. A 2019 ELLE survey of 101 medical students found that, among students who had performed a pelvic exam on an anesthetized patient, 61% had done so without explicit patient consent. Persaud said women had called her office asking whether that had happened to them. The Senate passed the bill 62–0 and the Assembly approved it in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 7, 2019, and it took effect 180 days later.
