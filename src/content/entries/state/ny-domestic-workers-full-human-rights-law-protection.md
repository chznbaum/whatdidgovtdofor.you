If you're paid to work in someone's home in New York, for example caring for children, cleaning, or helping an older person, the state's Human Rights Law now covers you like any other employee. Your employer can't treat you unfairly because of your age, race, religion, sex, disability, or any other trait the law protects.

Before this law, the Human Rights Law's definition of "employee" left domestic workers out. A separate section protected them from harassment but not from other kinds of discrimination. The new law removed that exclusion and repealed the separate section, so domestic workers get every workplace protection in the law. The only people still left out are those employed by their own parent, spouse, or child.

You don't have to sign up for anything. If you face discrimination, you can file a complaint with the New York State Division of Human Rights or go to court.

Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assembly Member Jenifer Rajkumar sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 47–16 in May 2021, with all 43 Democrats and four Republicans voting yes, and it passed the Assembly that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 31, 2021, along with Rajkumar's bill opening paid family leave to more domestic workers.
