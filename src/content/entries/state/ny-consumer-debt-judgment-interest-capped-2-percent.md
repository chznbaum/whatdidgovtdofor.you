When a creditor or debt collector sues you in New York and wins, the court enters a judgment for what you owe, and interest keeps adding to it until it's paid. For more than 40 years, that interest ran at 9% a year. On a $5,000 judgment, that's $450 a year on top of the debt. For personal debts like credit cards, medical bills, and student loans, the rate is now 2%, which comes to $100 a year on the same judgment.

You don't have to do anything to get the lower rate. It took effect April 30, 2022, and covers new judgments as well as old ones that were still unpaid that day. It doesn't refund interest you paid before then. Business debts still carry 9%.

Sen. Kevin Thomas, who chaired the Senate Consumer Protection Committee, and Assemblymember Helene Weinstein carried the bill. Groups including AARP and the NAACP backed it. It passed the Senate 45–18 in June 2021, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 31, 2021. Three credit unions sued, arguing that cutting interest on old judgments was an unconstitutional taking. In September 2025 a federal court ruled that it wasn't, and the 2% rate is still the law.
