In New Jersey, officers can't hide their faces behind a mask or disguise while dealing with the public on the job. The rule covers every agency: town, county and state police, and federal agents such as ICE. The law allows masks for undercover or tactical work, for officers facing threats of retaliation, and for health and safety reasons like an N95, smoke during an emergency, or chemical hazards. Clear face shields are allowed.

Before an officer detains or arrests you, they have to show who they are. They can do that by wearing an agency uniform, showing a photo ID, showing a badge with their number, or clearly stating their name and agency. Officers can skip this step if it would put an investigation or their own safety at risk, or if a supervisor decides stealth is needed. This protection is automatic. The state attorney general is directed to issue guidelines to carry out the law.

Sens. Benjie Wimberly and Andrew Zwicker sponsored S3114; Sens. Troy Singleton and Linda Greenstein sponsored S3216. The bills were combined with Nicholas Scutari as another Senate prime sponsor. Assembly members Verlina Reynolds-Jackson, Annette Quijano, Alixon Collazos-Gill, Gabriel Rodriguez and Michael Venezia carried companion A1743. One Republican in each chamber joined Democrats on final passage, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed the law two days later. The Justice Department sued in April to stop New Jersey from applying it to federal officers; the case is pending.
