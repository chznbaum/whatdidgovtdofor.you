Starting January 1, 2027, your insurer can't refuse coverage, cancel or decline to renew your policy solely because your owner-occupied Virginia home's asphalt shingle roof is under 15 years old. Receipts, permits or inspector reports can prove its age. Without that proof, the insurer must allow an inspection at your expense. It also can't act solely on the roof's condition if an authorized inspector finds at least five years of useful life. The insurer may require repairs to damage or defects, but after they're fixed it can't deny or end coverage solely for those conditions.

The law also restricts roofing contractors. They can't pay, waive or rebate your insurance deductible, or offer something of value for an inspection or insurance claim. Before you authorize repairs tied to a property-insurance claim, the contractor must give you an itemized estimate. If you sign a roofing contract in an affected area within 180 days after the governor declares a state of emergency, you may cancel by the earlier of 10 days after signing or the official start date. You must send the cancellation by certified mail or another mail service that proves delivery. The insurance protections apply automatically; contractor violations are enforceable under Virginia's Consumer Protection Act.

Del. Michelle Maldonado carried HB 677, and Sen. Scott Surovell carried the identical SB 402. The House first passed HB 677 97–0; the Senate amended and passed it 40–0; the House agreed to the amendment 95–4. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both bills on April 13, 2026.
