When a baby is born to a parent with a state-regulated health plan in New Jersey, the plan covers the baby from the moment of birth, including care for birth defects. That coverage used to end after 60 days unless the parents gave notice of the birth and paid any added premium. A new law extends that window to 90 days. Parents now have an extra month in the hectic first weeks to add the baby to the plan without a gap in care.

The 90-day rule covers individual and group plans that New Jersey regulates. It reaches each plan when the plan is issued or renewed after March 8, 2026, so plans that renew every January get it on Jan. 1, 2027. It doesn't cover self-insured employer plans, which federal law governs, public employee benefit programs, or group classes that offer no dependent coverage. A baby born to someone covered as another person's dependent isn't automatically covered under that plan. Families on excluded plans should check their own deadline. To keep the baby covered, tell your insurer about the birth and pay any premium the plan requires within the 90 days.

Assemblywomen Margie Donlon, Alixon Collazos-Gill and Andrea Katz and Sens. Joseph Lagana and Raj Mukherji, all Democrats, sponsored the bill. It passed both houses without a single no vote on Dec. 22, 2025, 72–0 in the Assembly and 37–0 in the Senate. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 7, 2026.
