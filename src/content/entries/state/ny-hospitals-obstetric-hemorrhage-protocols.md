Heavy bleeding during or after childbirth, called obstetric hemorrhage, can kill within hours. It caused 16% of maternal deaths in New York from 2012 to 2014. This law makes every hospital adopt standard protocols for it and keep them up to date. The protocols have to cover spotting the bleeding early, having a team ready to respond, assessing each patient's risk, and moving a patient to a hospital that offers a higher level of care when needed. Hospitals must hand their protocols to the state Health Department immediately when asked.

Patients don't need to do anything. The protections apply whenever you give birth in a New York hospital. The Health Department, working with clinical experts, writes guidance and a toolkit for hospitals that can include a hemorrhage checklist and simulation training, and posts them online.

Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assembly Member Latoya Joyner sponsored the bill in 2019, with Assembly Members Steven Otis, Richard Gottfried, and Gary Finch as cosponsors. Rivera and Joyner pointed to rising maternal deaths in New York and to how much more often Black women die in pregnancy and childbirth. The Assembly passed it 139–0. The Senate passed it 41–19: every Democrat and one Republican voted yes. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on December 16, 2019. Rivera and Joyner later sponsored a follow-up bill that dropped a requirement for hospitals to report data to the state. Cuomo signed that bill on April 17, 2020.
