If you clean, cook, or care for children or older adults in a New York home, the household you work for has to cover you for disability and Paid Family Leave once you work there at least 20 hours a week and on at least 30 days in a calendar year. Disability coverage pays part of your wages when an injury or illness that didn't happen on the job keeps you from working. Paid Family Leave provides paid time off to bond with a new child or care for a relative with a serious health condition. Your employer must buy the coverage, but you file a claim when you need benefits. For Paid Family Leave, a worker on this schedule becomes eligible after 26 consecutive weeks of employment.

Before this law, a household had to provide coverage only if a domestic worker put in 40 hours a week. The 20-hour threshold already applied in other industries, so part-time nannies and housekeepers were left out.

Lawmakers had meant to fix this in 2010. That year's Domestic Workers Bill of Rights aimed to cover workers at 20 hours, but it missed one section of the law, and the Workers' Compensation Board asked for a correction that summer. It took more than a decade. Assembly Member Jenifer Rajkumar and Sen. Julia Salazar carried the fix. The Assembly passed it 101–48 in April 2021. The Senate passed it 43–20 the next month, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican voting no. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 22, 2021, and it took effect Jan. 1, 2022.
