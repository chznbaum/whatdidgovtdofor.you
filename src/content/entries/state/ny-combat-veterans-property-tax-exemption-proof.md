Many New York localities offer an alternative veterans' exemption that lowers a veteran's home assessment: 15% for wartime service, plus another 10% for combat-zone service. Until now, the extra 10% depended on having a campaign ribbon, service medal, or expeditionary medal. Veterans whose service was classified or poorly recorded often couldn't prove they'd been in a combat zone, so they missed out.

The new law adds three more kinds of proof: a DD-214 discharge form that notes service in a combat zone, records showing hostile fire or imminent danger pay or the federal combat-zone income tax exclusion, and other service records the state tax commissioner approves. It also counts more medals, including other combat theater awards created by federal law or executive order. Under the basic state limit, the combat add-on is the lesser of $8,000 or $8,000 multiplied by the applicable equalization rate or class ratio. Localities may choose different maximums. The break isn't automatic: you apply to your local assessor on Form RP-458-a, usually by March 1.

Sen. Michelle Hinchey and Assembly Member Pamela Hunter introduced earlier Senate and Assembly versions in 2024. The final bill passed the Senate 58–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 10, 2026. The state tax department says property-tax exemptions are paid for through higher property taxes on other taxpayers in the locality.
