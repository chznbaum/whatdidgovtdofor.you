New York's Labor Law already protected most workers from being fired, threatened or punished for complaining about unpaid wages or other violations. This law says that, when it is retaliation for asserting those Labor Law rights, threatening to call immigration authorities counts as that kind of punishment. So does actually calling them, or reporting or threatening to report a worker's suspected citizenship or immigration status—or that of a family or household member—to any government agency.

You don't need to be a citizen to be protected, and you don't have to apply for anything. But Labor Law § 215 excludes New York State and municipal employees. If a covered employer retaliates, a worker can complain to the state Labor Department or go to court within two years. A court must award each harmed worker up to $20,000 in damages and can order back pay, reinstatement and lawyer's fees. The state can also fine the employer $1,000 to $10,000, or up to $20,000 if it has broken the rule in the past six years.

Attorney General Letitia James's office submitted the bill and vigorously advocated for it after investigating cases and receiving reports of employers using deportation threats to keep workers quiet. Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assemblymember Marcos Crespo carried it. The Senate passed it 59–3 and the Assembly 104–42, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 27, 2019. It took effect 90 days later.
