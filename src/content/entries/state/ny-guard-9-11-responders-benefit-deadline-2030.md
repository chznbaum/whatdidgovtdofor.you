Eligible members of New York's National Guard, Naval Militia and New York Guard who did qualifying 9/11 rescue, recovery or cleanup work now have until Sept. 11, 2030 to register. The old deadline was Sept. 11, 2026.

A 2023 state law created these protections. A qualifying 9/11 illness that permanently prevents a member from working can bring an annual pension based on 75% of final annual state-active-duty pay, multiplied by the member's disability rating. Related compensation can reduce the award. A surviving spouse, child under 21 or dependent parent can qualify for a death benefit of up to half of that pay. For a retired member, death generally must occur within 25 years of retirement. The state presumes qualifying conditions came from the cleanup unless competent evidence shows otherwise.

Eligibility requires state active duty at specified World Trade Center or debris-handling locations for any time in the first 48 hours after the attack or at least 40 hours through Sept. 12, 2002. Members also need an entry physical or earlier records showing no qualifying condition. You need not be sick to register: a World Trade Center notice preserves the right to claim later. Once registered, there is no later deadline for a disability claim; death benefits have separate eligibility rules.

Grace Lee and Brian Kavanagh sponsored the Assembly and Senate bills; Marianne Buttenschon cosponsored A11538. The memo says many first responders became ill after prolonged debris exposure. The Assembly passed it 139-0 and the Senate 60-0 on June 4; Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it Sept. 10, 2026.
