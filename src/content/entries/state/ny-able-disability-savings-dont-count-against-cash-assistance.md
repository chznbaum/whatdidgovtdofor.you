A NY ABLE account lets people with disabilities save for costs that come with a disability, often costs that Medicaid or disability benefits don't fully cover. Public assistance in New York checks how much you have saved, so a growing account could have pushed you over the limit. This law adds NY ABLE accounts to the state's list of savings that don't count when New York decides whether you qualify for public assistance and how much you get.

You don't have to apply for the protection. If you have a NY ABLE account and apply for or receive public assistance, the money in it doesn't count. As of 2026, you can open an ABLE account if your disability began before age 46. The standard contribution limit is $20,000 a year, and some working account owners can contribute more.

State rules and agency guidance already treated these accounts as exempt, according to the bill's sponsor memo. This law wrote the exemption into the Social Services Law itself, so it no longer depended on agency rules alone. Sen. Roxanne Persaud and Assembly Member Andrew Hevesi sponsored it. "Families that are saving money for things like college and healthcare expenses shouldn't be penalized for doing so," Persaud said. The Senate passed it 62–0 and the Assembly passed it 148–1 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Oct. 3, 2019.
