Before this law, counties had to provide one early voting site per 50,000 registered voters and were never required to provide more than 10. This law set higher minimums for general elections. A county with fewer than 500,000 registered voters needs one site for each full 30,000 voters, up to 10 and never fewer than one. A larger county needs one for each full 40,000, with no cap. Its largest city—or, if it has no city, its largest municipality—must have a site. For primaries and special elections, a county board of elections may vote to use fewer sites if it decides a smaller number will meet voters' needs.

The law also requires at least eight hours of early voting on Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, scheduled between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. The previous minimum was five hours. There is no separate early-voting application. A registered voter can use any early-voting site in their county unless the board assigns election districts to particular sites.

Sen. Michael Gianaris and Assembly Member Al Taylor sponsored the bill. Gianaris had pressed the New York City Board of Elections for more sites, and it added only a few. The Assembly passed it 108–41 and the Senate 45–18 on June 10, 2021. Every Democrat voting in both chambers supported it, along with two Republicans in each. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 22, 2021, and the new rules took effect April 1, 2022.
