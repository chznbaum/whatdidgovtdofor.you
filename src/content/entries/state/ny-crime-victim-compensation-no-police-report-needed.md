New York's Office of Victim Services helps crime victims pay for things like medical bills, counseling, lost wages, moving, and funerals. In the past, you usually had to show a police report to qualify. Since Dec. 31, 2025, you don't. A state- or city-funded victim service provider, or a licensed medical or mental health professional, can confirm the crime instead by filling out the office's Crime Verification Form. You also have three years to file a claim, not one.

The law covers people who never called the police, often out of fear, distrust, or danger at home. When you go through a victim service provider, the office won't share your identifying information with law enforcement. It also doesn't notify the district attorney about a crime that was never reported. The money comes from fines and fees paid by people convicted of crimes, not from taxes.

Common Justice led a statewide coalition for the change beginning in fall 2021 and introduced the first bill in January 2022 with Sen. Zellnor Myrie and Assemblymember Demond Meeks. The Senate passed the 2023 bill 60–0 with support from both parties, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. In 2024, Myrie and Meeks carried a follow-up amendment, negotiated with Hochul's administration, that specified what proof counts and delayed the start until Dec. 31, 2025. The Senate passed it 61–0, and Hochul signed it on Feb. 13, 2024.
