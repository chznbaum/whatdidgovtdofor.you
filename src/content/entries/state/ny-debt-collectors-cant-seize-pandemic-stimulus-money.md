Federal pandemic relief was meant to help families pay for food, rent, and medicine. But the American Rescue Plan didn't clearly protect its stimulus checks from garnishment, so a debt collector holding a court judgment could try to take that money out of your bank account. This New York law closes that gap. Stimulus checks, tax refunds, recovery rebates, and tax credits paid to people under four federal relief laws from 2020 and 2021 can't be taken to pay a money judgment.

You don't have to apply. If a creditor freezes your bank account, the notice you get must tell you that stimulus money is protected and include a form you can return to get it released. The protection doesn't cover child support, spousal support, maintenance or alimony, a property award in a divorce, restitution in a family-offense case, or a claim that the money was rightfully the creditor's. That way relief paid into an abuser's account can still reach the family it was meant for.

Sen. Kevin Thomas and Assemblymember Helene Weinstein sponsored the bill. It passed the Assembly 112–38 in April 2021 and the Senate 48–15 later that month. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on May 13, 2021.
