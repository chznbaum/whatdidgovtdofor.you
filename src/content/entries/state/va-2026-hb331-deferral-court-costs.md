In a Virginia criminal case, a judge can sometimes defer the case instead of finding you guilty right away. If you meet the conditions, such as staying out of trouble, finishing a class, or doing community service, the case ends in a dismissal or an alternative charge. Virginia law requires courts to charge court costs in these cases. Until now, a judge could also make paying those costs one of the conditions, so someone who did everything else right could still lose the deal because they couldn't afford the bill.

For deferrals entered on or after July 1, 2026, a judge can't make paying costs or fees a condition. Once you meet the other terms, the court has to resolve the case the way the parties agreed. If there was no agreement, the case ends with a conviction on an alternative charge or a dismissal. You don't need to apply. The protection is automatic. You still owe the costs, though, and the court can keep collecting them after the case is over.

Del. Adele McClure first carried the same reform as HB 1886 in 2025. It passed both chambers, but Gov. Glenn Youngkin vetoed it. McClure brought it back as HB 331 in 2026. It passed the House 65–32 and the Senate 21–19, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
