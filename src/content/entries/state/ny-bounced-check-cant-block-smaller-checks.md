If you write checks from a personal account at a New York-chartered bank, savings bank or credit union, your bank can no longer pay them in whatever order it likes. It has to pay each day's checks either in the order they come in or from smallest to largest. If one check bounces because your balance is too low, the bank still has to pay any later, smaller checks your balance can cover. Before this law, banks could pay checks in any order and charge a fee on each one they bounced, as long as they disclosed it.

You don't have to do anything. The rule has applied automatically since January 1, 2022. Your bank has to tell you in writing which order it uses when you open an account, and again before it changes that order. State regulators have said the law also covers state-chartered credit unions.

Sen. Neil Breslin first introduced the bill in 2010 and kept bringing it back for 11 years. Assemblymember Thomas Abinanti carried it in the Assembly. In 2021 it passed the Senate 48–15, with five Republicans joining every Democrat, and passed the Assembly 102–46. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 19, 2021.
