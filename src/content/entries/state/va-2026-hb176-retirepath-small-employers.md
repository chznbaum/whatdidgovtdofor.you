If your job doesn't offer a retirement plan, you are more likely to have a way to save at work now. RetirePath Virginia is a state-facilitated IRA program: money comes out of your paycheck and goes into an account that stays yours if you change jobs. Since July 1, 2026, nongovernmental Virginia employers that have operated at least two years, have five or more eligible employees and offer no qualified plan must facilitate RetirePath. The threshold was 25.

The law also removed the rule that an employee had to work at least 30 hours a week, so workers 18 and older who receive wages can now be included regardless of hours. You're signed up automatically and can opt out. If you have Virginia taxable income and meet federal IRA rules, you can open an account yourself if you're self-employed or your employer doesn't facilitate the program. Employers pay no fees. Savers pay $6.75 a quarter plus an asset-based fee between 0.22% and 0.32%.

The law also directs the program board to include a lifetime-income investment option and create procedures for federal matching contributions. The program estimates more than 350,000 additional Virginians will gain access. By mid-July, more than 35,000 businesses had received initial notices.

Del. Luke Torian carried HB 176, and Sen. Jeremy McPike carried the identical SB 149. HB 176 passed unanimously, 99–0 in the House and 40–0 in the Senate. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both bills on April 6, 2026.
