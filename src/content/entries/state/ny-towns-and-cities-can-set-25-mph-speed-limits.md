Until 2022, state law generally barred cities and villages outside New York City, and towns with local speed-limit authority, from setting an area-wide limit below 30 mph. This law lowered that floor to 25 mph. Each community now decides for itself. A new limit has to follow the engineering factors in New York's traffic-control manual and be certified by a licensed professional engineer who specializes in traffic operations. Speed matters for anyone on foot: the bill's sponsors cited AAA Foundation research estimating that a pedestrian hit at 25 mph has a 25% average risk of serious or fatal injury, rising to 50% at 33 mph.

You don't need to apply to benefit, but your city council or town or village board has to adopt the lower limit. Albany, Hudson, Kingston, Scarsdale and White Plains have done so. In Albany, the 25 mph limit on city streets took effect January 1, 2025. New York City had already lowered its own limit in 2014 under separate authority.

Assemblymember Amy Paulin and Sen. Rachel May carried the bill. It passed the Assembly on May 31, 2022, and the Senate approved it 59–4 the next day, with Republicans among its cosponsors. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on August 12, 2022, and it took effect right away.
