Under a 287(g) agreement, ICE deputizes local officers or uses a local jail to carry out federal civil immigration enforcement. New York law now bans these agreements for state police, local police, sheriffs and jails. It also bars informal arrangements that work the same way and the use of local jails to hold people for civil immigration violations. The prohibition took effect May 27, 2026; existing agreements became void on Aug. 25. The law doesn't end every kind of informal cooperation with federal agents.

You don't have to do anything to be covered. The Attorney General's new Office of Immigrant Trust enforces the law and takes complaints at 1-800-771-7755. Before 2025, only one of New York's 62 counties had such an agreement. By July 2026, 12 local agencies did.

Gov. Kathy Hochul introduced the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act on Jan. 30, 2026. Assembly Member Karines Reyes and Sen. Andrew Gounardes had sponsored the broader New York for All Act, which would have barred these agreements and more. Assembly Member Catalina Cruz explained and defended the immigration provisions on the Assembly floor. Hochul, Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie folded the ban into the state budget. The Senate passed the bill 39–22 on May 21, and Hochul signed it on May 27. The U.S. Justice Department and more than 15 county sheriffs have sued to overturn the ban. A federal judge has twice refused to block it while those cases go on.
