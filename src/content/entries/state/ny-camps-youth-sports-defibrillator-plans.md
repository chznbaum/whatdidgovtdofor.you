When a young athlete's heart stops, a defibrillator used within minutes can save their life. New York law now requires state-regulated overnight camps, summer day camps and traveling day camps to have a plan for getting one fast. Youth sports programs for kids under 17 or high schoolers must have a plan too if they host or play in games, tournaments or leagues with at least five teams. Public school sports already fall under a separate law and aren't covered.

Each plan must make an AED available, or show reasonable access to one (for example, a device the field or gym provides), at every camp session, game and practice. Programs must keep records showing they have at least one AED. They must also use their best efforts to have an adult there who has taken an approved AED course in the past 24 months. Parents and players don't have to do anything.

Rye Brook residents Dana Colasante and Alice Schoen took the idea to Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assemblyman Steve Otis after family members went into cardiac arrest at youth sports events. Damar Hamlin's collapse on the field during an NFL game in 2023 added urgency. Mayer and Otis's bill passed the Senate 62–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in November 2023. Mayer and Otis then passed a follow-up law, signed January 26, 2024, that narrowed which programs are covered and dropped the requirement to file plans with the state.
