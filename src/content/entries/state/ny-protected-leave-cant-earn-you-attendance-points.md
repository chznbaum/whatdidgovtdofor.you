Many employers use "no-fault" attendance systems: each absence earns a point, and enough points can cost you a raise, promotion, or your job. In New York, private employers can't assess points, demerits, "occurrences," or time-bank deductions for legally protected leave if they could lead to discipline. Firing or threatening you for taking that leave is also illegal retaliation.

The rule covers leave protected by federal, state, or local law, including paid sick and family leave, FMLA, disability accommodations, workers' compensation, jury duty, voting time, and military leave. It does not cover New York State or municipal employees. No separate application is needed for this retaliation protection, though the underlying leave may require notice or paperwork. A covered worker can sue or complain to the state Labor Department, which can order back pay and reinstatement. It can assess up to $10,000 for a violation, or $20,000 if the employer violated this section in the previous six years.

Assembly Member Karines Reyes and Sen. Liz Krueger sponsored the matching bills; Assembly Members Jonathan Jacobson and William Colton cosponsored Reyes's bill. Krueger's memo cited a survey of 66 large employers covering about 18 million workers: more than 80% of their policies did not clearly exempt disability-related absences from points. The Senate passed Krueger's bill in 2021 and March 2022. The Assembly passed the final bill 145–0 in May 2022, and the Senate passed it 63–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it Nov. 21, 2022; it took effect Feb. 19, 2023.
