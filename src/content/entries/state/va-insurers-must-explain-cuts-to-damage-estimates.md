When your home or car is damaged, an adjuster usually inspects it and writes up an estimate of what the repairs will cost. Sometimes the insurance company later lowers that number before paying you. In Virginia, if the insurer lowers an estimate totaling $3,000 or more, it now has to give you a detailed explanation for each change that reduced it.

The insurer also has to include a detailed list of every change in the report or an attached addendum, name the person who made or ordered each one, and keep every version of the report. That gives you a paper trail if you want to dispute the payout. You don't have to apply for this. The State Corporation Commission enforces the law. A violation does not itself give you a new right to sue, but the law preserves legal claims you otherwise have.

Del. Dan Helmer carried the bill. He said constituent complaints prompted it and gave examples of a $20,000 home estimate cut to $6,000 and an $8,000 vehicle estimate cut to $4,000 without explanation. Dels. Nicole Cole, Mark Downey, Phil Hernandez, May Nivar, and Stacey Carroll were co-patrons. The House passed it 97–0. The Senate passed an amended version 33–7, and the House agreed to that version 98–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
