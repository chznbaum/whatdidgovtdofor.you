Every year, New York's Health Department is required to audit each nursing home's infection control. The checklist follows the federal government's standards for infection control inspections and covers five areas: infection control, protective gear, staffing and cohorting, clinical care, and communication. A home passes only if it meets every item. If it misses any, the state audits it again within 90 days and keeps auditing until it passes. The state can also cite the home and fine it.

The law also requires homes to name an infection lead and train staff to put on and take off protective gear. During an outbreak they must assess staffing every day, and they can't discipline staff who stay home because they have symptoms or test positive for an infectious disease. Residents and families don't have to do anything. Each home must have a plan to keep them updated every day on infections in the building.

Sen. James Skoufis and Assembly Member Pat Burke sponsored the original law after COVID-19 hit nursing homes hard. It passed the Senate 63–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in December 2021. In early 2022 the two lawmakers sponsored changes that raised the passing score from 85% of the checklist to all of it and added fines. The Senate passed the changes 62–1, and Hochul signed them on March 18, 2022. The law set audits to start Dec. 1, 2022. The Health Department later told nursing homes the audits would begin Jan. 1, 2024.
