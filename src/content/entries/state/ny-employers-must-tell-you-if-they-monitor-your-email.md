If you work for a private employer in New York that monitors employees' phone calls, email, or internet use, it has to tell you. When you're hired, the employer must give you a written notice that your calls, messages, and internet use may be monitored at any time by any lawful means. You sign or electronically acknowledge it, and the employer must also post the notice where workers can see it.

You don't have to ask for the notice, because it's the employer's job to give it. Government employers aren't covered, and neither are automated systems that only manage email or internet traffic for maintenance or security without targeting a particular person. The law doesn't ban monitoring, and you can't sue over a missing notice yourself. The state attorney general enforces it, with fines of up to $500 for a first violation, $1,000 for a second, and $3,000 for each one after that.

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal first sponsored a version in 2009 and won passage 12 years later; Sen. James Sanders Jr. carried the Senate version. It passed the Assembly 98–50 on June 2, 2021, and the Senate 43–20 a week later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 8, 2021, and it took effect on May 7, 2022.
