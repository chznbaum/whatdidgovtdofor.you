When a New York public employee dies, their family usually gets an ordinary death benefit. This law let families of workers who reported to work in person and then contracted COVID-19 claim the accidental death benefit instead, the larger benefit usually reserved for deaths in the line of duty. It covered state and local workers, including New York City employees and teachers. To qualify, the worker had to report in person to their workplace, or to another employer-assigned site, on or after March 1, 2020. They also had to test positive or be diagnosed within 45 days, and die with COVID-19 as a cause. Some workers who retired in 2020 and died that year also qualified under system-specific rules.

The retirement systems paid the benefit, minus any ordinary death benefit already paid, and families could keep the ordinary benefit if they preferred it. The UFT says that for its members, the accidental benefit means a yearly pension of half the worker's salary, plus health coverage for a surviving spouse and for children up to age 26. The usual benefit is a one-time payment of three times salary.

Assemblymember Peter Abbate and Sen. Andrew Gounardes carried the bill. It renewed a 2020 law and pushed the deadline for deaths to the end of 2022. The Senate passed it 62–0, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on March 12, 2021. A 2022 law extended the deadline to deaths through Dec. 31, 2024. A 2024 bill to extend it again passed the Senate but never cleared the Assembly.
