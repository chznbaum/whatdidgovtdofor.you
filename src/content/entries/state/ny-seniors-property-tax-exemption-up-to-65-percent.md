New York's senior property tax exemption takes part of an older homeowner's assessed home value off the tax rolls when they meet the program's income and other eligibility rules. Since 1966, that break was capped at 50%. This law lets a town, city, village, county or school district offer more to the seniors with the least income: 55%, 60% or 65%, depending on how far below the limit they fall. It applies to taxable years starting in 2026.

Each taxing jurisdiction decides whether to offer the bigger break, and each sets its own income limit. Orleans County adopted it in March 2026: seniors there earning $16,000 or less qualify for a 65% exemption from the county's tax on their home. Putnam County also raised its cap to 65%. First-time applicants apply through their local assessor, and current recipients must follow their locality's renewal schedule.

Assemblymember David Weprin introduced an earlier version in 2023 and sponsored A3698-A, the version that passed both houses. Sen. Leroy Comrie sponsored the Senate companion beginning in 2024. The bill passed the Assembly 143–0 and the Senate 59–0 in June 2025, with support from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 5, 2025.

The exemption does not reduce property-tax levies, so it does not pay for the break by cutting services. As with other New York property-tax exemptions, the cost is shifted to the remaining property taxpayers through higher tax rates.
