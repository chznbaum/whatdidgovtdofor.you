If you speak up about discrimination at work in New York, file a complaint, or help in someone else's case, your employer can't punish you by releasing your personnel file. The state Human Rights Law already banned retaliation, and this law says plainly that leaking your file counts. Employers may still disclose records, as permitted by law, in the course of starting or responding to a complaint, civil or criminal case, or another judicial or administrative proceeding.

You don't have to apply for this protection. It took effect the day the law was signed. If it happens to you, you can complain to the state Attorney General as well as going through the usual routes. The Attorney General can take the employer to State Supreme Court and can step in before a leak happens if one looks likely.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes introduced the bill in 2021. He wrote that employers often leaked personnel files to discredit people who report discrimination and the witnesses who back them up. Assemblymember Jessica González-Rojas carried it in the Assembly. She pointed to figures showing that 1 in 3 women who file sexual harassment complaints also report retaliation. The Senate passed it 63–0 and the Assembly passed it 140–1. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on March 16, 2022, as part of a package of workplace harassment laws.
