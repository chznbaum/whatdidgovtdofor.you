Biomarker tests check blood or tissue for signs, such as gene changes, that help show which treatment is likely to work. Under New York law, state-regulated plans and Medicaid must cover them to diagnose, treat, manage or monitor disease. Commercial plans use four standards: FDA labeling, federal Medicare coverage decisions, national clinical guidelines or qualifying peer-reviewed studies. Medicaid uses the first three and its usual medical-necessity review. Commercial plans also must limit extra biopsies or samples.

You don't apply for anything. The mandate took effect Jan. 1, 2025. For private policies, it applies when they are issued, renewed, modified, altered or amended on or after that date. Large employers that self-insure are regulated by federal law, so their plans aren't covered.

Sen. Roxanne Persaud and Assemblymember Pamela Hunter carried the original bill. The Senate passed it 61–0 in June 2023, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. In early 2024, Persaud and Hunter carried a follow-up that rewrote the coverage standard and moved the start date from April 2024 to January 2025. The Senate passed it 59–0, and Hochul signed it on Feb. 7, 2024.

In 2026, Hochul proposed giving the Health Department more control over Medicaid coverage. Cancer groups pushed back, and the Senate left that proposal out of its one-house budget. The final budget kept Medicaid coverage but removed peer-reviewed research as a stand-alone route, leaving FDA indications, federal coverage decisions and national clinical guidelines. It also confirmed that Medicaid's ordinary medical-necessity reviews still apply.
