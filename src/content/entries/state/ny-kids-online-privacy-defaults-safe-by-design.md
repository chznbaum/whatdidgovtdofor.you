Starting Jan. 1, 2027, covered social media and gaming platforms must protect New York kids' accounts by default. When a platform determines a user is under 18, adults who aren't connected to the child can't privately message them, view their full profile, respond to or download their posts, tag them, or see their location. Built-in AI companions are disabled by default. On gaming platforms with in-game purchases, parents can set a monthly spending limit and see the transaction history. Parents can override the privacy and AI defaults or decline to set a spending limit.

The protections apply after a platform's age check finds a user under 18. Platforms can't rely only on self-reported ages and must offer more than one age-check method. The attorney general enforces the law and can seek up to $5,000 per violation. Civil rights groups warned that mandatory age checks and parental notifications could burden anonymous speech and expand sensitive-data collection, with particular risks for LGBTQ+ youth; the final law narrowed some provisions but kept mandatory age assurance.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Nily Rozic sponsored the Stop Online Predators Act in 2025. Gov. Kathy Hochul put key parts in her 2026–27 budget proposal that January, and lawmakers passed a version renamed the Safe by Design Act. The Senate voted 53–10 and the Assembly 110–33 on May 26, 2026, and Hochul signed it the same day. The same budget also pays to offer Teen Mental Health First Aid training to 10th graders across the state.
