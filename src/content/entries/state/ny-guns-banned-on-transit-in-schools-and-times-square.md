In New York, possessing a firearm, rifle or shotgun in a place you know or reasonably should know is a “sensitive location” is a class E felony. Covered places include subways, trains, buses, schools, child care, playgrounds, parks, libraries, hospitals, shelters, houses of worship, polling places, protests, bars and Times Square. A federal court has blocked the part covering airports. Police, lawfully authorized military members, some armed security guards and several other groups are exempt.

Applicants for a new or renewed concealed-carry license need at least 16 hours of classroom training and 2 hours of live-fire practice, a passing written test, an interview and character references. Depending on where it was issued, a license must be renewed or recertified every three years. Gun dealers and ammunition sellers must send background-check requests through State Police. Body armor purchases are generally limited to people in state-approved eligible professions. You don't need to do anything for these protections to apply.

The U.S. Supreme Court struck down New York's old “proper cause” requirement for a carry license in June 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul called lawmakers back to Albany and worked with them on a response. Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins sponsored the Senate bill, and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie sponsored its Assembly companion. The Senate passed it 43–20 on party lines, the Assembly 90–52, and Hochul signed it July 1. Courts have blocked the social-media disclosure, the public-facing private-property default and the airport ban. Court challenges to several other place bans remain pending.
