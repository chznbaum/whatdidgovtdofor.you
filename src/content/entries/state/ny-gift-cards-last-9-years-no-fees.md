If you buy a nonpromotional gift card covered by New York law, the money on it has to stay good for at least nine years from purchase. That's four years longer than federal law requires. The card also can't lose value while it sits in a drawer. Dormancy, service, redemption, reloading and other fees are banned. The one exception is a card usable at many unrelated stores, which can carry a reasonably related one-time activation fee of up to $9 when it's sold. If less than $5 is left on a store-specific card, you can ask for the rest in cash.

You don't have to do anything to get these protections. They apply to covered cards and certificates bought on or after Dec. 10, 2022. Cards bought from Dec. 25, 2016 through Dec. 9, 2022 could expire after five years, and stores could charge monthly inactivity fees after two years. Promotional certificates, such as free bonus cards, may expire sooner and aren't eligible for cash redemption, although the fee ban still covers them. A 2022 follow-up law also excludes MTA and other public-transit fare cards.

Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assemblyman Nader Sayegh sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 63–0 and the Assembly 149–0 in June 2021. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 10, 2021.
