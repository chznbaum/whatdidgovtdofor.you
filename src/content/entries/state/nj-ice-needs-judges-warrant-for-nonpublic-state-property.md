New Jersey state agencies can't let federal immigration agents enter, access, or use the nonpublic parts of state property for civil immigration enforcement unless a judge has signed a warrant or order. That covers places like state offices, child care centers, residential medical facilities, state college dorms, and senior homes. State parking lots, garages, and buildings can't be used as a staging area, processing site, or base for immigration operations either. Agents can still use public areas the same way anyone else can.

You don't have to do anything to be covered. The rule tells state agencies how to handle their own property, so it applies whenever you visit a state office or a state-run facility, or when you live or work in one. It doesn't cover federal, county, or city buildings, and it doesn't stop agents from enforcing criminal laws or acting under a judge's warrant or order.

Gov. Mikie Sherrill (D) signed the order on Feb. 11, 2026, less than a month after she took office. Twelve days later, the Justice Department sued, arguing that federal law overrides the order. On Sept. 4, 2026, U.S. District Judge Georgette Castner threw out the suit. She ruled that New Jersey was acting as a property owner and that refusing to help federal agents isn't the same as getting in their way. The order remains in effect.
