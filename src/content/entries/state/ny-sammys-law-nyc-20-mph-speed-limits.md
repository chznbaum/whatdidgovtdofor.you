State law had barred New York City from setting a citywide or area speed limit below 25 mph. Sammy's Law lets the city lower the limit to 20 mph on most streets. Outside Manhattan, roads with three or more lanes in one direction are left out. On streets redesigned to slow traffic, the limit can go as low as 10 mph. The city must give the local community board 60 days' notice before a change.

You don't need to apply for anything. Lower limits apply street by street as the city's Department of Transportation posts new signs. The first cuts came in fall 2024, starting on Prospect Park West in Brooklyn. By March 2025 the city had lowered limits at 70 locations and was creating 20 mph zones in neighborhoods in every borough. By March 2026 it had reached about 100 locations, short of its goal of 250 by the end of 2025.

The law is named for Sammy Cohen Eckstein, a 12-year-old who was killed by a driver in Park Slope in 2013. His mother, Amy Cohen, co-founded Families for Safe Streets and pushed for the law for about a decade, including a hunger strike in 2023. State Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal introduced the bill in 2020, and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal carried it in the Assembly. It finally passed as part of the state budget in April 2024: 36–24 in the Senate and 93–53 in the Assembly. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on April 20, 2024.
