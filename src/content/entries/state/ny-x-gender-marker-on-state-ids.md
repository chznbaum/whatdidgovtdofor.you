Your New York driver license, learner permit or non-driver ID can show X as your gender, not just M or F. You say which marker fits you. You don't need a doctor's note or medical records. If you already have a license or ID, you can make the change online or by filing the MV-44 form at a DMV office. If you're applying for the first time, you pick X when you apply in person. More than 5,200 New Yorkers had chosen X by June 2024.

The law also makes legal changes easier. You no longer have to publish a name change in a newspaper, which used to put your old and new names in public view. A court can seal name-change papers if making them public could put you in danger, while gender-change proceedings are sealed on request. People with a New York State-issued birth certificate can update its gender marker with a sworn statement, and a parent can be listed as "parent" instead of "mother" or "father."

State Sen. Brad Hoylman and Assembly Member Daniel O'Donnell carried the bill after versions had stalled in the prior two legislative sessions. Lambda Legal, which helped draft an early version, also sued on behalf of Sander Saba; a June 2021 agreement committed the state to issue X-marked licenses and Cuomo to sign the bill. It passed the Senate 46–17 on June 8, 2021, with four Republicans voting yes, and the Assembly passed it two days later. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 24. The DMV began offering X in May 2022 and added the online option that August.
