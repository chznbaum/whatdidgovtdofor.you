Minnesota added $40 million to its Family Homeless Prevention and Assistance Program. It provides rent, deposit and utility help to households that are homeless or at imminent risk. Eligible households generally must have income at or below 200% of federal poverty guidelines. You contact a regional provider, not the state. Minnesota Housing expects the money to help about 11,000 households, and it must be spent by December 31, 2026.

The law also authorized up to $100 million in housing infrastructure bonds for eligible affordable and supportive housing. It redirected $25 million in housing investment earnings, but $2 million of that is part of the $40 million homelessness total. Of the remaining $23 million, $14.275 million goes to Greater Minnesota workforce housing, $4 million to supportive housing, $4 million to manufactured-home park infrastructure, and $725,000 to a tenant hotline, homebuyer education and a home-sharing pilot.

The state used $38 million no longer needed for property-tax-forfeiture settlement claims and $25 million in accumulated housing investment earnings. Minnesota Housing says the investments required no additional General Fund appropriations. A later law fixed the homelessness funding at $38 million from the General Fund plus the $2 million from investment earnings.

Rep. Michael Howard (DFL-Richfield) and Sen. Lindsey Port (DFL-Burnsville) carried the bill. Howard and Republican Rep. Spencer Igo negotiated the final package. Gov. Tim Walz had proposed the $40 million homelessness investment in his budget and signed the bill on May 18, 2026. It passed the House 105–28 and the Senate 42–25.
