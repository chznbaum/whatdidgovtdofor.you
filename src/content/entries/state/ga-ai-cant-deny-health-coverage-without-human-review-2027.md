Starting Jan. 1, 2027, a computer program can't issue a denial saying your care is medically unnecessary without human review. Insurers and review companies may still use AI to sort requests, but a person must conduct the utilization review and a qualified clinical peer must participate. Under Georgia law, that peer is a licensed healthcare provider in the same or a similar specialty who knows the condition and treatment at issue. Software cannot override that peer's judgment.

The rule covers utilization review—the process used to decide whether an admission, longer hospital stay, treatment, test, or prescription is medically necessary. Prior authorization is one kind of utilization review. The protection applies automatically when an insurer or review company reviews care from a Georgia provider; you do not need to enroll or apply.

Sen. Kay Kirkpatrick, a Republican physician, wrote the bill. Rep. Lee Hawkins, also a Republican, carried it on the House floor. The House substitute added two safeguards: a clinical peer must participate, and software cannot override the peer's judgment. Kirkpatrick worked on those changes with Republican Rep. Brad Thomas, who presented the substitute in committee, and Democratic Rep. Stacey Evans. Senators from both parties sponsored the bill, including Democrat Ed Harbison. The House passed it 166–0, the Senate agreed to the House version 47–0, and Gov. Brian Kemp signed it May 5, 2026.
