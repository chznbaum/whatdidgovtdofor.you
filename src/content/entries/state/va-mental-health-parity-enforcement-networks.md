Federal and Virginia law already require covered health plans to treat mental health and addiction benefits on par with medical and surgical benefits. Virginia's new law gives the state more tools to enforce that rule. Insurers must send the Bureau of Insurance the analyses federal law requires them to prepare, comparing how they limit mental health care with how they limit other care. They must submit them at least every two years, and the law requires the Bureau to make current analyses public. If an analysis is noncompliant or insufficient, the Bureau can fine the insurer up to $100,000 for each violation or require it to remove or fix a noncompliant treatment limit.

The law also tells the Virginia Department of Health to set firm, measurable standards for plan networks: how long you wait for an appointment and how far you travel to see a provider. They must be at least as strict as the standards for Marketplace plans. Starting July 1, 2027, the conditions covered by the fairness rule will be defined using standard medical diagnosis manuals, so insurers cannot narrow them. You do not need to apply to benefit.

Del. Rodney Willett carried the bill in the House, and Sen. Creigh Deeds carried a matching bill in the Senate. Behavioral health providers said thin networks often made timely care impossible. The House initially passed HB 656 by 95–3. The Senate passed its substitute 40–0, and the House agreed to that version 92–6. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
