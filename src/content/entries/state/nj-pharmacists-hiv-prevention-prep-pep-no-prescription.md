PrEP is medicine used before a possible exposure to prevent HIV infection, and PEP is a short course started right after a possible exposure. Once New Jersey completes implementation, a trained pharmacist with a standing order from the state Health Department will be able to provide either one without an individual prescription.

For PrEP, you need a negative HIV test from the past seven days, or a test the pharmacist orders or gives. The pharmacist checks for warning signs and medicines that could interact with PrEP. You can get from 30 to 90 days' worth, up to 90 days in any two years. After that, you need a regular prescription. For PEP, the exposure must have happened within the previous 72 hours, and the pharmacist provides the full course. The pharmacist counsels you and tells your regular doctor, or gives you a list of providers if you do not have one or do not consent to notice.

The law also requires Medicaid, state and school worker plans, and state-regulated insurance to cover PrEP and PEP furnished this way without prior authorization or step therapy. When equivalent versions exist, a plan may put those restrictions on some versions if at least one is covered without them.

Sens. Vin Gopal and John McKeon and Assembly members Luanne Peterpaul and Shanique Speight sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 39–0 and the Assembly 74–0, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on January 9, 2026.
