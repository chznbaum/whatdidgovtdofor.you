If you accept an automatically renewing plan in New York for personal, family or household use, such as a yearly membership or software plan, the business has to warn you before the next renewal charge. The rule covers plans whose first paid term is a year or longer and that renew for six months or more. The business must send the notice at least 15 days, and no more than 45 days, before your deadline to cancel. The notice has to tell you how to cancel.

You don't have to apply for the notice. It's meant to prevent the surprise of a large charge you forgot was coming, and to give you enough time to cancel if you no longer want the plan. Some businesses are exempt under the law, including banks, credit unions, security alarm companies, and companies regulated by the state Department of Financial Services.

Sen. Brian Kavanagh and Assemblymember Amy Paulin sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Nader Sayegh cosponsored it. It passed the Senate 58–0 in June 2023, then passed the Assembly. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 13, 2023, and it took effect right away. The state added more subscription protections in 2025, and the yearly-plan notice remains part of the law.
