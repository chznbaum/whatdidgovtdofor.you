Rochester now has a permanent housing court: a part of Rochester City Court with its own judge. It handles cases over the city's housing, building, fire and zoning codes, including fines for violations, orders to make repairs, and putting a neglected building's rent into a receiver's hands. It also hears evictions in which a tenant argues the home isn't fit to live in. Cases about the same building are combined, so one judge sees the whole history of a problem property. The judge can order any lawful remedy that will work.

Tenants don't apply for anything. If you rent in Rochester and your landlord takes you to court, or the city goes after a landlord over code violations, the case goes to this court. A 12-member advisory council, which includes tenant groups and legal aid lawyers, reviews how it's working, and the court files a yearly report.

A smaller housing section opened in 2020, but at first it couldn't order landlords to make repairs. Assemblymember Harry Bronson first introduced the bill in 2018, and Sen. Jeremy Cooney carried it in the Senate. Mark Muoio of the Legal Aid Society of Rochester had pushed for a housing court for years. The Senate passed it 59–4 in June 2021, with votes from both parties, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that October.
