If you own your home in New York and have a dog, your insurer can't refuse to sell you a homeowners policy, refuse to renew it, cancel it, or charge you more just because of your dog's breed or mix. Since March 15, 2023, a follow-up law also bars insurers from cutting or excluding coverage for that reason. You don't need to apply for anything. The rule covers policies issued or renewed after the law took effect.

There's one exception. If a dog has been officially declared dangerous under state law, an insurer can still refuse or cancel the policy, or charge a reasonably higher premium if sound underwriting backs it up. The test is the dog's own record, not its breed.

Assembly Member Deborah Glick championed the bill for years and carried it in the Assembly. Sen. Michael Gianaris carried it in the Senate, where Insurance Committee Chair Neil Breslin helped it pass. It passed the Senate 49–14 and the Assembly 112–37, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Oct. 30, 2021. Gianaris and Glick passed the coverage-cut ban in 2022. A 2025 bill to extend the protection to renters insurance passed both houses, but Hochul vetoed it that December.
