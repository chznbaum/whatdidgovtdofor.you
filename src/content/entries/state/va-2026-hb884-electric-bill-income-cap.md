Virginia's Percentage of Income Payment Program caps a low-income household's electric bill at a set share of its income, and the program covers the rest. Starting January 1, 2027, that share drops sharply. You'll pay no more than 3% of your household income for electricity, or 5% if electricity is your main source of heat. Today the caps are 6% and 10%.

More households will also qualify. The income limit rises from 150% to 200% of the federal poverty level. You need to be a Dominion Energy or Appalachian Power customer, and you have to apply. You can apply online through CommonHelp or at your local Department of Social Services office.

The program is paid for by a small universal service fee on Dominion and Appalachian Power customers, charged by how much electricity each customer uses, so the biggest users pay the most. State law caps total program costs at $125 million a year. Nothing comes out of tax-funded services.

Del. Charniele Herring carried the bill. It passed the House 63–35 and the Senate 21–19, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
