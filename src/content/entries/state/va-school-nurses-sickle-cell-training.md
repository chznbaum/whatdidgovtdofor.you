If your child has sickle cell disease, the nurse at their Virginia public school now has to be trained to handle it. Every school division must make sure its nurses complete a course on recognizing and managing the disease within six months of being hired, then retake it at least every three years. The Board of Education approves the course together with the Virginia Department of Health, and nurses can take it online or in person.

The training covers how to spot symptoms, how to care for a student at school, how to give academic support to kids who miss class because of the disease, and how to work with families on schooling and treatment. Families don't need to apply for anything. The requirement falls on school divisions and has applied since July 1, 2026.

Del. Briana Sewell carried the House bill, and Sen. Todd Pillion, a Republican, carried an identical Senate bill. Neither bill drew a single no vote. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both on April 13, 2026. In June she celebrated them in Newport News as part of the Queen Candis Act. That package of sickle cell laws is named for Candis Gabriella King, a 15-year-old from Fairfax and the daughter of Secretary of the Commonwealth Candi Mundon King, who advocated for it after her daughter's death.
