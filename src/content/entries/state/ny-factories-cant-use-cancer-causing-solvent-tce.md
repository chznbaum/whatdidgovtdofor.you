Trichloroethylene, or TCE, is a solvent long used to strip grease from metal parts. The federal health department lists it as a known cause of cancer in people, and long-term exposure has been linked to lymphoma, leukemia and heart defects in babies. The EPA reports that it is one of the contaminants most often found in groundwater. Since Dec. 1, 2022, New York law has barred anyone from using TCE as a vapor degreaser, a refrigerant, an extraction solvent, or an ingredient for making other chemicals, or in any other manufacturing or industrial cleaning process.

You don't need to do anything to be covered. The ban protects the people who worked with the solvent and the neighborhoods and water supplies near the shops that used it.

Sen. Todd Kaminsky and Assemblymember Steve Englebright, who chaired their chambers' environmental committees, carried the ban. The Senate passed it 49–11 in July 2020 with support from both parties, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it that December. Cuomo asked for a year's delay so businesses could find safe replacements, and for a clause that would keep the rest of the law standing if federal rules overrode part of it. Englebright and Kaminsky carried that amendment in January 2021; the Assembly passed it 125–24 and the Senate 54–9. Cuomo signed it on Jan. 28, 2021, which moved the start date to December 2022. New York was the second state to ban TCE.
