Stores in New York can no longer turn away your cash. The law covers grocery and food sellers, including food carts, stands, and trucks, and any business that sells goods or services to consumers at retail. These businesses can't charge you a higher price for paying in cash than for paying by card or phone. You don't have to do anything to use this protection.

There are a few limits. A store can refuse bills larger than $20, and the rule doesn't cover orders by phone, mail, or online unless you pay in the store. Banks are not covered. A store can avoid taking cash directly only if it has a machine on site that turns your cash into a prepaid card for free, with no minimum deposit above $1. If that machine breaks, the store has to accept cash and post a sign saying so. A first violation costs a store up to $1,000, and each one after that costs up to $1,500.

Sen. James Sanders Jr. first introduced the proposal in 2019, and Assembly Member Catalina Cruz carried it in her chamber in 2025. They argued that people with low incomes, older people, and people without a bank account shouldn't be turned away from buying food or other basics. The bill passed the Senate 53–3 and passed the Assembly on May 29, 2025, and Republican Sen. Dean Murray was a cosponsor. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 21, 2025, and it took effect on March 21, 2026.
