This law lets New York State finance nonprofits to buy financially distressed hotels and office buildings and turn them into permanent affordable homes. At least half the homes in each building go to people who were homeless just before moving in. Other homes can go to households earning up to 80% of the area's median income, while each project must average no more than 50%. The homes are rent-stabilized where local rent stabilization applies, and a bad credit history or credit score can't get you turned down.

You don't apply to the state. At Baisley Pond, 125 homes were offered through NYC Housing Connect; the supportive homes are reserved for formerly homeless households and come with on-site services. The former JFK Hilton in South Jamaica, Queens, now has 318 apartments, 191 of them supportive homes. The state put in $47.7 million. The former Stewart Hotel in Midtown was acquired in December 2025 for another conversion; an October 2026 application proposes 572 homes.

Sen. Michael Gianaris and Assembly Member Karines Reyes sponsored the law, and the Community Service Society worked with them and other organizing groups to develop it. It passed the Senate 49–14 and the Assembly 146–2, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it in August 2021, two weeks before he resigned. Lawmakers set aside $100 million that year. After the program initially received no applications, the state added another $100 million in 2022 and loosened zoning and building rules to make conversions possible.
