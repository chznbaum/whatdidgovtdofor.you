If you're lactating and need a change at work, New York's Human Rights Law treats lactation as a pregnancy-related condition. Your employer must provide a reasonable accommodation that lets you perform the job unless it can show the change would cause an undue hardship. Depending on what you need, that could include a modified schedule or job restructuring. Separate state labor law guarantees pumping breaks and a private place to express milk.

Today the Human Rights Law covers employers of every size. That became true in February 2020 under a separate 2019 law. When the lactation amendment took effect in September 2019, the law generally covered employers with at least four employees.

You don't apply to the state for an accommodation. Tell your employer what you need. If you're refused, or punished for asking, you can file a complaint with the state Division of Human Rights.

Assembly Member Karines Reyes sponsored A5975, and Sen. Luis Sepúlveda carried the Senate version. Gov. Andrew Cuomo had proposed the same change in his FY 2020 Executive Budget. The Assembly passed A5975 by 141–1 in February 2019, and the Senate passed it 62–0 that March. Cuomo signed it on Sept. 13, 2019, and it took effect that day.
