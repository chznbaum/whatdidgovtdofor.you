New York City's speed cameras near schools stay on through July 1, 2030. The city operates cameras in 750 school zones, around the clock. When a camera records a vehicle traveling more than 10 mph over the limit, the registered owner gets a $50 ticket in the mail. You don't have to do anything to get the benefit: the cameras slow traffic on streets where kids walk to school.

The cameras need state permission, and that permission was set to run out on July 1, 2025. Albany has let it lapse before: in 2018, Republican lawmakers blocked a renewal of what was then a 140-zone program. This time the state renewed it for five years, longer than past renewals. A city report found that average daily violations per camera had fallen 94% since the program began. A later city analysis found that severe-injury crashes fell nearly 30% after one year at locations where cameras were installed in 2023, compared with nearby locations.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assembly Member Deborah Glick sponsored the renewal. The City Council approved the required home-rule request, sponsored by Council Member Lincoln Restler, and Families for Safe Streets successfully fought for renewal. The Senate passed the bill 38–21 on June 13, 2025; three Republicans voted yes and three Democrats voted no. The Assembly passed it 110–31 four days later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 30, the day before the cameras would have gone dark.
