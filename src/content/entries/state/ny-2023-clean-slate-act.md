A criminal record can follow someone for decades, turning up in background checks for jobs and apartments long after they've served their time. New York's Clean Slate Act changes that for most old convictions. Eligible records are sealed automatically, without the person having to hire a lawyer or file anything.

Most misdemeanor convictions are sealed three years after sentencing or release, and most felony convictions eight years after release, as long as the person hasn't been convicted of another crime in that time. Sex offenses, murder, and other non-drug Class A felonies are never sealed. Sealed records stay available to police, prosecutors, and courts, and they still show up in gun license checks and in fingerprint background checks for jobs like police officer or teacher.

Sen. Zellnor Myrie and Assemblymember Catalina Cruz sponsored the law. It passed the Senate 37–25 in June 2023, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 16, 2023. It took effect one year later. The state court system has until November 2027 to finish finding and sealing every eligible record, so someone who qualifies may not see their record sealed right away.
