Florida already required assisted living facilities that advertised special Alzheimer's or dementia care to disclose their services, train staff, offer activities for cognitively impaired residents, and meet safety standards. Facilities with 17 or more residents also had to keep a staff member awake around the clock; smaller facilities could instead use monitoring systems. This law goes further by creating a separate state license for memory care. Once the state's rules take effect, facilities will need that license to provide specialized memory care or use restricted terms such as "memory care," "dementia care" or "Alzheimer's care." A facility offering only optional supportive services available to all residents is exempt if it follows the advertising rules.

Families don't need to apply for anything. The Agency for Health Care Administration must adopt the license standards by June 1, 2027. Facilities licensed before the rules take effect get six months to obtain the new license. A facility that demonstrates it cannot reasonably get the license may keep memory care residents admitted before the rules took effect, but it must notify residents and caregivers, help with relocation upon request, stop accepting new memory care residents, and update affected contracts and records.

Sen. Colleen Burton carried the bill in the Senate, and Rep. Sam Greco sponsored the House version. Burton said families had trouble comparing homes because there was "no uniformity in what constitutes memory care." Both chambers passed it without a single no vote, and Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on May 22, 2026.
