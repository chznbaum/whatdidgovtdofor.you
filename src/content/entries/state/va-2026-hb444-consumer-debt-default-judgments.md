Consumer debt cases made up 48% of Virginia's general district court civil docket in 2024. When someone does not take part in a case, the collector can win by default. Starting July 1, 2027, a court cannot enter that judgment on a covered consumer debt unless the complaint states 12 required items, including the creditor, account identifier, last payment, balance, amount sought and the plaintiff's authority to sue. The law covers unsecured consumer debt, secured debt when the suit seeks only money, and balances left after collateral is sold. It does not cover suits to take or dispose of property or government-filed actions to collect government debt.

If the plaintiff is not the creditor, it must identify each person that acquired the debt after charge-off or default, give the acquisition dates and attach documents showing its authority to collect. The plaintiff must also attach a signed agreement or another record showing that the debt was incurred. To qualify for a default judgment, the complaint must come with a separate warning explaining that a judgment can last at least 10 years and lead to money being taken from your bank account, wage garnishment or other collection. The notice must include contact information for legal help. You do not have to apply for these protections, and a waiver of them is void.

Del. Marcus Simon carried the bill, which is based on a model law from the Uniform Law Commission. The House approved the final version 75–21 and the Senate passed it 40–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026.
