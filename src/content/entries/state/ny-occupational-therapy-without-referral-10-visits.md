In New York, you can now go straight to a licensed occupational therapist without first getting a referral from a doctor or nurse practitioner. Occupational therapists help people manage everyday tasks after an injury or illness and can check a home for fall risks. Without a referral, you can be treated for up to 10 visits or 30 days, whichever comes first. To keep going after that, you need a referral.

You don't have to apply. To treat you without a referral, the therapist must have at least three years of full-time practice. Before treatment starts, they must tell you in writing that your health plan may not pay without a referral, and that the same care may be covered if you get one. Ask your plan before you book.

Assembly Member John McDonald and Sen. Timothy Kennedy carried the bill. Republican Jake Ashby had introduced earlier versions in the Assembly in 2018 and 2019, and he cosponsored this one. The New York State Occupational Therapy Association advocated for the change for several years. Kennedy argued that occupational therapy cuts falls and hospital readmissions, and he noted that physical therapists could already see patients without a referral. The Senate passed the bill 61–0 in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 21, 2022, and it took effect 120 days later, in March 2023.
