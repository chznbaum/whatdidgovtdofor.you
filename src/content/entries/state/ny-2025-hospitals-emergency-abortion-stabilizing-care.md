If you go to a New York general hospital with a medical emergency, the hospital has to examine you to find out whether you have an emergency condition. It can't hold up that exam or your treatment to ask how you'll pay. If you do have an emergency condition, the hospital has to stabilize you or arrange a safe transfer to another hospital. State law now says that stabilizing care includes an abortion when going without one would probably make a pregnant patient's condition deteriorate upon or during transfer. You don't need to apply for anything: the duty applies to every general hospital.

A federal law, EMTALA, has long required most hospitals to provide emergency care. But federal guidance on emergency abortion can change. In May 2025, federal regulators withdrew their 2022 guidance on it. Writing the rule into state law keeps it in force in New York even if federal guidance shifts.

Assemblywoman Amy Paulin wrote the bill, A.1165, and Sen. Rachel May carried the Senate version. Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the requirement in her budget. Lawmakers added it to the state budget as Part P of the health budget bill, and Hochul signed it on May 9, 2025. The same budget set aside $20 million in flexible funding for providers to cover medication abortion care and other abortion services.
