Every New York nursing home has to post a statement of residents' rights. This law makes the state health department translate it into the 10 non-English languages most spoken by New Yorkers with limited English, based on the latest census. Homes must post the statement and give copies to residents. The state's residents' rights booklet and flyer now come in Spanish, Chinese, Haitian Creole, Italian, Korean, Russian, Yiddish, Polish, Bengali and Arabic, as well as English.

The law also helps residents find someone to call when something goes wrong. Each home must post contact details for the state and regional offices of the long-term care ombudsman program and for the health department's nursing home complaint hotline. It must give that information to every staff member and to each resident and their appointed representative by admission, or when a representative is appointed. It must also send residents updated ombudsman contacts twice a year. Residents do not have to ask for the contact information.

Sen. Rachel May and Assemblymember Monica Wallace carried the bill, which passed the Senate 63–0 in March 2021. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December after agreeing to changes. In early 2022, May and Wallace passed a follow-up law that raised the number of translations from six to ten and added the complaint hotline. It also replaced the plan to post ombudsman notices in every resident's room with a common posting, plus contact information given directly to each resident. The requirements took effect April 20, 2022.
