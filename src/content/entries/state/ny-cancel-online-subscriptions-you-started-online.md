If a covered business sells you something in New York that renews and charges you automatically, such as a streaming service, a gym membership, or a box delivered every month, it must lay out the terms clearly before you sign up. That includes how much it costs, how often you'll be charged, how long it runs, and how to cancel. It can't charge you until you agree, and it must send you a copy of the terms and cancellation steps that you can keep. If you signed up online, you can cancel online. Goods it ships under that auto-renewing deal without your consent are yours to keep as a gift.

You don't need to do anything to get these protections. The state attorney general can take businesses to court and win refunds for customers. A court can fine a business up to $500 for a single knowing violation, or up to $1,000 for multiple violations from one act or incident. Banks, credit unions, and some other regulated businesses are exempt.

State Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the bill. The Senate passed it unanimously in 2019 and again in 2020, and the Assembly passed it that July. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on November 11, 2020. Later laws built on it: one in 2023 added renewal reminders, and the 2025 state budget required that canceling be as easy as signing up.
