For 44 years, a New York law called Section 50-a let police departments keep complaints against officers, and the discipline that followed, out of public view. In 2020 the state repealed that law. Now anyone can ask for these records under New York's Freedom of Information Law. That includes complaints against officers and the results of disciplinary cases. Agencies still have to black out private details like medical history, home addresses and Social Security numbers.

You don't need to be part of a case to ask. You file a public records request with the police department or other law enforcement agency. Many departments resisted at first. In February 2025, New York's highest court ruled that departments cannot withhold records simply because a complaint was not substantiated, and that records from before 2020 are covered too. Agencies must review records individually and can still withhold or redact material under FOIL's specific exemptions.

Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assembly Member Daniel O'Donnell sponsored the repeal. Communities United for Police Reform led an eight-year grassroots campaign that included families of people hurt by police, and Eric Garner's mother, Gwen Carr, pushed for repeal after her son's death in 2014. The bill passed after the 2020 protests over George Floyd's killing. In the Senate every Democrat voted yes and every Republican voted no. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 12, 2020.
