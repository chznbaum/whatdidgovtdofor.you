If an ambulance crew treats you or takes you to the hospital in New York, the service can't sell, trade, hand over, or use information that identifies you for marketing. The ban covers ambulance services, advanced life support first response services, and everyone who works for them. Using your information internally for training, recruiting, or staff recognition takes a separate written consent form that you sign.

Ambulance services can still share your information where care and the law require it: with you, with the doctors treating you, with your insurer for payment, and with health officials and courts. Nonprofit and public services can still mail you a request for donations using your name and address. You don't need to do anything to be covered. The state health commissioner enforces the law, and the attorney general can sue services that break it.

The bill came after a 2014 news report that the FDNY's privacy notice said it could sell or use patient information for fundraising and marketing. The FDNY dropped that policy, but Assemblymember Edward Braunstein argued the law should forbid it. He first introduced the ban in 2014, and the Assembly passed it in 2016, 2017 and 2018. In 2019 Sen. John Liu carried it in the Senate, which passed it 62–0. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 7, 2019, and it took effect 180 days later.
