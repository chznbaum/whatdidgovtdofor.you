From 1961 to 1975, tens of thousands of Hmong and Lao men fought for the United States in a CIA-run war in Laos. The 2025 law created limited state veteran benefits for Minnesota residents who were naturalized under the federal Hmong Veterans' Naturalization Act of 2000 or whom the state verifies served honorably with a special guerrilla unit or irregular force. The latter group must be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident.

To get a state eligibility certificate, an applicant first files for a federal VA pre-need burial determination, then sends the result to Minnesota's Department of Veterans Affairs. If the federal VA denies eligibility, the applicant may ask the state commissioner for special consideration using service records, affidavits or other evidence. Verified veterans can receive honor guards, grave markers, public-employment hiring preferences and free vital records. Burial in a state veterans cemetery requires federal eligibility. A separate “SGU Veteran” mark on driver's licenses and state IDs starts Jan. 1, 2027. Health care is not included.

Sen. Foung Hawj led the Senate bill and Rep. Ethan Cha led the House version. Sen. Susan Pha helped Hawj narrow it after cost objections; House Veterans co-chair Jay Xiong helped move it, and scholar Lee Pao Xiong helped draft the language and testified. It went into the omnibus carried by Sen. Aric Putnam and Republican Rep. Matt Bliss. The final House vote was 130–0. Gov. Tim Walz signed it May 23, 2025, and a 2026 law put the application process in place.
