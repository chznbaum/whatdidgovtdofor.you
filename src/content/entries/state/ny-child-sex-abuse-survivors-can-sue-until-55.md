If a childhood sexual abuse claim was still within the old deadline when the law took effect on February 14, 2019, you can file it until you turn 55. If it had already expired, the permanent age-55 rule did not revive it. You can sue the abuser and any school, church, youth group, or public agency whose actions or neglect let the abuse happen. Public schools and government defendants no longer get the special short notice deadline they once had. The law also gives prosecutors more time to bring criminal charges.

For claims that had already expired, the law opened a separate window from August 2019 to August 2021, after it was extended because COVID disrupted the courts. More than 9,000 lawsuits were filed. The law gives those revived cases priority when they are ready for trial.

Assembly Member Margaret Markey first introduced the bill in 2006. The Assembly passed versions of it several times, but the Senate, then led by Republicans, never voted on it. Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal took over the Assembly version, and Sen. Brad Hoylman sponsored it in the Senate. When Democrats took the Senate in 2019, Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins brought it to the floor. It passed 63–0 in the Senate and 130–3 in the Assembly. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on February 14, 2019.
