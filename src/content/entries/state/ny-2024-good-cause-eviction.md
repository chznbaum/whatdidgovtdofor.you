If you rent a covered market-rate apartment in New York City, your landlord needs a listed good-cause reason to evict you or refuse to renew your lease. Reasons include nonpayment, a serious lease violation, a nuisance, denied access, or the owner moving in. An increase above inflation plus 5%, capped at a 10% benchmark, is presumed unreasonable; a judge can still accept a higher increase after considering the landlord's costs. You do not apply beforehand. If your landlord seeks eviction, you can raise the law in Housing Court, including as a defense to nonpayment of an increase the court finds unreasonable.

The law automatically covers unregulated rentals in New York City. Cities, towns, and villages elsewhere can opt in by local law. Exemptions include homes owned by landlords with 10 or fewer units statewide, owner-occupied buildings with no more than 10 apartments, regulated or subsidized housing, condos and co-ops, high-rent units, and buildings issued a certificate of occupancy on or after January 1, 2009, which are exempt for 30 years. It applies to cases filed on or after April 20, 2024.

Sen. Julia Salazar and Assemblymember Pamela Hunter sponsored broader stand-alone good-cause bills beginning in 2019, with support from tenant groups. Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins, Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie, and Gov. Kathy Hochul negotiated the final housing package. The narrower version became law when Hochul signed the budget on April 20, 2024. The carrying bill passed the Senate 36–24.
