The law and its implementing code provisions set a broader baseline for shared spaces. A residential building where at least three homes share common spaces must have operable smoke alarms or a smoke-detection system that makes an alarm clearly audible in each shared space. That includes lobbies, hallways and stairwells. Battery-operated alarms are allowed in existing buildings when construction is not taking place. Building owners are responsible for compliance, so residents do not apply. The requirement took effect December 31, 2025.

The idea came out of a January 2023 fire at 4055 Carpenter Avenue in the Bronx. About 200 firefighters and other emergency workers responded, four people were hurt, and many residents were displaced. The fire spread into the building next door. The sponsors said the people there did not know it was coming because nothing in the shared spaces sounded an alarm.

Assemblymember John Zaccaro Jr. and Sen. Jamaal Bailey, both Bronx Democrats, carried the bill. The Assembly passed it 136–0 and the Senate 60–0 before Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 22, 2024. A negotiated chapter amendment extended the requirement to public housing, directed the state's Code Council to use national standards and set the December 2025 start date. Hochul signed that amendment on February 14, 2025, after the Assembly passed it 149–0 and the Senate 61–0.
