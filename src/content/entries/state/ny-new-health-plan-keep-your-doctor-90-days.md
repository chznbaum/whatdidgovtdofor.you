If you join a new health plan while you're in the middle of treatment with a provider who isn't in its network, the plan must let you continue that treatment with your provider for up to 90 days. Until now, the window was 60 days. It also used to cover only people with a life-threatening or degenerative and disabling condition, or people at least in their second trimester of pregnancy. If you're pregnant when you enroll, at any stage, you can stay with your provider through the pregnancy and postpartum care. Your provider has to accept the new plan's payment rates and rules.

People with chronic conditions get fewer repeat checks. A chronic condition is one expected to last at least a year that needs ongoing treatment. Once a plan approves an outpatient course of treatment for it, the plan can re-review whether that care is needed only once a year, unless your provider changes the treatment. Plans must also post their lists of covered drugs on a public website you can see without logging in. Starting in September 2027, the state's yearly consumer guide will show how each plan handles prior authorization requests and appeals.

You don't have to apply. The rules cover New York-regulated insurance and HMO plans issued or renewed on or after Jan. 1, 2027. Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the changes in her January 2026 State of the State, and the Legislature passed them in the budget she signed on May 26, 2026.
