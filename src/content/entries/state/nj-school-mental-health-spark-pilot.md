When a student is struggling, getting them in front of a mental health professional can be slow and hard to navigate. This law creates SPARK, a state program that links schools to a team that can consult with a psychiatrist, find the student a provider at school or nearby, manage medication for a short time, and plan the student's return to class after a crisis or a hospital stay.

The program starts in early 2027 in up to 10 school districts picked by the Department of Children and Families. A teacher or school mental health professional makes the referral, and a parent or guardian must give written permission. Each district in the program needs a staff member for school mental health care, or a formal arrangement with a local provider or the state's existing student support program. After 18 months, every district in the state joins unless the department recommends against it. The state will also choose a mental health app for students and families.

Gov. Mikie Sherrill laid out the idea in her March budget speech, and the budget she signed set aside $8 million for it. Senate President Nick Scutari, Sen. Joe Vitale, and Assembly members Linda Carter, Andrea Katz, and Vinnie Kearney sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 31–6 and the Assembly 71–0 on June 30, 2026, and Sherrill signed it on Sept. 28.
