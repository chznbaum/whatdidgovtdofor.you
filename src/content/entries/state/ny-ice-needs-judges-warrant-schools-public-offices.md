New York public-school workers can't let immigration agents into nonpublic areas unless agents present a judicial warrant authorizing a search or arrest, or law otherwise requires access. ICE administrative warrants, detainers and notices to appear do not count. Covered civilian workers at state and local agencies face similar limits: they generally can't use work time for civil immigration enforcement or share personal information with immigration authorities. Police, peace officers, certain law-enforcement staff, local jails and the Port Authority are excluded from those employee rules.

Every child has a right to a free public education, whatever their own or their parents' immigration status. If a parent is detained, a school can't transfer a child to immigration authorities solely for that reason without a court order. Twelve kinds of private sites, including clinics, hospitals, day cares, private schools, houses of worship and housing, may adopt a warrant rule; state law protects them from liability for denying entry. You don't have to apply. The attorney general's Office of Immigrant Trust accepts complaints.

Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assemblymember Catalina Cruz sponsored the school-protection bill folded into the budget. Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Karines Reyes sponsored the broader New York for All Act; its limits on civilian agency workers entered the budget in narrower form. Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the sensitive-locations measure. The final deal still allows informal police cooperation with ICE, which advocates criticized. The budget passed the Senate 39–22 and Assembly 93–47 on May 21. Hochul signed it May 27.
