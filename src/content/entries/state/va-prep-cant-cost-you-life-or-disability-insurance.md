PrEP is a medicine that sharply lowers the chance of getting HIV. Some people have been turned down or charged more for life insurance because they take it, and some have stopped taking it so they could qualify. Since July 1, 2026, that's illegal in Virginia. Insurers can't refuse to sell or renew you a life or accident and sickness policy, which includes disability coverage, or cap your coverage or charge you a higher rate based only on your use of PrEP. Unless there's some other actuarial risk, it can't count against you.

You don't have to apply for anything. The rule is part of Virginia's unfair-discrimination law for insurers, so it applies to state-regulated life and accident-and-sickness policies. Health plans already have to cover PrEP itself.

Del. Jeion Ward of Hampton wrote the bill. She first tried in 2025, but her bill passed the House 53–44 with only two Republican votes, and Gov. Glenn Youngkin vetoed it. She brought it back in 2026, and this time it passed the House 89–8 and the Senate 40–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on March 31, 2026.
