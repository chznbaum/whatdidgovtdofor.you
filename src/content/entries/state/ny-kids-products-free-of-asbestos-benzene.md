Since January 1, 2023, it has been illegal in New York to distribute or sell a new product made for children 12 and under if asbestos, benzene or the flame retardant TDCPP was intentionally added to a part that can touch a child's skin or mouth during reasonably foreseeable use. Asbestos and benzene can cause cancer. The ban covers everyday items such as clothing, toys without electronics, car seats, kids' bedding and mattresses, school supplies and furniture. Electronics, batteries, sports equipment and science kits are left out, and so are used items sold secondhand. The ban also does not cover trace contamination, inaccessible components or products where federal law preempts the state. Families do not have to apply for anything.

The law also tells the state Department of Environmental Conservation to list other harmful chemicals that manufacturers will have to report when they're in children's products, and lets the state ban more of them. That reporting hasn't started yet. As of its January 2026 agenda, the department was still writing the rule.

Sen. Todd Kaminsky and Assemblymember Steve Englebright, who chaired the environmental committees in their chambers, carried the bill. Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie also backed it and helped lead the fight for it. Earlier versions had passed the Assembly as far back as 2012, and health, environmental and labor groups pushed for it for more than 10 years. Both chambers passed it on April 30, 2019, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it in February 2020. Lawmakers narrowed it in that year's state budget, so the 2023 ban covers three chemicals.
