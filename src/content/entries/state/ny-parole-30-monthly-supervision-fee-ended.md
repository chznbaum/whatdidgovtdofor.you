If you're on parole or post-release supervision in New York, the state no longer bills you $30 a month for supervising you. That fee added up to $360 a year, charged to people who were often just home from prison and looking for work. The repeal took effect the day it was signed in April 2022, and you don't have to apply. The state law that set the fee is gone.

The Fines and Fees Justice Center, which pushed for the change, says it keeps more than 25,000 New Yorkers a year from being charged.

Sen. Luis Sepúlveda carried a stand-alone bill to end the fee. It passed the Senate 43–20 in June 2021 but stalled in the Assembly, and Assembly Member Catalina Cruz introduced the Assembly version the next year. In January 2022, Gov. Kathy Hochul called for ending parole supervision fees in her State of the State and put the repeal in her budget. It became law as one part of the 2022–23 public protection budget bill. The Senate passed that bill 43–20 on April 7, 2022, with every Republican voting against the whole bill, and Hochul signed it on April 9. Advocates are still pushing a wider bill that would end other court and probation fees.
