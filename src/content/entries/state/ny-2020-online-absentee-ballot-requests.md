In 2020, as COVID-19 kept many people home, this law made it easier for New Yorkers to vote by mail. Starting a week after the June 23 primary, voters could request an absentee ballot by email or through a state or county board of elections website, and the request didn't need a signature. Voters still had to give their registration address and the address where the ballot should be mailed, and the usual request deadlines still applied.

The law also gave voters one more day to mail a ballot. Before, an absentee ballot had to be postmarked by the day before the election. Under this law, a ballot postmarked on Election Day counted if it reached the board of elections within seven days. Military and overseas ballots got the same change. Voters didn't have to do anything to get it.

Sen. Zellnor Myrie sponsored the bill, saying the pandemic shouldn't keep people from voting. Assembly Member Aravella Simotas carried the Assembly version. On May 28, 2020, the Senate passed it 39–22, with all Republicans present voting no, and the Assembly passed it the same day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 7, 2020. The signature-free electronic request rule ended Dec. 31, 2020, while the Election Day postmark rule remained in state law. A 2021 law signed by Gov. Kathy Hochul created a lasting statewide online application system; a 2022 chapter amendment moved its start to April 1, 2022.
