Many households pay their power or gas bill through a budget plan. The utility estimates your use for the next 12 months and bills you one-twelfth of that each month, so you pay the same amount even when winter or summer bills spike. The catch is that the utility can reset that amount, and a sudden jump is hard to plan for.

Since July 1, 2026, Virginia law limits those resets for residential customers. A utility can raise your budget plan payment no more than once in any 12-month period, and it has to tell you in writing at least 60 days before the higher amount starts. You don't need to apply for anything: if you're on a budget plan, the rule covers you. It doesn't apply to water or sewer utilities.

Del. Josh Cole carried the bill, with Dels. Nadarius Clark and Jackie Glass as joint sponsors. It passed the House 97–0 in January. After the Senate amended it, the Senate approved it 37–2 and the House agreed 93–4. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026.
