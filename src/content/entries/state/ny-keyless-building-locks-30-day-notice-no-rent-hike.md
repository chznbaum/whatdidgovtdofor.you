Before a company can put a keyless entry device on the front door, lobby or other shared doors of your New York apartment building, it needs written permission from the owner, the condo or co-op board, or someone else authorized to give it. You also have to be told at least 30 days ahead, either with a notice delivered to your apartment (on paper or electronically) or with one posted where residents will see it. Condos and co-ops can follow their own bylaws for notice instead. You don't need to do anything to get this protection.

The law also says installing one of these devices can't be used as a reason to raise your rent, and it can't make it harder for residents to get into the building.

The law grew out of complaints about Amazon's Key for Business devices, which let delivery drivers unlock building doors from a phone. At one Westchester building, residents said a device nobody had approved interfered with the intercom, made it hard to let in guests, and led to a $1,500 repair bill. Sen. Kevin Thomas and Assembly Member Jo Anne Simon wrote the 2023 law requiring consent, which passed the Senate 61–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in November 2023 after agreeing with the Legislature to add the tenant notice and rent protections. That amendment passed the Senate 56–4 with support from both parties, and Hochul signed it on January 26, 2024.
