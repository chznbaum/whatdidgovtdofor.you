Georgia used part of its budget surplus for a one-time refund. If you timely filed Georgia individual income tax returns for both 2024 and 2025 and had Georgia income tax liability for 2024, you generally qualified automatically: up to $250 if you filed single or married filing separately, up to $375 as a head of household, and up to $500 for a married couple filing jointly. Tax liability means the tax shown on your 2024 return before withholding and credits, not necessarily an unpaid bill. If your liability was below the cap, your refund was smaller. Dependents without earned income and nonresident aliens were excluded, while refunds for part-year residents and nonresidents were prorated.

You didn't have to apply. The Department of Revenue began sending refunds on May 4, 2026, by direct deposit or paper check, using the refund instructions on your 2025 return. The state could reduce a refund to cover money you owed it or overdue child support. You can check yours with the Surplus Tax Refund tool on the department's website.

Gov. Brian Kemp called for the refund in his January 2026 State of the State address. It was the fourth one he had pushed for. Rep. Matthew Gambill sponsored the bill in the House, which passed it 172–0, and Sen. Bo Hatchett carried it through the Senate, which passed it 53–0. Senate Democrats backed it too, and their leader said it did not "gut the budget." Kemp signed it on March 20, 2026. The money came from accumulated state surplus, more than $1 billion in all.
