Sometimes someone other than your employer causes a work injury, like a driver who hits your truck or the maker of faulty equipment. You can pursue money from that person and still get workers' comp. When a recovery is made, the employer or insurer gets a credit against future benefits. This law changes how that credit affects an award already in place.

Since July 1, 2026, a recovery from a third party can't, by itself, be used to suspend, change, or end your award. If you have a lifetime medical award, it stays in force, and you don't have to file new claims for related care from your authorized doctor. An ongoing wage-loss award also stays in place. Instead of cutting off benefits, the employer pays a reduced share of each benefit while the credit is being used. If you pay covered medical bills yourself, the employer must reimburse the portion tied to the case's attorney-fee-and-cost ratio. These protections apply when you are under the award at the time the recovery happens.

Del. LeVere Bolling, a Henrico Democrat, carried the bill. It was amended in committee to refine how medical expenses and fees are handled during the offset. The House passed it 99–0 and the Senate passed it 40–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
