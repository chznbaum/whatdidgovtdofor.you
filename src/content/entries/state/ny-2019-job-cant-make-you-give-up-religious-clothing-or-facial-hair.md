If your religion requires you to wear certain clothing, cover your head, or keep your facial hair, a New York employer can't make you give that up to get a job, keep it, or move up. The rule covers hiring, promotions, advancement and transfers. An employer can refuse only if it shows that, after a real effort, it can't accommodate you without undue hardship to its business.

You don't apply for anything. The protection is part of the state's Human Rights Law. This change took effect on Oct. 8, 2019, when that law generally covered employers with at least four workers; the law has covered employers of every size since Feb. 8, 2020. The law already protected days of religious observance such as a sabbath, and this change named attire, clothing and facial hair in the text. The sponsors described the change as making that protection clear.

Assemblymember David Weprin first introduced the bill in 2011, and the Assembly passed it every year from 2013 on. The sponsors pointed to a Sikh subway operator for the MTA who was told to take off his turban or put the agency's logo on it. Sen. John Liu carried the Senate version. The bill passed the Assembly 140–2 in February 2019 and the Senate 60–0 that April. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Aug. 9, 2019.
