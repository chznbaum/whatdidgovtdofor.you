Under federal Public Service Loan Forgiveness, the remaining balance on eligible Direct Loans can be canceled after 120 qualifying payments made while you work full-time for a qualifying government or nonprofit employer. You need your employer's sign-off for each stretch of work, and that paperwork is where many workers got stuck. This New York law requires employers to certify your work without unreasonable delay. They can send it straight to the U.S. Department of Education. If they don't, they must give you a form with their part already filled in each year when you ask for one, when you leave the job, or if they've certified you before.

The law also settles what counts as full-time: an average of 30 hours a week. For teachers and college faculty, including the many adjuncts paid only for class time, each hour in the classroom counts as 3.35 hours of work to cover prep and grading. You do not need to apply separately for the 30-hour rule or classroom multiplier. To receive forgiveness, you still need eligible Direct Loans, 120 qualifying payments, and proper documentation with the federal government. If your employer won't certify you, you can file a complaint with the state Department of Labor.

Sen. Kevin Thomas and Assemblymember Harvey Epstein sponsored the bill. Faculty unions at SUNY and CUNY backed it. It passed both chambers on May 31, 2022, without a single no vote. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on September 15, 2022, weeks before a temporary federal waiver for older payments was set to close.
