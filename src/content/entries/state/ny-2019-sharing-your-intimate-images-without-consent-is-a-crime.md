New York's 2019 law made it a crime to share or post a nude or sexual photo or video of someone without their consent when that person expected it to stay private and the sharer meant to harm them. The sharer is often an ex-partner after a breakup. The crime is a Class A misdemeanor, punishable by up to 364 days in jail. When the accused person is considered a family or household member under New York law, survivors can seek orders of protection in Family Court.

If someone shares or threatens to share a private intimate image to harass, annoy or alarm you, you can sue whether or not they are charged. A judge can award damages, court costs, and attorney's fees and can order a website within the court's jurisdiction to permanently remove images it controls. Braunstein's office said New York was the first state to let victims get such an order. You have three years from when the images were shared to sue, or one year from when you found out, whichever is later.

Assemblymember Edward Braunstein wrote the bill and carried it for years while internet companies worried they'd be held liable. Changes made in 2019 answered those worries. Sen. Monica Martinez carried it in the Senate, which passed it 62–0 on Feb. 28, 2019, the same day the Assembly passed it. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 23, 2019, and it took effect that September.
