If a corporation in New York is convicted under Carlos' Law of a felony involving a worker's death or serious physical injury, a judge can fine it up to $500,000. The cap is $300,000 for a class A misdemeanor or an unclassified misdemeanor punishable by more than three months. Courts can also order restitution above the usual limits. The law covers paid workers at private entities or businesses, regardless of immigration status, including part-time and seasonal workers, independent contractors, apprentices, domestic and home care workers, day laborers, and farmworkers. Workers do not apply; the law gives prosecutors stronger penalties to seek.

The law is named for Carlos Moncayo, a 22-year-old Ecuadorian immigrant who died in 2015 when an unshored trench caved in at a Manhattan construction site. The general contractor, Harco Construction, was convicted of manslaughter and other charges but paid a $10,000 criminal fine, the maximum under the standard corporate-felony schedule. Diana Florence prosecuted the case and worked with labor unions on the legislation.

The fight took about seven years. Then-Assembly Member Francisco Moya and then-Sen. Marisol Alcantara sponsored the first legislative versions in 2017; Ari Espinal and Marcos Crespo later carried Assembly versions. Assembly Member Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn and Sen. James Sanders Jr. sponsored the version that became law and its 2023 amendment. The original passed the Assembly 149–1 and Senate 60–3 before Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 23, 2022. The amendment removed minimum fines and set the final caps; it passed the Assembly 140–4 and Senate 61–1 before Hochul signed it on March 3, 2023.
