If you work for a private employer in New York, your employer must have a written plan to protect you from airborne diseases. Employers can use the state's model plan for their industry or write their own plan that meets or beats it. The plan covers health screenings, face coverings and protective gear, handwashing, cleaning, distancing, and building airflow. Your employer has to give you a copy and post it at work. It cannot retaliate against you for exercising your rights or reporting a violation. The law also protects certain refusals to work when you reasonably believe in good faith that conditions violate applicable safety rules and create an unreasonable airborne-disease exposure risk, after the employer has been notified and failed to fix them.

The plan switches on when the state health commissioner declares an airborne disease a serious public health risk. That happened for COVID-19 from September 2021 to March 2022. Employers must keep their plans ready for the next outbreak. Separately, at an employer with 10 or more workers, employees can form a joint safety committee. At least two-thirds of its members must be workers rather than supervisors. A 2023 chapter amendment gives an employer 15 business days to recognize a new committee.

Sen. Michael Gianaris and Assembly Member Karines Reyes, a nurse during the pandemic, carried the bill in their chambers. Maritza Silva-Farrell of ALIGN helped lead the New York Essential Workers Coalition's campaign, and more than 100 labor, community, and safety organizations backed the bill. It passed the Assembly 95–55 and the Senate 46–17, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on May 5, 2021. Changes made that June narrowed some of the rules for lawsuits. Gov. Kathy Hochul put the plans into effect that September.
