Data centers use huge amounts of electricity, and utilities often have to build new lines, substations and supply to serve them. This law says those costs belong to the data centers. Each New Jersey electric utility must put large data centers on their own rate class, charge them for the grid equipment built for them, and make sure other customers don't subsidize them. For new or expanded service above the size cutoff, a data center customer must guarantee payment for at least 85% of the power it requests over 10 years. In a grid emergency, large data center loads must be cut back before homes, except for designated critical load.

You don't have to do anything. The protections are built into how utilities set rates. Regulators decide what counts as a large data center, but the cutoff can't be higher than 50 megawatts. The Board of Public Utilities has until July 7, 2027, to set the standards. It began taking public input in October 2026 and has extended the comment deadline to December 14. Utilities then have 180 days to file their new rates.

Assemblyman David Bailey and Sens. John Burzichelli and John McKeon led the bill, along with Assembly members Joe Danielsen, Clinton Calabrese and Eliana Pintor Marin. Former Gov. Phil Murphy pocket-vetoed a similar bill. This version passed both houses on June 30, 2026, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on July 7.
