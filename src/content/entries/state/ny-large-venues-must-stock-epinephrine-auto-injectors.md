A severe allergic reaction can close someone's airway within minutes, and epinephrine is the first-line emergency treatment. Under current New York law, covered places of public assembly that hold at least 1,000 people must keep FDA-approved epinephrine delivery devices on site. Those can include auto-injectors or nasal sprays. Covered venues include stadiums, ballparks, gyms, arenas, concert halls, theaters, and indoor and outdoor amphitheaters. At every public or private sponsored event, at least one staff member or volunteer trained to use the devices must be there. Halls owned by churches, religious groups, granges, public associations and free libraries are exempt.

You don't need to do anything. The venue is responsible for having the devices and a trained person ready. The law treats their use under its rules as first aid and provides liability protection through New York's Good Samaritan law.

The idea came from Bay Ridge high school student Lucia Zaremba, whose brother survived anaphylaxis after receiving an EpiPen. She took the idea to State Sen. Andrew Gounardes and campaigned for it. Gounardes carried the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 61–0 in May 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that November. A 2025 follow-up law moved the start date to August 2025 so the state could update its rules. Later that year, another law broadened the requirement to cover FDA-approved epinephrine devices, including nasal sprays.
