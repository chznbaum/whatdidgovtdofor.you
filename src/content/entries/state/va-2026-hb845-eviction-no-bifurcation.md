When a Virginia landlord sues to evict, the case can cover both possession and how much rent and damages the tenant owes. Before this law, if the landlord asked at the initial hearing, the court had to split those issues. The landlord could receive a final possession judgment and an order of possession at that hearing, while the court delayed the final rent-and-damages decision for as long as 120 days.

Since July 1, 2026, the court cannot split the case if the tenant contests the claimed rent and damages at the initial hearing. That keeps the money dispute out of the 120-day bifurcation process. It does not by itself stop the eviction: the statute still allows a final possession judgment and order at the initial hearing if the landlord presents supporting evidence.

Del. Rae Cousins (D) carried the same change in 2024; it passed both chambers but Gov. Glenn Youngkin (R) vetoed it. In 2026, Cousins returned as chief patron, joined by co-patrons Dels. Katrina Callsen (D) and Virgil Thornton (D). The bill passed the House 74–23, with support from both parties, and the Senate 21–19. Gov. Abigail Spanberger (D) signed it April 8, 2026; it took effect July 1.
