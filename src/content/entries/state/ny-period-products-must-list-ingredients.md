Every box or package of tampons, pads, menstrual cups and similar products sold in New York must carry a clear printed list of their ingredients. The list counts anything the maker added on purpose and puts the biggest ingredient first. Federal rules treat these products as medical devices and don't require ingredient labels, so before this law you often couldn't tell what you were putting in or against your body. You don't have to do anything: the list is on the package.

The law took effect on April 8, 2020, and manufacturers had until April 11, 2021—18 months after it was signed—to change their packaging. A maker that breaks the law can be fined 1% of its yearly sales in New York, up to $1,000 for each package.

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal first introduced the bill in the 2015–16 session and carried it for three sessions. Sen. Roxanne Persaud sponsored it in the Senate. Environmental and women's health groups, including WE ACT for Environmental Justice, Clean and Healthy New York, the JustGreen Partnership, and Women's Voices for the Earth, pushed for it. The Senate passed the final version 51–11, and the Assembly passed it 146–0, according to WE ACT. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Oct. 11, 2019.
