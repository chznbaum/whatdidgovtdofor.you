"Cramming" is when an outside company slips a charge onto your phone bill for something you never agreed to buy, or got you to agree to by misleading you. Under this New York law, an outside company can't put a charge on your phone bill unless it can prove it clearly told you the terms, got your explicit OK for the service and the price, and gave you a toll-free number and an address for disputes. Charges from your phone company itself, its affiliates, and certain video providers it sells service with aren't covered.

You don't have to apply. You don't owe any charge put on your bill without your consent. The charge is void, and the company has to take it off your bill once you tell it. The law covers local phone companies and cable companies that sell phone service, and the state Public Service Commission enforces it.

Versions of the bill were introduced starting in 2010 and passed the Assembly in 2012 and 2014, but they never got through the Senate. Sen. Kevin Parker took it up in 2019 and got it through the Senate that year and again in February 2020. Assembly Member Jonathan Jacobson carried the Assembly version. The Assembly passed it 142–0 in July 2020. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Oct. 7, 2020, and it took effect in April 2021.
