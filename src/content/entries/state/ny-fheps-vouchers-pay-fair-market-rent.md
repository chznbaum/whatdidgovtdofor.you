FHEPS is a rent supplement for New York City households with a child or pregnant person that qualify for cash assistance. It serves families facing eviction, families that have been evicted, and families that lost housing because of domestic violence or health and safety problems. Before this law, the maximum rent for a home rented with FHEPS was 85% of HUD's fair market rent. The law raised that cap to 100%. A family may still owe its required share of the rent, but the higher cap opens more apartments to FHEPS and can help families stay out of shelter.

The law did not create a separate application. Families seeking FHEPS to stay in their home can apply through ACCESS HRA or an HRA Benefits Access Center. Families that need to move can contact a Homebase provider. The law also requires the state to reimburse the city for FHEPS payments under state social-services funding rules.

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal and Sen. Brian Kavanagh sponsored the bill. Sen. Roxanne Persaud, the Senate Social Services Committee chair and a co-sponsor, was also credited with leadership and strong support. More than 100 organizations in the city's Eviction Prevention Roundtable pushed for the change, and landlord groups supported it. The Senate passed it 45–18 in June 2021, with every Democrat and two Republicans voting yes. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 10, 2021, and it took effect that day. Rosenthal estimated that 2,300 more families could use FHEPS.
