An appraisal estimates what a home is worth. That number can affect how much a buyer can borrow, whether a sale closes, and whether an owner can refinance. New York law now says an appraiser can't base that value on the race, disability, sexual orientation, family status, source of income or other protected traits of the people who own or live in the home, or of their neighbors. Appraisers also can't discriminate in who they agree to serve.

The Human Rights Law rule covers any person or entity whose business holds itself out as doing residential appraisals, licensed or not. It covers oral communications and written material used to support the appraisal. You don't have to apply for anything. An appraisal based on a protected trait is unlawful discrimination under the state Human Rights Law. The Department of State can now fine licensed appraisers up to $2,000, on top of suspending or revoking a license. Half of the money collected from those fines goes to a state fund that pays for fair housing testing, outreach and complaints.

Gov. Kathy Hochul first proposed the policy in her FY 2026 Executive Budget. Assembly Member George Alvarez and Sen. Brian Kavanagh then sponsored standalone bills. It passed the Assembly 146–2 and the Senate 60–0, with support from both parties. Hochul signed it on October 16, 2025, and it took effect that day.
