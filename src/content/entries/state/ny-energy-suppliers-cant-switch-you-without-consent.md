In New York, a company that sells gas or electricity can't move you to a new supplier unless it follows the Public Service Commission's rules for getting and verifying your consent. Switching people without permission is called "slamming." The law covers utilities, energy service companies (ESCOs), marketers, brokers, and the salespeople who work for them. When an agent breaks the rule, the law treats it as the supplier's own act.

You don't have to apply for this protection. If you're switched without agreeing, the commission can hold a hearing and fine the supplier up to $1,000 per violation for each meter, and the state can also take the supplier to court. Fines go into the state's general fund.

Sen. Liz Krueger had introduced the bill since at least 2015. Assembly Member Jeffrey Dinowitz carried it in the Assembly. Krueger's office said ESCO "consultants" sometimes posed as utility workers to get signatures on pricier contracts, and the state found ESCO customers had been overcharged by hundreds of millions of dollars. The bill passed the Assembly 137–0 and the Senate 62–0 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on November 25, 2019, and it took effect in March 2020.
