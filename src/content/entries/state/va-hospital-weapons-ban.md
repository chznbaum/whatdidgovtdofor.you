Virginia hospitals that offer mental health or developmental services, emergency departments included, are now weapon-free zones by state law. Knowingly carrying a gun, a knife with a blade longer than 3½ inches, a stun gun, explosives or another dangerous weapon into one of these buildings is a Class 1 misdemeanor, punishable by up to 12 months in jail, a $2,500 fine, or both. Hospitals must post the rule at every public entrance. Police and armed security on duty are exempt, and so are people the hospital authorizes in writing, such as a cook who needs a knife for work.

Many hospitals already had their own weapons bans, and the law lets those bans be enforced. Sentara Health, which led hospital-system advocacy for the legislation for three years, says its screeners stopped more than 15,660 weapons at its hospital doors starting in October 2022, including 537 handguns and nearly 9,000 knives.

Del. Phil Hernandez and Sen. Angelia Williams Graves, both Norfolk Democrats, carried the matching bills. Earlier versions were vetoed in past years. HB 229 passed the House 63–34 and the Senate 21–19; SB 173 passed the Senate 21–19 and the House 63–35. Gov. Abigail Spanberger approved both bills on May 14, 2026, and the law took effect July 1.
