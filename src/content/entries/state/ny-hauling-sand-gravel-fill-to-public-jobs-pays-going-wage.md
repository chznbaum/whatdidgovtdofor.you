Workers who build New York's public projects already had to be paid the local prevailing wage, a rate set to match union pay in the area. Truck drivers who brought in the materials weren't clearly covered. This law covers aggregate-hauling work. For covered public contracts, time spent delivering sand, gravel, stone, crushed stone, dirt, soil, millings or fill at a public worksite earns the prevailing wage. The rule also covers hauls between the worksite and a dedicated central stockpile, return hauls on that route, and loading or unloading at either location. Other workers who help load or unload there are covered too.

You don't have to apply. The pay requirement is written into public works contracts, and the state Department of Labor enforces it. For deliveries from a vendor such as a quarry or plant, work and travel within 50 miles of the worksite are covered. An empty return trip is covered within that radius. A supplier's own driver who makes only one delivery to a site that day is exempt.

Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assemblymember Harry Bronson carried the bill. Their memo called hauling an essential part of the job site. It passed the Senate 48–15 in June 2021, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 31, 2021. Mayer and Bronson also sponsored the follow-up law, signed in February 2022, that changed “project” to “worksite.” The Labor Department adopted implementing rules in May 2023. It began enforcing most provisions for projects solicited on or after November 15, 2023, and the 50-mile provision for projects solicited on or after July 1, 2024. A lawsuit challenging the 50-mile rule was dismissed for lack of jurisdiction, and an appeals court affirmed that dismissal.
