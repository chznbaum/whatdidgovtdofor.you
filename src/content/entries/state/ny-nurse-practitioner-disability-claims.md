When an illness or injury that didn't happen on the job keeps you from working, New York's disability benefits program can replace part of your pay. To claim it, you need a provider to sign a statement saying you can't work. The current statute lists physicians, podiatrists, chiropractors, dentists, psychologists and certified nurse-midwives, but not nurse practitioners. Starting Jan. 19, 2027, your nurse practitioner can sign it too.

That matters if a nurse practitioner is the person you actually see. The bill's sponsor says about a quarter of New Yorkers get their primary care from one. Under current law, those patients can need a second provider just to fill out the form. The new law also lets nurse practitioners do the exams an employer or insurer can request. It also covers medical statements for disability-related hunting accommodations. You don't have to apply for anything new. Your nurse practitioner can complete the form you already file.

Assembly Member Amy Paulin and Sen. Michelle Hinchey sponsored the bill. It passed the Assembly 138–2 and the Senate 60–1 on June 3, 2026, with support from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 20, 2026.
