If you sign a New York car lease mainly for personal, family or household use, the lease can't charge you a "turn-in fee" when you return the car at the end of the term if the fee is only for paperwork, handling or clerical work. The rule is part of the state law on what car leases may not contain. It covers leases signed on or after Jan. 1, 2025. There is no application. Charges for real damage or extra miles are a separate matter.

The fee was rarely mentioned when people signed a lease. Assemblymember Michaelle Solages, the bill's sponsor, said dealers used it to hold onto customers: they would offer to waive it only if the customer signed a new lease with them. That made it harder to shop around for a better deal.

Solages carried the bill in the Assembly, which passed it 100–47 in April 2024. Sen. James Sanders Jr. sponsored the Senate version, and the Senate passed it 41–20 that June, with Democrats voting yes and Republicans voting no. The idea wasn't new: Republican Sen. Kenneth LaValle had proposed the same ban in 2015, but his bill never got a floor vote. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the law on Nov. 25, 2024, along with four other consumer bills.
