Many students at New Jersey's community colleges work full time or raise kids while they take classes, and plenty of them qualify for help they never apply for. This law gives each county welfare agency (the county board of social services) a new duty: name a staff member to work with its community college. That person makes sure students' applications and paperwork are complete, and finds ways to make applying faster for enrolled students.

Each county college must work with that staff member to help students directly. The state Department of Human Services must help college staff assist students with applications for SNAP food aid, TANF cash aid, child care subsidies and other programs. The New Jersey Council of County Colleges must work with each college to find where low-income students' basic needs go unmet. Colleges must also work with local workforce boards to reach unemployed and underemployed residents who could gain from enrolling. Students don't sign up for anything new. They can ask at their college for help applying for benefits. The law doesn't come with new money, and it took effect right away.

Sens. Andrew Zwicker and Joe Cryan sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assembly members Heather Simmons, Margie Donlon and Lisa Swain carried it in the Assembly. It passed the Senate 37-0 in December 2025 and the Assembly in January 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, 2026, his last day in office.
