In summer 2025, New York funded entry-level jobs and limited career-exploration activities for young people with low incomes. The budget appropriated $56.5 million in federal TANF funds, and the state expected the program to employ about 21,000 young people. Participants could work at parks, summer camps, child care programs, cultural centers, schools and community groups while building job skills and exploring possible careers.

Participants had to be 14 to 20 and meet the program's TANF eligibility rules. They could qualify by receiving Family Assistance, by having moved from Family Assistance to Safety Net Assistance after reaching the 60-month TANF limit, or through household income below 200% of the federal poverty level. For the income route, the limit was $53,300 for a family of three. Young people applied through locally run programs. The state allocated funding to all 57 counties and New York City, which received about $29.3 million.

The money came from the state's 2025–26 budget. The final appropriation was $1.5 million more than the year before to address the rising minimum wage. Sen. Roxanne Persaud, who chaired the Senate Social Services Committee, listed the funding among her committee's budget wins. The Legislature passed the budget bill on May 8, 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on May 20.
