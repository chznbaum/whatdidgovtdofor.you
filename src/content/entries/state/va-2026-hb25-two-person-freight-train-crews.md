Since July 1, 2026, a railroad in Virginia can't move freight unless at least two qualified people are on the train, locomotive or light engine. The rule doesn't cover hostler or utility service. The law also requires the State Corporation Commission's annual report to count suspected violations of the federal crew-size rule that state inspectors investigated and sent to the Federal Railroad Administration.

Rail workers don't need to do anything to be covered, because the rule applies to the railroads. A federal rule from 2024 also requires two-person crews on most trains, but it lets railroads ask for approval to run with one person. Virginia's law has no such exception.

Del. Bonita Anthony carried HB 25 and Sen. Angelia Williams Graves carried an identical Senate bill. Ronnie Hobbs led the push for the rail workers' union SMART-TD. The General Assembly passed the same idea in 2024, but Gov. Glenn Youngkin vetoed it. This time HB 25 passed the House 65–31 and the Senate 21–19, and the House then agreed to the Senate's changes 66–33. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed both bills on April 13, 2026.

The Association of American Railroads has sued in federal court to stop the law. It argues that federal rail law overrides Virginia's rule.
