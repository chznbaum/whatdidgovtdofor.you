In New York, you usually can't collect unemployment benefits if you quit a job without good cause. The law already made exceptions for "compelling family reasons," like domestic violence or caring for a seriously ill family member. This law added a new one: needing to care for your own child.

To qualify, you must make reasonable efforts to find other child care. You generally must also try to preserve your job, such as by asking for leave, different hours, or another accommodation, unless doing so would be futile. If those efforts fail and you have to leave work to care for your child, the child care reason is good cause for quitting. You apply the usual way, and the other rules for receiving unemployment still apply.

Versions of the bill were introduced in Albany starting in the 2013–2014 session, but they stalled for years. In 2021, Sen. Robert Jackson sponsored it in the Senate and Assembly Member Jen Lunsford carried it in the Assembly. It passed the Senate 43–20 on March 9, 2021, and the Assembly 103–44 two weeks later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 22, 2021, and it took effect right away.
