New Jersey law now bars data brokers, data collectors and other covered controllers from selling or licensing consumers' most sensitive personal data. That includes health conditions and treatment, financial account logins, race, religion, sex life or sexual orientation, transgender or non-binary status, citizenship or immigration status, genetic and biometric data, data about known children, and location data precise to within 1,750 feet. The protection applies automatically to New Jersey residents acting in personal or household contexts. Public information, employment data and several federally regulated categories are exempt.

Data brokers, and businesses that sell customer data to them, must register annually. The state will publish a registry with relevant opt-out information. Fees range from $5,000 to $1.5 million based on how many New Jersey consumers' records a company sells or licenses, but the first registration period is scheduled for April through June 2027.

Sen. John McKeon prefiled S2316 in January to register data brokers and restrict health-record sales. Sen. Raj Mukherji introduced S4109 in May to ban sensitive-data sales. A Senate committee merged the bills; Assemblyman William Moen carried the Assembly version, and Sen. Britnee Timberlake cosponsored the combined legislation. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it June 30.

On July 10, a senior Sherrill administration official told New Jersey Globe that the state would not enforce the law until lawmakers fixed defects. The public consumer-affairs notice explicitly delayed registration and fees and promised more guidance, but did not itself suspend the sales ban.
