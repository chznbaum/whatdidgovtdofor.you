Since Sept. 4, 2022, anyone buying or taking ownership of a semiautomatic rifle in New York must first get a license. Most applicants must be 21. State law waives the age restriction for applicants honorably discharged from the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force or Coast Guard, or the New York National Guard. The process uses the same licensing system New York used for pistols and revolvers. Buying a rifle without a license is a crime, and selling one to someone who lacks a license is a felony. Licenses must be recertified every five years. People who already owned a rifle before the law took effect don't need the new license to keep it.

Lawmakers acted after two mass shootings in May 2022: one at a Tops supermarket in Buffalo, the other at a school in Uvalde, Texas. Both gunmen were 18.

Sen. Kevin Thomas sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Chantel Jackson sponsored it in the Assembly. Both chambers passed it on June 2, 2022. The Senate vote was 43–20, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican voting no. The Assembly vote was 102–47. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it four days later as part of a package of gun bills. Federal trial courts rejected challenges to the rifle-license requirement in December 2025 and March 2026.
