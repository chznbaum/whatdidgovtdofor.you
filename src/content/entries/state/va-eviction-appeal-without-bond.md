If you lose an eviction case in a Virginia general district court, you can appeal to circuit court and get a new hearing there. To do that, you usually have to post an appeal bond, which is often set around the back rent the judge says you owe. In most kinds of civil cases, Virginia already let people who can't afford it skip the bond. Eviction cases were an exception, except for some public housing tenants. That meant the tenants least able to pay were the ones shut out of an appeal.

This law closes that gap. A tenant the court finds indigent, under the same standard Virginia uses to decide who gets a court-appointed lawyer, doesn't have to post an appeal bond. You still have to file within 10 days of the judgment. You also still have to keep paying your ongoing rent to your landlord while the appeal is pending. A sheriff can't evict you during the 10-day appeal window.

Del. Patrick Hope carried the bill, with Del. Rae Cousins as co-patron, and the Virginia Poverty Law Center pushed for it. It passed the House 62–35 and the Senate 21–17. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
