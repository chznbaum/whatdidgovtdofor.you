When someone is losing a lot of blood, minutes matter. Since 2021, New York has let air ambulances carry blood and give transfusions on the way to the hospital. Ground ambulances could only continue a transfusion for a patient being moved between hospitals, and they couldn't carry their own blood. This law gives road ambulances and paramedic response services the same power helicopters have.

You don't have to do anything to benefit. Each ambulance service decides whether to offer transfusions. The law requires a service to tell the state at least 60 days before starting and comply with applicable EMS requirements. Statewide regulations covering applications, training and blood storage were still proposed as of Nov. 25, 2026. The first ground program started on Sept. 14, 2026, near Rochester. CHS Mobile Integrated Healthcare runs it with University of Rochester Medicine, and blood comes from Strong Memorial Hospital's blood bank. CHS expects to give prehospital transfusions to about 40 patients a year, mostly for injuries from crashes, shootings, stabbings, and work or farm accidents. The rest would be for heavy bleeding after childbirth or bleeding in the gut.

Sen. Michelle Hinchey and Assemblymember Carrie Woerner sponsored the bill. Nobody voted against it in either chamber: it passed the Assembly 147–0 and the Senate 61–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 17, 2024.
