Leaving an abuser often means facing the rent or mortgage alone. Before this law, a Virginia judge could already let a survivor stay in the home and stop the abuser from shutting off necessary utilities. Now Virginia law also says plainly that when a judge issues a permanent protective order in a family abuse case, the judge can order the abuser to pay all or part of the rent or mortgage on that home. The law also clarifies that a judge may order the abuser to restore and maintain utilities there, or to provide and maintain suitable alternative housing for the survivor and, where appropriate, pay utility deposits for that housing.

The money isn't automatic. A survivor can ask the judge for it as part of the protective order case. Many people seek these orders without a lawyer, and advocates said spelling out this relief in the law helps survivors know they can ask for it.

Del. Lindsey Dougherty introduced the bill in her first session in the House of Delegates, and the Virginia Poverty Law Center pushed for it. It passed the House 87–10 in February. The Senate passed an amended version 38–2, and the House approved that version 92–6, with lawmakers from both parties voting yes both times. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026, and it took effect July 1, 2026.
