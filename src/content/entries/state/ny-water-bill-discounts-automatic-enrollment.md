Some New Yorkers get water from private companies regulated by the state. Veolia, for example, offers a low-income discount worth up to 25% of a bill. But customers must find the program and apply. This law requires the state to identify eligible customers instead. The matching is not fully running: on Oct. 1, 2026, Liberty said it had no automatic process and planned its first match by year-end; Veolia still told customers to apply and reapply annually as of Nov. 13.

Under the law, OTDA will compare its list of households on public assistance or HEAP with a participating water company's customer list. It must provide matches at least twice a year. If the company confirms that a matched customer qualifies for an available program, it has 60 days to enroll that customer and must send written notice. Once matching begins, confirmed customers should not need a separate company application. OTDA may still require written consent before using some assistance-program data. The data can be used only to check eligibility and send enrollment notices.

Sen. Kevin Parker and Assemblymember Michaelle Solages sponsored the 2023 law that created automatic matching for gas and electric discounts. In 2024 they sponsored the extension to water companies, which Gov. Kathy Hochul signed that December. Their follow-up amendment gave the state 18 months to implement water matching. It passed the Senate 61–0, and Hochul signed it on February 14, 2025. The sponsor memo reported no state fiscal cost. Veolia's rate plan uses $1.5 million in shareholder money first, then spreads remaining discount costs through customer rates.
