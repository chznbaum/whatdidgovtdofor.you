If you have an intellectual, developmental, cognitive or psychosocial disability, New York law lets you make a supported decision-making agreement. You name trusted people to help gather information, weigh options and carry out choices about money, health care, relationships or other parts of life. They cannot decide for you or pressure you. It is a less restrictive option than guardianship, where a court gives someone else decision-making power.

Every adult is presumed able to make an agreement. A diagnosis or unusual way of communicating is not proof otherwise. Signing one, or refusing to, cannot be used to deny benefits or as evidence you lack capacity. You can change or cancel it at any time. Decisions made under a formal agreement created through authorized facilitation must be honored by doctors, banks, schools and others unless they have substantial cause for concern, such as abuse, coercion or imminent harm. Making an agreement is not limited to adults eligible for OPWDD services, though free facilitation through AIM currently is.

OPWDD asked for the law after a pilot program that began in 2016. Sen. John Mannion and Assembly Member Jo Anne Simon carried it. The Senate passed it unanimously three times, and the final Assembly vote was 145–3. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on July 26, 2022. It took effect on November 18, 2025, 90 days after OPWDD rules appeared in the State Register. In March 2026, OPWDD and AIM launched the statewide service; eligible New Yorkers can contact AIM to start.
