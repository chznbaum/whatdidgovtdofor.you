For most traffic tickets, New York stopped suspending driver's licenses over unpaid fines in 2021. But if an unanswered ticket led to a default conviction, drivers could still have to pay the resulting fine in full to end the failure-to-answer suspension. This follow-up fixed that. Entering an installment plan for that fine now ends the suspension just as paying it off would.

Once an unanswered ticket has produced a default conviction and fine, courts and the DMV's Traffic Violations Bureau must offer you a plan. The plan costs nothing extra. Each monthly payment can be at most 2% of your monthly net income, or $25 if that is more. If you miss a payment, your license cannot be suspended for it. A court can call you in to review your finances, but no more than once a year. To start a plan, contact the court that handled a ticket outside New York City, or apply through the DMV for a New York City Traffic Violations Bureau ticket.

State Sen. Timothy Kennedy and Assembly Member Pamela Hunter, who led the original license suspension reform, sponsored this follow-up. The Senate passed it 46–17, with every Democrat and three Republicans voting yes. The Assembly passed it 114–35. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 21, 2021.
