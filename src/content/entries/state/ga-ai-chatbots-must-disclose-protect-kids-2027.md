Starting July 1, 2027, operators of AI companion chatbots offered to users in Georgia—systems designed to sustain human-like relationships—must disclose that users are interacting with AI at the start of each session and at least every three hours. For users known or reasonably believed to be minors, or chatbots aimed at minors, that reminder must appear every hour. Operators must take reasonable measures to prevent sexual or romantic interactions with minors and manipulative tactics such as encouraging isolation. Known minor accounts must also offer tools to manage screen time, privacy, notifications, safety settings, and relationship features.

Every operator must maintain a protocol to detect and address suicide, self-harm, and other severe crises, including referrals to 988 or similar services. Unless lawfully authorized, operators cannot knowingly program a chatbot to claim that it is licensed or authorized to provide professional mental health care. These duties fall on operators, and Georgia’s attorney general can sue over violations.

Senate Majority Leader Jason Anavitarte authored the bill; seven other senators co-sponsored it, and Rep. Todd Jones sponsored it in the House. The Senate first passed it 54–0. After the House passed a substitute 166–0, the Senate approved that final version 44–1, with support from both parties. The Computer & Communications Industry Association asked Gov. Brian Kemp to veto it, but he signed it on May 11, 2026.
