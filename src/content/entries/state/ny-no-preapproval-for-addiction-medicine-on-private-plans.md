If you have a New York-regulated health plan that covers prescriptions and you're starting or continuing treatment for addiction, your insurer can't make you wait for its approval before you get buprenorphine, methadone or long-acting injectable naltrexone. That holds for a first prescription and for refills, whether the medicine is meant to help you through withdrawal or to keep you in recovery. Large group plans must also cover these drugs. You don't have to apply for anything. Your plan can still charge copays, coinsurance or a deductible, but they must be consistent with what it charges for other benefits.

Prior authorization means a doctor has to get an insurer's sign-off before a prescription is filled. That can take days, and for someone ready to start treatment, the wait can be dangerous. Sen. Pete Harckham's sponsor memo said the goal was to give patients the same reasonable access to these medicines no matter which plan they're on.

Assemblymember Dan Quart sponsored the bill and Harckham carried it in the Senate. Republican Assemblymember Chris Tague joined as a multi-sponsor. The Assembly passed it 139–2 in April 2019, and the Senate passed it 62–0 that June. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Dec. 31, 2019, and it took effect 60 days later. A separate 2021 law did the same for people on Medicaid.
