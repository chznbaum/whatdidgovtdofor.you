New York law bans disposing of firefighting foam that contains PFAS by burning it in Cohoes. PFAS are "forever chemicals" that have been linked to cancer and other illnesses. The law is written to cover any city with a state-designated environmental justice area and 16,000 to 17,000 residents, a description that fits only Cohoes. Neighbors don't have to do anything: the ban covers every form of burning, from incinerator kilns to boilers.

The law grew out of what happened at Norlite, a hazardous waste incinerator in Cohoes that sits near public housing. Under Pentagon contracts that were later canceled, the plant burned more than 2 million pounds of the foam, some of it shipped in from fire departments along the East Coast. Scientists weren't sure burning destroys the chemicals. The state ordered Norlite to stop in 2019, and the city then passed a one-year moratorium. The new law made the stop permanent.

Sen. Neil Breslin and Assemblymember John McDonald, a former Cohoes mayor, sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 62–0 on June 9, 2020, with every Democrat and Republican voting yes, and passed the Assembly the same day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on November 23, 2020. The ban covers only Cohoes, and advocates say the foam could still be burned at other kinds of incinerators elsewhere in the state.
