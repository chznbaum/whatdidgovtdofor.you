In New York, you can get birth control pills, a vaginal ring, or a patch from a participating pharmacist without first getting your own prescription from a doctor. You fill out a health screening form. The pharmacist reviews it, helps you select a method, and can dispense up to a year's supply, though your insurance may limit how many months are filled at once. You repeat the screening at least every 12 months. Unless you opt out, the pharmacist notifies your regular health care practitioner within 72 hours. A pharmacist can refuse if your answers point to a health risk.

You don't apply for anything. Just ask at a pharmacy that offers the service, but not every pharmacy does. New York's NYRx Medicaid program covers birth control dispensed this way. Shots aren't included.

Assemblymember Amy Paulin carried versions of the measure beginning in 2015 and sponsored the Assembly bill; Sen. Toby Ann Stavisky sponsored it in the Senate. The Assembly passed it 109–37, and the Senate passed it 48–15 with votes from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on May 2, 2023. The law allows trained pharmacists to dispense under a non-patient-specific order from a doctor, nurse practitioner or the state health commissioner. Health Commissioner James McDonald issued the statewide order on March 19, 2024, letting participating pharmacists use it across New York.
