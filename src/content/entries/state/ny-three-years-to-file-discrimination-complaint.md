If you face discrimination in New York, for example at work, when renting a home, or in a store, you can ask the state Division of Human Rights to investigate. For an unlawful discriminatory practice that happened on or after February 15, 2024, you have three years from the most recent incident to file. Before this law, you had one year for most claims. Workplace sexual harassment already had three years after a 2019 change.

Reporting is free, and you do not need a lawyer. You can start by calling the Division at (844) 697-3471 or using its online reporting form. A report is only the first step: the Division reviews it and may ask for more information. Under current rules, the complaint counts as filed only after it is verified, submitted, and received by the Division. The extra time matters because people can need time to process what happened before they come forward.

State Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal sponsored the bill, and versions of it were introduced in every session from 2017 on. Assembly Member Nily Rozic carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 63–0 in March 2023, and the Assembly passed it 102–47 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 17, 2023. It covers only discrimination that happened on or after the February 2024 start date. Claims about earlier incidents still follow the old deadlines.
