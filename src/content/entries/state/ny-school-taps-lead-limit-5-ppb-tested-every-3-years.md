New York public schools, charter schools and BOCES must test every outlet used—or that could be used—for drinking or cooking at least once every three years. A narrow waiver is available only after compliant testing, any needed repairs and results below 5 ppb. If a tap tests over 5 ppb, it must be taken out of drinking and cooking use until it is fixed and retests at or below 5 ppb. Meanwhile, the school must give occupants safe water for free.

Families don't need to apply. The law ended the blanket exemption for buildings labeled "lead-free." Schools must tell staff and parents in writing within 10 business days of a result over 5 ppb, and post lab reports and remediation plans online. State funds can reimburse eligible repair costs. The law took effect Dec. 22, 2022. The first testing cycle ran through Dec. 31, 2025; current-cycle sampling is due within three years of each school's prior test and no later than Oct. 31, 2028.

New York first required school-water testing in 2016. In those results, about 82% of public schools had at least one tap over the old 15 ppb limit. Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assemblymember Richard Gottfried sponsored the update. NRDC says it and partner groups developed changes included in the bill. It passed both houses without opposition in June 2021, including a 63–0 Senate vote, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that Dec. 22. A 2022 chapter amendment carried by Rivera and Gottfried restored the narrow waiver before the law took effect.
