New York has its own Voting Rights Act, modeled on the federal one. It bans local election methods that wipe out the voting power of a racial, ethnic, or language-minority group, like at-large races that let a majority win every seat. It also bans voter suppression, intimidation, deception, and obstruction. If your town, county, or school district breaks these rules, voters can take it to state court.

Since September 22, 2024, places with a history of civil or voting rights violations must get approval from the state attorney general or a court before they change certain election rules, such as moving polling places or redrawing districts. Since June 20, 2025, places with enough voters who have trouble with English must offer more help in other languages than federal law requires. You don't have to apply. The protections come with your right to vote, and you can file a complaint with the attorney general's office.

Sen. Zellnor Myrie and Assembly Member Latrice Walker, who chaired the Assembly's election committee, carried the bill. It passed the Senate 43–20, with every Democrat voting yes and every Republican voting no, and it passed the Assembly 106–43. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 20, 2022. The Town of Newburgh tried to strike the law down, but in November 2025 the state's highest court left it fully in force.
