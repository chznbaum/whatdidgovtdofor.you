If you lose your job in New York and qualify for unemployment, you can now get up to $869 a week, up from $504. The higher rate started October 6, 2025, and showed up in payments the week of October 13. You don't have to apply for the raise: the Department of Labor works out your weekly amount from your past earnings. Workers who already got less than the old cap may also get more.

The benefit had been stuck at $504 since 2019. During the pandemic, New York borrowed from the federal government to keep unemployment checks going, and state law freezes the top benefit while that debt is unpaid. Before this budget, businesses had been paying the debt down slowly through higher taxes.

Gov. Kathy Hochul's January 2025 budget plan didn't deal with the debt. In March, the Assembly proposed paying it off, and Assembly Labor Committee Chair Harry Bronson pushed for the plan. Unions and business groups both backed it. Hochul agreed in the final deal and signed the budget in May 2025. The state used its reserves, not service cuts, to pay off the federal loan of nearly $7 billion, and it raised the amount of each worker's pay that employers are taxed on starting in 2026 to rebuild the fund. The same budget also cut the wait before striking workers can collect unemployment from three weeks to two.
