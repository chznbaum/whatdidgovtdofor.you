Virginia state agencies, cities, and counties hold a lot of information about you, from tax records to benefit applications. Since July 1, 2026, for personal-information systems covered by the Act, agencies generally may share data only in six situations: to comply with law, administer a government program, answer legal process, fulfill certain contracts, act with your clear consent, or accomplish a proper agency purpose. That last category includes improving services, preventing fraud, research, and data analysis. The Act exempts some systems, including those maintained by courts, criminal justice databases, and certain law-enforcement investigations. For covered systems, agencies and political subdivisions also cannot sell personal information.

The law also widens what counts as personal information. It now expressly includes immigration status, alien registration and tax identification numbers, national origin, voting history, faceprints and other biometric data, and photos, video, and audio. You do not need to apply; the rules attach to covered systems. If an official knowingly and willfully breaks the sharing rules, a court can impose a $500 to $2,500 penalty, rising to $2,500 to $10,000 for a repeat violation.

Del. Kathy Tran carried the bill, with Dels. Alfonso Lopez, Cia Price, and Irene Shin as chief co-patrons. Immigrant advocates backed it as part of a 2026 package intended to limit how state data can reach federal immigration enforcement. It passed the Senate 21–19 on a party-line vote and the House 67–32, with four Republicans voting yes. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
