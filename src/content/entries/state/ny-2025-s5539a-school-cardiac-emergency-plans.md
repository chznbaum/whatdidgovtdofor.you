If someone's heart stops at a New York public school, staff now have to follow a written plan. Every district safety plan must include a cardiac emergency response plan. It says who responds and what they do when someone goes into sudden cardiac arrest at school, on any school-run site, or at a school event such as a game. For athletic practices and events on school grounds, procedures must be specific to the venue. Schools must also consider how the plan fits with local emergency medical services.

You don't need to do anything to get this protection. Schools must train their staff on the emergency plan, including cardiac arrest, every year. They must also keep their defibrillators (AEDs) clearly marked, easy to get to, and maintained to American Heart Association or similar standards. Changes to building-level response plans took effect Sept. 1, 2025. The district-wide plan, staff training and AED rules took effect Jan. 20, 2026.

The law is named for Desha Sanders, the 12-year-old cousin of state Sen. April Baskin. Desha collapsed while playing basketball in her middle school gym. Her school had an AED, but no one could find the keys to open it because there was no plan. Baskin sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assemblymember Michaelle Solages carried it in the Assembly. The American Heart Association helped craft and advocate for the bill. Both chambers passed it without a single no vote on June 11, 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on July 24, 2025.
