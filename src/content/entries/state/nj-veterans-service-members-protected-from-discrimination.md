If you're in the military or you're a veteran, New Jersey's main civil rights law now explicitly protects you. Employers, landlords, lenders, real estate agents, unions and businesses open to the public can't treat you worse because you serve or served. That covers anyone on active service in any branch of the U.S. Armed Forces or the National Guard, and anyone discharged or released from it. The old law only protected people who might be called up to serve.

The law also protects your job when you come back, but only when you qualify for reemployment under federal USERRA rules. An employer that denies reemployment in that situation is now also breaking state law. The discrimination protection itself does not require an application; reemployment depends on meeting federal notice and return-to-work requirements. If you're treated unfairly, you can file a complaint with the state Division on Civil Rights or go to court under the Law Against Discrimination.

The same law makes the statute's wording gender-neutral. It also states that the law bans only unlawful discrimination, and it spells out when a practice that hurts a protected group crosses that line.

Sens. Gordon Johnson and Nilsa Cruz-Perez introduced the bill in October 2024. Assembly members Andrea Katz, Luanne Peterpaul and Cleopatra Tucker carried it in the Assembly. It passed the Senate 37-0 in December 2025 and the Assembly 71-0 in January 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on his last morning in office, Jan. 20, 2026, and it took effect that day.
