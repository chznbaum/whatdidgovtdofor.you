Before 2020, every advanced practice nurse in New Jersey needed a joint protocol with a collaborating physician to prescribe medicine or order devices. The state waived that rule during the COVID-19 emergency. The waiver was due to end Feb. 16, 2026, but Gov. Mikie Sherrill extended it through April 2. This law made independent practice permanent for qualifying nurses when the waiver ended. If you see one for primary care or mental health care, they can prescribe and order your treatment without maintaining a physician agreement.

To qualify, a nurse must have more than 5,000 hours of licensed, active advanced nursing practice in an eligible population focus and must provide primary or behavioral health care. General obstetrics and elective cosmetic work are excluded. Independent nurses must carry malpractice coverage and complete added pharmacology training. A transition rule temporarily covered nurses already practicing without a protocol under the emergency waiver: those expected to reach 5,000 hours within 12 months could continue, while the others had six months before they had to establish a protocol. Patients do not need to apply.

Sens. Joseph Vitale and Troy Singleton and Assemblymembers William Spearman, Katie Brennan and Annette Quijano sponsored the bill. The final votes on March 23 included support from both parties: 32–5 in the Senate and 58–9 in the Assembly, with one Assembly abstention recorded. Sherrill signed it March 30.
