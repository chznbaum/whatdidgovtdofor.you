Federal law already requires allergen labels on food packaged by retail and food-service establishments before sale. New York’s sponsors described a federal gap, but FDA guidance says this food was already covered. The state law creates a separate New York requirement. Since November 12, 2026, inspectors can cite missing or incomplete labels on covered self-service food as critical violations.

The law covers sandwiches, salads, cookies, muffins and similar food that a deli, bakery, cafeteria, food truck or store prepares and packages before an order and puts out for customer self-service. The label must name any of nine major allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, sesame and soybeans. It does not cover food sold unwrapped, packaged after an order, or packaged ahead only for convenience but kept behind the counter until an employee adds it to an order. You do not need to apply; check the label.

Assemblymember Jen Lunsford carried the bill after her son had an anaphylactic reaction to a bakery cookie in 2020. Sen. Pete Harckham sponsored it in the Senate. Student advocate Jared Saiontz asked Harckham to pursue the issue; a meeting with Saiontz and other advocates helped advance the bill. A 2023 version died in committee, and a 2024 version passed the Assembly but not the Senate. This version passed the Assembly 138–7 and the Senate 47–12 in June 2025. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 12, 2025, with a one-year lead time.
