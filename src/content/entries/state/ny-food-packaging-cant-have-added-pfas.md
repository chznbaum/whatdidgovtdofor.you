PFAS are a family of man-made chemicals that keep grease and water from soaking through paper. They break down so slowly that they're called "forever chemicals," and they can move from packaging into food. They have been linked to some cancers, kidney disease, weaker immune systems, lower fertility and low birth weights. Since Dec. 31, 2022, no one in New York may sell, distribute or offer food packaging with PFAS added on purpose.

The ban covers packaging that touches food and is made mostly of paper, paperboard or other plant fibers: pizza boxes, pastry boxes, sandwich wrappers, soup cups, and retail packs of paper plates, cups and bowls. Glass, metal and plastic aren't covered. You don't need to do anything. Restaurants, stores and suppliers have to follow the law, and the state can fine them $10,000 for a first violation and up to $25,000 after that.

Assembly Member Patricia Fahy introduced the bill in 2019, and Sen. Brad Hoylman carried it in the Senate. Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins made it a priority, and advocates in the JustGreen Partnership organized for it. Hoylman's earlier version passed the Senate in June 2019, but the final bill didn't clear both chambers until July 2020. With support from members of both parties, it passed the Senate 48–12 and the Assembly 118–23. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Dec. 2, 2020.
