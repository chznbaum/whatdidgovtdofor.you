If a job injury leaves you with a lasting impairment, such as a damaged back, a lost finger, or reduced use of a limb, Minnesota workers' compensation pays a permanent partial disability benefit based on your impairment rating. This law raises every dollar amount in that schedule by about 20% for injuries on or after Oct. 1, 2026. At the low end, the multiplier rises from $114,260 to $137,240. For the most severe impairments, it rises from $567,840 to $682,045. You don't apply separately: the higher amounts are used when your claim is paid.

The law also lets psychiatric mental health nurse practitioners diagnose post-traumatic stress disorder for workers' comp, alongside psychiatrists and psychologists. That can make it easier for first responders and other workers to get a diagnosis and coverage. Other changes cut both ways. It specifies that a worker's witness at an employer-requested exam must be unpaid and that each side covers its own physician or witness costs. It updates required retainer agreements to reflect the attorney-fee maximum enacted in 2024. If an employer or insurer starts paying compensation but later decides the disability is not work-related, it now has 90 days instead of 60 to file a denial.

The Minnesota AFL-CIO says it led the push for the bill. The Workers' Compensation Advisory Council, made up of business and labor representatives, recommended the changes. Sen. Jennifer McEwen (D-Duluth) and Rep. Dave Baker (R-Willmar) carried the bill, and Sen. Paul Utke (R) joined as a coauthor. It passed the Senate 65–0 and the House 132–1. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 18, 2026.
