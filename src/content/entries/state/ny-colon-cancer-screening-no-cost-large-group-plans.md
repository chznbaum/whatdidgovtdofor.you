If you get health coverage through a large employer plan regulated by New York State, your plan must cover colon cancer screening the way the American Cancer Society recommends, starting at age 45 for people at average risk. When you use an in-network provider, you pay no deductible, copay, or coinsurance. You need an order from your doctor or another licensed provider, but you don't have to apply.

The law also closes a gap that used to surprise patients. If a take-home stool test comes back positive, the colonoscopy that follows is covered with no cost-sharing as well. Insurers must also remind you at least once a year, by mail unless you agree to another method, which screenings your plan covers.

Assembly Member Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the bill, and it passed the Assembly in 2021 and again in 2022. Sen. James Sanders Jr. sponsored the Senate version, which passed 61–0 in May 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 23, 2022. Her office insisted on limiting it to large-group plans, which the American Cancer Society Cancer Action Network estimated covered roughly 2.3 million people at signing. A follow-up law sponsored by Sanders, with Dinowitz carrying its Assembly companion, was signed in March 2023 and put that limit into the statute.
