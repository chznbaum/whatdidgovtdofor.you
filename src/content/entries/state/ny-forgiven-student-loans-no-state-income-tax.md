If the U.S. Education Department forgives or discharges a federal student loan under a federally authorized program, New York won't charge state income tax on that amount. The law subtracts the forgiven amount from your income on your state return when it was included in your federal adjusted gross income. It applies to tax years from 2023 on. You claim the subtraction when you file your New York taxes.

This matters more now. A federal law excluded most forgiven student debt from federal income from 2021 through 2025, but that exclusion ran out. Starting in 2026, forgiveness under some repayment plans counts as federal taxable income again. Because New York starts from your federal income, it would have taxed that too. This law stops it.

State Sens. Michael Gianaris and Brad Hoylman-Sigal announced the push in 2022 to put the tax treatment into state law and cover future forgiveness programs. Gianaris and Assemblymember Karines Reyes carried the Tax-Free Debt Forgiveness Act, which Gov. Kathy Hochul signed in December 2023. A negotiated follow-up law set the covered tax years, extended the subtraction to forgiveness by the education secretary under any federally authorized program, and limited it to amounts included in federal income. The follow-up passed the Senate 59–1 and was signed on March 22, 2024. The Assembly memo reported no fiscal impact. By preventing a double deduction, the amendment saved the state an estimated $1 million, according to the Division of the Budget.
