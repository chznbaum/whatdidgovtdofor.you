Every New York general hospital must run a staffing committee. At least half its members are frontline nurses and care staff selected under a union agreement or by coworkers. Worker and management sides each have one vote on the annual unit-and-shift plan. Without consensus, the hospital's CEO decides disputed parts. The plans took effect in 2023. The state posts them online, and units post plans and daily staffing.

No one applies. Staff or their union first report violations to the committee; unresolved complaints may go to the health department. Retaliation for concerns raised with the committee or administration is prohibited. After finding a violation, the department requires a corrective plan within 45 days and may fine a hospital for not submitting or implementing one. In 2025 Albany Medical Center agreed to a $375,000 settlement; $125,000 was to be waived if it complied.

Health care unions pushed for safe staffing laws for more than two decades. Richard Gottfried held the first Assembly hearing in 1996 and carried an early version; Aileen Gunther later took over and sponsored the final Assembly bill. Gustavo Rivera sponsored it in the Senate. NYSNA, 1199SEIU and CWA District 1 advocated for it. An earlier version set fixed ratios for every unit; the compromise lets committees set most levels. In 2023 the state adopted a minimum of one nurse for every two intensive or critical care patients. The Assembly passed the bill 144–5 and the Senate 63–0. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 18, 2021.
