Starting July 1, 2027, if your Virginia landlord says you're behind on rent and moves to end your lease, the notice has to show the math. It must list your charges and payments for the past 12 months, or for the whole lease if you've lived there less than a year. It also has to list any late fees, attorney fees, and other charges the landlord says you owe. If your building bills utilities through submeters or splits a shared bill among tenants, the notice must also show what you were charged and credited for utilities. A notice without this list doesn't count.

The law also changes when a landlord can go to court. Today, a landlord needs a proper notice before a judge can order you out. Under the new law, a landlord can't even file an eviction case over a lease violation until you've received a proper notice. Buildings that bill utilities separately must also keep records of how monthly utility fees are figured and what each tenant paid. You can ask to see those records, and the landlord can no longer charge you a fee for them.

You don't need to apply. These protections come with your lease. Del. Michelle Maldonado carried the bill. It passed the House 79–18 and the Senate 21–19, with every Democrat in the Senate voting for it and every Republican against. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
