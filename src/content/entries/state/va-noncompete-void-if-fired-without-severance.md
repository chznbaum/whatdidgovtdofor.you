A noncompete is a contract that can stop you from working for a competitor after you leave a job. Under this law, a Virginia employer that fires you without cause can't enforce your noncompete unless it pays you severance or some other payment. The payment has to be spelled out when you sign the noncompete, so you know up front what you'd get. If you're fired for cause, the noncompete can still apply.

This protection covers workers at every pay level, not only the low-wage workers already shielded by earlier Virginia law. It applies to noncompetes signed, amended or renewed on or after July 1, 2026. Older agreements aren't affected. You don't need to apply for anything. If an employer tries to enforce a banned noncompete, you have two years to sue, and a court can void the agreement and award damages and attorney fees. The state can also fine the employer $10,000 per violation.

Sen. Jeremy McPike carried the bill. The Senate Commerce and Labor Committee merged in a similar bill from Republican Sen. Glen Sturtevant. The Senate passed it 40–0 in February 2026, and the House of Delegates passed it 87–12 on March 4. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
