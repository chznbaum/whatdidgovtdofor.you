At the time, New York generally ended special education services at the close of the school year in which a student turned 21. Some students aged out while schools were closed or teaching remotely during COVID. Students missed services their education plans promised, while evaluations and testing were suspended. This law let districts give those students more time. Students who turned 21 during the 2019–20 or 2020–21 school year could receive services in 2021–22 and 2022–23. They could continue until they finished the services in their individualized education plan (IEP) or turned 23, whichever came first.

To qualify, a student had to be enrolled in the district and receiving special education services under an IEP when they turned 21. The law let districts offer the extra time but didn't require them to, so the decision remained with each district. The bill's sponsors said turning 21 doesn't end a district's duty to make up services a student missed during the pandemic.

Assemblyman Thomas Abinanti and Sen. Pete Harckham carried the bill. Assembly Members Angelo Santabarbara and Melissa Miller co-sponsored it. The Assembly passed it 148–0 on June 9, 2021, and the Senate passed it 63–0 the next day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 29, 2021. It expired on June 30, 2023. A 2022 law gave students who turned 21 in 2021–22 the same option.
