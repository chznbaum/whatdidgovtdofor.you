Georgia has begun funding literacy coaching in every public school with kindergarten through third-grade classes. State grants reimburse districts for the beginning salary and benefits of one school-based literacy coach per eligible school; schools with 200 or fewer students receive half that amount for part-time support. Coaches must spend at least 70% of their day in classrooms working directly with teachers and students.

Families don't need to apply. The fiscal 2027 budget added $70.4 million for school-based coaches beginning in July 2026. The positions are still being filled, and the bill's author expected statewide hiring to take a couple of years. The law also requires district literacy plans and instruction aligned with structured literacy and the science of reading. A state task force will recommend approved curricula and universal reading screeners, including one screener provided free to schools.

Rep. Chris Erwin wrote and carried the bill. House Speaker Jon Burns co-authored it and made it his top priority. Senate Appropriations Chairman Blake Tillery authored the amendment that shaped the final compromise, while Senate Education Chairman Billy Hickman helped develop Georgia's literacy approach. Dayle Burns, a retired educator, championed the proposal. Both chambers adopted the final version unanimously on March 31, 2026, and Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 5.
