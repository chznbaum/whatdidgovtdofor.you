New York's Human Rights Law bans harassment and discrimination at work. In 2019 the state widened it to cover all employers. But people who worked directly for elected officials or judges were still left out. Courts had ruled that the state wasn't the real employer of legislative aides or judges' court attorneys, following a federal rule that leaves out elected officials' personal staff. Some cities and counties argued the same thing about their elected officials.

This law closes that gap. The state now counts as the employer of everyone in its executive, legislative and judicial branches, including elected officials, judges, and their staff. Every city, county, town and village is the employer of the same people in its own government. If a lawmaker or judge unlawfully harasses or discriminates against someone on staff, that worker can file a claim against the government, which can't say they aren't its employee. The law took effect when signed and is deemed in force from Feb. 8, 2020, when the 2019 employer-definition change took effect. You don't need to sign up for anything.

Assembly Member Yuh-Line Niou and Sen. Andrew Gounardes sponsored the bill. The Senate passed an earlier version in 2020 and passed it again in 2021. The Sexual Harassment Working Group, a collective of former state and city legislative staffers, also advocated for the law. The Assembly passed it in February 2022, the Senate followed 59–4, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on March 16, 2022.
