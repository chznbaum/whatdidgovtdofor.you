If you're 65 or older and in EPIC, New York's program that helps pay what your Medicare Part D drug plan leaves you owing, a yearly cost-of-living raise could push your income past the program's limit. This law means that won't cut you off right away. If your income went over only because your Social Security or a public or private pension went up, and the raise was no bigger than inflation that year, you stay eligible for one more calendar year.

You don't have to apply for this. It covers people already enrolled in EPIC. The program is open to New Yorkers 65 and older who aren't on full Medicaid, with incomes up to $75,000 if single or $100,000 if married. Members with lower incomes also get help paying their Part D premiums.

Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assemblymember Robert Carroll sponsored the bill. Carroll first filed a version in the 2017–18 session. It passed the Senate 63–0 and the Assembly 145–0 in 2022, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 28, 2022. The original bill set no time limit on the protection. A negotiated 2023 amendment carried by Carroll and Bailey limited it to one subsequent calendar year; the Assembly passed it 142–0, the Senate passed it 63–0, and Hochul signed it on March 3, 2023. The law took effect April 1, 2023.
