If your health plan is regulated by Virginia and covers prescription drugs, it has to cover at least one drug that reverses an opioid overdose, such as naloxone. Your share of the cost for at least one of these drugs must be on the plan's lowest cost tier, where copays are smallest. The plan also can't make you or your prescriber get advance approval first, or make you try a different drug before it pays.

You don't need to apply. The drug can be dispensed on a prescriber's order or a standing order, which Virginia's health department issues statewide so pharmacies can dispense naloxone without an individual prescription. The rules apply to plans issued or renewed on or after Jan. 1, 2027. They don't cover short-term travel, accident-only, limited or specified-disease policies; Medicare or similar government-plan contracts; or short-term nonrenewable policies lasting no more than six months. In individual and small-group plans, the new rule doesn't apply to any part of the benefit that Virginia's standard benefit package already covers.

The bill came from the Joint Commission on Health Care. Del. Atoosa Reaser carried it in the House, and Sen. Barbara Favola carried an identical Senate bill. No one voted against it in either chamber. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026.
