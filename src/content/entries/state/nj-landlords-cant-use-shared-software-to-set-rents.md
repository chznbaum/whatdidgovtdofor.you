Some landlords set rents using software that collects private data from competing landlords, such as their rents, vacancies, and lease renewal dates, and then recommends what to charge. Because many landlords feed in their data and follow the same advice, rents can climb as if they'd agreed to raise them together. Starting July 1, 2027, New Jersey makes that illegal. Landlords can't pay for these coordinating services, software companies can't provide them, and the conduct counts as a violation of the state's antitrust law. Affordability limits set by government and free public rent estimates are not covered.

Renters don't have to do anything. The attorney general must set up a page on the state's website where anyone can report suspected violations.

Assemblywomen Yvonne Lopez and Katie Brennan carried the bill in the Assembly, with Margie Donlon and Chigozie Onyema. Senate Majority Leader M. Teresa Ruiz and Sen. Brian Stack carried it in the Senate. Lopez's version had also passed the Assembly last session. On June 30, 2026, the Assembly passed it along party lines and the Senate passed it 33–4, with most Republicans voting yes. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on July 20, 2026. The law came after the state's attorney general sued RealPage, a rent-software company, and 10 landlords in 2025.
