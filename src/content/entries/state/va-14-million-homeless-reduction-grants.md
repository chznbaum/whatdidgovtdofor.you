Virginia is putting $14 million into 61 local projects that help people without a home find one and keep it. Of those, 29 are rapid rehousing projects, which move families and individuals from a shelter or the street into a rented home, with short-term rent help and support. Another 19 pay rent and services for people in permanent supportive housing who have been homeless for a long time. The last 13 try new approaches for groups that are often missed, such as homeless youth and older adults.

The grants go to shelters, nonprofits, and local governments across the state, from Northern Virginia and Richmond to Hampton Roads, Roanoke, and the far southwest. Awards run from $30,000 to almost $690,000. People who need help don't apply to the state. They reach the homeless services in their area through its main contact number.

The money comes from the Virginia Housing Trust Fund, which also pays to build and fix up affordable homes. These grants used 16% of the fund's money for the fiscal year. The state Department of Housing and Community Development, led by Director Tamarah Holmes, runs the Homeless Reduction Grant and chose the projects in a competitive round. Gov. Abigail Spanberger announced the awards on March 11, 2026.
