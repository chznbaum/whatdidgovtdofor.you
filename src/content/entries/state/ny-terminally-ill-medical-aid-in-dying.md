If you live in New York, are an adult, and have a terminal illness that doctors expect to end your life within six months, you can ask a doctor for medication to end your life. You have to take the medication yourself, and the choice must be voluntary and informed.

The law has several safeguards. You make an oral request, which is recorded on video or audio, and a written request signed in front of two witnesses. Two doctors must confirm your illness and that you can make your own decisions, and a psychologist or psychiatrist must also evaluate you. Your doctor must explain other options, including hospice and palliative care. After the prescription is written, you wait five days before it can be filled. People who could gain money from your death can't serve as witnesses. Doctors, pharmacists, and other providers can choose not to take part.

Assemblymember Amy Paulin first introduced the bill in 2016. Nine years later, it reached the floor and passed the Assembly 81–67 in April 2025. Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal carried it through the Senate, 35–27, that June. Sen. Gustavo Rivera then sponsored amendments with added safeguards, which Gov. Kathy Hochul had worked out with the Legislature. Hochul signed both on Feb. 6, 2026, and the law took effect Aug. 5. Days before that, a federal judge dismissed a lawsuit from disability rights groups that sought to block it.
