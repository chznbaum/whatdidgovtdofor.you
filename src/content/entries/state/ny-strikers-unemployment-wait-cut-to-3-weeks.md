New York is one of the few states that pays unemployment benefits to workers on strike. Before 2020, strikers had to wait out a seven-week suspension, then serve the one-week waiting period every claimant serves. That added up to eight weeks with no benefits, which made a long strike hard for many families to hold out through. This law cut the suspension to two weeks, so a striker could start collecting after three weeks.

The shorter wait covers strikes whether or not the union authorized them. Workers who are locked out, or whose employer hires permanent replacements, wait only the usual one week. Strikers file for unemployment insurance the same way anyone else does.

State Sen. Tim Kennedy and Assembly Member Sean Ryan sponsored the bill, which passed the Senate in May 2019 and the Assembly that June. It first set the suspension at one week. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Feb. 6, 2020, along with a follow-up bill from the same sponsors that set the suspension at two weeks. In 2025, the state budget Gov. Kathy Hochul signed cut the total wait again, to two weeks. A federal bill introduced in 2025, H.R. 4424, would end strike-related unemployment payments nationwide if Congress passes it.
