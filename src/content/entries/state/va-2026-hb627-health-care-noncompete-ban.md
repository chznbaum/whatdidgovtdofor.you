If you're a nurse, doctor, nurse practitioner, counselor, psychologist, optometrist, or social worker licensed in Virginia, your employer can no longer put a noncompete in a new or renewed contract. Noncompetes block you from working nearby for a competitor after you leave. Some covered up to 60 miles and two years. Each employer violation carries a $10,000 civil penalty.

You don't have to apply for this protection. It covers agreements entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2026; earlier contracts are not undone unless renewed after that date. Employers can still use confidentiality agreements, narrow limits on soliciting patients you treated, noncompetes tied to selling a practice, and repayment of things like signing bonuses or training costs if you leave within five years. When you leave a job, you may tell patients you were treating where you're going to practice next and remind them that they can choose their provider.

Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg started the push after hearing from clinicians who felt stuck in their contracts, and Del. Charniele Herring carried the identical House bill. Nurse practitioners and doctors backed it, saying noncompetes drive clinicians away from areas that already don't have enough of them. Sentara, a large health system, opposed it. The Senate passed the bill 40–0 and the House agreed to the Senate version 95–2. Gov. Abigail Spanberger added amendments and signed it on May 14, 2026.
