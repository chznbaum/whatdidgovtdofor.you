Starting January 1, 2027, boxes and packages of tampons, pads, discs, menstrual cups, period underwear and similar products manufactured for sale or distribution in Virginia must list their ingredients. The law defines an ingredient as an intentionally added substance that serves a technical or functional purpose. Ingredients go in order from most to least, on a label that's easy to see and read. Makers also have to post the list on their websites. When a company changes an ingredient, it has to post the change online within six months and fix the package label within 18 months.

You don't need to do anything to get this; the labels will be on the shelf. Products made before 2027 can still be sold. Companies do not have to reveal the identity of substances that qualify as confidential under the law, including claimed trade secrets, but they must still list each one under a common name. A company that breaks the rule can be pursued under Virginia's consumer protection law.

Del. Holly Seibold carried the bill. She was worried about toxic metals and "forever chemicals" (PFAS) in menstrual products, after a 2024 study found metals in several kinds of tampons. Dels. Kathy Tran and Rae Cousins co-sponsored it. The House passed it 69–28, the Senate amended and passed it 24–16, and the House agreed to the amendment 69–25. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it in April 2026.
