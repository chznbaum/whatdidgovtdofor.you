New York's coverage rule for most state-regulated health plans used to apply only to vaccines a federal advisory panel recommended. Now those plans must also cover any vaccine New York's health commissioner recommends based on standard medical practice and advice from groups like the American Academy of Pediatrics. If the federal panel drops a vaccine, your New York plan can still be required to cover it. For most state-regulated plans, in-network vaccines on that list come with no copay or deductible. Medicaid, school vaccine rules and newborn schedules now follow the state's standards too.

You don't need to apply. The coverage is built into most state-regulated plans. A second law also lets pharmacists give COVID-19 shots to children 2 and older with a doctor's or nurse practitioner's order. Before, state law limited them to adults.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed both bills in March 2026 with Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie. Hochul and the sponsors said the bills were needed because federal vaccine advice no longer matched the medical consensus. Assemblymember Erik Dilan and Sen. Jamaal Bailey carried the insurance bill. Assemblymember Amy Paulin and Sen. Toby Ann Stavisky carried the second bill. Both passed the Assembly on April 21. Hochul signed them on May 15, 2026, and they took effect that day.
