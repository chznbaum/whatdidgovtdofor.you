If your phone, laptop or other digital device was first manufactured on or after July 1, 2023 and first sold or used in New York on or after that date, its maker must offer you and independent shops the manuals, tools and parts needed to fix it. Electronic manuals are free. Software tools used with maker-provided parts are free; printed manuals and physical tools may carry reasonable copy or procurement costs. Parts must be reasonably priced, without unnecessary strings. No application is needed. The attorney general can sue violators and seek civil penalties up to $500 per violation.

Among other limits, the law does not cover cars, medical devices, e-bikes, home appliances, public-safety communications gear, or many farm, construction, yard and commercial electrical products. It excludes equipment sold under a specific business or government contract if not otherwise offered at retail. Makers need not reveal trade secrets or license intellectual property, and may sell whole assemblies when separate pieces pose a safety risk.

Assembly Member Patricia Fahy and Sen. Neil Breslin sponsored the bill. The Senate passed it in 2021, but it died in the Assembly. Both chambers passed it with votes from both parties in June 2022, making it the nation's first broad consumer-electronics right-to-repair law. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it Dec. 28 after agreeing with legislators to narrow it. Final terms passed in early 2023, 143-0 in the Assembly and 60-1 in the Senate. Hochul signed them March 3, and the law took effect Dec. 28, 2023.
