When New York wins money in a lawsuit or settlement with companies that made, shipped, sold or advised on opioids, that money now goes into a dedicated opioid settlement fund. It can be spent only on things that fight addiction: prevention and school programs, treatment, harm reduction such as overdose prevention, recovery housing, help for pregnant women and new parents, and job training. The money has to be spread across the state's regions, and it must add to what the state already spends, not replace it.

You don't apply for the fund yourself. The Legislature sets the spending, an advisory board recommends where the money should go, and the state hands it out as grants to local programs. As of July 2026, more than $454 million had been made available through the fund.

The law came after the state put $21 million of a $32 million McKinsey settlement into its general budget instead of addiction care. Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assemblymember Carrie Woerner sponsored the bill, working with Attorney General Letitia James, who won the settlements. Sen. Pete Harckham was a lead co-sponsor, and Republicans co-sponsored it too. It passed the Senate 63–0, also passed the Assembly without opposition, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 29, 2021.
