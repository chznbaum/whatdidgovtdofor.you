To get services for a developmental disability in New York, you first apply to the state's Office for People With Developmental Disabilities (OPWDD), which decides whether you're eligible and which services it will approve. This law gives that office a deadline. If you send your application online or by email, OPWDD has 120 days to finish processing it.

If your application is unclear, is missing something, or raises a specific concern about fraud, OPWDD must tell you in writing within 30 calendar days and say exactly what it needs. The 120-day clock pauses until you send the complete application, then picks up where it left off. You don't need to do anything extra to get the deadline. It applies to applications sent online or by email, not to paper ones sent by mail.

Sen. John Mannion and Assembly Member Thomas Abinanti sponsored the original bill. It passed the Senate 61–0 in May 2022 and the Assembly 143–1 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 28, 2022. Mannion and Assembly Member Tony Simone then sponsored negotiated changes, which Hochul signed in March 2023. Those changes added the pause in the clock and dropped a planned quarterly public report on application numbers and wait times. The deadline took effect 180 days after the original law was signed, in June 2023.
