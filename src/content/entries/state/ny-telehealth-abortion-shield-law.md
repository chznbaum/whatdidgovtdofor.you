This law protects doctors and other clinicians working in New York who give abortion care, including prescribing pills, by telehealth to patients in states where it's banned or restricted. New York won't send them to another state to face charges unless they were physically in that state, and police here can't arrest them for that care. State and local officials can't help with another state's investigation, New York courts won't issue subpoenas for those cases, and the care can't cost clinicians their license or their malpractice coverage. The protections cover clinicians while they're in New York.

Patients don't apply for anything. The law lets New York clinicians keep treating people who live where abortion is banned. It has been tested: in February 2025 Gov. Kathy Hochul refused Louisiana's request to extradite a New Paltz doctor, and the Ulster County clerk refused to file a Texas penalty of more than $100,000 against her. A state judge upheld the clerk in October 2025, and Texas's appeal remained pending in November 2026. Louisiana, Arkansas, and Alabama separately filed a U.S. Supreme Court challenge to New York's shield law in September 2026.

Sen. Shelley Mayer and Assembly Member Karines Reyes sponsored the bill. They wrote it because New York's 2022 shield laws didn't clearly cover telehealth. The Senate passed it 39–22, with every Republican present and two Democrats voting no. Hochul signed it on June 23, 2023, a day before the first anniversary of the Supreme Court decision overturning Roe v. Wade.
