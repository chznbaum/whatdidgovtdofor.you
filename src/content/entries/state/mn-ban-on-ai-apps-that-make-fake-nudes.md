"Nudify" apps use AI to take an ordinary photo of a real person and make a realistic fake nude image or video. Minnesota's law says anyone who runs a website, app or other service can't let users do this or do it for them. It also bans advertising these services. Tools that need a person's own real artistic skill to alter an image aren't covered.

If someone makes a fake nude of you with one of these services, you can sue the company in Minnesota district court. You can recover up to three times your actual damages, including for mental anguish, plus punitive damages and lawyer's fees. The attorney general can also seek fines of up to $500,000 for each use. That money goes to grants for groups that serve crime victims.

Sen. Erin Maye Quade and Rep. Jessica Hanson carried the bill. Victims pushed for it, including Molly Kelley, who learned in 2024 that a family friend had made fake porn of her and 79 other Minnesota women from their social media photos. It passed the House 132-1 and the Senate 65-0, and Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 7, 2026. That made Minnesota the first state to ban these services.

The law took effect Aug. 1, but xAI sued, saying it violates free speech. On Oct. 2, a federal appeals court barred the attorney general from enforcing it against xAI while the appeal proceeds. The order does not block private lawsuits or state enforcement against other companies.
