Starting Jan. 1, 2027, you won't need the internet to apply for housing help from New Jersey's Department of Community Affairs, as far as federal law allows. The law requires the department to set up a non-internet way to apply. It covers DCA's housing, shelter and rental aid programs, including federal Section 8 vouchers it runs and the State Rental Assistance Program. The law leaves DCA to choose or arrange the method.

This matters because the department has run its statewide Section 8 waiting list online only. When the list last opened in 2023, paper pre-applications weren't accepted unless a person needed one as a disability accommodation. People without a computer, a smartphone or a steady connection could fall behind before they even got in line.

Once the law takes effect, you can use the offline process DCA makes available for the covered program. The law does not say whether that process will use mail, in-person delivery, fax or another method.

Sens. Shirley Turner and Benjie Wimberly and Assembly members Verlina Reynolds-Jackson and Linda Carter sponsored the bill. Turner first introduced the proposal in March 2019 and refiled it in three later sessions before it passed nearly seven years later. It passed both houses with no votes against on Jan. 12, 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, 2026, his last day in office.
