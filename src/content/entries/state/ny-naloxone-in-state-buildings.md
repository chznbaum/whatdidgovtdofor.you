Erin's Law directs New York's public institutions and state buildings to keep opioid antagonists on hand, but the statewide stocking duty depends on regulations that have not yet been issued. These medicines, such as naloxone (sold as Narcan), can reverse an opioid overdose. An overdose can kill within minutes, and the idea is to treat these medicines the way buildings already treat defibrillators: keep them close by so someone can use them before help arrives.

Once the rollout begins, you won't have to apply or do anything to benefit. Each building's superintendent or chief executive will be responsible for stocking the medicine under rules written by the state Office of General Services. Those rules must address how many doses a building needs based on its size or occupancy, staff training and use of the medicine, and the phase-in schedule. The statute took effect on Dec. 17, 2025, but as of Nov. 13, 2026, OGS listed no proposed rules and no opioid-antagonist regulation, so no statewide rollout schedule was available.

The law is named for Erin Giarruso, who died of an accidental fentanyl overdose in November 2019. Her mother, Kelhi Giarruso, pushed lawmakers to act. Sen. John Mannion first introduced Erin's Law in 2022 and carried it in the Senate, where it passed 51–6 in May 2024. Assemblywoman Marianne Buttenschon sponsored it in the Assembly, which passed it 140–0 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 17, 2024.
