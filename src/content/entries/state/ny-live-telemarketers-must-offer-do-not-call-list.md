When a real person calls you in New York to sell something, they have to tell you that you can ask to be put on the seller's own do-not-call list. If you say yes, they must end the call right away and add your number. You don't have to sign up anywhere first. You just say so on the call. Before this law, New York's seller-specific opt-out requirement applied to prerecorded sales calls, not calls made by a person.

The law also stops telemarketers and sellers from passing along the name, phone number or email address you gave them, unless you agree in writing or electronically, or a law or court order requires it. They have to keep their calling records for two years. The state can fine them up to $20,000 per violation under the law as it reads today.

Sen. Todd Kaminsky and Assemblymember Amy Paulin wrote the bill after complaints about telemarketing calls in New York more than doubled in three years. It passed the Senate 62–0 in June 2019 and also passed the Assembly without opposition. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on December 2, 2019, and it took effect March 1, 2020.
