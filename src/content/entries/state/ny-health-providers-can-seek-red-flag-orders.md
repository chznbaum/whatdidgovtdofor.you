New York's red flag law lets a judge temporarily bar someone from having guns when they are likely to seriously hurt themselves or others. This 2022 law adds health care workers to the list of people who can ask for such an order. A doctor, psychologist, nurse, social worker, mental health counselor, or marriage and family therapist who treated the person in the past six months can file. Providers must make reasonable efforts to limit disclosed health information to the minimum the petition needs. They are shielded from liability when they decide reasonably and in good faith whether to disclose it.

The law also requires police and district attorneys to file a petition when they have credible information that someone is likely to cause serious harm, unless they determine there is no probable cause to file. State Police must write guidance to help officers spot when a petition is warranted. There is no benefits application: an eligible provider files a sworn petition in the person's local state Supreme Court, and a judge decides whether to issue an order.

State Sen. James Skoufis introduced the health-provider measure on May 9, 2022. After the May 14 racist mass shooting at a Buffalo supermarket, lawmakers expanded it to add the law-enforcement requirement, and Assemblymember Kevin Cahill carried the Assembly version. It passed the Senate 49–14 and the Assembly 119–30 on June 2. Gov. Kathy Hochul worked with legislative leaders on the post-Buffalo package and signed it on June 6. By October 2024, courts had issued more than 22,000 red flag orders, and 92% came after the 2022 changes. A state appeals court was still applying the law in September 2026.
