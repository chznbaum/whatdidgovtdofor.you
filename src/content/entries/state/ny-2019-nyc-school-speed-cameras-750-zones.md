This law expanded New York City's school speed cameras more than fivefold. Before it, the city could use cameras in only 140 school zones, during school-day and student-activity windows. Starting in July 2019, it could put them in up to 750 zones, anywhere within a quarter mile of a school. It could also run them on weekdays from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., summer included. The city had to post signs warning drivers and put cameras where speeding and crash data showed the most danger. When a vehicle is recorded going more than 10 mph over the limit, its owner gets a $50 ticket in the mail.

You don't have to do anything to benefit. The goal is slower traffic where kids walk. The city's transportation department reported that speeding violations fell 60% in school zones with cameras.

Assembly Member Deborah Glick had carried school speed cameras for years. Sen. Andrew Gounardes sponsored the Senate bill in his first term, after defeating a senator who had blocked cameras. In 2018 the program briefly went dark when its extension stalled in the Senate. Gov. Andrew Cuomo had proposed 290 zones, but lawmakers pushed it to 750. The Senate passed it 43–18, with four Republicans voting yes, and Cuomo signed it on May 12, 2019. Later laws made the cameras run around the clock and kept the program going through 2030.
