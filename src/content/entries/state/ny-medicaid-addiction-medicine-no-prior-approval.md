If you're on Medicaid in New York and need one of the medicines this law covers, your prescriber doesn't have to get Medicaid's approval first. The law covers all buprenorphine products, methadone and long-acting injectable naltrexone used for detox or ongoing treatment, as long as the prescription follows generally accepted national guidelines. It applies to fee-for-service Medicaid and Medicaid managed care. Other coverage rules, including quantity limits for some buprenorphine products, can still apply.

You don't have to apply for anything. The protection took effect March 22, 2022. Before then, prior approval could take one to three days, while withdrawal can start within six to 12 hours.

Sen. Pete Harckham and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal carried the bill. In 2019, New York enacted the same protection for people with private insurance, but Gov. Andrew Cuomo vetoed the Medicaid bill Harckham and Rosenthal carried, citing a $30 million cost. A 2019 RTI International study estimated that dropping the requirement would save nearly 600 lives and more than $50 million a year in hospital and treatment costs. The sponsors brought the bill back, and it passed the Assembly 147–0 in May 2021 and the Senate 55–8 that June, with Republican Sen. Fred Akshar among its cosponsors. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 22, 2021.
