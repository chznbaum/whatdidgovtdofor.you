If you live in a New York nursing home or adult care facility, you can name at least two people (family members, close friends, or a legal guardian) as your personal caregiving visitors. The home has to record who you chose. If a local or state public health emergency is declared, the home generally must let those people in to help care for you, subject to safety rules and limited exceptions. Caregivers can also come in for compassionate care, which means care near the end of life or during a serious mental, physical or social decline. Compassionate-care visits must remain available at all times, though visitors must follow screening and infection-control rules.

Other personal caregiving visits can still be limited or paused temporarily to keep residents safe, for example when local infection rates are high or the home is short of staff. Medicaid doesn't cover the visitors' help, and the home still owes you all the care it normally provides.

Sen. Rachel May and Assemblymember Harry Bronson sponsored the law after the 2020 ban on visitors cut residents off from their families. It passed both houses without a single no vote, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on March 29, 2021. May and Bronson then carried a follow-up chapter amendment, with Republican Sen. Pamela Helming as a cosponsor. It also passed unanimously, and Cuomo signed it on May 13, 2021.
