No teacher, principal, or other staff member at any school in New York can use corporal punishment on your child. That means spanking, slapping, hitting with a paddle, ruler, or belt, or forcing a student into painful exercise as punishment. The ban covers public and private schools alike. State rules define corporal punishment as any physical force used to punish a student. Staff may still restrain a student to keep someone from being hurt, but only when they can't reasonably handle it another way.

State education rules already barred public schools from hitting students, but they did not explicitly cover every private school. The 2023 law closed that gap by putting the ban into state law for every school. It also made corporal punishment part of what counts as child abuse in a school setting under the Education Law. Families don't need to do anything to be covered.

Assemblymember Charles Lavine wrote the bill, and Sen. Julia Salazar carried it in the Senate. Their memo pointed to research tying school paddling to lower test scores and behavior problems, and to reports of private schools still using it. The Assembly passed it in April 2023, and the Senate followed 61–1 that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 25, 2023, and it took effect right away.
