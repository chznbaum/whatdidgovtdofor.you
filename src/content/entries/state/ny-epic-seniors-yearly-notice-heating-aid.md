If you're enrolled in EPIC, New York's prescription drug coverage program for older adults, the state Health Department must tell you about the Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) every year. The notice explains who qualifies for HEAP and how to apply, and the same information also goes out with every paper EPIC application. HEAP is federally funded and helps lower-income homeowners and renters pay heating and cooling bills. For the 2026–27 season, a household can get up to $1,086, depending on how it heats its home.

EPIC does not sign you up for HEAP automatically. If you do not already receive HEAP automatically through another benefit program, you must apply through your local social services office or online. If you need help with the forms, your local office for the aging or the NY Connects helpline (1-800-342-9871) can help.

The bill's sponsors said many seniors who qualify for HEAP never get it. A state-commissioned study found that only about 39% of eligible senior households received it in 2017. Assemblymember John McDonald and Sen. Cordell Cleare, who chairs the Senate Aging Committee, wrote a bill that would have matched EPIC records with HEAP and enrolled eligible seniors automatically. It passed both chambers without a single no vote in 2024. AARP New York advocate Reggie Nance publicly urged the governor to sign it. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December on the condition that it be amended. In February 2025 the same sponsors passed a change, again unanimously, that replaced automatic enrollment with the yearly notice. The law took effect in June 2025.
