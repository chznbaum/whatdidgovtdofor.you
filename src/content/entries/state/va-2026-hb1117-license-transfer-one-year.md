If you're licensed in a trade or profession in another state and want to work in Virginia, the standard DPOR recognition route now lets you apply after holding a similar out-of-state license for just one year. Before HB 1117, that route required three years. Virginia's Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR) runs the program. It covers jobs from electricians and plumbers to barbers, opticians and real estate agents. A separate rule can immediately recognize certain real estate, appraisal, water and waste-system licenses from neighboring states.

You still have to apply through the right DPOR board before you start working and pay the applicable Virginia fees. Your original licensing state must have required an exam and standards involving education, training or experience. You also need no pending investigations or unresolved complaints and no disqualifying criminal or disciplinary record. If your state does not license your occupation, a separate route still requires three years of work experience and any examination required by the Virginia board.

Virginia created universal license recognition in 2023 with a three-year requirement for the standard route. Del. May Nivar, a first-term delegate, carried HB 1117 to cut it to one year. The bill received support from both parties, passing the House 97–1 and the Senate 38–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 8, 2026, and it took effect July 1.
