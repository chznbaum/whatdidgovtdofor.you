Some health plans cover a yearly checkup only if a full 12 months have passed since your last one. If last year's physical was in March and this year's only open appointment is in February, the plan could refuse to pay. The sponsors said that rule kept people from scheduling the preventive care their plans already promise.

This law fixes that. Health plans that New Jersey regulates must cover one wellness visit each plan year or calendar year, whichever the plan uses, for every member over age 3, with no waiting period. That includes individual and small-employer plans, HMOs, and plans sold by insurers and health service companies, plus the State Health Benefits Program and the School Employees' Health Benefits Program that cover public workers and teachers. You don't have to apply. The rule starts on Jan. 1, 2027, and kicks in when your plan is issued or renews on or after that date. Plans with only federal oversight, such as many large self-insured employer plans, fall outside state insurance law.

Assemblywoman Heather Simmons and Sens. John Burzichelli and Angela McKnight sponsored the bill. It passed without a single no vote, 74-0 in the Assembly and 38-0 in the Senate, on Jan. 12, 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, 2026, among the more than 120 bills he signed on his last day in office.
