Every time you buy groceries in Virginia, you pay less tax than you used to. Until the end of 2022, food for home consumption was taxed at 2.5%: 1.5% for the state and 1% for your city or county. On Jan. 1, 2023, the state's share went to zero. The same cut covers essential personal hygiene products, including diapers, incontinence products, and menstrual products like pads and tampons. You don't have to do anything; the lower rate is charged at the register.

The 1% local tax remains. The state paid for the cut out of a record budget surplus. By law, it also replaces the school money localities would have received from the old state tax, about $275 million a year in the current budget. It did not replace the rest of the state tax, which paid for roads and transit; lawmakers instead cut expected transportation revenue by about $190 million over the first two years.

Del. Joe McNamara, a Roanoke County Republican, and Sen. Jennifer Boysko, a Fairfax Democrat, sponsored identical bills. The approach that passed, ending the state's share while keeping the local 1% and replacing school money, was first proposed by Gov. Ralph Northam (D) in his December 2021 budget. The final bill passed the Senate 39–0 and the House of Delegates 88–1, and Gov. Glenn Youngkin (R) signed it.
