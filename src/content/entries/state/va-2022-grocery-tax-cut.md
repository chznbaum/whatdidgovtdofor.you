Every time you buy groceries in Virginia, you pay less tax than you used to. Until the end of 2022, food for home consumption was taxed at 2.5%: 1.5% for the state and 1% for your city or county. On Jan. 1, 2023, the state's share went to zero. The same cut covers essential personal hygiene products, including diapers, incontinence products, and menstrual products like pads and tampons. You don't have to do anything; the lower rate is charged at the register.

The 1% local tax remains, and the state now sends localities money to replace the school funding they would have received from the old state tax.

Gov. Glenn Youngkin (R) made ending the grocery tax part of his day-one campaign agenda, and he signed it into law. It was also a bipartisan effort. Del. Joe McNamara, a Roanoke County Republican, sponsored the House bill, and Sen. Jennifer Boysko, a Fairfax Democrat, sponsored an identical Senate bill. The final version passed the Senate 39–0 and the House of Delegates 88–1. The 2022 state budget, which Youngkin also signed, included the same exemption.
