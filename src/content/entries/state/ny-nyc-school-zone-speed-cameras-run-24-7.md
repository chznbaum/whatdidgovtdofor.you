New York City's speed cameras near schools now work around the clock. A camera in one of the city's 750 school speed zones issues a violation to a vehicle's owner when it records the car traveling more than 10 mph over the posted limit, at any hour on any day. Before this law, state rules made the cameras shut off at 10 p.m. and stay off all weekend. City figures showed that 59% of traffic deaths happened during those off hours.

You don't have to do anything to get the benefit. The cameras went to 24/7 operation on Aug. 1, 2022. In the program's first year, city transportation officials reported that speed-camera violations on camera corridors fell an average of 30%.

State Sen. Andrew Gounardes sponsored the bill, and Assembly Member Deborah Glick carried it in the Assembly. Glick also sponsored the original 2013 school-zone camera law and its 2019 expansion. The Adams administration urged the state to authorize 24/7 enforcement, and city transportation commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez led the rollout. The bill passed the Senate 51–12 and the Assembly 93–56, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 24, 2022. The authorization was set to expire July 1, 2025. Gounardes and Glick then sponsored the 2025 extension, which Hochul signed on June 30 and which keeps the cameras running 24/7 through July 1, 2030.
