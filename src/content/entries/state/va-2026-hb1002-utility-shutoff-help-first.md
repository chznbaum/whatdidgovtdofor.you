Say you fall behind on a power, gas, or water bill from a utility that Virginia's State Corporation Commission regulates. The company can't shut off your home's service until your account is at least 60 days behind. It also has to make a reasonable effort first to offer help paying, set up a payment plan, or point you to assistance or energy-savings programs. Earlier law already required utilities to reach out after each missed payment. Now that outreach must happen before they can cut you off.

The law also changes the language of your notices. Shutoff notices already had to be in English and Spanish. Now, if your city or county must print election materials in another language, your utility has to send its notices in that language too. The state's posted list, dated December 2021, names Vietnamese in Fairfax County. The law also told the Department of Elections to post an updated list by Sept. 1, 2026. You don't have to apply for any of this.

Del. Kathy Tran (D) carried the bill. It passed the House of Delegates 62–35 and the Senate 21–18. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026, and it took effect July 1, 2026.
