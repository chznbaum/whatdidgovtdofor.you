If you buy a used car from a dealer in New York, its airbags have to work. When federal safety standards require a vehicle to have airbags, the dealer can't sell it unless the airbags meet that standard and the readiness light on the dashboard shows nothing wrong. For most sales, the dealer has to say so on the certificate you get at the sale. The certificate rule has a narrow exception for certain lessees, their family members, or their employees who have already possessed the vehicle under a lease for at least 120 days. You don't have to apply for anything. The working-airbag rule covers retail sales by dealers, not private sales between people.

Before this law, nothing in state law required a used car sold by a dealer to have a working airbag. The law is named for Anthony Amoros, an 18-year-old from Rockland County who died in a 2013 crash. The car he had bought had no airbags, and he didn't know it. His parents, Alberto “Al” and Ivis Amoros, and his brother Angelo spent years pushing lawmakers for a change.

State Sen. James Skoufis and Assemblymember Ken Zebrowski sponsored the bill. All 42 Senate Democrats and five Republicans voted for it; the other 16 Republicans voted no. The Assembly passed it 147–2 the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 7, 2021, and it took effect 90 days later.
