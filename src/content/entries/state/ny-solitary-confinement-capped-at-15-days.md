The HALT Act limits how long New York prisons and jails can lock someone alone in a cell. Legally, “segregated confinement” means more than 17 hours a day in a cell, except during a facility-wide emergency or for medical or mental-health treatment. It is normally limited to three consecutive days and six in 30; longer confinement requires specified serious conduct and written findings after a hearing. Even then, it cannot exceed 15 consecutive days or 20 in 60. After that, the person must be released or moved to a rehabilitation unit offering at least six hours of daily group programming plus recreation.

People 21 or younger or 55 or older, people with a qualifying disability, and people who are pregnant, recently gave birth, or are caring for a child in prison generally cannot be put in segregated confinement. A pre-hearing hold is limited to 48 hours and must allow seven hours out of cell daily. Nobody has to apply.

Assemblymember Jeffrion Aubry sponsored the Assembly bill, Sen. Julia Salazar carried the Senate version, and Assembly Correction Committee chair David Weprin shepherded it through committee. #HALTsolitary organizer Jerome Wright helped lead the years-long campaign. It passed the Assembly 101–49 and Senate 42–21. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on March 31, 2021; it took effect one year later.

During the 2025 guard strike, DOCCS suspended parts of the law, and a court blocked the blanket suspension. In September 2026, another court decision said the agency still appeared to violate numerous HALT provisions. Repeal bills A3217 and S2656 remained active in committee as of Nov. 20, 2026.
