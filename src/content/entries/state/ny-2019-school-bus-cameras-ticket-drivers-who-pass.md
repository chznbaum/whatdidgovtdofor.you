In New York, it's illegal to pass a school bus that's stopped with its red lights flashing and its stop-arm out. Before this law, only a police officer who saw a driver do it could write a ticket. Now a camera on the bus can record the car, and the owner gets a ticket in the mail: $250 for a first violation, $275 for a second and $300 for a third within 18 months. Camera tickets carry no license points.

The cameras don't go up on their own. A school district and its county, city, town or village have to agree to use them. Beacon has had cameras on 53 buses since January 2023, and by spring 2024 Dutchess County had issued more than 30,000 tickets.

Assembly Member William Magnarelli had pushed for school bus cameras since at least 2015. Sen. Tim Kennedy carried this version in the Senate. He said 50,000 drivers pass a stopped school bus in New York every day. The Senate passed the bill 58–0, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 6, 2019. It took effect that September 5. The program was due to expire, but the 2024–25 state budget extended it to Dec. 1, 2029. After courts threw out tickets for insufficient evidence, the budget created rebuttable presumptions for qualifying camera evidence.
