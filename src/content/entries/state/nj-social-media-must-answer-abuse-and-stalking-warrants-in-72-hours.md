Stalking and abuse often happen through phones and social media, so the evidence sits with tech companies, and investigators can wait a long time for those companies to answer a warrant. This New Jersey law sets deadlines. A social media platform must turn over the records within 72 hours of being properly served. Phone carriers, internet and internet-phone providers, and other communications companies get five business days.

The deadlines cover warrants in cases of domestic violence, stalking, or violations of a domestic violence restraining order. To qualify, the warrant must say "Immediate Response Required" in its title and body and state the deadline. Victims don't have to apply for anything. Police and prosecutors use the faster warrant when they ask a judge for one.

Assemblywoman Marisa Sweeney and Sen. Raj Mukherji led the bill. Assemblywomen Heather Simmons and Luanne Peterpaul joined Sweeney as prime sponsors, and Republican Sen. Anthony Bucco joined Mukherji. It is modeled on Oregon's "Kristil's Law," named for Kristil Krug, a Colorado woman whose husband stalked her online for months while pretending to be someone else, then killed her. The Assembly passed it 79–0 in June 2026 and the Senate 39–0 that October. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on Nov. 12, 2026, and it took effect that day.
