The ban remains in effect: if your family has unpaid school-meal debt at a school covered by this law, the school can't take you to court over it. The school may ask a parent or guardian to pay, but it can't use a debt collector, file a lawsuit, or pursue the child for payment. The protection applies automatically at participating public, charter, and private schools, except those already serving every pupil free breakfast and lunch through Community Eligibility or Provision 2.

Since July 1, 2025, New York law has also required every school in the federal meal programs to serve reimbursable breakfast and lunch at no cost. Families at those schools should no longer incur new debt for those meals, although the lawsuit ban still protects covered unpaid debt. The ban built on New York's 2018 meal-shaming law, which stopped covered schools from singling out children over meal debt and from using debt collectors.

Sen. Rachel May and Assemblymember Anna Kelles carried matching versions of the lawsuit ban. The Senate passed it 63–0 on June 3, 2021, and the Assembly passed it 118–30 on June 9. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 29, 2021, and it took effect that day.
