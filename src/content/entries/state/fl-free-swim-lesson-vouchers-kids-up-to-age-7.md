Florida pays for free swim lessons for young children in lower-income families, and this law changed which ages can qualify. Starting July 1, 2026, it added children ages 5 through 7 but ended eligibility for children younger than 1. If your household earns no more than 200% of the federal poverty level and you live in Florida, you may apply for a child who is 1 through 7. Vouchers are awarded only while funding lasts. A voucher is generally worth about $200, and the statewide program flyer lists eight sessions with a certified local instructor. By April 2026, the Department of Health had received 16,663 applications during fiscal 2025–26 and approved 4,945. The law did not add money to the program; a Senate analysis said available funding remained unchanged and subject to appropriation.

The law also requires the Department of Health to write materials on drowning risks and safe bathing for babies and toddlers. Hospitals, birth centers, home birth providers, and childbirth educators must give those materials to new parents or people receiving childbirth education.

Sen. Clay Yarborough carried SB 428. Sen. Carlos Guillermo Smith co-introduced it and moved the amendment that added the drowning-prevention education requirements. Reps. Kim Kendall and Anna Eskamani sponsored the related House bill on the voucher age change. The Senate passed the law 36–0, and the House passed it 108–0. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed it on April 21, 2026.
