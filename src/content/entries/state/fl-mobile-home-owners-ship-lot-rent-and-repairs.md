Florida's SHIP program sends state housing money to all 67 counties and 55 participating cities to help residents with housing. Each local SHIP plan now has to include a strategy for helping mobile home owners, and that strategy must include lot-rent assistance. An award may cover up to six months of lot rent. Local plans may also use SHIP money for mobile-home rehabilitation and emergency repairs, and the law removes the old rule that capped manufactured housing at 20% of a locality's SHIP funds.

To seek help, contact your county or participating city housing office. Local programs set their own application windows, eligibility rules, and award amounts, and funding can be first come, first served. Depending on the local plan, households earning up to 140% of area median income may qualify. Statewide SHIP rules reserve at least 30% of funds for households at or below 50% of area median income and another 30% for households at or below 80%.

Sen. Colleen Burton (R) sponsored the Senate bill. Reps. Paula Stark (R) and Kimberly Berfield (R) jointly sponsored the House version, and Sen. Kristen Arrington (D) co-introduced the Senate bill. Burton, Stark, and Berfield had sponsored similar bills in 2025, but those versions died. The 2026 bill passed 39–0 in the Senate and 110–0 in the House. Gov. Ron DeSantis (R) signed it on June 26, 2026. The law changes how the existing SHIP program's money can be used; Florida's separate 2026–27 budget appropriated $165.7 million to SHIP.
