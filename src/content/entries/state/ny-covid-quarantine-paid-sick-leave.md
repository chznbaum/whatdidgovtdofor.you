If a COVID-19 quarantine or isolation order kept you home and you couldn't work remotely, this law protected your job and, at most employers, your pay. Employers with 100 or more workers, and every public employer, had to give at least 14 paid sick days. Employers with 11 to 99 workers, and the smallest ones with more than $1 million in net income, had to give at least 5 paid days. The smallest employers had to give unpaid leave. Paid family leave and disability benefits helped cover the time that wasn't paid, starting on the first day. You also had to get the same job and pay back when you returned, and your employer couldn't punish you for taking the leave.

You didn't apply to the state for the sick days. Your employer owed them. Any federal COVID leave you got reduced the state benefit.

Gov. Andrew Cuomo proposed the bill. Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assembly Member Catherine Nolan carried it. Both chambers passed it on March 18, 2020, the Senate 50–6 and the Assembly 132–3, and Cuomo signed it that day. Lawmakers took out a permanent paid sick leave plan that Cuomo had included.

The law had no end date at first. Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed ending it in July 2024. The final 2024 budget pushed that back a year, and the leave ended July 31, 2025.
