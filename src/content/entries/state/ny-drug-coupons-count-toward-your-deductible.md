Drugmakers often offer coupons or copay cards to help pay for expensive medicines. Some insurers and pharmacy benefit managers used to take that money without counting it toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. When the coupon ran out, you still had your whole deductible to pay. In New York-regulated health plans, this law ends that practice. For drugs the law covers, any coupon, discount, voucher, or other payment help used for your prescription has to count toward your deductible, copays, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket maximum.

You don't have to apply. The rule covers brand-name drugs that have no equivalent generic. It covers brand-name drugs that do have a generic when your plan approved the brand through prior approval, an appeal, or step therapy. It also covers generics your plan pays for. If you have a high-deductible plan that goes with a health savings account, the rule starts once you meet the federal minimum deductible.

Assembly Member Richard Gottfried and Sen. Gustavo Rivera first introduced versions of the bill in 2019. Their 2022 bill passed 129–9 in the Assembly and 61–0 in the Senate, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 23, 2022. In 2023, Rivera and Assembly Member David Weprin sponsored a follow-up law that narrowed which drugs are covered and moved the start date from January to July 1. Both chambers passed the amendment unanimously, and Hochul signed it on March 24, 2023.
