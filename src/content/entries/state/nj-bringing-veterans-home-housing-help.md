If you're a veteran in New Jersey with nowhere to live, Bringing Veterans Home can help you find housing. Once you're referred, someone has to contact you within 24 hours, and the program aims to get you into housing within 30 days. Help includes VA housing vouchers, state rental vouchers, rapid rehousing, emergency shelter, and support services. Service members and National Guard members qualify too.

You can refer yourself through the online form at bvh.dca.nj.gov, by calling NJ Vet2Vet or NJ 211, or through a participating provider. Outreach teams also go to veterans living in encampments and on the street. Six regional hubs, each run by a social services group the state picked through competitive bidding, coordinate the help.

The state Department of Community Affairs and the Department of Military and Veterans Affairs launched the program in November 2024, under Gov. Phil Murphy, with more than $30 million in state and federal money committed over two years. Community Affairs Commissioner Jacquelyn Suárez and Military and Veterans Affairs Commissioner Yvonne Mays led it. Gov. Mikie Sherrill proposed $11 million for it, and the enacted fiscal 2027 budget kept that funding. By Nov. 10, 2026, the program had recorded 3,058 permanent housing placements; the state said more veterans were moving into housing each month than were becoming homeless.
