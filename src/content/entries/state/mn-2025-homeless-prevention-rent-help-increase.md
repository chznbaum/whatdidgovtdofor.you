If you're about to lose your home in Minnesota, the Family Homeless Prevention and Assistance Program can help pay rent, a security deposit or utility bills, and connect you with services to stay housed. Minnesota's 2025 housing budget added a one-time $8.35 million to the program. The law appropriated $28.9 million for it across fiscal years 2026 and 2027.

To qualify, your household income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty guidelines, and you must be homeless or at real risk of becoming homeless. You don't apply to the state. You contact the local provider that serves your county, and they check whether you qualify and how much help you can get. Money is limited, so not everyone who asks will get help. The 20 two-year grants awarded in 2025 run from October 2025 through September 2027 and were expected to reach about 7,300 households.

Sen. Lindsey Port and Rep. Spencer Igo, a Republican, carried the bill in a House split evenly between the parties. Rep. Michael Howard helped negotiate the final version. The Senate passed it 36–31 and the House 108–26, and Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 23, 2025. Overall, the law increased General Fund housing spending by $18 million over the state's base budget.
