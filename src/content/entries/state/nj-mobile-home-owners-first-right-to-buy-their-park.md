Many New Jerseyans own a manufactured or mobile home but rent the lot it sits on. If the park is sold or redeveloped, they can lose their homes, since moving one is hard and open lots are scarce. The law does not apply to sales already under a written agreement, letter of intent, or other contractual commitment on Jan. 20, 2026. When a park owner offers the land for sale or plans to consider or counter a bona fide offer, the owner must notify each resident homeowner, the town, the state, and affordable-housing nonprofits on the state's list, and give them the price and terms.

Residents don't have to apply for anything. They form a resident homeowner group, and if 51% of resident homeowners agree, they have 120 days to sign a contract that meets the price and terms. Under the old law they needed two-thirds and had only 45 days. The owner has to negotiate with them in good faith. Residents can also hand their purchase rights to a town, a housing authority, a state agency, or a nonprofit to keep the land a home community. Residents and the attorney general can sue to enforce the law, and a court can cancel a sale that breaks it.

Assembly members David Bailey Jr., Dan Hutchison, and Benjie Wimberly and Sens. John Burzichelli and Paul Moriarty carried the bill. It passed both houses in December 2025. Gov. Phil Murphy sent it back with changes, lawmakers adopted them on Jan. 12, 2026, and Murphy signed it on Jan. 20.
