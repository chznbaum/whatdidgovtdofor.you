Virginia already imposed a three-year firearms ban after a misdemeanor conviction for assault and battery against a spouse, ex-spouse or co-parent. But its gun-ban statute did not cover dating partners or people who lived together unless they also fit one of those categories. Advocates call the gap the "intimate partner loophole."

This law closes it. A misdemeanor conviction for assault and battery against an intimate partner now triggers the same three-year ban. An intimate partner is someone who, within the previous 12 months, was in a romantic, dating or sexual relationship with the person. The law separately adds anyone who lives with the person or did so during the past 12 months. Knowingly buying, possessing or transporting a gun during the ban is a Class 1 misdemeanor. The dating-partner provision applies to assaults committed on or after July 1, 2026. Victims do not have to apply; the ban follows the conviction automatically. Under related surrender rules that took effect the same day, the convicted person must surrender, sell or transfer any guns within 24 hours after sentencing, or after release from custody if taken into custody at sentencing.

Del. Adele McClure carried the bill in the House, and Sen. Russet Perry carried an identical Senate bill. The House passed it 61–35. The Senate first changed it, then dropped its changes and passed the House version 22–18. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 10, 2026. Her office said it passed with support from both parties.
