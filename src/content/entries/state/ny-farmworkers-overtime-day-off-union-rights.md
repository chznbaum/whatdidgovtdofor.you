For decades, farmworkers lacked protections other workers had. Covered farmworkers get 24 consecutive hours off each week; if they voluntarily work that day, they earn time and a half. They also get overtime for long weeks and may organize and bargain as a group. The law expanded workers' compensation and unemployment insurance coverage. Employers cannot retaliate against workers for seeking workers' compensation.

No application is needed for overtime or weekly rest; those protections come with covered farm work. The law first set overtime after 60 hours a week. In September 2022, Labor Commissioner Roberta Reardon accepted a wage board plan to lower the threshold, and final regulations took effect in 2023. It fell to 52 hours in 2026 and drops four hours every other year until reaching 40 in 2032.

Assembly Member Catherine Nolan carried farmworker protections for years, while Sen. Jessica Ramos sponsored the Senate bill. Nolan credited Senate Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins, Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie and Gov. Andrew Cuomo with putting the compromise together; Assembly Labor Chair Marcos Crespo also led the Assembly effort. The bill passed the Assembly 94 to 54 and Senate 40 to 22 on June 19, 2019. Every Senate Democrat voted yes and every Republican no. Cuomo signed it July 17.

The union provisions face an active federal challenge. In 2024, a court blocked enforcement of one clause that barred employers from discouraging union activity. In September 2026, a magistrate judge recommended denying a newer bid to block union contracts; the case remains pending.
