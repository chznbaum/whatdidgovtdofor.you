Starting Jan. 1, 2027, Georgia's surprise-billing law covers emergency ground ambulance rides requested by a first responder. If your health plan is covered, you pay no more in copayments, coinsurance, or deductibles when the ambulance is out of network than you would for a similar in-network ride. The insurer pays the ambulance provider directly, and after that payment the provider cannot bill you for the balance.

You don't need to apply. The law covers Georgia-regulated health plans and the state plans for state employees, public-school workers, and university employees. Federally regulated self-funded employer plans are excluded unless they opt into Georgia's surprise-billing law. For non-state plans, the law sets what insurers pay out-of-network ambulances: a locally established rate or, where there isn't one, the lesser of 325% of the Medicare rate or the billed charge.

Sen. Shawn Still introduced the ambulance language as SB 462 and put it into HB 506, while Rep. Alan Powell carried a parallel House bill. HB 506 began as Rep. Scott Hilton's tobacco-cessation bill, but the Senate replaced that text with the ambulance protections and passed it 48–0. The House agreed 168–2 on the last day of the session. Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 12, 2026; the tobacco-cessation coverage was not in the final law.
