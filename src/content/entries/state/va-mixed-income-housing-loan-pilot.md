Building homes that working families can afford often stalls because the money costs too much to borrow. This law sets up a two-year pilot to help close that gap. The state housing department and Virginia Housing, the state's housing finance agency, must make lower-interest loans to buy, build or fix up mixed-income housing. The loans can also pay for roads, pipes and other work that gets a site ready to build.

Builders, nonprofits, public bodies and other eligible entities seek the loans; residents do not apply to the pilot. The projects must combine homes for low- and moderate-income residents with market-rate homes, using income categories defined in the program guidelines. How residents apply for or rent those homes will depend on each project. Each Nov. 1, the two agencies must report how many loans they made and how many homes each project has at each income and rent level.

Gov. Abigail Spanberger proposed a revolving loan fund for mixed-income development. Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg carried SB 490, and Dels. Josh Thomas and Dan Helmer carried identical House bills. The Senate changed a permanent fund proposal into a two-year pilot. The conference version passed the Senate 39–0 and the House 66–30. After Spanberger recommended an amendment, the final votes were 39–0 and 75–23. She approved the bills on April 22, 2026. The state budget put $20 million from the general fund toward the pilot for its first year.
