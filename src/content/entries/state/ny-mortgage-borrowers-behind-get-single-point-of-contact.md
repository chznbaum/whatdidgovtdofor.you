Falling behind on a mortgage often means calling your lender over and over, explaining your situation to a new person each time, and losing track of paperwork. This New York law is meant to stop that. If you're 30 or more days behind and want to modify your loan or find another way to avoid foreclosure, your lender or its servicer must give you one point of contact. That contact has to explain your options, help you figure out which documents you need, and tell you accurately where your application stands. The contact stays assigned until you're caught up or the lender determines that all loss-mitigation options have been exhausted. If the contact information changes, the lender has five business days to tell you.

The law covers home loans made on or after Jan. 2, 2022 for a one- to four-family home, condo or co-op in New York that you live in, up to Fannie Mae's loan size limit. Reverse mortgages aren't covered.

Sen. James Sanders Jr. and Assembly Member Nick Perry sponsored the bill, which they had first introduced the session before. The Senate passed it 43–20 with only Democrats voting yes, and the Assembly passed it 149–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 3, 2021. In early 2022, Sanders and Perry passed an amendment that the governor had agreed to. It lowered the point when you get a contact from 60 days behind to 30, so you no longer have to ask in writing, and it let state banking regulators write rules for it.
