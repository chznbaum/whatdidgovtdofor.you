New York now has its own rent voucher, modeled on federal Section 8. If you're homeless or about to lose your home, and your household earns no more than half your area's median income, you usually pay about 30% of your adjusted income toward rent. The voucher pays an approved subsidy directly to your landlord. Immigration status doesn't matter, and landlords can't legally turn someone away for paying with it.

You can't apply on your own. In New York City, the city's housing agency, HPD, takes referrals from its emergency housing program and from shelters, so shelter residents should ask their housing specialist. Elsewhere in the state, your county social services department refers you to the state housing agency, HCR, and referred households are picked by lottery. The money is limited: New York City expected its first-year share to cover about 900 to 1,100 vouchers. The first vouchers became available on March 1, 2026.

Sen. Brian Kavanagh and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal, who chair their chambers' housing committees, carried the program for years. Kavanagh first introduced it in 2020. Both chambers sought $250 million. The budget Gov. Kathy Hochul signed on May 9, 2025 created a four-year pilot instead, with $50 million for its first year. The 2026–27 state budget kept it going at $50 million.
