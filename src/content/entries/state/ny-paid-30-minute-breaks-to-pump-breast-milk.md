If you work in New York and need to pump breast milk on the job, your employer must give you 30 minutes of paid break time each time you reasonably need it. You can take as many of these breaks as you need, for up to three years after your child is born. If you need more than 30 minutes, you can add your regular paid breaks or meal time. Your employer can't make you make up the time.

The law covers every public and private employer in the state, of any size. You must notify your employer in writing in advance, preferably before returning from leave. Your employer must give you the state's written policy when you're hired and every year after that. If you're denied a paid break, you can complain to the state Department of Labor.

Before June 19, 2024, New York required employers to give only unpaid break time for pumping, or let you use breaks you already had. Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed a 20-minute paid break in her January 2024 budget. In March, Assembly Member Yudelka Tapia and Sen. Jessica Ramos introduced bills for a 30-minute paid break, and the final budget set it at 30 minutes. The Legislature passed the budget bill that April, and Hochul signed it on April 20, 2024.
