New York law has a crime written for police who choke people. If a police officer or peace officer uses a chokehold or a similar restraint, or otherwise intentionally blocks someone's breathing or blood flow, and that person is seriously hurt or dies, the officer can be charged with aggravated strangulation. It's a class C violent felony, punishable by up to 15 years in prison. The law covers every police and peace officer in the state, not just the NYPD.

You don't have to do anything to be protected. The law gives prosecutors a specific charge to bring when an officer's restraint causes serious injury or death.

The law is named for Eric Garner, who died in 2014 after an NYPD officer put him in a chokehold on Staten Island, even though the department had banned chokeholds since 1993. Assemblymember Walter Mosley first filed the bill in August 2014 and reintroduced it until it passed nearly six years later. Sen. Brian Benjamin carried it in the Senate. After protests over George Floyd's killing, the Senate passed it 62–0 and the Assembly passed it 139–5 on June 8, 2020. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 12, 2020.
