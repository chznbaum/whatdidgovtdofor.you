Some car insurers search address databases, credit reports and public records, and when someone else shows up at your address, they add that person to your policy. That can be a grown child who moved out, an old roommate or a past owner of the house, and it can raise your premium. Starting in 2027, Virginia law says insurers can't add a person to your policy or household based on that kind of information alone.

If your insurer believes someone lives with you, it must send you written or electronic notice at least 45 days before making the change. To stop it, show that the person lives somewhere else. Accepted proof includes a Virginia driver's license issued in the past six months, or a utility bill, mortgage statement or renter's insurance summary from the past 60 days with their current address. The person can also do a verification interview with the insurer. The rule covers policies written, changed or renewed on or after January 1, 2027. You don't need to apply.

Sen. Bill DeSteph, a Virginia Beach Republican, carried the bill. It passed without a single no vote: 39–0 in the Senate and 97–0 in the House of Delegates. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
