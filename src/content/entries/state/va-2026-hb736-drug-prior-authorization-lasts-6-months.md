Many private health plans won't pay for some prescriptions until your doctor gets the insurer's approval first, a step called prior authorization. Until now, Virginia law set no minimum on how long that approval had to last, so patients on long-term medicines could have to go through it again and again. For carrier-provider contracts entered into, amended, or renewed on or after Jan. 1, 2027, a first approval for a drug must last at least six months, and a continued approval at least 12 months.

Once the prescription has been scheduled, provided, or delivered under the approval, your plan can't revoke or limit it except for reasons the law lists: fraud, a regulatory or manufacturer action over the drug's safety or effectiveness, a dangerous drug interaction, a newly covered generic or biosimilar, or when added safety and effectiveness monitoring is medically called for. You don't have to apply. The protection covers state-regulated private plans; Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP, federal employee coverage, TRICARE, and most self-funded employer plans are exempt.

Del. Michelle Maldonado carried the bill, with Del. Amy Laufer as co-patron. It passed the House 97–0 and the Senate 39–0 without a single no vote. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it in April 2026 as one of several laws aimed at health care costs.
