New Jersey is giving $75.5 million in grants and loans to towns and counties for 85 park and open-space projects. Of that, $23.2 million supports 28 land-acquisition projects. About $51.5 million supports 53 park-development projects, and $847,874 supports four stewardship projects. Another $1.9 million pays the state's administrative costs.

Of the park-development money, $29 million goes to projects in urban aid municipalities or projects sponsored by densely populated counties. Newark gets $489,000 for Broadway Park in the North Ward, and Camden gets $1.35 million to improve Union Field. You don't have to apply for anything. The local governments receiving the funds carry out the work for public recreation and conservation.

The law uses constitutionally dedicated corporation business tax revenue, interest and loan repayments from Green Acres funds, and money made available when earlier projects were cancelled or withdrawn. The Department of Environmental Protection and the Garden State Preservation Trust approved the project list.

Sens. John McKeon (D) and Latham Tiver (R) carried the bill in the Senate. Assembly members Marisa Sweeney, Carmen Theresa Morales and Verlina Reynolds-Jackson carried it in the Assembly. It passed both chambers without a single no vote, 40–0 in the Senate and 78–0 in the Assembly. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on July 30, 2026.
