If you're going through perimenopause or menopause and have health insurance regulated by Virginia, your plan has to cover medically necessary treatment and care for it. The law names the symptoms it covers: irregular periods, hot flashes, vaginal or bladder problems, bone loss, rising LDL cholesterol and trouble sleeping.

You don't need to apply. Coverage is required in policies, subscription contracts and HMO plans that are issued, reissued or extended in Virginia on or after Jan. 1, 2027, so it reaches people as their plans start or renew. It doesn't cover short-term, travel, accident-only or limited-disease policies, or plans designed for people on Medicare. In the individual and small-group markets, the new mandate doesn't add any benefit that's already part of Virginia's essential health benefits package. Coverage for that care continues through the package.

Sen. Ryan McDougle, a Republican, carried the bill. It passed the Senate 40-0 in February 2026. The House changed it and passed it 97-0, and the Senate agreed to the House version 39-0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
