Some carmakers have started charging monthly or yearly fees to switch on features that are already built into the car, such as heated seats. Starting Feb. 21, 2027, New York bans that. A carmaker, dealer, or anyone acting for them can't sell you a subscription for a feature that uses hardware your car already had when you bought or leased it and that works without ongoing service from the company. Each violation can bring a civil penalty of up to $250 per point of sale, and the state Department of State can write rules to enforce it.

You don't need to do anything to be covered. The law has limits: it doesn't cover features that depend on ongoing service or data, including navigation updates, infotainment, satellite radio, in-car Wi-Fi, telematics, roadside assistance, connected services that use a cell network, or software-based driver-assistance and self-driving features. Your regular car loan or lease payments don't count as a subscription. Companies can still send software updates, but they can't charge for a safety recall fix that federal law requires them to provide free.

Assemblymember William Magnarelli and Sen. James Skoufis carried the bill, first introduced in 2024. Magnarelli's version passed the Assembly that year but stalled in the Senate. Both chambers passed a new version in 2025, but Gov. Kathy Hochul vetoed it. The sponsors returned in 2026; the Assembly passed the revised bill 138–3 on May 19, and the Senate passed it 57–3 on June 4. Hochul signed it on Nov. 23, 2026.
