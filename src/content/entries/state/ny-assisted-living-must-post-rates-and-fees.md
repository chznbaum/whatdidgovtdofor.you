If you're shopping for assisted living for yourself or a parent in New York, you can see starting prices and fee details without calling each residence. Every licensed assisted living residence, including enhanced and special needs residences, must post four things on its website and in a public space inside the building: its starting monthly service rate, its range of starting rents, its approved admission or residency agreement, and a plain-language summary of all service fees. The Health Department sets the forms so people can compare residences. You do not need to apply.

The law also requires annual reporting on quality measures the department sets with input from providers, consumers and the state's long-term care ombudsman. The statute set April 2025 deadlines for the first report and public results, but the department's implementation came later. In January 2026, it chose three measures—complaint activity, staffing levels and food-and-dining violations—and told operators to submit their posting forms by January 31. The state's comparison page is now live and says information will continue to be added and updated.

Assemblymember Amy Paulin and Sen. Cordell Cleare carried the original bill and the follow-up amendment. The original passed 147–0 in the Assembly and 60–0 in the Senate. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 13, 2024. The amendment moved several reporting deadlines so the state had more time to choose quality measures. Hochul signed it on February 14, 2025.
