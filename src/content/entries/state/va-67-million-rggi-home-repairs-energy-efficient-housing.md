Many homes can't get free weatherization, like insulation and air sealing, because they first need a leaking roof, bad wiring, broken plumbing or mold fixed. Virginia's Weatherization Deferral Repair program pays for those fixes. In October 2026 it got $15 million, enough for critical repairs at more than 1,000 homes, which can then get the federal Weatherization Assistance Program's upgrades. Another $52 million goes to the state's Affordable and Special Needs Housing program to build energy-efficiency standards into affordable homes being built, rehabbed or converted.

To get the repairs, apply through your local weatherization provider. The state's Department of Housing and Community Development works through 15 providers statewide. For 2026–27, a one-person household can earn up to $44,776 a year and a four-person household up to $86,109. If your home gets repair work, you also have to accept weatherization. The program doesn't pay utility bills.

The money comes from Virginia's carbon allowance auctions. A 2020 law carried by Del. Charniele Herring and Sen. Lynwood Lewis sets half of that revenue aside for low-income energy efficiency, and unspent money stays in that account. After Gov. Glenn Youngkin's administration pulled Virginia out of RGGI and held the money back, DHCD reported that about $341.6 million of $474.4 million meant for efficiency programs was unspent. Virginia rejoined in July 2026 under a bill Herring carried, and Gov. Abigail Spanberger released the withheld $67 million on Oct. 8.
