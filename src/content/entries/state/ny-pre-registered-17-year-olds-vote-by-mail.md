In New York you can pre-register to vote before you turn 18. If you've pre-registered and will be 18 by Election Day, the law now treats you as a qualified voter for absentee and early mail voting. That means you can apply for a mail ballot while you're still 17, just like any other voter.

Before this law, you had to be 18 to ask for an absentee ballot. Applications are due weeks before the election, so a young voter whose birthday fell close to Election Day couldn't apply in time. The bill's sponsors said these voters were "effectively disenfranchised." You still have to meet every other requirement, and your ballot counts only if you're 18 by Election Day.

Sen. Rachel May and Assemblymember Harvey Epstein carried the bill. The Assembly passed it on May 29, 2024, and the Senate passed it 48–9 the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Aug. 6, 2024, the anniversary of the federal Voting Rights Act, and it took effect right away.
