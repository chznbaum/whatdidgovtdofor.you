Starting July 1, 2027, if you own a home in a Virginia single-family zone covered by the law, you can add a second, smaller home on your lot. It can be a garage apartment, a basement or in-law suite, or a cottage out back. Localities must treat these accessory dwelling units, or ADUs, as an allowed use. You won't need special zoning approval or a rezoning hearing, but you still need an ADU permit. That permit fee is capped at $500, although inspection, site, building and other applicable fees may still be charged. The locality must issue the permit if your plans meet the law's rules and applicable local codes.

Localities can't require bigger side or rear setbacks for an ADU than they require for your house, and they can't require the occupants to be relatives. The law therefore allows rentals to nonrelatives, although a locality can require leases of at least 30 days. Localities may also require parking, require the owner to live in either home when applying, and set a maximum size. The smallest maximum they may set is 350 square feet on lots under 2,500 square feet or 500 square feet on larger lots. Homeowners association and other private covenants still apply, and localities that adopted their own ADU rules before 2026 are exempt.

Sens. Kannan Srinivasan and Saddam Salim carried the bill after earlier attempts failed. It passed the Senate 21–19 and the House 62–37. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
