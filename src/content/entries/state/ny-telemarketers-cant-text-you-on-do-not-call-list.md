New York's telemarketing law used to cover only phone calls, even though many sales pitches had moved to text messages. This law added texts to the definition of telemarketing, so many of the rules for sales calls now apply to sales texts too. The main rule: a telemarketer can't send you an unsolicited sales text once your number has been on the national Do Not Call Registry for 31 days.

You don't need to do anything new if your number is already on the registry. If it isn't, you can sign up for free at donotcall.gov or by calling 1-888-382-1222. Sales texts you asked for and texts from a business you already deal with generally aren't covered, unless you've told that business to stop; purely informational messages aren't covered. The New York Department of State can fine a telemarketer up to $20,000 for each violation.

Assembly Member Kenny Burgos sponsored the enacted bill, and Sen. Leroy Comrie carried the Senate version. Assembly Member Michael DenDekker sponsored the earlier version that passed the Assembly in 2020 but stalled in the Senate. In 2021 the bill passed both chambers with no votes against: 149–0 in the Assembly and 63–0 in the Senate. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on July 13, 2021, and it took effect 30 days later.
