If you're hurt at work in New York and don't read English well, the Workers' Compensation Board must provide the forms injured workers complete and its guides to applying for benefits in the state's 12 most common non-English languages. It must also provide free interpretation in your preferred language when you deal with the Board about services, information or benefits.

You do not need to pass a separate eligibility test for language help, but you should ask for it. The Board lists Spanish, Chinese, Russian, Yiddish, Bengali, Korean, Haitian Creole, Italian, Arabic, Polish, French and Urdu. Call (877) 632-4996 for free assistance or to request a translation in another language. If the Board does not provide adequate interpretation or denies an available translated document, you can file a complaint.

Versions of this proposal had been introduced since 2017. State Sen. Jessica Ramos carried its Senate versions beginning in 2019, and Assembly Member Nily Rozic carried its Assembly version in 2021. The Senate passed the enacted bill 46–17, the Assembly passed it 112–37, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed Chapter 622 on Nov. 15, 2021. It took effect 90 days later.

After the governor and legislators agreed to revise its scope, Ramos and Rozic carried a 2022 follow-up that clarified and narrowed the written-material requirement to Board-published forms injured workers complete and guides to applying. In 2023, they carried another update, signed by Hochul, tying the Board to the statewide language access law and raising the minimum from 10 languages to 12.
