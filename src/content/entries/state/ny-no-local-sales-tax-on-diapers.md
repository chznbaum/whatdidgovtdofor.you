When you buy diapers in New York, there's no sales tax on them. That's true in every county and city. It covers diapers for babies and for adults, disposable or reusable. You don't have to apply or show anything at the register: the exemption is built into the sales tax law, and stores stopped charging it on Dec. 1, 2022.

Diapers were already free of the state's 4% sales tax, but local sales taxes were a different story. Some counties and other local governments never exempted diapers, so families there still paid tax on them. Children's diapers counted as clothing, so they went untaxed only where a locality chose to exempt clothing. Diapers are a big, steady cost: a baby can go through up to 12 a day, which can come to $70 to $80 a month.

Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assemblymember Nader Sayegh sponsored the DIAPER Act. It passed both houses without a single no vote, 63–0 in the Senate and 144–0 in the Assembly. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on July 19, 2022. The cost falls on the localities that had been taxing diapers. They gave up that revenue, and the state doesn't make it up to them.
