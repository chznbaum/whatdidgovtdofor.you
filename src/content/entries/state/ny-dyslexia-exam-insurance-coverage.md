A comprehensive neuropsychological exam can diagnose dyslexia. New York lawmakers found that these exams often take two days, can cost more than $6,000, and typically were not paid by insurance. Schools generally offer only limited assessments. The law now requires covered health plans to include the exam.

Coverage applies to individual plans bought in New York, fully insured employer plans bought in New York, and NYSHIP. It does not apply to self-funded employer plans, out-of-state coverage, or Medicare. It applies when a policy is issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2025. A physician must refer you, and a licensed or otherwise authorized health professional must perform the exam within their scope of practice. Insurers may review medical necessity, require an in-network provider, and charge the plan's usual deductible, copay, or coinsurance.

Assemblymember Robert Carroll, who was diagnosed in 1993, said he authored the bill after hearing from families about costs and had worked on dyslexia policy since taking office in 2017. He and Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal sponsored it. The Assembly first passed it in 2023 and again in January 2024; the Senate approved it 60–0 in June 2024. The sponsors called it the first state law of its kind. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 11, 2024. They then carried a negotiated chapter amendment clarifying referrals, provider licensing, utilization review, and networks. Both parties supported it; the Assembly voted 147–0 and the Senate 62–1 before Hochul signed it February 14, 2025, effective retroactively to January 1.
