New York bars manufacturers, importers, distributors, wholesalers, retailers, and secondhand dealers from selling or leasing padded crib bumpers, the soft pads that tie around the inside of a crib. Babies can suffocate against them or be strangled by their ties. Federal safety staff found 107 reported deaths in which a crib bumper was present between 1990 and March 2016; they classified 31 as incidental, with no evidence that the bumper was involved. The state law excludes mesh liners.

The law also covers places where other people put your baby to sleep. Child care programs and hotels and motels cannot use or keep padded bumpers unless a medical professional decides a particular child needs one. Families do not have to do anything. The attorney general can seek a court order against a business that violates the sales and leasing ban, and a judge can impose up to $500 for each violation.

Assemblymember Amy Paulin wrote the ban. The Assembly passed it in 2016 and again in 2017, but it stalled in the Senate. Sen. David Carlucci carried it in the Senate from 2017, and in 2019 the Senate passed it 61–0. The Assembly passed it 129–13, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 13, 2019. It took effect 60 days later.
