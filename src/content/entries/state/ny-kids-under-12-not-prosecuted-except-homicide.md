New York no longer takes children under 12 to Family Court as juvenile delinquents for most alleged crimes. Before this law, the state could charge a child as young as 7, the lowest age set by statute in the country. Now the general floor is 12. The exception is specified homicide offenses, including murder and manslaughter. A child under 13 generally can't be held in secure detention; the law allows that only for children at least 10 covered by the homicide exception.

Instead of court, a child under 12 whose behavior once could have led to charges can be referred to a "differential response" program run by the local social services department. Families choose whether to take part. Staff assess the child and connect the family with help, such as mental health care, aimed at keeping kids out of the justice system. Every local social services district had to establish a program when the law took effect on December 29, 2022.

Sen. Velmanette Montgomery and Assemblymember Ellen Jaffee pushed the change in 2020, after it was left out of the state's 2017 Raise the Age law. The Legal Aid Society and Children's Defense Fund helped draft that proposal; Families Together in New York State, Citizens' Committee for Children, and Youth Represent also fought for passage. Sen. Jamaal Bailey and Assemblymember Andrew Hevesi carried it to passage in June 2021, and the Senate voted 44–19. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 29, 2021, and signed follow-up changes in February 2022. The Legal Aid Society counted at least 800 arrests of children under 12 in 2019.
