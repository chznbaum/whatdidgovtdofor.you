Now every public school district and BOCES must have a written policy for very hot days. When a classroom or other occupied space reaches 82°F, the school has to do something about it. That can mean turning off overhead lights, lowering shades, running fans, opening doors and windows, shutting off electronics that aren't in use, or giving water breaks. Temperature is measured in the shade, about three feet off the floor, near the middle of the room.

If a room hits 88°F, the district must have a plan to move students and staff out of it where it's practical to do so. Families and staff don't have to apply for anything. The rules took effect on September 1, 2025. The law doesn't force schools to close or require them to install air conditioning.

State Sen. James Skoufis, who said he had pushed for cooler classrooms for more than seven years, sponsored the bill. Assemblymember Chris Eachus carried it in the Assembly. NYSUT members pressed for the law for years, gathering classroom stories and staging a sauna at the Capitol. It passed the Senate 57–3 in June 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December. The original bill said rooms could not be used at all at 88°F. In February 2025, lawmakers passed a follow-up law, sponsored by Eachus and Skoufis, that replaced this ban with the requirement to plan to move people out where practical.
