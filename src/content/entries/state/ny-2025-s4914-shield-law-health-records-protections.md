If you get an abortion, birth control, or gender-affirming care that's legal in New York, this law makes it harder for another state to use New York to punish you, your provider, or someone who helped you. An out-of-state subpoena must include a sworn statement that it has a permitted purpose. New York state and local police generally can't arrest people for protected care.

The law covers your records. A New York business, clinic, or app receiving a covered request must notify the state attorney general. It generally must try to notify affected people and wait 30 days before releasing information. Without the required sworn statement, it cannot release the records. Requests for your own records and enforceable court orders are exceptions. A knowing or reckless violation can bring a $10,000 penalty. Clinicians cannot face license discipline or an adverse insurance action solely for protected care within their scope of practice. You do not need to apply.

Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Harry Bronson carried the bill, developed with Attorney General Letitia James's office. A 2024 version passed the Senate but stalled in the Assembly. The 2025 bill passed the Senate 37–20, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19, 2025. A 2026 chapter amendment sponsored by Sen. Liz Krueger and Bronson set the five-business-day notice deadline and lowered the disclosure penalty from $15,000 to $10,000. The law remains in effect, but Louisiana, Arkansas, and Alabama have asked the U.S. Supreme Court to block New York's abortion shield laws.
