PFAS, often called "forever chemicals," are added to clothing to make it repel water and stains. They break down very slowly and build up in people and the environment. Starting Aug. 27, 2028, no one can sell, make or distribute clothing or diapers with intentionally added PFAS in New Jersey. The ban covers everyday and formal clothes, underwear, swimwear, shoes, school uniforms and workwear, plus outdoor gear made for heavy rain. It also covers disposable and reusable diapers.

You don't have to do anything. Manufacturers must certify to the state Division of Consumer Affairs that their products contain no intentionally added PFAS and give a copy to each person or business that purchases from the manufacturer. Protective safety equipment, military-only clothing and motorcycle or off-road riding gear are exempt. Tiny trace amounts that can't be avoided don't count as a violation. Violators face the penalties in New Jersey's 2025 forever chemicals law, which already covered products such as cosmetics, carpets, cookware and food packaging. Civil administrative penalties can reach $20,000 per violation, with each day of a continuing violation counted separately.

State Sens. Linda Greenstein and Shirley Turner sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Assembly members William Sampson, Chigozie Onyema and Katie Brennan sponsored it in the Assembly. Both chambers passed it on June 30, 2026: unanimously in the Senate and 59–16 in the Assembly. Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on Aug. 27, 2026. The two-year wait gives clothing makers time to switch, and Turner noted that many companies have already begun doing so.
