New York now treats a domestic violence victim's relatives as members of the same family or household as the abuser for family-offense cases, whatever the relatives' ages. That lets adult relatives ask Family Court for their own orders of protection. Before, only unrelated children under 18 could be added to an adult victim's order.

Melanie Chianese's case exposed that gap. In May 2022, Melanie, 29, was killed in her Wappingers Falls home by her mother's ex-boyfriend. Her mother had an order of protection against him, but Melanie was too old to be included, and a court denied the order requested for her. The law changes the Family Court Act and Criminal Procedure Law so qualifying relatives can use the same court process as the victim. They must ask the court for protection; a judge still decides whether to issue an order and whom it covers. The law took effect the day it was signed.

Then-Sen. Sue Serino, a Republican, introduced the original Senate bill in August 2022. Assemblyman Anil Beephan Jr., also a Republican, introduced the first Assembly version in February 2023. Sen. Michelle Hinchey and Assemblymember Brian Cunningham, both Democrats, carried the version that passed, with Beephan and Republican Sen. Rob Rolison as co-prime sponsors. Melanie's mother, Cheryl, led the push. The Senate passed it 61–0 in 2023, but it died in the Assembly. In 2024, the Senate passed it 60–0 and the Assembly passed it 143–5. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Nov. 25, 2024.
