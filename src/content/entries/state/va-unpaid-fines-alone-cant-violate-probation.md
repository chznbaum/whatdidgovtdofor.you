If you're on probation in Virginia and owe court fines or costs, falling behind on paying them no longer counts, by itself, as breaking your probation. Before a judge can treat it as a violation, you and your lawyer must get notice and a hearing. The judge must also find that you willfully refused to pay.

If a court has already found you indigent, either under Virginia's standard for appointing a lawyer or at any point during your criminal or traffic case, the judge must presume you can't afford to pay. Unless the judge makes a specific finding that you could pay, the alleged violation must be dismissed. You don't have to apply for this protection because it's written into the probation law. You still owe the fines and costs, though, and the court can still use its usual ways of collecting them.

Sen. Tammy Brankley Mulchi (R) carried the bill. The Senate passed it 34–6 in February 2026, and the House passed it in March with no votes against. The Senate then accepted the House's changes 40–0. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026, and it took effect July 1, 2026.
