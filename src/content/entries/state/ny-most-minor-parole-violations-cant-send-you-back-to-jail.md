If you're on parole in New York, breaking a rule that isn't a new crime, like missing curfew or an appointment, usually can't send you back to jail. A first or second violation of this kind can't lead to jail at all. After that, jail stays are capped: up to 7 days for a third violation, 15 for a fourth, and 30 after that. A few exceptions remain. Drinking can still mean jail for someone on parole for drunk driving, and absconding can bring short stays.

If you're arrested on a parole warrant, you must generally see a local judge within 24 hours, and the judge must presume you'll be released while your case goes on. You can also earn your way off parole: for every 30 days without a violation, 30 days come off your term. The state awards these credits, so you don't have to apply. A coalition that pushed for the law reported that New York's parole population fell by nearly 40% in its first year.

Sen. Brian Benjamin and Assemblymember Phara Souffrant Forrest carried the bill; Assembly correction chair David Weprin worked on it for years. It passed the Senate 38–25 and the Assembly 85–64 in June 2021. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 17, 2021, a week after Benjamin became her lieutenant governor. At the signing she moved to release up to 191 people being held for technical violations. Most of the law took effect March 1, 2022.
