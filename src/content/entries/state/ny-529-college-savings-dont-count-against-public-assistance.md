Public assistance in New York limits how much a household can have in countable resources. Applicants generally face a $2,500 limit; it is $3,750 if someone in the household is at least 60 or disabled. Current recipients may have up to $10,000. Money in a 529 qualified tuition plan does not count toward those limits or lower benefit amounts.

You don't have to apply for the exemption. It's part of how your local social services office works out your eligibility. A parent can keep putting money into a child's 529 plan, and a relative can contribute, without putting the family's cash assistance at risk.

State regulations and agency guidance already provided this exemption. State Sen. Roxanne Persaud sponsored legislation to put it directly into state law, and Assembly Member Michele Titus sponsored the Assembly version. The Senate passed it 45–17 in May 2019, and the Assembly passed it that June. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 3, 2019, and it took effect immediately.

The section of law containing this exemption must be renewed every two years. Persaud has carried those renewals as well. The latest, signed in July 2025, keeps it in force through August 22, 2027.
