If someone owes you money, such as a security deposit, a contractor's refund, or a repair bill after a fender bender, you can sue for up to $10,000 in New York City's small claims court. The court is meant for people handling their own cases without hiring a lawyer. The old limit was $5,000, so anyone owed more could recover no more than $5,000 there or use regular civil court.

To use small claims court, the person or business you're suing must live, work or have an office in the city, unless the claim is against your landlord and concerns a New York City property the landlord owns. Only individuals can file in the Small Claims Part. Its general rules bar corporations, partnerships and people who bought or were assigned someone else's claim. The higher limit applies only in the city. Outside it, city courts remain at $5,000 and town and village courts at $3,000.

The state's court system asked for the change. Chief Administrative Judge Lawrence Marks requested it on the advice of his civil practice advisory committee. Assembly Member Michael DenDekker and Senate Deputy Leader Michael Gianaris carried the bill; Assembly Members Jeffrey Dinowitz, Tremaine Wright and Richard Gottfried cosponsored it. The Senate passed it 62–0 in June 2019. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on December 16, and the court listed December 17 as the effective date.
