New York's overdose law lets people give naloxone, the medicine that reverses an opioid overdose, and protects reasonable, good-faith use under the law. Before 2020, that protection named only certain groups, such as people at risk and their families, registered overdose prevention programs, schools and public libraries. This law opened it to any person or business. It names restaurants, bars, retail stores, shopping malls, barber shops, beauty parlors, theaters, sporting and event centers, inns, hotels and motels.

Stocking naloxone is optional under this 2020 law, but a business can keep it and let staff use it. If the owner or a worker acts reasonably and in good faith under the law, they can't face criminal, civil or administrative liability solely for that action. The law treats it as first aid, and it took effect the day it was signed.

The sponsors said overdoses often happen in places open to the public. Many businesses had restricted naloxone use on their premises because they weren't sure the Good Samaritan law covered them. Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal sponsored the bill with Assembly Member Walter Mosley as co-sponsor; Sen. Pete Harckham sponsored its Senate counterpart. It passed the Assembly 138–0 in March 2020 and the Senate 60–0 that July. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 24, 2020.
