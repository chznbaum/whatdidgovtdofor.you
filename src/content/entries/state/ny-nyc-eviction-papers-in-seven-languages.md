If your landlord takes you to New York City housing court for unpaid rent, the court papers can't come in English alone. The landlord must serve a Spanish copy of the notice of petition, the paper that tells you about the case, along with the English one. A second notice in the six next most common languages tells you where to find the form on the court's website and gives a phone number for help with the eviction process. The postcard the court sends in eviction cases must carry the same notice in seven languages.

Inside housing court, the state's court system must hand out notices in those seven languages. They tell you that you can have the court proceedings, and any settlement you're asked to sign, interpreted out loud in your own language. The notices come automatically. If you need an interpreter, ask the court's Chief Clerk's Office as early as possible; court interpreters are free.

Assemblymember Latoya Joyner first sponsored a version in the 2015–16 session and carried it in three consecutive sessions. Sen. Jamaal Bailey sponsored the Senate bill that passed in 2019 and again in 2020. Bailey's memo said a 2011 executive order already required state agencies to translate key documents, but the courts weren't covered. The Senate passed the bill 61–1, the Assembly passed it 140–2, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on October 7, 2020. It took effect 180 days later.
