If you want to train for a high-demand job at a Virginia community college, the state may cover what your other aid doesn't. G3 is a "last dollar" program: after federal and state grants are applied, it pays the remaining tuition and mandatory fees and provides a textbook stipend, which can bring your tuition-and-fee cost to zero. Full-time students who receive a full federal Pell Grant can get extra help on top of that.

To qualify, you must be a Virginia resident eligible for in-state tuition, have a household income at or below 400% of the federal poverty guideline applicable to your award year, and apply for other federal and state aid you may qualify for. You must enroll in an approved program in education, health care, hospitality and culinary arts, information technology, public safety, or skilled trades, manufacturing, and construction. Credit-bearing students must take at least six credit hours per semester; approved noncredit programs may also qualify.

G3 was Gov. Ralph Northam's (D) proposal; he announced it in December 2019 with $145 million in his budget plan. House Speaker Eileen Filler-Corn (D) and Senate Majority Leader Dick Saslaw (D) carried the identical bills that wrote it into law. HB 2204 passed 93–7 in the House and 37–2 in the Senate; SB 1405 passed 39–0 in the Senate and 93–7 in the House. Northam signed both on March 29, 2021.
