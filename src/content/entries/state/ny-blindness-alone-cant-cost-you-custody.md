If you're blind and involved in a New York family case, a judge can't deny or decide your petition for custody, visitation, guardianship or adoption just because you're blind. Your blindness can come into the case only if the court finds, from evidence in the record, that it affects the child's best interests. The law covers people with 20/200 vision or worse, or a field of view of 20 degrees or less, in the better eye with correction.

The same limit applies to social services in guardianship, custody and visitation matters: the department can't deny or oppose a request solely because the petitioner, parent, guardian or custodian is blind. It also can't seek custody or guardianship of a child solely because the parent, guardian or custodian is blind. You don't have to apply for this protection. It applies automatically in covered family cases.

Assembly Member Jo Anne Simon carried the bill, and Sen. Kevin Parker sponsored it in the Senate. Simon's sponsor memo said courts and caseworkers often wrongly assume blind parents can't safely care for their kids. Versions of the bill had been introduced since 2014. The Senate passed it in two earlier sessions, but it stalled in the Assembly. In 2021 the Assembly passed it 147–0 on May 25, and the Senate passed it in June with support from both parties. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Oct. 8, 2021, and it took effect 90 days later.
