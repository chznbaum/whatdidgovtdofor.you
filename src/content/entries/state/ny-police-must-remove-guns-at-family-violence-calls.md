When New York police answer a report of a family offense, they must take any listed weapon possessed by someone arrested for or suspected of the offense. They must also take weapons in plain sight or found in a lawful search when the victim answers yes to threat or danger questions on the domestic incident report, or when an officer believes removal is needed to prevent more violence. Any weapon license issued to the arrested or suspected person goes with the weapons.

A seized weapon generally cannot be returned to its owner until at least 120 hours (five days) have passed, up from 48 under the 2020 law. A different person who proves they lawfully own it can get it back sooner if officials find no legal barrier. Otherwise, return requires a written finding that no protection order, extreme risk order, pending charge or conviction bars the owner from having the weapon. Victims do not need to apply; these rules apply when officers respond to a legally defined family offense.

Sen. Pete Harckham and Assemblymember Amy Paulin had carried versions of the bill since 2021. They paused it while the U.S. Supreme Court weighed a challenge to the federal ban on guns for people under domestic violence orders, then brought it back after the Court upheld that ban. The Senate passed it 43–19 on June 9, 2025, and the Assembly passed it two days later. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 16, 2025, and it took effect right away.
