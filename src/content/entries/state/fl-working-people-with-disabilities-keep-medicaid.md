If you're a Florida adult with a developmental disability and you get services through a Medicaid home and community-based waiver, you can hold a job and keep your Medicaid. Under the Working People with Disabilities program, an individual can earn up to 550% of the federal SSI benefit rate, which is $5,467 a month in 2026. You can also keep up to $13,000 in savings and other assets ($24,000 for a couple), and retirement accounts don't count toward that limit. Without the program, earning more could push you over Medicaid's usual limits and cost you the waiver services you depend on.

You don't have to apply. If you're 18 or older, have a job, and are enrolled in a qualifying waiver, you're enrolled automatically, though you can choose not to take part. The state Department of Children and Families must send you a written notice when you join a waiver and at least once a year after that. The notice explains how the program works, what a special needs trust is, and who to call with questions.

Florida started the program in its 2019–2020 budget, but it existed only in budget language that lawmakers had to renew every year. Rep. Allison Tant (D) sponsored the 2026 bill that wrote it into permanent law, and Sen. Jennifer Bradley (R) carried the Senate version. It passed both chambers without a single no vote, and Gov. Ron DeSantis (R) signed it on May 21, 2026.
