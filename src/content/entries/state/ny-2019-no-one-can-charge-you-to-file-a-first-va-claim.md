If you're a veteran or a veteran's family member in New York, nobody can charge you for help with a VA or state veterans' benefits matter unless federal or state law allows it. For VA benefits, that means no fee for preparing or filing your initial claim. No one can charge you for steering you to someone else, and any fee must be reasonable. Anyone you do pay needs a written fee agreement signed by both of you before work begins. No one may guarantee that you will receive a particular benefit or amount.

Paid helpers also have to tell you, out loud and in writing, that they aren't part of the VA, the state's veterans' agency, or any chartered veterans' group. Their ads must say the same thing. Free help is available from the state and county veterans' offices. Breaking these rules counts as a deceptive business practice, and if you're harmed by a violation, you can sue over it.

The law targets "pension poaching": financial planners and insurance agents who pose as benefits advocates so they can sell older and disabled veterans products they don't need. Assembly Member Walter Mosley and Sen. David Carlucci sponsored it after a similar bill passed the Assembly in the previous session. It passed both chambers without a single no vote, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on August 23, 2019. A 2025 law that took effect May 4, 2026 strengthened it: courts may impose civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation, and a person harmed by a violation may recover actual damages or $2,500, whichever is greater.
