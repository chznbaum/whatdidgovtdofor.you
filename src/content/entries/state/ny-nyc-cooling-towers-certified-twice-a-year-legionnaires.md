Legionnaires' disease is a serious pneumonia. People catch it by breathing in mist from water that carries Legionella bacteria, and rooftop cooling towers on big buildings are a common source. In summer 2025, an outbreak in Central Harlem traced to cooling towers sickened 114 people and killed seven. About 90 of them were hospitalized.

This law tightens the rules for every cooling tower in New York City. Owners used to certify once a year that their towers had been inspected, tested, cleaned, and disinfected. Now they must do it twice, every January and July. When a test finds a maintenance problem, owners must fix it as soon as possible and within 48 hours at the latest. When bacteria levels are a serious health threat, they must notify the city and clean and disinfect the tower within 24 hours. Owners must keep their records for 10 years instead of three. Maximum fines rose to $2,500 for a first violation, $7,000 for repeat violations, and $12,500 when a violation leads to a death or serious injury. You don't need to do anything: the rules apply to building owners.

State Sen. Cordell Cleare said she drafted the bill during the Harlem outbreak. Assembly Member Jordan Wright, who represents Harlem, carried it in the Assembly, where it passed in March 2026. The Senate passed it 56–4 that June, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on August 5, 2026. Most of the law took effect 90 days later.
