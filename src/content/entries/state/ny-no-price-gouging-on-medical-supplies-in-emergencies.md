During an emergency or another sudden break in the market, New York sellers can't charge wildly inflated prices for medical supplies and services, like masks, gloves and ventilators, or for other goods and services the public needs to stay healthy and safe. The ban covers everyone along the supply chain, from manufacturers and wholesalers to stores.

Before this law, the ban covered only consumer goods. That left the attorney general unable to act when sellers charged hospitals, local governments and businesses huge markups for protective gear early in the COVID-19 pandemic. The law now protects small businesses, hospitals, other health care providers and the state, too. You don't have to do anything to be protected. If you see gouging, you can report it to the attorney general, who can take the seller to court. A court can fine a seller up to $25,000 per violation, or three times what they took in from the sales if that's more, and order refunds.

Sen. Brad Hoylman and Assembly Member Nily Rozic carried the bill, and Hoylman wrote it with Attorney General Letitia James, whose office had received more than 7,000 pandemic price gouging complaints. It passed the Senate 61–1 and the Assembly 141–3 on May 27, 2020, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 6, 2020.
