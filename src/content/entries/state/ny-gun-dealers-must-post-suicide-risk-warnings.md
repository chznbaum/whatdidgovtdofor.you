Licensed gun dealers and gunsmiths in New York must post a warning in large bold type at their entrance and in at least one other spot where guns are sold. It says that having a gun in the home significantly increases the risk of suicide, of death during domestic disputes, and of accidental deaths of children and others. It also tells anyone in distress to call 988, the national suicide and crisis lifeline.

You don't need to do anything to get it. A firearms dealer has to hand each buyer a paper copy at the sale, and licensing officers give one out when they issue or change a gun license. A dealer that skips the signs or the handouts can be fined up to $1,000, jailed up to 15 days, or both, and each day counts as a new violation.

Sen. Michael Gianaris and Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz introduced the bill in 2023. It passed the Senate that June but stalled in the Assembly. Both chambers passed it in 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Oct. 9, 2024. It took effect 90 days later. In April 2025, Hochul signed an amendment from Dinowitz that names rifles and shotguns in the warning alongside other firearms.
