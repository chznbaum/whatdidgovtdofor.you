If you're in a custody or visitation case in Virginia, the judge can't limit your time with your child only because you legally use cannabis, drink alcohol, or take medicine you're allowed to have. The judge can still act if other facts show your use isn't in your child's best interest, but legal use alone isn't enough.

The law also covers drug tests. Judges can still order them, but a positive result for a legal substance, such as cannabis an adult is allowed to have, doesn't count as failing. You don't have to apply for anything. The rule has applied in every Virginia custody and visitation case since July 1, 2026.

Del. Nadarius Clark carried the bill, with Del. Virgil Thornton as a co-patron. A similar bill Clark sponsored passed in 2025, but Gov. Glenn Youngkin vetoed it. In 2026 the House passed Clark's bill 62–36. The Senate narrowed it to custody and visitation cases and passed it 22–17, and the House accepted that version 62–34. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
