Ghost guns lack required serial numbers and registration. Many are built from kits bought without background checks, making them hard to trace and available to people who might not pass one. In New York, selling, trading, or giving one away when you know or have reason to know it is a ghost gun is a class E felony. Disposing of 10 or more that way is a class D felony. The law generally bars possession by anyone other than a licensed gunsmith or firearm dealer. They must add a unique serial number and their name, then register the gun or frame with the Division of Criminal Justice Services.

The rules took effect April 26, 2022. During a six-month grace period, people could possess a ghost gun before serialization and registration or transfer it to a licensed gunsmith. Voluntarily surrendering one to designated law enforcement remains an exception to the law’s ghost-gun possession and sale provisions.

Sen. Brad Hoylman and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal sponsored the law. Nathalie Arzu, Jose Webster’s sister, advocated for it; the law honors Webster, who was shot to death in the Bronx at age 16. The Rockefeller Institute reported a 479% increase in statewide ghost-gun recoveries from 2018 to 2020. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the law in October 2021. In March 2022, she signed a follow-up from the same sponsors that clarified coverage of gunsmiths, firearm dealers, and guns without serial numbers.
