If you are on New York Medicaid or Child Health Plus, some visits with a doctor, therapist or other covered provider can be paid as telehealth when they happen by regular phone call. The 2020 law added audio-only calls to the state’s permanent telehealth statute. Before then, that statute excluded audio-only calls from reimbursable telehealth, although other remote services such as store-and-forward and remote patient monitoring already existed.

The health commissioner sets the detailed rules, and Medicaid payment is contingent on federal financial participation. Under the July 2026 Medicaid guidance, audio-only care is covered only when video is unavailable because the patient lacks equipment or connectivity, the patient prefers a phone call, and the provider can effectively deliver the service without a visual or in-person component. The provider must offer a video or in-person appointment if asked, and the phone visit must meet the same billing and documentation requirements. Ask your provider whether a service qualifies; not every service does. Medicaid lists four telehealth modalities: audio-only, audio/video, remote patient monitoring and store-and-forward.

Sen. Jen Metzger sponsored S8416 during the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic. She said the bill was meant to reach rural residents who could not use video visits because they lacked broadband or a suitable device. Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal sponsored the Assembly version, A10404-A. Both chambers passed S8416 on May 27, 2020: the Senate 61–1 and the Assembly 134–10. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 17, 2020.
