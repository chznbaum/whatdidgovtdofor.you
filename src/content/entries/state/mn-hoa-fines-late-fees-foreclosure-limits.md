If you own a home in a Minnesota HOA, condo or townhome association, this law puts limits on what the association can charge you. Starting Jan. 1, 2027, most fines can't be more than $100 per violation unless a majority of owners approve a bigger amount. The cap doesn't cover repeat violations or ones that threaten health or safety, damage property, or involve things like unauthorized rentals. Late fees on dues and special assessments are capped at $20 or 5% of what you owe, whichever is more, and interest on overdue dues is capped at 8%.

The law also makes losing your home over HOA debt harder. An association can't start a foreclosure until you're more than three months behind. Before it sends your account to a lawyer or collector, it has to send you at least three notices, one of them by certified mail. Boards must also give you 21 days to comment before they change a rule, solicit three written bids before most maintenance, construction or repair contracts over $50,000, and try to resolve your complaints or point you to the state's HOA ombudsperson. You don't need to apply for any of this. These protections apply to existing communities as well as new ones.

Sen. Eric Lucero, a Republican, and Rep. Kristin Bahner, a DFL member, wrote the bill and worked on it over two sessions with Sen. Susan Pha and Rep. Shane Mekeland. The Senate first passed it in 2025, the House passed it 100–34 in April 2026, and the Senate gave final approval 56–9. Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 12, 2026.
