Starting Jan. 25, 2027, covered social media apps can't give New York users under 18 a feed that an algorithm builds around what keeps them scrolling unless a parent agrees. Without that consent, a minor can still see content from accounts they follow or choose, arranged in a set order such as chronological. Platforms also can't send minors notifications tied to an addictive feed from midnight to 6 a.m. Eastern without a parent's OK. The rules apply only to covered platforms; one requirement is that addictive feeds account for at least 20% of users' time.

Families don't need to apply. A covered platform has to check a user's age before showing them an addictive feed, and it must offer at least one way to do that besides a government ID. It must delete or de-identify the age and consent data right after using it. A platform can't lock a teen out because a parent says no, and parents and teens can withdraw consent at any time. The attorney general enforces the law and can seek up to $5,000 per violation.

State Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Nily Rozic sponsored the bill, and Attorney General Letitia James's office helped introduce it and push it through. It passed the Senate unanimously, and only one Assembly member voted no. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 20, 2024. The law couldn't take effect until the attorney general wrote rules for it, and those were finished in July 2026.
