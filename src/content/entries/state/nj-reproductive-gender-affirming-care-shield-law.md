If you get or provide reproductive or gender-affirming care that's legal in New Jersey, the state now does more to keep other states from punishing you for it. The law covers abortion, birth control, miscarriage care, fertility treatment, and care related to gender identity, including telehealth. New Jersey won't send you to another state to face charges over that care if you weren't there when it happened. State courts won't issue other states' subpoenas or warrants over it, and police and state agencies can't help with those cases. Your providers can't hand over your records for those cases without your written consent.

Providers are protected too. Licensing boards can't discipline them, and malpractice insurers can't drop them or raise their rates, just for giving care that's legal here.

The law also makes it a crime to injure, threaten, or physically block patients or providers, or to damage a clinic, to stop that care. If it happens to you, you can sue for $1,000 per violation plus other damages. The attorney general and local prosecutors can seek fines of up to $25,000 for repeat violations. You don't need to do anything to be covered.

Senate Majority Leader Teresa Ruiz and Senate President Nicholas Scutari led the bill in the Senate. Assembly members Shanique Speight, Ellen Park, Annette Quijano and Luanne Peterpaul carried it in the Assembly, and Assembly Speaker Craig Coughlin helped lead the legislative effort. Civil rights attorney Khadijah M. Silver was instrumental in its passage. It builds on New Jersey's 2022 protections and puts into law protections that had rested on an executive order. Both chambers passed it on June 30, 2026, and Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed it on Aug. 20.
