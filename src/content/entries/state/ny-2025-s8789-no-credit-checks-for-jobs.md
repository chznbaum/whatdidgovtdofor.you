A bad credit score shouldn't cost you a job, and in New York it mostly can't. Employers, unions and employment agencies can't ask for your credit report or score, or ask you about your credit accounts, when they decide whether to hire, promote, pay, discipline or fire you. State and local agencies also can't use your credit history to turn down most licenses and permits. You don't have to do anything to be protected.

Some jobs are exempt. They include jobs where a state or federal law requires a credit check, jobs subject to a background investigation by a state agency, police and peace officer jobs, jobs that require bonding or a security clearance, and jobs with authority over $10,000 or more in funds or deals. New York City's own credit-check ban, which has narrower exemptions, still applies there.

Sen. James Sanders Jr. and Assemblymember Jeffrey Dinowitz carried the ban. Sanders argued that there is little evidence credit history predicts how well someone does a job, and that credit reports often contain errors. The Senate passed it 40–20 in April 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19, 2025. In early 2026 the two lawmakers carried a follow-up bill that makes clear state and local governments count as employers under the ban. It also broadened the exemption for jobs subject to a state-agency background investigation by removing language that had limited credit checks to appointed positions with a high degree of public trust. The Senate passed that bill 38–18, and Hochul signed it on Feb. 13, 2026. The ban took effect April 18, 2026.
