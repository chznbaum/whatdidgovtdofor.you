If you live in New York and are 16 or 17, you can fill out a voter registration form now instead of waiting until you're 18. Your registration stays on hold until your 18th birthday, and then your local board of elections signs you up automatically. You don't have to file again, but you can't vote in any election held before you turn 18.

You pre-register the same way adults register: online, by mail, or in person. The registration form asks if you're at least 16 and confirms you understand you must be 18 by Election Day to vote. The law also requires local school boards to adopt policies that encourage students to register and pre-register, and it lets them work with county boards of elections to hold registration drives in high schools. Filling out the form can't be a class requirement or a graded assignment.

Sen. David Carlucci had introduced versions of the proposal since 2011, and Assembly Member Charles Lavine sponsored the Assembly bill that became law. They argued that young people who sign up early are more likely to keep voting. The bill was part of a package of election changes, including early voting, that the Legislature passed on Jan. 14, 2019, the first month after Democrats took control of the state Senate. The vote was 47–14 in the Senate and 107–39 in the Assembly. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Jan. 24, 2019, and it took effect on Jan. 1, 2020.
