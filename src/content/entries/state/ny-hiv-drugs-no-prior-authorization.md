If your health insurance is regulated by New York and covers antiretroviral drugs, your insurer can't make you or your doctor get prior authorization before it pays for them. That covers drugs that treat HIV and drugs that prevent it, like PrEP. For a covered drug on your plan's formulary, your doctor can prescribe it or switch your treatment without waiting for prior authorization. You don't have to apply for anything. The rule applies to insurance policies and contracts regulated by New York, including individual, group, nonprofit, HMO, student, and municipal cooperative plans. It took effect on Dec. 25, 2024.

According to the bill's memo, insurers started adding prior authorization for these drugs after 2011, when New York moved drug benefits into managed care and old protections dropped away. The sponsors argued the delays were dangerous. Steady treatment keeps HIV under control, and PrEP cuts the risk of getting HIV from sex by about 99% when taken as prescribed.

State Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal first introduced versions of the bill in 2022, but both died in committee. They tried again in 2023. It passed the Senate that year but stalled in the Assembly. In 2024 it passed the Senate 62–0 in March and 57–0 after May amendments, then passed the Assembly with Rosenthal carrying it. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 28, 2024. The law doesn't cover Medicaid. In December 2025, Hochul vetoed a follow-up bill from the same sponsors that would have given Medicaid enrollees the same protection.
