Before you sign a lease in New York, it has to tell you about the home's flood risk. The lease must say whether any part of the property is in a FEMA floodplain, in the higher-risk "100-year" flood zone, or in the moderate-risk "500-year" zone on FEMA's flood maps. It must also describe any past flood damage from heavy rain, storm surge, tides or an overflowing river that the landlord knows about or reasonably should know about. Leaks from burst pipes don't count.

Every new or renewed lease must also include a notice that renters can buy flood insurance through FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program, and that a standard renter's policy usually doesn't cover flood damage. You don't have to ask for any of this. It applies to apartment leases, co-op proprietary leases and sublets.

Assembly Member Robert Carroll and Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal carried the original bill with the Rise to Resilience Coalition, which helped secure passage. The Senate passed it three times between 2021 and 2022, with the final Senate vote on May 31, 2022; the Assembly passed it on June 3. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in December 2022, and it took effect 180 days later, on June 21, 2023. Before it took effect, Carroll and Hoylman-Sigal carried this 2023 follow-up bill. It made clear that landlords must disclose the past flood damage they know or should have known about. Hochul signed it on March 3, 2023.
