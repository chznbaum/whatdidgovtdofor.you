If you have a disability that makes it hard to explain your symptoms or everyday needs, such as autism or cerebral palsy, a New York hospital can't keep your support person away. During a pandemic or a disaster emergency declared by the governor, hospitals may not adopt rules that bar that person from intake through discharge.

You, or your legal guardian, choose one main support person. It has to be your parent, your spouse or your caretaker: someone who regularly helps you and is part of your care team. You don't apply to anyone. You tell the hospital who your support person is.

The law is named for Fred D'Amico, a man with autism from Glendale, Queens. He died in a hospital on March 31, 2020, after COVID rules kept his parents, his lifelong caretakers, from his bedside. His family brought the story to Sen. Joseph Addabbo, who wrote the bill. Assemblymember Stacey Pheffer Amato carried it in the Assembly, and the Senate passed it 63–0. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 21, 2021. In February 2022, a follow-up bill from Pheffer Amato made clear that a legal guardian can also name the support person.
