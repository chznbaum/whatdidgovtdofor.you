After two years of the pandemic, New York paid a thank-you bonus to eligible front-line workers at hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, schools, and other health and mental hygiene programs. Covered jobs ranged from aides, technicians, and nurses to certain support staff. Each worker could get up to $3,000 in total. A worker could earn a bonus for up to two six-month periods, from $500 per period for averaging at least 20 hours a week to $1,500 for averaging at least 35 hours.

Workers didn't apply on their own. Their employers signed them up through a state portal and passed the money along in their pay. The program counted hours worked from October 2021 through March 2024. Part-time and temporary workers could qualify too. Under the state's program, a worker's base pay had to be $125,000 a year or less.

Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed $1.2 billion for the bonuses in her January 2022 budget as part of a plan to rebuild the health care workforce. The Legislature, led by Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie, enacted the bonus program in Part ZZ of the final budget bill. That bill passed the Senate 37–25 on April 8, 2022, and Hochul signed it the next day. By August 2024, the state said it had paid more than $2 billion to over 800,000 workers.
