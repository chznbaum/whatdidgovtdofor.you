Manufactured homes are built in a factory and cost far less than a comparable house built on site. In 2020, a new single-section manufactured home cost about $57,000, roughly 35% of the price of a similar site-built home. But Virginia's zoning rules mostly kept them in agricultural areas. State law only required localities to allow them there, and most localities didn't go further.

Since July 1, 2026, any Virginia zoning district that allows site-built houses must also allow manufactured homes that meet three conditions. The home has to be converted to real property, which means it's attached to land the owner holds. It has to sit on its own lot. And its certificate of occupancy has to be issued within five years of when it was built. Localities can't hold these homes to stricter rules than a single-family house in the same district, but historic-district protections and private covenants, including HOA restrictions, can still apply. There is no separate application for this zoning protection, but owners still must get the required permits and complete the real-property title process.

Del. Michelle Maldonado sponsored the bill, and Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg carried an identical one in the Senate. It also absorbed similar bills from Del. Nicole Cole and Del. Dan Helmer. Both chambers passed it with near-unanimous, bipartisan votes. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on March 31, 2026, one of the first bills she signed.
