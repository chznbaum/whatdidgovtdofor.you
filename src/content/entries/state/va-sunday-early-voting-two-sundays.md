Every city and county in Virginia now has to offer early in-person voting on the second and third Sundays before each election. On each of those Sundays, the voting office has to stay open for at least five hours, sometime between 11 a.m. and 5 p.m. That's on top of the two Saturdays early voting already offered. Local election officials can still add more Sundays if they want.

You don't have to sign up or apply. Any registered voter can go to their local early voting site on those Sundays, the same as on any other early voting day. This fall was the first time it applied statewide: offices opened on Oct. 18 and Oct. 25 ahead of the Nov. 3 general election. Before the law, each locality decided whether to offer Sunday voting at all, and some, like York County, never did.

Sen. Lamont Bagby of Henrico and Richmond carried the bill. As introduced, it covered only one Sunday. The Senate's elections committee expanded it to two Sundays, and the House set the five-hour minimum. The legislature's Black caucus counted it among its wins for the session. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it, and it took effect July 1, 2026.
