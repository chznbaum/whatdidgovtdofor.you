Crypto ATM scams are hard to reverse. Georgia now caps a new customer's kiosk transactions at $2,500 a day for the first 72 hours and an existing customer's at $10,000. Total fees, commissions, spread and other charges cannot exceed 18%. Kiosks must warn that crypto transactions are irreversible. A new customer deceived during the first 72 hours can get a full refund by notifying the operator and a government or law enforcement agency within five days of the last such transaction. The operator then has 72 hours to pay.

The law also lets banks and credit unions pause a transaction when they reasonably suspect financial exploitation involving someone 65 or older or a disabled adult. An institution must first train employees and adopt written review procedures. It may hold the transaction for 15 business days and extend the hold for up to 15 more. Within three business days it must notify everyone authorized on the account and any trusted contact, except anyone it reasonably suspects of taking part in the exploitation. Institutions do not have to use this power. If yours offers the option, you can name a trusted contact.

Rep. Bruce Williamson (R) introduced the bill at the state banking department's request, and Sen. Mike Hodges (R) sponsored it in the Senate. Reps. Noel Williams Jr. (R) and Johnny Chastain (R) co-sponsored it. It passed the House 161–5 and the Senate 50–0, and Gov. Brian Kemp (R) signed it on May 11, 2026.
