New Jersey's law limits when a college can hold back your transcript over debt. For a term whose credits were not paid with federal or state student aid, the school cannot withhold a transcript over $2,000 or less in non-mandatory charges. If you owe tuition, room and board or mandatory fees—or more than $2,000 in other charges—the school may require a good-faith repayment plan and its first payment before release.

For aid-funded terms, the school must provide an official transcript for the term if its charges were paid or covered by a loan or payment plan. If you attest that you need a transcript to apply for or refinance a student loan, it cannot restrict or condition release under the non-aid rules. You must request the transcript; the school must let you send it to yourself or a third party. The debt is not forgiven, and the school may still withhold your diploma. Certain students living outside the United States are excluded from the $2,000 and first-payment rules.

The law covers public and private colleges and degree-granting proprietary schools. Violations are unlawful practices under New Jersey's Consumer Fraud Act. Assembly members Linda Carter, Benjie Wimberly and Verlina Reynolds-Jackson were the prime sponsors. Sens. Andrew Zwicker and Shirley Turner sponsored the Senate version. The Assembly passed it 71–0, with two abstentions, in December 2024; the Senate passed it 39–0 in January 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it in his final act in office on Jan. 20, 2026.
