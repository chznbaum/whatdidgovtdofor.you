If your health plan is regulated by New York and includes comprehensive medical and prescription-drug coverage, it must cover medically necessary epinephrine devices used to stop a life-threatening allergic reaction. Your deductible, copays and coinsurance generally cannot bring your total cost above $100 a year. You do not need a separate application, but you do need a prescription.

There is an exception for some HSA-qualified high-deductible plans. To preserve eligibility for a health savings account, those plans may make you pay the plan's full annual deductible before the $100 cap applies, unless the epinephrine product qualifies as preventive care under federal rules. The coverage requirement applies to individual and group plans, including HMOs and nonprofit insurers, for policies issued or renewed starting January 1, 2026.

The governor's office says EpiPens once cost about $60 each, but a two-pack can now sell for more than $600. They expire after about a year, so people need to replace them regularly. A 2025 follow-up law carried by Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal extended the coverage to other FDA-approved epinephrine products, including nasal sprays.

Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Assemblymember Daniel O'Donnell carried the original bill. It passed the Senate 61–0 in May 2024, with support from both parties, and the Assembly passed it the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on December 12, 2024.
