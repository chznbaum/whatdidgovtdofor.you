Many baby monitors stream video and sound over the internet, and that can leave a child's room open to strangers. Security researchers at Rapid7 tested nine internet-connected monitors from eight companies. Eight got an F for security, and the ninth got a D. Starting January 26, 2027, a new monitor that streams over the internet can be made, sold, or offered for sale in New York only if it has security features that keep unauthorized people from watching or listening.

You don't need to apply for anything, but you should use the monitor's security features. Each covered monitor must come with a clear warning in at least 12-point type saying that it connects to the internet and telling you to use those features. The law covers devices sold only for watching a baby or child. It doesn't cover phones, security cameras, or other devices with many uses. Local consumer affairs officials can fine violators up to $250 for a first offense and up to $500 for each one after that.

Assemblymember Michaelle Solages first introduced the bill in 2023. It stalled in committee that session, and she brought it back in 2025. Sen. Andrew Gounardes carried it in the Senate. It passed the Assembly 135–0 and the Senate 60–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 28, 2026.
