Nourish New York gives emergency food providers money to buy food straight from New York farms and food makers, including produce, milk and meat, at wholesale prices. Those providers distribute it free through food banks, pantries, soup kitchens and other local programs. Families receive the food through those providers rather than from a separate state benefit.

The program started in spring 2020, when food bank lines grew and farms lost buyers like schools and restaurants. By December 2024 it had delivered about 100 million meals and bought more than 118 million pounds of New York food. The state's 2026–27 budget includes $55 million for it.

Sen. Michelle Hinchey, a Democrat who chaired the Senate Agriculture Committee, sponsored the Senate bill to make the program permanent. Its co-prime sponsor was Sen. George Borrello, the committee's top Republican. Assemblymember Catalina Cruz carried it in the Assembly, with Assemblymembers Donna Lupardo and Chris Tague helping build support in that chamber. The Senate passed it 63–0 in March 2021, the Assembly passed it that May, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 20, 2021.
