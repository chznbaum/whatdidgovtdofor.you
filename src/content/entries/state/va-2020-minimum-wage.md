For years, Virginia's minimum wage was the same as the federal one: $7.25 an hour. A 2020 law changed that. The state minimum rose to $9.50 on May 1, 2021, then to $11 on Jan. 1, 2022, and to $12 on Jan. 1, 2023. Workers didn't have to do anything; employers had to pay the new rates.

The law also brought more people under the minimum wage, including domestic workers in private homes and people paid by the piece. It planned two more steps, to $13.50 and $15, but those needed a second vote by July 1, 2024, and they didn't get one. Instead, the wage started rising each year with inflation in 2025 and reached $12.77 in 2026.

Del. Jeion Ward and Sen. Dick Saslaw sponsored the bills. Their conference deal passed both chambers on party-line votes. Gov. Ralph Northam proposed changes, and when the Senate split 20–20 on accepting them, its presiding officer broke the tie. The law was enacted on April 22, 2020. Ward later sponsored the 2026 law that takes the wage to $15 by 2028.
