If you work for a New York government, your employer has to have a written plan for a disease emergency declared by the governor. The plan lists which types of jobs must still be done in person. It explains how everyone else will work from home, how in-person shifts will be staggered to cut crowding at work and on transit, and what happens if you're exposed or get sick, including cleaning and leave. The plan also has to explain how your employer will procure enough protective gear for each essential worker on any given shift and store it for immediate access during an emergency.

You don't have to apply. If you're represented by a union, your employer had to share a draft with it, answer its recommendations in writing, and post the final plan where you can see it and online. Your employer can't retaliate against you for recommending changes. School districts and BOCES put matching rules in their school safety plans.

Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assembly Member Peter Abbate sponsored and carried companion bills after COVID killed many public workers. By that fall, New York City's largest public workers' union had lost 151 members. The legislation passed the Senate 58–2 and the Assembly 141–0 in July 2020, and Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Labor Day. A follow-up law that Abbate and Gounardes sponsored in their respective chambers set April 1, 2021 as the deadline for final plans. It also loosened some details: the PPE provision no longer required a plan for a fixed six-month supply, but still required a procurement protocol sufficient for every shift.
