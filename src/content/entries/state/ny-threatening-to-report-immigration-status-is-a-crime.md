If someone forces you to do something you have a legal right to refuse—or stops you from doing something you have a legal right to do—by threatening to report your immigration status or suspected status, New York law treats that as third-degree coercion, a class A misdemeanor. If someone gets money or other property by threatening to have removal proceedings started, that is larceny by extortion. The lowest charge is fourth-degree grand larceny, a class E felony.

Before this law, the Penal Law specifically treated immigration-reporting threats as crimes in labor- and sex-trafficking cases, but not as their own basis for coercion or extortion. Now prosecutors can use those charges outside trafficking cases when the legal requirements are met, whether the person making the threat is a boss, a landlord or someone else. You do not have to apply. The law applies statewide and took effect 30 days after it was signed.

Assembly Member Michaelle Solages and Sen. Anna Kaplan sponsored the bills. The legislation was based on model legislation from the Public Leadership Institute; California, Colorado, Maryland and Virginia had enacted similar measures. The Assembly passed it 106–41 on June 1, 2021. The Senate passed it 48–15 two days later, with all Democrats and five Republicans voting yes. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on October 8, 2021.
