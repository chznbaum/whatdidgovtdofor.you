When New Jersey pays for a public project—like a school, road, or government building—contractors must pay workers the local prevailing wage and document it in certified payroll records. This law makes it a fourth-degree crime for an employer to knowingly falsify one of those records or another record required by the Prevailing Wage Act. Knowingly lying on the state's public works contractor registration form is covered too.

Falsifying these records was already a disorderly persons offense. Under the Prevailing Wage Act, an employer could face 10 to 90 days in jail and a $100 to $1,000 fine. For the same falsification or a false registration statement, the Public Works Contractor Registration Act allows fines of $2,500 to $25,000 and up to a three-year ban from public work. Those penalties still apply. The new fourth-degree charge carries up to 18 months in prison and a fine of up to $10,000. A county prosecutor or the state labor commissioner must notify the Attorney General of a violation; the Attorney General can take over or refer it for prosecution. Workers do not have to apply.

Senators Joseph Cryan and Benjie Wimberly authored the Senate bill. Assembly members Linda Carter, Yvonne Lopez, and Verlina Reynolds-Jackson sponsored its Assembly counterpart. The measure passed with support from both parties: 53–20 in the Assembly in December 2025 and 31–6 in the Senate in January 2026. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 20, his last day in office, and it took effect immediately.
