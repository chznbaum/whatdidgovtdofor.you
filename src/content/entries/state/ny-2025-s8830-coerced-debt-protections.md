Abusers force people to take on credit cards or loans. New York lets survivors challenge qualifying debt incurred on or after June 17, 2026. Send the creditor a sworn statement and proof, such as a police report, court order, or notarized statement from a social worker. With the required documents, the creditor must stop collecting within 10 business days and review the claim within 30. During review, it cannot contact the accused person or use contact information you did not provide. If it finds the debt was coerced, it must tell credit bureaus to delete the debt information.

If a creditor sues, coerced debt is a defense. You may also sue the creditor after the required review and reconsideration. If you already paid any of the debt, you can sue the person who caused it within three years after the later of a creditor's or court's finding that it was coerced. An injured debtor can recover $1,000 plus losses and legal fees for a violation not cured within 15 days, unless the failure was a bona fide error. The Attorney General can enforce the law. The creditor-review process does not cover debt secured by personal property, and most protections exclude mortgages and real-estate debt.

Sen. Cordell Cleare and Assemblymember Linda Rosenthal carried the bill after two years of advocacy led by Urban Resource Institute. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Dec. 19, 2025. Cleare and Rosenthal sponsored the negotiated amendment, which Hochul signed on March 18, 2026.
