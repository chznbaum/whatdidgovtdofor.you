If you live in a New York nursing home, adult care facility or assisted living residence, the home and its staff cannot discriminate against you because you are gay, lesbian, bisexual or transgender, or because you have HIV. They cannot deny admission, transfer or evict you for those reasons. They also cannot deny a room-sharing request on those grounds, assign a transgender resident a gender-based room inconsistent with their gender identity, bar them from the matching restroom, or willfully and repeatedly use the wrong name or pronouns after being told. The protections do not apply when they conflict with a professionally reasonable clinical judgment based on specific medical facts.

Homes must protect your sexual orientation, transition history and HIV status from unauthorized disclosure. They must post a notice telling you how to complain to the state long-term care ombudsman, and train staff who work directly with residents at least every two years. You do not need to apply.

State Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal first introduced a version in 2018. He and Assemblymember Harry Bronson sponsored the 2023 bills. Hoylman-Sigal credited SAGE, the NEW Pride Agenda and Equality New York with helping get the bill across the finish line. It passed the Senate 55–7 and the Assembly 142–2 before Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on November 30, 2023. It took effect May 28, 2024.
