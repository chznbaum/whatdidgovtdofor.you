If you rent a rent-stabilized apartment in New York, your landlord now has to follow the same security deposit rules as landlords of other apartments. The deposit can't be more than one month's rent. When you move out, the landlord has 14 days to return it or give you an itemized list of what it kept and why. A landlord who misses that deadline loses the right to keep any of it. It can keep money only for unpaid rent or utilities, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or moving and storage costs, and it has to prove those charges are reasonable.

You also have the right to inspect the home with the landlord before you move in, and the landlord must notify you of your right to request an inspection before you move out so you can fix problems first. You don't need to apply. The rules cover leases and renewals signed on or after Nov. 15, 2025.

The gap took several sessions to close. Sen. Brian Kavanagh and former Assemblymember Daniel O'Donnell carried versions starting in 2021. Assemblymember Micah Lasher sponsored the final Assembly bill in 2025, and Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the change in her State of the State that January. It passed the Senate 38–23 in April and the Assembly 95–50 in May. Hochul signed it on Oct. 16, 2025.
