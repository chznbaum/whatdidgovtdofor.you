If you work at a big New York warehouse and are held to a speed quota, your employer has to give you a written description of every quota you're under, in your primary language. You can't be disciplined or fired for missing a quota nobody told you about. You also can't be required to meet a quota that keeps you from taking your meal or rest breaks or using the bathroom. You can ask for a written description of your quotas at any time. If you believe you were disciplined for missing one, or that meeting one denied you a break or bathroom access, you can also ask for your last 90 days of work speed data. The employer has 14 days to respond. Retaliation is illegal. If your employer acts against you within 90 days of your first information request in a calendar year, or of a complaint about a quota, the law presumes retaliation.

The law covers employers with at least 100 workers at one warehouse or 1,000 across the state. Drivers and couriers aren't covered. You don't have to sign up for anything. To report a violation, contact the state Labor Department or the Attorney General.

Sen. Jessica Ramos and Assemblymember Latoya Joyner carried the bill, and the Retail, Wholesale and Department Store Union pushed for its introduction. The Senate passed it 45–18, with every Democrat and two Republicans voting yes. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in December 2022 after agreeing to changes that she signed in March 2023. Those changes raised the statewide threshold from 500 workers to 1,000, gave employers more time to respond to requests, and pushed the start date to June 19, 2023.
