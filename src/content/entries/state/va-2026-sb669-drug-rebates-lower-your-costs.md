Drug companies pay rebates to the pharmacy benefit managers, or PBMs, that run prescription coverage for health plans. Patients often never see that money. Starting July 1, 2027, a PBM that negotiates rebates for a Virginia-regulated plan must pass along 100% of them. The money has to go either to the plan, to offset enrollees' deductibles, copays and coinsurance and lower premiums, or straight to you at the pharmacy counter to cut what you pay. PBMs must also use "pass-through" pricing, so they can't quietly keep a markup on each drug.

You don't need to do anything. The rules cover state-regulated health plans and, for the first time, the state employee health plan. They don't cover self-funded employer plans, Medicaid, CHIP or Medicare drug plans. The law also limits when PBMs may reverse or reduce pharmacies' payments after the fact and spells out prohibited retaliation against pharmacies. It also orders the State Corporation Commission to study whether insurers push patients toward pharmacies they own and to report by December 2027.

Sen. Aaron Rouse (D) carried the bill, and Sen. Mark Peake (R) folded his own PBM bills into it. Del. Katrina Callsen (D) carried the identical House version. It passed both chambers with no votes against. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on March 31, 2026.
