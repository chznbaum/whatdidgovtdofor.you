If your health coverage is a large group plan regulated by New York, the plan must cover PrEP and PEP. PrEP is medicine you take to keep from getting HIV, and it cuts the risk of getting HIV from sex by about 99% when taken as prescribed. PEP is a short course of HIV drugs you start after a possible exposure, such as a needle stick at work, sex without a condom, or a sexual assault. The law also covers older "grandfathered" large group plans that can skip some federal preventive-care rules.

You don't need to apply. The requirement applies to policies issued, renewed, modified, altered, or amended on or after Dec. 21, 2022. When PrEP is provided in line with the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force's A-rated recommendation, you pay no deductible, copayment, or coinsurance. A plan may impose cost sharing for PEP.

Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal and Assemblymember Daniel O'Donnell carried the bill. It passed the Assembly in 2021 and again in 2022 before clearing the Senate in June 2022. Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins brought it to the Senate floor. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it in December 2022 after agreeing with lawmakers on changes. Those changes passed early in 2023 and were signed that March. They narrowed the law from every plan with drug coverage to large group plans and allowed some cost sharing.
