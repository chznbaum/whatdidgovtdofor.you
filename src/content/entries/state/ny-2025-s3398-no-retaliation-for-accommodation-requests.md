New York's Human Rights Law already gives you the right to a reasonable accommodation. At work, that might be a modified schedule or workstation because of a disability or pregnancy, or a schedule change for a religious observance. In housing or a public place, it might be a change to rules because of a disability. This law makes it illegal to punish you for asking. An employer, landlord, business, or government office covered by the law can't retaliate because you made the request.

You don't have to apply for this protection. The new express language took effect when the law was signed on December 5, 2025, and applies to actions filed on or after that date. If you're punished for asking, you can file a complaint with the state Division of Human Rights or go to court.

The law closes a gap in the statute's wording. Several state appeals courts had treated an accommodation request alone as unprotected, although lawmakers declared that the amendment clarifies existing law and does not mean such retaliation was legal before it passed. Federal law and New York City's Human Rights Law already expressly protected accommodation requests. Sen. Andrew Gounardes first introduced the fix in the 2023–24 session. He brought it back in 2025, and Assembly Member Sarah Clark carried it in the Assembly. The Senate passed it 60–0 in June 2025, the Assembly approved it 135–0 a week later, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that December.
