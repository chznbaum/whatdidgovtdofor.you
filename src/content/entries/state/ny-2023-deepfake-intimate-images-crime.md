New York has made it a crime since 2019 to share someone's intimate images without their consent to hurt them, a practice often called revenge porn. Before this law, that crime covered only real photos and videos. Now it also covers "deepfakes": images made with AI or edited to look real, using someone's face or body, that show the person nude or in a sexual act.

The crime applies when someone shares or posts a fake image to harm another person's emotional, financial or physical well-being, knowing or having reason to know the person shown didn't agree to it. It's a class A misdemeanor. That means up to 364 days in jail and a standard fine of up to $1,000. If it happens to you, you don't apply for anything. You can report it to police, since the law treats a realistic fake like the real thing.

State Sen. Michelle Hinchey sponsored the bill, and Assemblymember Amy Paulin carried it in the Assembly. Paulin said deepfakes are often used to manipulate women and can have lifelong consequences. The Senate passed the bill 61–0 in May 2023, and the Assembly passed it that June. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 29, 2023, and it took effect 60 days later.
