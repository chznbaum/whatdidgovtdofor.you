New Jersey's Family Leave Act lets eligible workers take up to 12 weeks off in a 24-month period to bond with a new child or care for a seriously ill family member, then return to the same job or an equivalent one. Until July 2026, it covered private employers with 30 or more workers worldwide; state and local government agencies were covered regardless of size. It now covers private employers with 15 or more workers, and employees qualify after 3 months and 250 hours worked in the preceding 12 months.

NJFLA leave itself is unpaid, but many workers can collect Family Leave Insurance while they are out. In 2026, that pays 85% of average weekly wages, capped at $1,119 a week. The same law separately gives job-restoration rights to workers receiving Family Leave Insurance or Temporary Disability Insurance when NJFLA or FMLA does not already protect their leave, with no minimum employer size or work history with the current employer. The law also lets workers choose the order in which they use earned sick leave and state benefits, though they cannot receive both at once. Cash benefits require a separate state or private-plan claim; NJFLA job protection starts with notice to the employer.

Assembly members Annette Quijano and Verlina Reynolds-Jackson and Assembly Speaker Craig Coughlin were the Assembly prime sponsors. Sens. Paul Moriarty and Andrew Zwicker carried the Senate bill. The Assembly first passed a version that would eventually cover employers with as few as five workers in 2024. After business opposition, the Senate set the private-employer cutoff at 15. The Assembly gave final approval 46–25 in January 2026, and Gov. Phil Murphy signed the law on January 17, shortly before leaving office.
