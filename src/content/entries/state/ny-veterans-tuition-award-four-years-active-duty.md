New York's Veterans Tuition Award pays for college or job training up to the cost of SUNY undergraduate tuition, or your actual tuition if it's lower. For years, it went only to veterans who had served in combat or in the combat theaters of Vietnam, the Persian Gulf, or Afghanistan. Since July 1, 2025, a veteran who served at least four years on active duty can qualify too, whether or not they saw combat. National Guard members qualify once their active-duty deployments add up to four years.

You have to apply through the state's Higher Education Services Corporation, which takes applications year-round. You must be a New York resident, not dishonorably discharged, and enrolled at an approved school or vocational program, either full time or part time. There's no income limit. For most undergraduate programs, the award can cover up to four years of full-time study; approved five-year programs can receive a fifth year. Graduate study is capped at three years and vocational training at two. The award isn't reduced by Montgomery GI Bill benefits or a Pell Grant, but you cannot receive it if Chapter 33 or Yellow Ribbon benefits cover your tuition.

Sen. Jessica Scarcella-Spanton and Assemblymember Marianne Buttenschon sponsored and carried the expansion. It passed the Senate 61–0 in June 2024, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it that November. A follow-up chapter amendment set the four-year service threshold and July 2025 start date. It also passed the Senate 61–0, and Hochul signed it on February 14, 2025.
