If you provide health care services for an employer covered by New York's health care whistleblower law, the employer can't fire, demote, or otherwise punish you for reporting a workplace practice that breaks a safety law or rule and puts workers at risk. You're also protected if you object to that practice or refuse to take part in it. Before 2020, the state's health care whistleblower law covered only problems with patient care. This law added unsafe conditions for workers, and it lets you take a violation affecting patient or worker safety to a news outlet or a public social media forum, not just a supervisor or a government agency.

You don't need to apply. In most cases, you first have to raise the problem with a supervisor and give the employer a fair chance to fix it. If you're reporting the violation, you can skip that step when there's an imminent threat to public health, a specific patient, or a specific worker and you reasonably believe going to a supervisor won't fix it. If you're punished anyway, you can sue for reinstatement, back pay and benefits, and your legal costs.

Sen. Diane Savino and Assemblymember Karines Reyes sponsored the bill after early COVID-19 reports of PPE shortages, and of nurses and other staff being fired or threatened for speaking out. The Senate passed it 61–1 on May 27, 2020, and the Assembly passed it the same day. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on June 17, 2020, and it took effect immediately.
