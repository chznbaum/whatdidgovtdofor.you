When this law passed, New York generally provided public education through the end of the school year in which a student turned 21. For many students with disabilities, the pandemic took that last stretch of school. Remote classes couldn't replace hands-on teaching, job training, and planning for life after school. This law let school districts keep serving students who turned 21 during the 2021–22 school year. Services could continue through the 2022–23 and 2023–24 school years, until the student finished their IEP services or turned 23, whichever came first.

To qualify, a student had to be enrolled in the district and getting IEP services in 2021–22. The law allowed districts to serve these students but didn't require it, so families had to make their case to the district, often with records showing what their child lost during COVID.

Assemblymember Tom Abinanti and Sen. Pete Harckham sponsored the bill. A year earlier they had sponsored a similar law for students who turned 21 in 2019–20 or 2020–21. Assemblymember Angelo Santabarbara cosponsored this bill. The Senate passed it 61–0, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on June 13, 2022. It expired on June 30, 2024.
