If police or any other law enforcement officer in New York arrests you or holds you in custody, the law says they must attend to your medical and mental health needs. That means getting you reasonable help and treatment, in good faith. You don't have to apply for anything; the duty applies to every arrest.

The law can be enforced. If you don't get that care and as a result suffer a serious physical injury, or an injury or condition gets significantly worse, you can sue the officer or agency responsible. A court can award damages, court costs, and your lawyer's fees. This adds to any other legal claims you might have.

State Sen. Jamaal Bailey of the Bronx sponsored the bill in the Senate, and Nathalia Fernandez, then an Assembly member, sponsored it in the Assembly. The law added an explicit duty to New York's Civil Rights Law and a state-law right to sue. Bailey said that care had often been denied, or delayed so long that people suffered needlessly. The bill passed both chambers on June 9, 2020, the Senate by 60–2, as part of a package of police reforms that followed the killing of George Floyd. Most police unions opposed the package. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed the bill on June 15, 2020.
