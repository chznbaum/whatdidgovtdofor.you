New York has let nursing home residents sue a home that violates their rights since the 1970s, after a nursing home fraud and abuse scandal. The rights covered include those set by a contract, a law, or a regulation. If the home can't show it took every reasonable step to prevent the harm, it owes damages: at least a quarter of its daily per-patient rate for each day the injury lasts. Punitive damages are possible when the violation was willful or reckless.

Many residents are too sick to sue, or die before a case begins. This law says plainly that a resident's legal representative or estate can bring the same lawsuit and win the same relief. A New York appeals court had already allowed an estate's claim in 2021, so lawyers saw the change as making existing rights clear in law, not as creating new ones. It applies to suits filed on or after August 17, 2022. No one has to apply. The right is part of the law.

Assembly Member Richard Gottfried sponsored the bill, and Sen. Brad Hoylman (now Hoylman-Sigal) carried it in the Senate. The Assembly had passed versions of it in six sessions, starting in 2007–08, but none passed the Senate. In 2022 it passed both chambers without a single no vote, 144–0 in the Assembly and 63–0 in the Senate. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on August 17, 2022.
