If you're deaf or hard of hearing, you can now catch a new movie in Virginia with the words right on the screen, and you don't need a special device. Theater companies with five or more locations in the state must show open captions on any film that plays at least seven times a week for more than a week, as long as the studio supplies captions. Drive-ins are exempt.

In a film's first two weeks, a big chain must run at least four captioned showings, and at least one has to fall at a weeknight, Friday night, or weekend time people can actually make. From the third week on, you can ask for a captioned showing, and the theater has 72 hours to add one. It doesn't have to change a showing that had already sold tickets before you asked. Theater companies with four or fewer locations must add a captioned showing within eight days of a request. Theaters must post contact details for requests on their websites, list captioned showings like any other marked "OC", and provide working closed-caption devices. You can report a theater that doesn't follow the rules to the Attorney General's Office of Civil Rights.

Del. Phil Hernandez of Norfolk, whose sister is deaf, carried the bill after deaf and hard-of-hearing advocates pushed for it. Sen. Angelia Williams Graves carried an identical Senate bill. The House passed it 79–17, with Republicans split. The Senate passed it 23–17, with two Republicans joining every Democrat. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 6, 2026, and the law took effect July 1.
