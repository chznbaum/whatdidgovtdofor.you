If you're a student at any SUNY or CUNY campus, your school has to help you get medication abortion, the pills used to end an early pregnancy. That covers all 89 public campuses in the two systems, community colleges included. Each campus picks one of two ways to do it. It can employ or contract with clinicians licensed to prescribe the pills, so you can get care through the school. Or it can give you information and refer you to authorized providers. There is no separate state application; check your campus health or student-services website to learn which route it uses.

The law took effect on Aug. 1, 2023. SUNY's and CUNY's boards of trustees must report to the governor and legislative leaders on which option each campus uses and how many prescribers each one has. The first report was due within two years, and new reports are due every year after that.

Assembly Member Harvey Epstein first introduced a campus medication-abortion bill in November 2019 and sponsored the bill that passed the Assembly on April 18, 2023. Sen. Cordell Cleare carried the Senate version, which passed the next day by 42 to 18. Democrats supplied most of the yes votes, and two Republicans joined them. Gov. Kathy Hochul had also proposed campus access in her January 2023 State of the State, and she signed the bill on May 2, 2023.
