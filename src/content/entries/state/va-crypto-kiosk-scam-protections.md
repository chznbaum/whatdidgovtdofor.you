Crypto kiosks look like ATMs but turn cash into cryptocurrency, and scammers often tell victims to feed money into them. Starting July 1, 2027, a company that runs these machines in Virginia needs a license from the State Corporation Commission. Each kiosk must post a plain warning that it can be used to defraud you. Operators can't call the machines "ATMs."

If you're new to a kiosk company, your deposits are held for 48 hours, and you can cancel and get all of your money back during that time. New users can put in up to $2,000 a day. Anyone else can put in up to $5,000 a day, and no one can go over $10,000 a month. Fees can't top 18% of a transaction. If you were scammed, report it to the operator within 90 days and file a police report or sworn statement within 120 days, and the operator must refund your fees. Operators must also verify IDs, staff a live help line, and work to spot fraud. Violations can bring fines of up to $1,000 each, and the attorney general can sue under the state's consumer protection law.

Sen. Saddam Salim developed the proposal through a legislative committee in 2025 and carried SB 489 in the Senate. Del. Michelle Maldonado carried the identical HB 665 in the House, and the two worked with the kiosk industry on the final version. The House first passed HB 665 84–13. The Senate passed a substitute 40–0, and the House accepted it 84–10. Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed it on April 13, 2026.
