Starting January 1, 2027, a trained pharmacist working at a Georgia-licensed pharmacy or hospital can dispense self-administered hormonal birth control—including pills, patches and rings—or give contraceptive shots without a patient-specific prescription. Pharmacists can choose whether to participate.

Before providing birth control, the pharmacist must review a self-screening questionnaire, assess whether the medication is appropriate, provide counseling and give you a written visit summary. If it is not safe for you, the pharmacist cannot provide it and may refer you to a doctor or another practitioner. The pharmacist must also advise you to consult a practitioner.

If your health plan covers outpatient prescription drugs, it must cover at least a three-month supply the first time you obtain a covered contraceptive drug and a 12-month supply on later fills, up to 12 months in each 12-month period. Georgia Medicaid must provide the same supply amounts to women recipients.

Rep. Beth Camp, a Concord Republican, was the lead House sponsor. Sen. Kay Kirkpatrick, a Marietta Republican, carried the bill in the Senate, and House Speaker Pro Tempore Jan Jones was a co-author. The Senate passed the final substitute 49–1, and the House agreed to it 158–11. Gov. Brian Kemp signed it on May 11, 2026.
