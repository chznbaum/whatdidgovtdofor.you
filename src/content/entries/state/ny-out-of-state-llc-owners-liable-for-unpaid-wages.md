Many businesses are set up as limited liability companies, which usually shields their owners from the company's debts. New York carved out an exception for workers: when an LLC doesn't pay what it owes them, the ten owners with the biggest stakes can be held personally responsible for the unpaid wages. Before this law, that rule covered only LLCs formed in New York. Since Feb. 10, 2020, it also covers LLCs formed in other states, as long as the work was done in New York.

To use it, you send each owner you want to hold liable a written notice within 180 days after your work ends. If you win a judgment against the company and a formal attempt to collect it comes back unpaid, you have 90 days to sue those owners. Any one of them can be made to pay the whole amount. The ten owners are the ones with the largest shares when the unpaid work began. An owner who pays more than their share can collect from the others; you don't have to.

Assembly Member Phil Steck first introduced the bill in 2016 and carried it through three legislative sessions. Then-Sen. David Valesky sponsored its Senate versions in 2016 and 2017, and Sen. James Skoufis carried the final Senate version in 2019. The final sponsor memo said companies shouldn't escape the rule just by forming in another state. It passed the Assembly 133–6 in March 2019 and the Senate 61–1 that May. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed it on Dec. 12, 2019.
