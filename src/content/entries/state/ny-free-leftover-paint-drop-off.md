If you have half-used cans of paint in the basement, you can drop them off for free at one of 392 year-round sites across New York, most of them paint stores, plus local government facilities and reuse shops. Every site takes up to 5 gallons a visit, and it covers latex and oil-based house paint, primers, stains, sealers and clear coatings like varnish. You don't need to sign up. If you have 100 gallons or more, you can ask for a free pickup.

The law requires paint makers to fund and run the program, through a nonprofit called PaintCare, under state environmental regulators' oversight. It's paid for by a small fee on each container of new paint: 45 cents for a quart, 95 cents for a 1- to 2-gallon can, and $1.95 for anything up to 5 gallons. That moves the cost off local taxpayers. Before the law, counties and towns paid for most paint collection, and many people just threw paint in the trash. Sen. Tom O'Mara's office estimated the change would save local governments about $25 million a year.

Sen. Tom O'Mara, a Republican, and Assemblymember Al Stirpe, a Democrat, sponsored versions of the bill beginning in 2015. They passed the Senate several times but stalled in the Assembly. In 2019, Sen. Tim Kennedy and Assemblymember Steve Englebright sponsored its Senate and Assembly versions, which passed 62–0 and 138–3. Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed the law that December, and collection began in May 2022.
