From May 11 until New York's COVID-19 state of emergency ended, this law barred electric, gas, water, phone, cable and internet providers from cutting off homes for unpaid bills. It also covered qualifying small businesses with 25 or fewer employees. Covered service cut off for nonpayment had to be restored within 48 hours; for cable and internet, that applied only to service cut on or after May 11 and generally after a customer asked. Local governments also could not put liens on covered property over unpaid bills.

The emergency ended June 24, 2021. Customers whose finances had changed because of COVID got 180 more days of protection if they contacted their provider and certified the hardship. Providers had to offer them a payment plan with no down payment, late fees or penalties. The bills still had to be paid. That protection expired Dec. 21, 2021. Municipal water providers still had to give 30 days' notice before a shutoff or lien through June 30, 2022. By Sen. Kevin Parker's estimate, about 700,000 households owed energy utilities when the main protection ended.

Sen. Kevin Parker and Assembly Member Diana Richardson carried the bill, known as the Parker-Richardson Act. Gov. Andrew Cuomo had separately proposed extending emergency shutoff protections to cable and internet. The Legislature passed its version outside the budget. It passed the Senate 54–9, with 11 Republicans joining all 43 Democrats, and passed the Assembly on March 31, 2021. Cuomo signed it May 11.
