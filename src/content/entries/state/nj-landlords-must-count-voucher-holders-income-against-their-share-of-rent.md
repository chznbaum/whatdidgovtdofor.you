Many landlords require renters to earn a set multiple of the rent. For someone with a housing voucher, a full-rent test can shut them out even when the voucher pays most of it. New Jersey already treated those tests as discriminatory. This law put the rule directly into the state statute: a housing provider covered by the Law Against Discrimination can base a minimum-income or financial test only on the part of the rent the tenant pays. Covered providers also can't refuse a lawful payment source or post ads like "no Section 8."

The law also defines lawful income broadly. The state's Division on Civil Rights lists child support, alimony, SSI, unemployment, disability and veterans' benefits, government vouchers and subsidies, and nonprofit rental help. The law generally exempts an owner renting one unit in a two-family home they occupy or rooms in their one-family home, unless the housing is publicly assisted. Lenders and other businesses also can't discriminate based on these sources. No application is required. If you think you've been turned away, you can file a complaint with the Division on Civil Rights.

Then-Assembly members Verlina Reynolds-Jackson, Linda Carter and Benjie Wimberly were primary sponsors of the Assembly bill. Sens. Angela McKnight and Shirley Turner sponsored the Senate companion; Wimberly, by then a senator, cosponsored it. The bill passed the Assembly 42–25 in January 2025 and the Senate 25–13 that December. Gov. Phil Murphy signed it on Jan. 12, 2026, and it took effect right away.
