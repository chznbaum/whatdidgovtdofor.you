Illnesses from the World Trade Center site can show up decades later. Under New York law, people who did rescue, recovery or cleanup work in the year after 9/11 can protect their right to claim workers' comp or a disability pension later, but they first have to register that work. The deadline was Sept. 11, 2026, and this law moves it to Sept. 11, 2030.

If you worked at Ground Zero, the Fresh Kills landfill, the barges, the piers or the morgues between Sept. 11, 2001 and Sept. 12, 2002, you can file Form WTC-12 with the state Workers' Compensation Board. The state urges you to file even if you aren't sick now. Public workers file a separate notice with their retirement system. The law also helps people who became disabled between 2021 and 2025. They now have until 2030 to file claims, and the board must take another look at claims it turned down for missing the deadline.

Sen. Robert Jackson and Assemblymember Charles Fall sponsored the bill. It passed the Senate 61–0 on June 2, 2026, and the Assembly passed it the next day. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed it on Sept. 10, 2026, the day before the attacks' 25th anniversary, along with two other bills that extend 9/11 benefits.
