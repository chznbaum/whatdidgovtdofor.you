The MTA runs New York City's subways and buses, the Long Island Rail Road and Metro-North. Its approved 2025-29 plan directs $68.4 billion to fixing and modernizing that system. It calls for 2,000 new subway and commuter rail cars, modern signals, 500 zero-emission buses, and full accessibility upgrades at more than 65 stations. It also funds design, site preparation and the start of construction for the Interborough Express between Brooklyn and Queens. Riders do not apply; the benefits arrive as projects are built.

The plan originally had a funding gap of more than $33 billion. In 2025, Gov. Kathy Hochul, Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins and Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie agreed on a package that allowed it to win approval. It committed $3 billion each from the state and New York City and redirected up to $1.2 billion from Penn Station. The framework also assumes $14 billion in federal support and requires the MTA to find another $3 billion. The State Comptroller says that last $3 billion remains unidentified and the federal money is not guaranteed.

Most of the new revenue is expected from the regional payroll tax paid by employers. Employers with more than $2.5 million in quarterly payroll now pay the higher top rate. Roughly 10,000 small businesses pay less, and self-employed people with $150,000 or less in earnings attributed to the transit district no longer owe the tax. The Senate passed the revenue bill 35-27, with every Republican and five Democrats voting no. Hochul signed the transportation and revenue bills on May 9, 2025, and the capital-projects bill carrying the state's $3 billion appropriation on May 20.
