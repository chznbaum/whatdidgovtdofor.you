Some Atlanta water customers have opened bills showing water use many times higher than normal, with no leak to explain it. The city's appeals board could hear their cases, but it had no power to lower the bill even when it found evidence that the city made the mistake. One business owner received a $29,695.67 bill. A then-deputy watershed commissioner wrote that department-caused leakage was responsible and gave a $219.24 corrected balance, but the city later said that correction was made in error.

This ordinance gives the board authority to order a credit. To qualify, the spike must cover one billing period and exceed 300% of your average use for the prior year. You also need steady use for the year before and three months after the spike, plus an inspection by a licensed plumber or leak specialist within 30 days of the bill's payment due date that finds no leak. The spike cannot be part of a seasonal pattern such as summer watering.

You first file an adjustment request with the Department of Watershed Management. If the department denies it, you can appeal within 14 days of receiving its decision. If the board finds a city recording error, the standard formula leaves you responsible for 150% of normal use and credits the rest; an alternative calculation is allowed when that comparison is not representative. Relief is limited to one billing period in any rolling 18 months.

Council member Liliana Bakhtiari sponsored the ordinance with 12 co-sponsors. The council adopted it 15–0 on Oct. 19, 2026. The credits end once the city has fully switched to smart water meters.
