If you live in one of the pilot's 35 affordable buildings in the Bronx or Upper Manhattan, you can get home internet at no charge during the three-year pilot. About 2,200 households are covered, including many that receive Section 8 rental assistance. The New York Public Library manages outreach, enrollment, installation and ongoing service. Its staff also offer free one-on-one technology help.

HPD is using $3.25 million in federal Section 8 funding for the pilot. HPD owns the network equipment, while the library operates it. About half of the pilot funding was set aside for minority- and women-owned firms to install, maintain and operate the infrastructure.

The program began inside HPD. Chief Digital Equity Officer Dave Seliger said he came up with the idea of a city-run network for households receiving Section 8 assistance. Mayor Eric Adams previewed the pilot in his 2025 State of the City, and Acting HPD Commissioner Ahmed Tigani and New York Public Library President Anthony Marx helped launch it that July as Liberty Link. It is now called Neighborhood Internet. The city's November 2026 internet plan says the pilot will serve about 2,200 households across 35 buildings, so the full rollout is still underway. What tenants might pay after the three-year pilot has not been decided.
