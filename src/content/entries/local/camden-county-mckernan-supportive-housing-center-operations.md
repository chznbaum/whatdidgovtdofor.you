Camden County built a new home base for people living without housing: the Martin McKernan Supportive Housing Center, on the county's Lakeland campus in Blackwood. It has 60 private studio apartments, each with its own bathroom and kitchenette. Fifty are transitional units and 10 are for people who need emergency shelter. The operating contract pays Volunteers of America Delaware Valley to staff the center around the clock.

When services begin, people will not need a traditional referral, and substance use will not disqualify them. Residents will work with a case manager and can get addiction counseling, job help, health care, and help finding permanent housing. The county dedicated the building on Oct. 23, 2026; NJ PEN reported that shelter and services were scheduled to start in January 2027.

On July 16, 2026, the Board of Commissioners, led by Commissioner Director Louis Cappelli Jr., approved the $5,567,660 contract. Only $537,842 was available when it was approved; the rest depends on the county adopting its 2027, 2028, and 2029 budgets. Most of the building's construction cost was paid with remaining federal COVID relief money, with county bonds covering the rest. The center is part of the county's push toward functional zero homelessness.
