If you live in a Broward County condo or another residential building with an elevator covered by a service maintenance contract, the owner or condo association must post an elevator outage plan where residents can see it. The plan must list the contracted elevator company and, unless the contract prohibits it, a backup or alternate registered company. It also must explain how to report outages, what response steps to expect, how residents will get updates, and what help is available for people with mobility limitations. If the building has no working elevator for more than 24 hours and the contracted company cannot restore service within that time, the owner or association must seek another registered elevator company that can make the repairs as quickly as possible, unless the contract prohibits that.

You don't have to apply for anything. Failure to post or maintain the plan carries a $250 fine for a first violation and $500 for a repeat violation. An inspector does not have to offer time to fix the violation before issuing a citation, and each day can count as a separate violation. The fine schedule does not list a separate penalty for failing to seek another repair company.

Mayor Mark Bogen (D) directed the county attorney to draft the ordinance in December 2025 and sponsored the final measure. Commissioners Nan Rich (D) and Hazelle Rogers (D) asked to be added as cosponsors; Rich also said she regularly heard from older residents who could not use the stairs. The county commission enacted it 9–0 on June 9, 2026.
