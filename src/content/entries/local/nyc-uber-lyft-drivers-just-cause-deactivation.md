Uber and Lyft can shut off a driver's account with one tap, and for many drivers that means losing their income overnight. New York City's Local Law 52 would change that. The apps could deactivate a driver only for just cause, for a genuine economic reason, or because the law requires it. In most cases they would have to give 14 days' written notice first. Account sharing, a pattern of repeated fraud, and serious misconduct such as violence, sexual harassment, or discrimination could still mean immediate removal.

The company would have to prove it had a valid reason. A driver who thinks they were cut off unfairly could ask the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection to investigate or could go to court. If the deactivation was wrongful, the driver could get their account back and back pay. The protections would start after a driver's first 30 days driving for that service.

Council Member Shekar Krishnan introduced the bill in 2024. Drivers organized by the New York Taxi Workers Alliance campaigned for it. The City Council passed it in December 2025, and Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it on his last day in office. The Council overrode the veto on January 29, 2026.

The law never took effect. A week before its July 28, 2026 start date, a federal judge granted Uber and Lyft a preliminary injunction blocking it while their lawsuits continue. The city appealed, and the Second Circuit appeal was still pending as of November 5, 2026.
