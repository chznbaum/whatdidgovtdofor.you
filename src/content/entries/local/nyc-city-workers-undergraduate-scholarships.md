If you work full time for New York City government, the city's scholarship program now offers help toward a bachelor's or associate degree. Ten colleges took part in the first round: Adelphi, Albertus Magnus, Columbia's School of General Studies, the CUNY School of Labor and Urban Studies, Fordham, Lehman, Marist, Metropolitan College of New York, Montclair State, and SUNY Purchase. The city expects more schools to join.

You apply through your agency's personnel office, and you also have to be admitted by the school. You need a high school diploma or equivalent, and you have to stay a full-time city employee and a student in good standing. Classes are on your own time. There's no minimum time on the job to qualify, and you don't have to keep working for the city after you finish. The first application window ran from March 30 to April 27, 2026. The city said selected participants were expected to begin their studies that fall.

The colleges pay for the scholarships themselves, so the size of the award depends on the school. The city pays nothing toward them. For more than 50 years, the city's scholarship program helped workers only with graduate school, even though nearly half of the city workers in a recent sample of about 100,000 had no undergraduate degree. Mayor Zohran Mamdani opened the program to undergraduates. The Department of Citywide Administrative Services, led by Commissioner Yume Kitasei, runs it.
