If police stop you on a bike or e-bike in New York City for running a red light, rolling through a stop sign or riding the wrong way, you get a traffic ticket, the same kind a driver gets for those violations. The same goes for ignoring a traffic sign and riding under the influence. Since March 27, 2026, these stops no longer bring a criminal summons. Reckless riding can still get one, as reckless driving can.

The change ends a crackdown Mayor Eric Adams and Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch began in April 2025. A criminal summons meant appearing in person at criminal court, often losing hours of work, and a skipped court date could bring a warrant for your arrest. Most of these cases were dismissed in the end. Immigrant advocates also warned that the crackdown left undocumented delivery workers at risk from federal immigration enforcement. Streetsblog counted 19,039 criminal summonses for violations cyclists can receive from the start of the crackdown through March 2026. The NYPD data often omit vehicle type, so the outlet described the cyclist count as an inference from the sudden surge after the policy began.

You don't have to do anything to get this protection. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the change on March 18, 2026, and said cyclists should be treated like others on the road. In June, Council Member Frank Morano and other Staten Island residents sued to void the change. The plaintiffs reported that oral argument is scheduled for Jan. 26, 2027.
