Hampton is using $4.6 million in federal money to fight gun violence where it happens most. The plan calls for neighborhood offices in five areas with high rates of shootings. Each office is meant to bring help close to home: case managers, counseling and mental health care, conflict-resolution programs for young people, job training, and wellness programs. The grant also pays for more police patrols in those areas. The city's goal is to cut gun violence there in half by the time the grant ends.

The program focuses on the small number of people most likely to shoot or be shot, along with their families and friends. The city's Office of Youth and Young Adult Opportunities runs it with the Hampton Police Division. The first center, the Urgency of N.O.W. Center at 21 Rip Rap Road, is open on weekdays and helps both kids and adults with jobs and housing. The city says more centers are coming.

Rep. Bobby Scott won the money as a community project in Congress's 2023 spending law. He presented the check in Hampton in April 2023. Hampton City Council, led by Mayor Donnie Tuck, accepted the grant on Sept. 13, 2023. The city doesn't have to put in any money of its own.
