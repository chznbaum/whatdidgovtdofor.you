College Point in Queens flooded often in heavy rain because its old sewers couldn't carry the water away. A city project finished in September 2024 rebuilt the pipes under more than 100 blocks. Crews laid nearly 8.5 miles of new storm and sanitary sewers and about 7 miles of new water mains, replacing mains more than 50 years old. They also put in 300 new catch basins, so rain drains off the street, and a tide gate that keeps high tide from backing up into the storm sewers. The city says the work tripled the neighborhood's drainage capacity.

Residents don't need to sign up for anything. The city also repaved the streets it dug up and rebuilt sidewalks and curb ramps along the way. Keeping stormwater out of the combined sewers also cuts the sewage that overflows into Flushing Bay.

DEP funded the sewer and water work, while DDC managed construction with DOT on the street work. Acting DEP Commissioner Vincent Sapienza and DDC Commissioner Feniosky Peña-Mora headed those agencies when work began in 2016. After taking office in 2022, Council Member Vickie Paladino made the delayed project a top priority; the completion release says she held weekly calls with DDC and regularly visited the site. DDC Commissioner Thomas Foley, DEP Commissioner Rohit Aggarwala and DOT Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez headed the agencies when the $139 million project was finished in 2024.
