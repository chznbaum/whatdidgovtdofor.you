If you ride the bus along Madison Avenue, it now has two bus lanes between East 42nd and East 23rd Streets, where there was no dedicated bus lane before. That links up with the double lanes already north of 42nd Street. About 92,000 people a day ride the 34 local and express routes on this stretch, including the M1 through M4, the Q32, and express buses into Midtown. South of 42nd Street, buses had slowed to as little as 4.5 mph. On nearby Fifth Avenue, double bus lanes sped up local buses by up to 12% and express buses by up to 20%.

On Lexington Avenue, the bus lane between East 52nd and East 60th Streets moved off the curb, where delivery trucks and pickups kept blocking it. It now runs one lane out from the curb. That stretch carries about 71,000 riders a day on the M101, M102, M103 and express buses from the Bronx and Staten Island. When the city made the same change farther north in 2019, buses got 26% faster and pedestrian injuries fell 35%. Riders don't need to do anything.

The city's transportation department proposed the Madison extension in 2025, but the project stalled that year. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration restarted it in January 2026. Deputy Mayor for Operations Julia Kerson and Commissioner Mike Flynn announced the restart, and Kerson said her office would coordinate across agencies to get stalled projects implemented. Flynn's department began the Lexington work in May, and the city finished both projects in July.
