The Bx36 runs along Tremont Avenue in the Bronx, and its buses used to move as slowly as 5 mph. Now a stretch of Tremont is a busway. Eastbound, it runs from Third Avenue to Southern Boulevard; westbound, from Southern Boulevard to Belmont Avenue. From 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. every day, only buses, big trucks, Access-A-Ride vans, emergency vehicles and bikes can drive the whole length. Cars, taxis and for-hire vehicles can still reach any block from a side street, but they have to turn right at the next corner.

Riders don't have to do anything to benefit. More than 39,000 people ride the Bx36 on an average day, and 72% of households along the corridor don't own a car. The city also made safety changes at eight intersections. Tremont is a Vision Zero priority street: from 2020 to 2024, four people were killed and nearly 630 were hurt in crashes there.

NYC DOT planned to build the busway in spring 2025, but the Adams administration paused it, saying it lacked enough community support. Mayor Zohran Mamdani restarted the project in July 2026 as part of the Next Stop bus plan the city made with Gov. Kathy Hochul and the MTA. Commissioner Mike Flynn's DOT broke ground in August, and the busway opened on September 19.
