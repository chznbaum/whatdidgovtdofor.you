Stores in New York City can't sell e-cigarettes or vape liquids in any flavor except tobacco. The ban covers fruit and candy flavors, mint, menthol and wintergreen. It also covers made-up "concept" flavor names that are meant to slip past flavor bans. Stores can still sell tobacco-flavored pods and the devices themselves. The law also requires the city Health Department to tell people where to find free and low-cost help quitting.

You don't have to do anything to be covered, because the rule applies to sellers. It took effect July 1, 2020, after stores had six months to clear their shelves. New York State banned flavored vapes statewide that May. Vaping is still common among teens: in the city's 2023 survey, 14% of public high school students reported vaping in the past month.

Council Member Mark Levine, who chaired the Health Committee, introduced the bill in January 2019, and 30 colleagues signed on with him. It passed the Council 42–2 on Nov. 26, 2019, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on Dec. 16, 2019. In 2018, about 1 in 15 city middle schoolers reported using e-cigarettes in the past month.
