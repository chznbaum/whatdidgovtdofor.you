Every year, Florida sends cities and counties money from its State Housing Initiatives Partnership program, known as SHIP. In July 2026, Jacksonville's City Council approved spending this year's grant of $7.5 million. It also approved about $236,000 in program income, money that came back to the program from earlier aid, bringing the total to about $7.74 million. The money is for families with very low, low and moderate incomes.

SHIP can pay for emergency repairs and rehabilitation of homes, help with a down payment and closing costs, construction of new affordable homes, and help with rent and deposits. State rules require that at least 65% of the money go to homeownership and at least 30% to households earning no more than half of the area's median income. In recent years, Jacksonville has spent much of its SHIP money repairing homes that lower-income residents own and live in. In 2026, the city also began using SHIP to build 10 homes for buyers at that income level. Residents apply through programs run by the city's Housing and Community Development Division. The division says its owner-occupied repair programs are currently closed to new applicants because of long waiting lists.

The ordinance was filed at Mayor Donna Deegan's request. Council members Tyrona Clark-Murray, Jimmy Peluso and Rahman Johnson co-sponsored it. It passed two committees without a no vote and then cleared the full council 18-0 on July 28, 2026. The money comes from the state, so it doesn't cut any city service.
