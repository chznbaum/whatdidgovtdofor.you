Lead service lines run from the water main to a house or building. If they contain lead or galvanized steel, corrosion can put lead into drinking water. Phases 4 and 5 call for replacing 1,600 lead service lines and digging at approximately 5,400 locations to identify the pipe material. Liberty Water Company operates Elizabeth's water system and is carrying out the work with the city. Liberty schedules replacements by area rather than on individual request. Customers can check or update the online inventory of their pipe material.

Elizabeth's City Council authorized the $22.2 million project through Bond Ordinance 6159 in 2025. On May 26, 2026, it authorized the issuance and sale to the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank of a construction-financing note for up to that amount. The city then closed on a $19.9 million Water Bank loan. The Infrastructure Bank says Elizabeth is eligible for an estimated $15,774,579 in principal forgiveness at long-term closing—about 80% of the project cost—which would leave far less to repay locally.

New Jersey law requires all lead service lines to be replaced by July 2031. As of November 2026, Liberty says contractors are replacing lines in parts of Elizabeth. Its current program information says inspections, replacement, and restoration are free to customers.
