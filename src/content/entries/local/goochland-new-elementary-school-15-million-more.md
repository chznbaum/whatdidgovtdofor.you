Goochland County has a new elementary school. The new Goochland Elementary School replaced a 67-year-old building and was built for 700 students: 650 in kindergarten through fifth grade and 50 in preschool. It opened for the 2024–25 school year. Families didn't need to do anything: children in the school's attendance zone go there. Moving some students out of the Byrd and Randolph zones also let those schools get rid of their classroom trailers.

Voters approved bonds for the project, and the county set aside $40.3 million for it. Then construction bids came in higher than expected, and the School Board asked for more. On Jan. 3, 2023, after a public hearing, the Board of Supervisors voted 5–0 to add up to $15 million. Neil Spoonhower chaired the board that night. The bond money gets spent first. The rest comes from bond premiums and interest, developer proffers set aside for schools, regional transportation funds, and county savings held for rising construction costs. County officials said the money went only to construction and wouldn't affect day-to-day services.

A week later, the School Board signed the construction contract. The school held its ribbon cutting in July 2024 and opened to students that August.
