If you're homeless in Hennepin County, you can see a doctor, nurse or counselor right where you're staying. The county's Health Care for the Homeless team runs clinics in six shelters and sends staff out to people living outside. They treat illness, refill prescriptions, give vaccines and care for wounds and feet. They also offer therapy, addiction treatment such as Suboxone and Narcan, and help connecting to other services.

Anyone who has been homeless in the past year can use it, including kids and people staying with friends or relatives. You don't need an appointment, though hours differ by site. The county's main line is 612-348-5553. In 2025, the program served 6,006 people over more than 20,000 visits.

The federal Health Resources and Services Administration has paid for the program since 1988. On May 19, 2026, the Hennepin County Board of Commissioners approved a new award that added $1,144,558 and ran the funding through January 31, 2027. That brought the federal total since February 2023 to just over $8 million. In September 2026, HRSA announced an estimated $953,798 continuation award for Hennepin County, extending the funding period through June 30, 2027. The board's Health Committee had passed the May agreement on consent.
