Tampa approved a construction contract to replace aging water infrastructure in Hampton Terrace Phase A. The work includes installing new water mains, managing traffic, rebuilding pavement and sidewalks, and restoring landscaping and irrigation. Residents don't need to apply. The schedule allows 458 consecutive calendar days, and the contract says construction begins after the city issues a notice to proceed.

City documents disagree on the project's length. The adopted resolution describes about 7 miles of existing 4- to 8-inch mains. But the contract's itemized schedule budgets exactly 20,000 feet—about 3.8 miles—of new 2-, 6-, 8- and 12-inch pipe, while the city's Phase A page estimates 19,403 feet. The city says replacing the aging mains should improve water pressure and water quality, increase capacity, and reduce interruptions from breaks or leaks.

The Tampa City Council approved the $10.5 million agreement with Dallas 1 Corporation unanimously on Sept. 3, 2026, with Alan Clendenin as chair. The money comes from the Water Future Debt Capital Projects Fund.
