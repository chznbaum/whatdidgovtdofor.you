Each year, Gwinnett County receives three kinds of entitlement funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD): community development, affordable housing and homelessness grants. The county's locally approved 2026 plan directs more than $8.9 million from those grants across 54 projects. They cover housing, homeless services, food programs, public facilities and infrastructure. The county estimates that more than 15,000 lower-income residents will benefit.

Gwinnett Housing Corporation and Habitat for Humanity are expected to build more than 90 affordable homes. The plan also sets aside $200,000 for Homestretch, which offers eligible first-time buyers up to $10,000 toward down payment and closing costs as a zero-interest, five-year deferred loan. Rental assistance is expected to help 379 households. The plan puts $700,000 toward a new facility for Norcross Meals on Wheels and about $1.5 million toward a Hamilton Mill food pantry. Eligible buyers apply through Homestretch; other services are delivered through the nonprofits receiving project funds.

The Board of Commissioners, chaired by Nicole Love Hendrickson, voted 5-0 on Aug. 4, 2026, to approve submitting and implementing the plan. The grants require no county match. Gwinnett opened digital Homestretch applications in October, but as of Nov. 16 its HUD page still listed the 2026 plan as a draft and did not post final HUD approval or grant agreements.
