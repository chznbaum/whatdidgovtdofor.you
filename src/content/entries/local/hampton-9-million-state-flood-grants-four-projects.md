Hampton is using $9.16 million from Virginia's Community Flood Preparedness Fund on four projects designed to hold back stormwater before it floods streets and yards. The biggest share, $3.84 million, goes to Lake Hampton and North Armistead Avenue. There the grant helped add stormwater storage and turn a detention pond into a park, and it is helping raise a stretch of road that floods often. Another $3.01 million supports turning the drainage canal starting at Big Bethel Road and Albany Drive into a "blueway" that slows and stores water and adds public green space. Sunset Creek gets $2.02 million to restore a tidal channel and add storage, and the Billy Woods Canal gets $291,850 to hold more water upstream.

You don't have to do anything to benefit. These are public works projects; the city, not individual residents, is responsible for carrying them out.

The state awarded the grants in December 2021, and Hampton's share was more than a third of that round. The fund was paid for with money Virginia earned through the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative, a carbon market. Hampton is adding its own money, including proceeds from an environmental impact bond. Hampton City Council approved accepting the grants 7–0 on April 13, 2022, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding. Lake Hampton was completed in December 2024. As of September 2026, Big Bethel and North Armistead were under construction, Sunset Creek was in design, and Hampton listed Billy Woods in preliminary design.
