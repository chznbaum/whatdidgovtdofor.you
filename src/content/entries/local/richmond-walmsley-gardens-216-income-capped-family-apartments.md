The family apartments on Walmsley Boulevard were planned as Walmsley Gardens and are now marketed as The Warwick at Foundry Creek. The grant agreement requires at least 216 apartments and says occupancy and rents must average 60% of the Richmond area's median income under Virginia Housing standards. A July 2026 report listed monthly rents of $1,200 for a one-bedroom, $1,450 for a two-bedroom and $1,670 for a three-bedroom.

The money comes from Richmond's Affordable Housing Performance Grant program. Building on the site raises its real-estate tax bill, and the city's Economic Development Authority refunds the increase above the property's pre-project tax baseline—estimated here at $702,661 a year. The grant does not reduce the tax revenue the site produced before construction. Payments begin only after the apartments receive a certificate of occupancy and remain conditional on affordability, annual compliance and upkeep.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the patron of the 2024 ordinance authorizing the agreement. City Council adopted it 7-0 on June 24, 2024, in a meeting chaired by Council President Kristen Nye. Crescent Development and Spy Rock Real Estate finished the $92 million Foundry Creek project in 2026; it also includes a separate 128-unit senior building.
