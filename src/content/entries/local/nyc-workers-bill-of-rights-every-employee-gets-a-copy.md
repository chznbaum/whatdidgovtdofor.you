If you work in New York City for a private employer, your employer has to give you a multilingual “Know Your Rights at Work” notice and post it where employees can see it. New hires must get the notice on or before their first day. If your employer regularly uses an intranet or app to communicate with staff, the notice must appear there too. The notice sends you to the city's online Workers' Bill of Rights; it is not a printed copy of the full guide. The guide explains minimum wage, paid sick leave, fair scheduling, organizing and anti-discrimination rights, and how to file complaints. Government employees are excluded from the employer-notice requirement.

You do not have to apply for the notice. Anyone can read the online guide in 15 languages, request the poster, or report a missing notice through nyc.gov/workers or 311. DCWP says it keeps complainants' identities confidential unless disclosure is required or authorized, and it does not ask about immigration status. Enforcement is complaint-based. A first violation gets a warning; later violations can cost an employer $500.

The law also directs the Mayor's Office of Immigrant Affairs to run outreach with labor and community groups, including information about organizing a union and what to expect if immigration agents come to a workplace. Council Member Shahana Hanif introduced the bill in July 2022, and the Council passed it in November 2023. Mayor Eric Adams let it become law without signing it.
