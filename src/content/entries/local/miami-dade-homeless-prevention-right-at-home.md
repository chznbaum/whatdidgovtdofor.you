Miami-Dade approved a three-year homeless-prevention budget of up to $10 million to help households stay housed before they need a shelter. Local providers selected competitively will offer rapid, flexible financial help and case management. The grant agreement sets a stretch goal of serving at least 1,000 eligible households at the greatest and most imminent risk of homelessness during fiscal years 2026-27 through 2028-29.

Destination: Home SV is offering up to $7 million, paid in six installments; later payments depend on reports, spending, and program progress. The other $3 million comes from proceeds of the county's 1% food and beverage tax that are dedicated to homelessness. Miami-Dade is one of 10 pilot sites in the national Right at Home initiative, which builds on a Santa Clara County, California, model. County officials said more than 90% of households in that model were still stably housed two years later.

Residents can't apply yet. As of late October, the Homeless Trust was still finishing contracts with the providers. The Trust asked for the item, and Commissioner Marleine Bastien sponsored it. The County Commission approved it 9-0 on October 20, 2026.
