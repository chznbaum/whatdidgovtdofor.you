Since 2023, stores in New York City may sell or rent e-bikes, e-scooters and their batteries only if a lab has certified them as safe. These two laws give that rule teeth. A store that keeps selling uncertified devices can now be padlocked. The top fine is $2,000 for each type of device, and the Fire Department can enforce the rule alongside the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection. Shops must also post the city's battery fire-safety sign, and online sellers must show each product's certification on its page.

Buyers don't have to do anything. The rules apply to the sellers, and the city enforces them through inspections. By September 2025, the city had padlocked three retailers for repeat violations.

Council Member Gale Brewer was the prime sponsor of both bills. They passed the City Council 45–0 on Feb. 28, 2024, with votes from both parties. Mayor Eric Adams's administration testified for stronger rules that January. The bills became law on March 29, 2024, without his signature, and the city began enforcing them that fall. They came after a deadly run of battery fires: 268 in 2023, which killed 18 people.
