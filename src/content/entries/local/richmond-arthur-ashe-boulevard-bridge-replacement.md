The bridge that carries Arthur Ashe Boulevard over the CSX railroad, between Scott's Addition and the Diamond District, is more than 75 years old and rated in poor condition. Richmond is replacing it. The new bridge will have two lanes for cars in each direction and a shared walking and biking path on each side: 14 feet wide on the west and 10 feet on the east, with shade and benches. It will also sit higher, so it can clear the two existing rail lines and two planned ones.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. The city plans to keep the bridge open during construction, with one lane each way and the sidewalk open to people on foot.

The biggest share of the money is an $18.4 million federal RAISE grant, which Sens. Mark Warner and Tim Kaine announced in 2022. Their office said it followed their direct advocacy. In November 2024, the City Council adopted an ordinance from Mayor Levar Stoney accepting the grant and adding it to the city's capital budget. The Central Virginia Transportation Authority added $13 million in early 2026; the federal grant, city bond funding and CSX funding make up the rest. The latest CVTA update estimates the project at $44 million and says construction is anticipated to begin in April 2027. Building the bridge should take about two years.
