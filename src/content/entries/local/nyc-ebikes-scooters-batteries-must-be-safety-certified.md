If you buy, rent or lease an e-bike, e-scooter or a spare battery in New York City, it has to carry a certification from an accredited testing lab. E-bikes must meet the UL 2849 standard, other devices like scooters must meet UL 2272, and batteries sold on their own must meet UL 2271. The lab's name or mark generally has to appear on the product, its packaging or its paperwork. You don't need to do anything: the rule is on the businesses that sell or rent the gear, and the city inspects stores and takes enforcement action against online sellers.

Lithium-ion batteries in e-bikes and scooters had been causing fires in homes and shops. The FDNY investigated about 220 of these fires in 2022, with 147 injuries and six deaths, and 18 people died in 2023. Delivery workers rely on e-bikes, and a battery fire can also endanger neighbors in the same building.

Council Member Oswald Feliz sponsored the bill. The Council passed it on March 2, 2023, as part of a package on battery fires, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it on March 20. It took effect that September 16. In 2024, a follow-up law by Council Member Gale Brewer raised the maximum penalty for repeat sales violations to $2,000, with each product SKU treated as a separate violation. It also let the FDNY enforce the rule and allowed the city to padlock stores that keep breaking it.
