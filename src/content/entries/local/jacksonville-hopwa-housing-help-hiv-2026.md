Jacksonville is spending $3.27 million on housing help for low-income people in Duval County who are living with HIV or AIDS, and for their families. Most of it, $3.26 million, is the city's yearly grant from the federal Housing Opportunities for Persons with AIDS program, or HOPWA. The other $8,308.69 is unspent money moved over from earlier years. The money pays for housing assistance and the support services that help people keep a stable home.

HOPWA is the only federal program built around the housing needs of people with HIV/AIDS. A steady home makes it easier to stay on treatment. Cities that get the grant work with nonprofits and housing agencies, and those groups deliver the help. To get it, you have to have a low income and be living with HIV/AIDS.

The ordinance also creates a program manager job to run the grant and lets the city shift money between uses if needs change. Mayor Donna Deegan's administration asked for the ordinance. Council members Tyrona Clark-Murray, Jimmy Peluso and Rahman Johnson co-sponsored it. Three council committees approved it, and the full City Council passed it 16-0-1 on July 28, 2026. Council member Ju'Coby Pittman abstained.
