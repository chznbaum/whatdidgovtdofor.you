Jacksonville's budget for the year that started Oct. 1, 2026 puts $4.9 million into a city affordable housing trust fund. About $2 million will help people make a down payment on a home. City officials say the help can go to households earning up to 120% of the area median income. The city's July plan described it for first-time buyers who are just above the income limit for federal down payment aid, with up to $15,000 each and a homebuyer class. Another $1.4 million will help pay to build affordable homes, and $1.5 million will cover fees for new homes' JEA utility connections.

The fund nearly didn't happen. Mayor Donna Deegan proposed it in July, but the council's Finance Committee moved the money into a public safety contingency account. On budget night, at-large council member Matt Carlucci's amendment brought it back on a 12–7 vote. Council member Ron Salem helped by moving more of the money toward down payment help. ICARE, a coalition of 38 congregations, pressed for the fund. The council then passed the full budget 18–1 without raising the property tax rate, and police and fire funding was not cut.

The programs are still being set up. In October, the council blocked an attempt to use $2.8 million of the fund for a loan to a single apartment project.
