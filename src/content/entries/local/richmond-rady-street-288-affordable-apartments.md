Richmond has authorized a grant agreement to support a new apartment complex on a roughly 23-acre site at 2811 Rady Street, near Six Points in the Northside. The agreement requires at least 288 apartments with occupancy and rent limits monitored under Virginia Housing standards. Across the apartments, those limits must average 60% of the area's median income and remain in place throughout the 30-year grant period. The agreement does not specify when leasing will begin or how renters will apply.

The developer must first pay the project's full real-estate tax bill. It can then request a grant equal to 100% of the amount above the property's $9,180 annual tax baseline. Payments begin in the first tax year after construction is completed and continue for up to 30 years, subject to the agreement's requirements. This structure preserves the city's baseline tax revenue but commits the additional property-tax revenue generated by the project to the housing subsidy.

Mayor Danny Avula's administration proposed the grant, and Council Member Ellen Robertson, whose 6th District includes the site, co-sponsored it with him. City Council adopted it on July 27, 2026. It repealed a January 2025 grant for an earlier version of the project. A separate permit approved in February 2025 allowed up to 352 apartments; the new grant agreement requires at least 288 affordable apartments. The authorized agreement gives the developer 18 months from its effective date to start construction and three years after work starts to finish, although the city may extend those deadlines.
