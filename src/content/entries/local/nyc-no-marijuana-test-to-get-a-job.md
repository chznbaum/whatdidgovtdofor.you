If you're applying for a job in New York City, most employers can't make you take a marijuana (THC) test before they hire you. The city's Human Rights Law treats that kind of test as illegal discrimination, so you can report an employer to the NYC Commission on Human Rights. You don't need to do anything to be protected. The ban covers job applicants, not people who already work there, and employers can still test for other drugs.

Some jobs are exempt: police and peace officers, construction jobs that require OSHA safety training, jobs that need a commercial driver's license, and jobs caring for children, patients, or other vulnerable people. Testing is also allowed where federal or state safety rules, a federal contract or grant, or a union contract calls for it. In 2020 the Commission's rules added a few more safety-sensitive jobs, such as work near gas or power lines and driving on most work shifts.

Jumaane Williams introduced the bill, and Majority Leader Laurie Cumbo and Council Member Carlina Rivera were Council co-sponsors. The Council passed it on April 9, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio returned it without signing it, and it became law on May 10, 2019 without his signature. Employers had a year to get ready before the ban started on May 10, 2020. Williams later spoke out against the city adding more exemptions.
