When a violent crime happens in Hampton, the city's Victim Witness Assistance Program is usually the first group to reach out to victims and witnesses after the police investigate. Its staff help people deal with the emotional and money troubles that follow a crime. They point people to help such as financial aid for injuries and counseling, and they make sure victims know their rights under Virginia's crime victims' rights law.

This grant paid for that work from July 1, 2019, through June 30, 2020. The Virginia Department of Criminal Justice Services awarded $402,934, a mix of federal money passed through the state and state money. Hampton added $137,880 of its own, for a total of $540,814. The program expected to help about 1,925 victims and 275 witnesses that year. The grant was the second year of a three-year cycle, and the city has helped fund the program since 1984.

Hampton City Council approved the money on Aug. 14, 2019, in a meeting led by Mayor Donnie Tuck.
