If you delivered food through Uber Eats, Fantuan or HungryPanda in New York City in late 2023 or 2024, you may have received money from these settlements. City investigators found that all three paid some workers less than the city's minimum pay rate for app delivery workers. Together the apps paid $5,195,000 in restitution, damages and penalties for violations affecting more than 49,000 workers.

Uber Eats paid $3.15 million to more than 48,000 workers who weren't paid the minimum for time spent on trips that were canceled between December 2023 and September 2024. Streetsblog reported individual amounts from $8.79 to $276.15. HungryPanda paid more than $1 million to over 1,000 workers, and Fantuan paid more than $468,000 to 285 workers. Uber was also required to reinstate workers it wrongly deactivated in that period, which the city said could affect as many as 10,000 people. The Nation reported that Uber's automated rules had cut workers off even when cancellations weren't their fault.

The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, led by Commissioner Sam Levine, caught the violations using the monthly pay reports the apps must file and by talking directly with workers. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the settlements with Levine on January 30, 2026.
