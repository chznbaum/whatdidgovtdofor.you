Hanover's public water system relies heavily on Richmond. When Richmond's main water treatment plant failed in January 2025, many Hanover customers east of I-95 lost water for 24 to 36 hours. This money is meant to keep water flowing the next time Richmond's supply goes down.

The $4 million pays for portable booster pumps at the county's existing Henrico County water connections on Route 1 and Route 33, plus upgrades to the Airport East pump station. Together, the work can move Henrico's backup supply through the system and increase west-to-east capacity toward Mechanicsville. The county says these fixes would let it keep up with average daily use of about 8.4 million gallons. It could draw nearly 6 million gallons a day in emergency supply, plus 4 million gallons a day from its own Doswell treatment plant. Customers don't need to do anything.

Public Utilities Director Matt Longshore presented the plan on March 25, 2026, with engineers from Dewberry and Arcadis, who studied the county's water supply after the outage. The Board of Supervisors approved the money that day. It comes from capital funds already set aside for the 2026 budget year, moved over from the county's Richmond joint capital account, so no new taxes or fees are needed. The county called these fixes a first step. Later phases could add a new storage tank, another emergency link to Henrico County, and possibly a county-owned water source on the Pamunkey River.
