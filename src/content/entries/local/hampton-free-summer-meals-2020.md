In 2020, Hampton served free meals to children across the city while school was out. The goal was to give kids the same kind of meal they get at school through the National School Lunch Program. The grant period ran from April 1 to September 1, 2020. Children 18 and under could eat. People over 18 could also eat if a state or local education agency had determined they had a disability and they participated in a public or nonprofit private school program for people with disabilities.

Meals came from sites run by the city's Department of Parks, Recreation and Leisure Services. At an open site, families did not have to prove individual eligibility: USDA rules made meals available to every child there, while the site qualified based on the share of children in the surrounding area eligible for free or reduced-price school meals. The city expected more than 2,500 children to take part.

The U.S. Department of Agriculture funded the program's direct costs. The council accepted and appropriated $272,753.44, with no local cash match required, while the city covered coordination and other indirect costs. Hampton has run the summer program with USDA money since 1969. On July 8, 2020, the City Council, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, voted 7-0 to accept the grant and add it to the city budget. The final reimbursement could rise or fall with participation.
