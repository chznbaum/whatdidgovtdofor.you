For more than 40 years, the old school at 70 Mulberry Street was where Chinatown went for help: a senior center, job training through the Chinatown Manpower Project, youth sports with the United East Athletic Association, the Chen Dance Center and the Museum of Chinese in America. A fire in January 2020 badly damaged the building, and all five groups had to move. Most kept serving people from temporary sites, but with less room.

The city is now rebuilding it as an eight-story building, at a cost of $184 million. It will have about 30% more public space than before, a new kitchen for the senior center, six community rooms and a multipurpose room for sports and events. The community rooms can hold up to 475 people. Three elevators and accessible restrooms will make the building fully accessible for the first time. The old facade's lower two stories are being kept. The city held a groundbreaking on Nov. 9, 2026. Its current tracker says construction is expected to start in the first quarter of 2027, and the city expects to finish in 2030. Then the five groups can move back in.

Council Member Margaret Chin, who represented Chinatown, secured the initial money with Mayor Bill de Blasio, while then-Borough President Gale Brewer pushed for city funding after the fire. DCAS Commissioner Lisette Camilo helped secure the initial $80 million and led the community visioning process. De Blasio raised the commitment to $170 million in 2021. The Department of Design and Construction, led by Commissioner Paul Ochoa, and DCAS, led by Commissioner Yume Kitasei, are implementing the project. Mayor Zohran Mamdani joined them at the groundbreaking.
