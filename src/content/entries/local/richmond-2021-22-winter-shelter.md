In the 2021–22 winter, Richmond paid for a shelter where people without homes could come in out of the cold. Commonwealth Catholic Charities ran it out of a local hotel, the Quality Inn. The site was open 12 to 15 hours a day and offered two meals, showers, case management, vaccinations, and referrals to health care and counseling. Security was on site. Between its fall 2021 reopening and early April 2022, the shelter served 1,120 people. It closed for the season on April 15, 2022.

On Oct. 11, 2021, the City Council approved $1,781,510 in federal housing money for the shelter. That was mostly community development block grant and emergency solutions grant money, including pandemic relief funds. Mayor Levar Stoney's administration brought the plan, and Council Member Stephanie Lynch co-sponsored the ordinance.

The plan also had a second part that didn't happen. The money was meant to help the charity expand its building on Oliver Hill Way into a permanent shelter with at least 75 beds, open around the clock. In June 2022 the charity dropped that project after no contractors bid on it, and Richmond had to look for other places to shelter people the next winter.
