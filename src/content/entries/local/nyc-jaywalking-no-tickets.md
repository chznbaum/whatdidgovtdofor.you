In New York City, you can cross a street anywhere other than on a limited-access highway, including mid-block or against the signal, and it isn't a violation of city law. Police can't write you a summons for it. Before the change, a jaywalking ticket could cost up to $250. You still don't have the right of way when you cross outside a crosswalk or against the light: you have to yield to cars and bikes that do, and you can still be held responsible in a civil case if your crossing causes a crash. The law also requires the Department of Transportation to run a public education campaign on what walkers and drivers owe each other.

The city had banned jaywalking since 1958, but police rarely enforced it, and when they did, the tickets fell mostly on Black and Latino New Yorkers. According to a report the Council commissioned, they received 92% of the 467 jaywalking summonses issued in 2023.

Council Member Mercedes Narcisse of Brooklyn introduced the bill in February 2024 to end that disparity. Council Members Tiffany Cabán, Shahana Hanif, Lincoln Restler and others cosponsored it. NYPD and DOT officials opposed it at a hearing, and the bill was amended before the Council passed it 40–8 on September 26, 2024. Mayor Eric Adams neither signed nor vetoed it, so it became law on October 26, 2024. It took effect 120 days later. The Department of Transportation changed its own traffic rules to match, effective June 26, 2025.
