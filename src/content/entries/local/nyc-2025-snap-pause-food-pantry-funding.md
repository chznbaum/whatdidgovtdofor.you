In fall 2025, the federal government shutdown put food aid at risk for nearly 1.8 million New Yorkers. Their SNAP benefits for November were set to stop on Nov. 1. SNAP normally brings about $420 million a month into the city, and the U.S. Department of Agriculture had not released contingency funds to cover the gap. In response, New York City added $15 million in one-time emergency money to Community Food Connection. The program funds more than 700 community pantries and kitchens across the five boroughs.

You didn't need to apply for anything. The funding was for increased food distributions through the pantries and community kitchens, where people could get free groceries or meals. The city's Food Help NYC map shows the locations. The city also said school meals and its meals for older adults would continue as usual.

Mayor Eric Adams announced the money on Oct. 30, 2025. The Department of Social Services, led by Commissioner Molly Wasow Park, implemented the funding through Community Food Connection. The city paid for it by transferring $15 million from the fiscal 2026 Cash Assistance budget. That program was chronically underbudgeted; in February 2026, the city added $825.5 million based on projected caseload needs. The emergency money could cover only a small part of a normal month of SNAP, but it was intended to increase food distributions while benefits were delayed.
