If a business places you on New York City construction or manual-labor jobs, it now needs a license from the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection. Running one of these "body shops" without a license costs $500 a day in penalties, and clients that hire an unlicensed one can be fined too. General contractors, subcontractors and licensed employment agencies don't need the license.

Licensed providers must give you three written notices, in English and your primary language, and get your signature. Before you're hired, you learn what certifications or training the job needs, what they cost, and whether the provider pays. When you're hired, you get a notice explaining your rights to sick leave, a safe workplace, minimum wage and overtime, and workers' comp. Before each new site, except in some short-notice cases, you're told the job, the safety gear you need, and your pay. These rights apply whatever your immigration status or record. You can sue in court over violations or retaliation.

Council Member Diana Ayala sponsored the bill. Laborers Local 79 pushed for it, saying body shops send formerly incarcerated people to dangerous, low-paying jobs. The Council passed it on Nov. 10, 2021, and it became law on Dec. 11 without Mayor Bill de Blasio's signature. Licensing began June 9, 2022.
