The Battery, at the southern tip of Manhattan, sits on a waterfront wharf built in the 1940s. Hurricane Sandy's surge flooded the park in 2012. As the sea rises, the city expects high tides to start covering the wharf twice a day. The city is rebuilding the promenade about 5 feet higher, so it stays above the sea level projected for 2100. The project also keeps the park's harbor views, seating and gardens, including the Gardens of Remembrance for victims and survivors of Sept. 11.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. Much of the park stays open during construction, with parts of the wharf closed in stages, and Statue of Liberty ferries keep running. During phase two, passenger screening and ferry docking are near Castle Clinton.

Mayor Bill de Blasio's administration announced the project and its initial city funding in March 2019. The city pays for it from its own capital budget. Under Mayor Eric Adams, the design was finished and construction began; Adams announced the groundbreaking in May 2024. Under Mayor Zohran Mamdani, crews finished the first section, from Pier A to the East Coast Memorial, in June 2026. The rest of the wharf is due in 2027.
