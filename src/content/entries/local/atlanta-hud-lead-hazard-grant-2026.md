Atlanta is taking in up to $7.75 million in federal money, spread over four years, to remove lead hazards from homes. Lead paint in older homes is one of the main ways young children get lead poisoning, which can cause lasting harm to learning and growth. The money comes from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development's lead hazard reduction program. That program pays local governments to find and fix lead paint hazards in privately owned homes, whether owners live in them or rent them out. Protecting children under 6 is its main goal.

The city's Department of City Planning applied for the grant and will run it. The ordinance adds staff positions in that department to oversee the work. Residents don't get the money directly. The city uses it to find homes with lead hazards and fix them.

The Atlanta City Council approved the ordinance 11-0 on Oct. 5, 2026, at a meeting led by Council President Marci Collier Overstreet. The ordinance also signed off on the city's grant application after the fact and added the money to the city's grant budgets.
