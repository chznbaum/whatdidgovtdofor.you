If you ride the B6 or the B82 through Midwood, Bensonhurst or Bath Beach, your bus now has its own lane. The new lanes run on Bay Parkway from Avenue J to Shore Parkway and on Cropsey Avenue from Bay Parkway to 26th Avenue, three miles of streets. The X28 and X38 express buses use them too. The B6 is Brooklyn's busiest bus route and the B82 its fourth busiest, and on Bay Parkway they had been slowing to as little as 3 miles an hour. You don't need to do anything to benefit: about 35,000 riders a day travel this corridor.

The city also worked on safety for people walking. Both streets are Vision Zero priority corridors: from 2021 to 2025, four people were killed there and 26 were seriously injured. The final design called for up to 18 new pedestrian median islands and median extensions, upgraded curb ramps and signals, and left-turn lanes at 14 busy intersections. It also restricted left turns at most other Bay Parkway intersections. A city study found those turn changes were associated with fewer injuries for pedestrians and cyclists. Where the street had room, the bus lanes sit one lane away from the curb, preserving most parking.

NYC DOT began public outreach on the project in November 2024 under Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez and developed a draft proposal in 2025. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's Next Stop plan with the MTA and Gov. Kathy Hochul later included Bay Parkway among 50 priority corridors. Mamdani launched construction on Oct. 6, 2026, and Commissioner Mike Flynn's DOT carried the redesign through final design and construction. Boro Park 24 reported it complete on Nov. 2.
