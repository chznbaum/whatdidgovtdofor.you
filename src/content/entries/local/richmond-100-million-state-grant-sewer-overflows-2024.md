Much of older Richmond has combined sewers, where rainwater and sewage share the same pipes. When a heavy storm overloads them, the mix can spill into the James River and its creeks at 25 outfalls around the city. The state is requiring Richmond to cut those spills, and the work costs hundreds of millions of dollars.

On April 22, 2024, the Richmond City Council accepted a $100 million grant from the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality and added it to that year's capital budget for the city's combined sewer overflow project. Residents don't need to do anything. The money pays for sewer construction that the city would otherwise have to cover mostly through utility bills.

Virginia's 2022 state budget set aside $100 million for Richmond's overflow projects, paid for with federal pandemic recovery money. Mayor Levar Stoney brought the ordinance to the council, which adopted it. Under the state-approved schedule, the 10 interim projects are due by July 1, 2027, and the four final-plan projects by July 1, 2035.
