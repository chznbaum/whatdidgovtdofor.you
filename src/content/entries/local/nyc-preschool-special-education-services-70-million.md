New York City must give preschoolers with disabilities services like speech therapy, occupational therapy and counseling, but many families waited months. At a Council hearing in May 2025, the city said 7,156 preschoolers were missing at least one service they were owed, and 3,729 were getting none at all.

The 2025–26 city budget added $70 million to close that gap. Of it, $50 million pays for more therapy and related services, $10 million hires more administrators and coordinators to shrink caseloads, and $10 million lets city schools do more first-time evaluations themselves, so parents don't have to find a private evaluator. The city expected to evaluate about 1,800 more children a year. Families don't apply for the money. It reaches children through their special education plans.

Council Member Rita Joseph, who chaired the Education Committee, said the Council kept pressing City Hall to make the services a priority. Speaker Adrienne Adams and Mayor Eric Adams agreed on the budget, and the Council passed it unanimously on June 30, 2025. In the 2026–27 budget, the city made the $70 million recurring and added $67.5 million more.
