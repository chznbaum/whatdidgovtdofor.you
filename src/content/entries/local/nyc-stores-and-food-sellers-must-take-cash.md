If you shop in person at a New York City store, deli, restaurant, or food cart, the business has to take your cash. It also can't charge you more than someone paying by card or phone. A store may turn down bills larger than $20, and orders placed by phone, mail, or online don't count unless you complete them in the store. A business can skip cash only if it has a machine on site that turns your cash into a prepaid card with no fee and no minimum deposit over $1.

You don't need to apply for anything. If a business refuses your cash, you can report it to the city's consumer agency online or by calling 311. Fines run up to $1,000 for a first violation and up to $1,500 for each one after that. In 2022, Van Leeuwen Ice Cream paid $33,000 and agreed to accept cash at all 19 of its city shops after the agency brought more than 90 cases against it.

Council Member Ritchie Torres introduced the bill in November 2018. It stalled for months in 2019 and was sent back to committee before the Council passed it 43–3 on January 23, 2020. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature on February 23, 2020. The city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, led by Commissioner Lorelei Salas, wrote the rules to carry it out, and the ban took effect November 19, 2020.
