Richmond now gives its Affordable Housing Trust Fund predictable yearly funding. The fund makes loans and grants to developers and housing organizations—not direct payments to residents—to build and preserve affordable rental and owner-occupied homes. Each year's amount is tied to 2.5% of the real estate taxes the city collected in the last completed fiscal year. Because that figure is based on actual collections, the amount is known when the mayor introduces the budget. The first budget under the new rule, for the year that began July 1, 2026, set aside $11.7 million.

The ordinance replaced two older funding sources from 2019 and 2021: proceeds from selling tax-delinquent properties and taxes that came back as old tax breaks expired. A city audit in February 2026 found those sources unpredictable and never fully put in place. Money promised to the fund often never arrived. The city reports each year on where the money comes from and how many homes it produces. Council must reauthorize the funding every four years, with the first review due by June 30, 2031.

Councilmember Ellen Robertson and Mayor Danny Avula introduced the ordinance after months of work between his administration and Council. Six other members co-sponsored it, and Council passed it unanimously on February 23, 2026. For FY27 and FY28, the city can use capital-budget resources to meet the amount. Starting July 1, 2028, it must come from real estate-tax revenue. The ordinance did not change Richmond's real estate tax rate.
