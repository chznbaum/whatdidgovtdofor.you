If you own a single-family home in unincorporated Cobb County, you can now apply to build a small second home behind it. This is often called an accessory dwelling unit, or ADU. It could house an aging parent or a grown child, or bring in rent. Before this vote, Cobb allowed attached habitable additions by right; this change creates a permit path for detached backyard units.

The unit can be as large as 850 square feet or half the main house's floor space, whichever is smaller. It must be one story, can't be taller than the house, and has to share the main house's utility meters. You need at least one off-street parking space, and you can't rent the unit short-term. The owner has to live on the property, either in the main house or in the ADU. Only one ADU is allowed per lot. Building one isn't automatic: each project needs a special land use permit, which commissioners approve at a public zoning hearing.

The Board of Commissioners approved the change 3–2 on October 27, 2026. Chair Lisa Cupid made the motion, Commissioner Erick Allen seconded it, and Commissioner Monique Sheffield also voted yes. Commissioners Keli Gambrill and JoAnn Birrell voted no. Cobb had tried to allow ADUs in 2024 but pulled the plan after neighborhood pushback. Several cities in Cobb, including Marietta, already allow them.
