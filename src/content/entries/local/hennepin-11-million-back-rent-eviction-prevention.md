If you rent in Hennepin County, are behind on rent, and are facing eviction, this money can pay what you owe. On Aug. 4, 2026, the County Board accepted $11 million more from the state's Family Homeless Prevention and Assistance Program. That brings the county's grant to $19.1 million through September 2027. The money covers back rent, help finding a new place for people who have already lost their homes, and case managers to help families stay stable. The county added nine grant-funded staff to handle the work.

You apply through RentHelp Hennepin, the county's single application for rent help. Starting Sept. 1, 2026, renters can apply with either a pre-filing notice from their property manager or an eviction filing with the court. Under state rules, household income must be at or below twice the federal poverty level. Everyone completes a prescreen, and funds are not guaranteed.

The money is Hennepin's share of $40 million in one-time state funding enacted after housing instability tied to Operation Metro Surge. Rep. Liish Kozlowski wrote the rental-assistance item. Rep. Michael Howard and Sen. Lindsey Port carried HF 1141; Rep. Greg Davids and Sen. Ann Rest carried HF 2438, which finalized $38 million of the appropriation. The bills passed 105–28 and 42–25, then 126–8 and 52–15, respectively, with support from both parties. Gov. Tim Walz proposed the aid and signed the bills on May 18 and May 27. Of the $40 million, $38 million was appropriated after unused Tyler tax-foreclosure settlement money was returned to the general fund; another $2 million came from Minnesota Housing investment earnings.
