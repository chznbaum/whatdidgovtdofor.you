Renting a new apartment in Newark could mean paying a broker's fee on top of a security deposit. That was true even when the landlord had hired the broker. Now, if a landlord or developer hires a broker, the landlord pays. They can't charge you the fee, ask you to pay them back, or quietly recover it with higher rent, new fees, or smaller move-in deals.

The rule covers both market-rate and affordable apartments. You don't need to apply for anything. Before you sign a lease, the landlord or agent has to give you a written, itemized list of every fee you'll owe. Rental applications must include a statement, signed by the landlord, that you won't be charged a broker fee. A lease also can't require you to use a particular broker. The city's Division of Rent Control and Department of Public Safety enforce the rule. Each improper fee counts as a separate violation, with a fine of up to $1,250.

Mayor Ras Baraka proposed the ban as part of his Keeping Newark Affordable package. Council members Dupré L. Kelly and Louise Scott-Rountree sponsored it. The Municipal Council introduced it on September 9, 2026, and passed it unanimously on September 23. It took effect 20 days after passage and publication.
