A city-owned lot in East Harlem that held NYPD parking is becoming Timbale Terrace: a 20-story building with 341 affordable homes and a music and arts center for the neighborhood. The development will also restore parking for the local police precinct.

Of the 341 homes, 97 are permanent supportive housing for New Yorkers leaving homelessness, including 30 for young people aging out of foster care. Lantern Organization will provide on-site health, education, and employment help. Another 243 apartments are for households earning less than 70% of area median income; the announced income bands run from about $30,000 to $130,000 a year. Those apartments are expected to be offered through NYC Housing Connect when leasing opens.

The project grew from the 2016 East Harlem Neighborhood Plan. Former Council Speaker Melissa Mark-Viverito, then-community advocate Elsie Encarnación, and HPD staffer Leila Bozorg were among its early champions. HPD Commissioner Louise Carroll's agency sought proposals in 2019 and chose the developers in 2021. Manhattan Borough President Mark Levine, Council Member Yusef Salaam, Assembly Member Eddie Gibbs, and Community Board 11 backed it through opposition. The Council approved it 48-0 in March 2024; Rafael Salamanca Jr. and Kamillah Hanks sponsored the resolution. City subsidies, Housing Development Corporation bonds, and grants from Salaam, the Manhattan borough president, and the cultural affairs department help pay the $255 million cost. Construction began in February 2026 under HPD Commissioner Dina Levy, with HDC led by Eric Enderlin.
