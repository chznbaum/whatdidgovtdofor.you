If you give birth at one of four New York City public hospitals, you go home with a free box of supplies for your baby and for yourself. The four hospitals are Jacobi and Lincoln in the Bronx, Kings County in Brooklyn and Elmhurst in Queens. Each box holds diapers and wipes, baby clothes and pajamas, a baby carrier, a thermometer, nail clippers and a nasal aspirator, plus postpartum pads, nipple cream and a board book. It also comes with a health guide for mothers and newborns and a guide to city programs for new families.

You don't need to apply or pay. Every family that delivers at these hospitals gets a box, and more than 7,000 babies are born there each year. The goal is to take some of the cost and worry out of a family's first weeks at home.

Mayor Eric Adams announced the program in his 2025 State of the City address. In March 2025 the city put $2.6 million of its own money behind it. NYC Health + Hospitals, led by Dr. Mitchell Katz, formally launched the program in October 2025. The work was done with United Way of New York City and the nonprofit Welcome Baby USA, which supplies the boxes. Mayor Zohran Mamdani has since proposed giving a basket of baby supplies to every newborn in the city.
