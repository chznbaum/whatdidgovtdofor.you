New York City's Nest, Horizon, AIMS, Path and ACES programs previously served students beginning in kindergarten. In the 2026–27 school year, the city opened pre-K versions for the first time. The expansion creates 250 seats at 26 schools in 14 districts across all five boroughs, bringing specialized classes closer to more 3- and 4-year-olds with autism and other disabilities.

The $67.5 million investment also funds staff and services beyond the new classrooms. That includes 35 psychologists, social workers, speech evaluators and occupational therapists for 10 additional preschool evaluation teams. Another 29 therapists and specialists serve children in community-based 3-K and pre-K programs, and the city is expanding the program that places special education teachers in regular preschool classrooms. Families do not apply for these specialized seats separately: the city's Committee on Preschool Special Education makes a placement when a child's IEP recommends one of the programs.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani put the investment in his Fiscal Year 2027 Executive Budget, which the City Council adopted on June 30, 2026. Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels led NYC Public Schools as it opened the programs and added the staff positions. By the start of school in September, NYCPS said every preschooler with a disability who needed a special education seat had an appropriate placement, though evaluations and placements continue throughout the year as more children become eligible.
