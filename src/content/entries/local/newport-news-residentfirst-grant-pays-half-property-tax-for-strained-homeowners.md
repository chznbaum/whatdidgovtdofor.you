If a Newport News home is your main home and you own no other property, the city may pay half your real estate tax for one year when housing costs take at least 30% of household income. People living in a care facility can qualify if the home is not rented out. For a home at the city's median assessed value of $287,100, a full year's tax at $1.18 per $100 is nearly $3,400, so the grant would be about $1,700. You don't pay it back.

Household income must be within HUD's limits: $60,350 for one person and $86,150 for a four-person household. You cannot also receive other city real estate tax relief for the same year, the disabled-veterans real estate tax exemption, or Housing Choice Voucher mortgage assistance. Apply online, by phone at 757-926-8653, or at a Commissioner of the Revenue office by Dec. 31, 2026, and submit proof of income and housing costs. Keep paying your tax while you wait. Approved grants are credited to your June 2027 bill, or the credit can be refunded if that bill is already paid.

City Manager Alan Archer proposed $300,000 for the pilot. The city funded it in the FY2027 budget as real estate assessments and expected tax collections rose, while keeping the tax rate at $1.18 per $100. City Council, with Mayor Phillip Jones presiding, established the program and adopted its funding 7–0 on May 12, 2026. Grants go out first come, first served until the money runs out, and the city will decide later whether to continue the program.
