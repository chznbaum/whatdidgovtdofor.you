If you work for New York City, you can use Summer's online student loan platform at no cost. It checks which federal and state programs could lower your monthly payment, helps you pick a repayment plan, and handles the paperwork for Public Service Loan Forgiveness. Student loan experts can answer your questions. Family members can use it too if they qualify for forgiveness through their own government or nonprofit job.

The service doesn't cancel debt on its own. Under the federal forgiveness program, what's left on your federal Direct Loans is wiped out after 120 qualifying monthly payments while you work full time for a public employer or qualifying nonprofit. The process is hard to get right: the city says that since 2017, fewer than 11% of eligible borrowers who sent in forgiveness forms had their loans forgiven.

The program started in May 2025 with workers at the Administration for Children's Services, the Department of Social Services and NYCHA, then expanded to all city employees that September. In its first three months it served more than 2,000 city workers and helped more than 380 of them apply for forgiveness or income-based plans. The city estimates that pilot users cut their payments by $3,800 a year on average.

Mayor Eric Adams announced the program in his 2025 State of the City address and funded it in his 2026 budget. A May 2026 City Council report projected the contract's fiscal 2026 cost at $12.5 million. The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection launched the partnership, and the Department of Citywide Administrative Services runs it for city workers. In August 2025, the city opened the platform free to every New York City resident.
