Miami-Dade County approved transferring three empty lots it was not using so developers can build homes for income-qualified households to buy. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Miami, a nonprofit, is to receive one lot. Elite Equity Development, a private builder, is to receive two. Each would pay the county $10 for its conveyance. All three lots are in Commission District 8.

The homes must be sold to very low-, low- or moderate-income households earning no more than 120% of area median income. The builders have two years from the day each county deed is recorded to build and sell the homes, and the resolution says no further extensions will be approved. If a builder misses the deadline or violates the deed restrictions, the county can demand a cure and then exercise its option to take the land back. County staff must monitor compliance. The resolution does not provide an application process; the developers would handle the home sales.

Commissioner Danielle Cohen Higgins, who represents District 8, sponsored the resolution. The board's housing committee recommended it in June, and Cohen Higgins asked to push the final vote from July to September. On Sept. 1, 2026, the full commission adopted an amended version 12-0. The resolution does not say how many homes the lots will hold.
