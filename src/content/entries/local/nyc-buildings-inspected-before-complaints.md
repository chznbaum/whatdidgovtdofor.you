New York City's Department of Buildings used to act mostly after someone called in a problem. This law makes it look for trouble first. The department uses predictive analytics to weigh building age and other characteristics, permit and violation history, compliance filings, 311 complaints, nearby construction, and the disciplinary records of engineers, architects, contractors and licensees. It inspects buildings whose data indicates a potential hazard or other safety concern.

When an inspection leads DOB to require a corrective action plan, the owner gets no more than 30 days to submit one. A licensed architect or engineer must seal the plan, which documents each violation with photos, construction documents and a repair timeline. DOB reinspects as needed. The owner must post a lobby notice about violations, with versions in the city's designated languages available upon request. Tenants do not apply; DOB chooses which buildings to inspect.

Council Member Pierina Sanchez introduced the bill after part of 1915 Billingsley Terrace in the Bronx collapsed in December 2023. More than 170 people were displaced after years of neglect and hundreds of violations. Buildings Commissioner Jimmy Oddo and DOB worked with the Council to draft the bill, and the Council secured a $4.7 million budget commitment for 60 new DOB positions. The Council passed it on June 20, 2024. It became law that July without Mayor Eric Adams's signature and took effect in January 2025. By March 2025, DOB had filled 25 of the positions and inspectors were already in the field.
