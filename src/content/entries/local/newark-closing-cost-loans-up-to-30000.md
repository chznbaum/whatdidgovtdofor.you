Closing costs can stop a home purchase even after you've saved for the down payment. They include lawyer, appraisal, title, inspection and recording fees, plus the transfer tax. Through its Live Newark program, the city now advances up to $30,000 toward those costs, up from $20,000. The money goes to your attorney's trust account at closing, and anything left over goes back to the city.

The loan charges no interest. For each full year in a row the home stays your main residence, Newark forgives 10% of it, so after 10 years you owe nothing. If you sell or move out early, you repay only the part that hasn't been forgiven yet. To apply, you provide recent tax returns, pay stubs, employment information and a lender's cost estimate. You must be a first-time buyer, meet HUD income limits and complete a city-approved homebuyer program. Applications are first-come, first-served while funds last. The program is paid for with Newark's share of federal HOME housing money and with local funds.

Council members Patrick O. Council and C. Lawrence Crump sponsored the change. The ordinance was introduced at $50,000, and on Aug. 5, 2026 the council lowered the figure to $30,000. It passed 9-0 on Sept. 9, and Mayor Ras J. Baraka approved it on Sept. 15. This was the third increase since 2021, when the loan's maximum was $5,000.
