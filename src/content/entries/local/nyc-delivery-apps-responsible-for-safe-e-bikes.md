If you deliver for a covered service in New York City, the company has two ways to comply with the law. It can ensure that powered e-bikes used for its deliveries meet the city's sales standard, including certification by an accredited testing lab, and provide a compliant device at company expense. It may also let you use your own compliant device. It can't require you to buy one or deactivate you for failing to do so.

Alternatively, a service is exempt from that per-device duty if it participates in a qualifying trade-in or subscription program. A service with at least 5,000 workers must contribute 1,600 compliant devices a year until the program has provided at least 8,000; smaller services must contribute 500 a year until at least 2,500 are provided. A qualifying subscription program must provide at least half of the service's workers with 52 weeks of access to compliant equipment at reduced or no cost. Workers using the trade-in route must turn in an eligible device, and the subscription route may not cover everyone. The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection enforces the law and can impose civil penalties; city lawyers can seek court orders requiring compliance.

Council Member Oswald Feliz of the Bronx sponsored the bill, and the Council passed it on June 30, 2025. Delivery companies had fought earlier versions that would have required a free certified e-bike for every worker, and the final bill was a compromise. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature on July 30, 2025. It took effect on January 26, 2026.
