A "ghost gun" starts as an unfinished frame or receiver: the core part of a gun, sold online partly made and with no serial number. Someone with ordinary hardware-store tools can finish it, add the rest of the parts, and end up with a working gun that is difficult for police to trace. This law makes it a crime in New York City to have one of these unfinished parts, or to sell or give one away.

Having or passing along one of these parts is a misdemeanor. Each one counts as a separate offense, and each can bring a $1,000 fine or jail time. The ban took effect in February 2020. Nobody has to apply or do anything for the protection to apply. The NYPD had seized 67 ghost guns from the start of 2017 to June 2019.

Council Member Helen Rosenthal introduced the bill in May 2019. After two Public Safety Committee hearings, the full Council passed it on September 25, 2019. That same day it also passed Council Member I. Daneek Miller's companion bill, which makes the NYPD report how many ghost guns it recovers. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign the bill or veto it, so it became law on October 26, 2019.
