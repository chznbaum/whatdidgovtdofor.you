Since 2018, Newark has given free lawyers to low-income tenants facing eviction. Until this year, your household income could be no more than 200% of the federal poverty guidelines to qualify. Starting June 1, 2026, the limit is 300%. Many working households that earned a little too much before can now get a free lawyer when a landlord takes them to court.

You do need to ask for the help. Newark's eviction legal services program checks your income before it takes your case. The ordinance puts the city's Deputy Mayor and Director of Economic and Housing Development in charge of how income is checked and what paperwork you'll need. Those rules must follow the federal poverty guidelines.

Council members Patrick O. Council and Dupré L. Kelly sponsored the change. The Municipal Council first approved it on May 6, 2026. After a public hearing, it passed 9–0 on May 20. When Newark created its right to counsel in December 2018, it was one of the first cities in the country to guarantee lawyers to low-income tenants facing eviction.
