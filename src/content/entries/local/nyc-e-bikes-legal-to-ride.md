You can ride any of New York's three legal e-bike classes in NYC, including in vehicle lanes on streets with speed limits of 30 mph or less. Class 1 and 2 motors assist up to 20 mph; Class 3 motors assist up to 25 mph. A newer city rule limits all e-bike riders to 15 mph, so a bike may be built for more assistance than you may legally use. You don't need to sign up for anything. E-bike riders generally follow the same traffic rules as other cyclists, and Class 3 riders must wear helmets.

Before 2020, city rules banned many of these bikes, and riders could be fined $500 and lose the bike to an impound lot. The old ban hit food-delivery workers hardest. Many are older immigrants who rely on throttle e-bikes to make a living, and advocates said impound fees could exceed $1,000 on top of fines. For devices that are still banned, the same legislative package cut the fine to $250 and limited impoundment to cases where riding endangered the rider, another person, or property.

Council Member Fernando Cabrera introduced the bill in November 2018. It passed in June 2020, a few months after the state legalized e-bikes in its April budget. The Council approved it 42–5 as part of a package that also legalized e-scooters. Mayor Bill de Blasio let it become law without signing it, and it took effect on Nov. 23, 2020.
