If you hire a home improvement contractor in New York City, they must tell you in writing whether the job is likely to need permits. They must give you the notice no later than the date you sign the contract or any change to it. If permits are foreseeably needed, it must list them, say who will get them (the contractor or someone else, such as an architect), give the steps and estimated fees, and show you how to check their status in city databases. You do not have to ask. A contractor who breaks this law can face a civil penalty of up to $500 per violation.

Work done without a required permit can leave the owner with building violations they never saw coming. The law also requires the city's Department of Buildings to tell the public about its watch list of contractors caught working without permits. The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection must run a year of outreach to home improvement contractors about the new notices.

Council Member Yusef Salaam introduced the bill in February 2025. He said homeowners, especially middle-class families, often get hit with violations without knowing a permit was needed. The final bill had 39 co-sponsors and passed the Council on November 12, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams neither approved nor vetoed it, so it became law in December 2025 without his signature. The law took effect June 10, 2026. The consumer protection department's implementing rules took effect June 19, 2026.
