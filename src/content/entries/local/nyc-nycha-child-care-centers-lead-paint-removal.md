Many child care centers on New York City public housing campuses are in buildings put up before lead paint was banned in 1978. The city is now paying to permanently abate that paint instead of only stabilizing damaged spots. Abatement can mean full paint removal, replacing painted components, or another federally approved method. NYCHA tested 76 centers and found lead paint in 70. Its status page listed 52 as abated on Nov. 24, 2026. Until the remaining work is done, the centers are inspected regularly and any damaged paint is stabilized and repaired.

Families do not need to apply. NYCHA schedules and runs the work. Lead paint is dangerous when it chips or peels, and young children are most at risk. Four children at three of these centers had high blood lead levels in 2024.

The federal monitor overseeing NYCHA asked the authority to test the older centers and abate those that tested positive. NYCHA, headed by CEO Lisa Bova-Hiatt, requested more than $10 million from the city and is carrying out the work. The Mayor's Office secured the funding, and Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the $10.2 million investment with Bova-Hiatt in July 2026. It is part of $195 million in city capital funding for lead testing and abatement in NYCHA buildings in fiscal year 2027.
