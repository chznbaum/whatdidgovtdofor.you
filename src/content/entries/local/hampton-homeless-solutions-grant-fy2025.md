For the year that ran from July 2024 through June 2025, Hampton accepted a $1,085,922 state-and-federal grant for homeless services across the Virginia Peninsula. Of that award, $940,083 came from Virginia's general fund and $145,839 from the federal Emergency Solutions Grant. The award supported emergency shelters, rapid re-housing (including for veterans), help that keeps people from losing their homes, and a single front door where people can ask for help.

The program served Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County. Help came through local agencies and nonprofits in the Virginia Peninsula Collaborative, among them ForKids, HELP, Menchville House, LINK, Transitions, and the Newport News and James City County human services offices. You didn't apply to the city directly. People in a housing crisis go through the region's central intake, which matches them to shelter or rehousing help.

Hampton's Department of Human Services applied for and won the DHCD grant, as it had every year since fiscal 2015. For this grant, it served as the lead agency for the region's homelessness consortium. The City Council accepted the money and added it to the budget on Aug. 14, 2024. The grant required no local cash or in-kind match, so Hampton did not have to add local money to receive it.
