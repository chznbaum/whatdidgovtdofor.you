For the year that ran from July 2024 through June 2025, Hampton accepted a $1,085,922 state-and-federal grant for homeless services across the Virginia Peninsula. Of that award, $940,083 came from Virginia's general fund and $145,839 from the federal Emergency Solutions Grant. The award supported emergency shelters, rapid re-housing (including for veterans), help that keeps people from losing their homes, and a single front door where people can ask for help.

The program served Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County. Help came through local agencies and nonprofits in the Virginia Peninsula Collaborative, among them ForKids, HELP, Menchville House, LINK, Transitions, and the Newport News and James City County human services offices. You didn't apply to the city directly. People in a housing crisis went through the region's central intake, which matched them to shelter or rehousing help.

Hampton's Department of Human Services served as fiscal agent for the consortium's community application and received the DHCD award, as it had done each year since fiscal 2015. Director Tamara Temoney-Porter led the department. Mayor Donnie Tuck presided when the City Council accepted and appropriated the money on Aug. 14, 2024. VHSP required a 25% community match, but the application documented that match through existing Newport News and nonprofit resources rather than a new cash or in-kind contribution from Hampton.
