If you work for the City of Richmond in an administrative or technical job covered by the unit, you have a union contract. It took effect July 1, 2024, and runs through June 30, 2027. Pay went up 4% in July 2024, followed by 3.25% in July 2025 and 3.25% in July 2026. The contract also provides at least $1 million for salary upgrades, protects union activity on city property, and requires the city to prove it has a legitimate reason for discipline or firing. Workers in covered job titles receive the contract's protections whether or not they are union members.

Virginia enacted a law in 2020, effective in 2021, allowing local governments to bargain collectively with their employees. Richmond authorized collective bargaining in 2022. Employees in the Administration and Technical unit then chose SEIU Virginia 512, bargained with Mayor Levar Stoney's administration, and ratified the deal in January 2024, with 98% of voting members in favor.

Councilmember Reva Trammell had fought for collective bargaining for more than 20 years and was lead patron of Richmond's earlier collective-bargaining ordinances; Council President Kristen Nye co-led the council effort. On May 13, 2024, the council approved this contract 8–0 alongside the police and fire contracts. The city's estimated three-year cost was about $20 million.
