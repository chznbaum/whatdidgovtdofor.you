If you or someone you love is in a mental health crisis in Fulton County, you can go to the county's Behavioral Health Crisis Center at 2805 Metropolitan Parkway SW in Atlanta, or call 404-616-4784. It's open around the clock, every day of the year. It offers three levels of care: a "Living Room" staffed by peers, chairs where people can be watched over for a short stay, and 24 beds for people who need a few days to stabilize.

The center opened in August 2024 as the county's first publicly funded crisis center. It was built with $15 million in federal pandemic relief money. It gives people in crisis somewhere to go besides an emergency room, which matters more since two Wellstar hospitals closed in 2022.

Grady Health System runs the center under a county contract. On June 3, 2026, the Fulton County Board of Commissioners, chaired by Robb Pitts, voted 5–0 to renew it. The renewal pays Grady up to $12.6 million to keep the center running from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027. It's the third of eight one-year renewals the contract allows.
