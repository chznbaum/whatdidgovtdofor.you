If you live in a New York City homeless shelter, the city must tell you in writing what you're owed. The Department of Homeless Services has to write a statement of the rights and services available to people in shelter, post it on its website, and send copies to shelters and social services offices to hand out. It must be available in the city's designated languages.

The law collects rights that already existed in court orders and other laws; it doesn't create new ones, and you can't sue under it directly. News coverage describes the statement as including New York's long-standing right to shelter, the right to apply for rental help, the right to complain about shelter conditions without retaliation, protections tied to gender identity, and diapers for parents with babies. Its sponsor called it a tool to help people stand up for themselves.

Public Advocate Jumaane Williams introduced the bill in April 2022 with Council Member Diana Ayala and other cosponsors. The City Council passed it unanimously on April 27, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, but he also didn't veto it, so it became law in late May 2023. The department had until late August 2023 to put it into effect.
