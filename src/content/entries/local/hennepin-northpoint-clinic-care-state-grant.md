NorthPoint Health & Wellness is a community health center in North Minneapolis. People who are uninsured or underinsured can get care there. This year it has $1.85 million in state money for care and related clinic improvements. In August, Hennepin County accepted $526,240 more in state funding, raising the grant from about $1.32 million.

The county says the money pays for medical and behavioral health services at youth program sites, help coordinating care during pregnancy, and help managing chronic illnesses. It also covers financial counseling and help signing up for health coverage, plus vaccines and prescriptions for people with gaps in coverage. Part of it upgrades the dental clinic on Broadway Avenue with new chairs, an upgraded X-ray room, and an improved intake and queueing system that helps enroll uninsured patients in coverage or financial assistance. You do not apply for the grant; NorthPoint uses the money to provide these services.

The money comes from a Minnesota program created in 2007. It gives federally qualified health centers money to maintain and expand care for low-income patients. Each center's share depends on how much in discounts it gave patients the year before. NorthPoint operates under an agreement between Hennepin County's primary care division and an independent governing board. The County Board of Commissioners approved accepting the added funding on Aug. 4, 2026, and the grant runs through Dec. 31, 2026.
