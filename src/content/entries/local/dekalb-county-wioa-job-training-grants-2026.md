DeKalb County authorized acceptance of up to $2,863,013 in federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act money. The county described the awards as projections that would flow through the Technical College System of Georgia and support programs from April 1, 2026, through June 30, 2028. It projected $871,602 for adults, $1,029,001 for dislocated workers and $962,410 for youth. The resolution requires no county money or local match.

For people who qualify, WorkSource DeKalb offers career counseling, skills assessments, resume and interview help, job listings and no-cost training for in-demand work. Its youth program serves eligible low-income residents ages 16 to 24 who face barriers to school or work and includes paid work experience. As of Nov. 16, 2026, WorkSource DeKalb was not accepting new Adult or Dislocated Worker WIOA applications because many applicants were awaiting review and services. General career-center resources remain available to all job seekers.

The DeKalb County Board of Commissioners, led by Presiding Officer Chakira Johnson, approved the grant acceptance 7–0 on Aug. 11, 2026. The funding now faces a federal threat: H.R. 9260, reported by the House Appropriations Committee, would rescind $712 million in already enacted WIOA adult-training funds and provide no new adult or youth funding for fiscal 2027. A continuing resolution keeps current funding in place only through Dec. 11, 2026.
