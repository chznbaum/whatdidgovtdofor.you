New York City has a program for apartment buildings whose landlords keep leaving tenants without heat. The city's housing agency, HPD, picks buildings with heat violations in each of the past two heat seasons and heat complaints from more than one apartment. Their landlords must put an internet-connected heat sensor in each apartment and send HPD the temperature readings every month from October through May. HPD inspectors also come at least twice a month from October through January, whether or not anyone calls in a complaint. The agency may stop those inspections after January only if no heat violations were issued since October and no heat-sensor violations remain open. A building stays in the program for up to four years and can leave sooner if it meets the discharge rules after a heat season.

This law makes the city add 50 buildings every year, starting in 2024, instead of 50 every two years. Tenants don't have to apply. HPD posts a notice in buildings it picks. Tenants can turn down a sensor in their own apartment, and landlords who don't comply face fines.

Council Member Pierina Sanchez introduced the bill in May 2022 with Council Members Althea Stevens, Amanda Farías and Carmen De La Rosa. They acted after a January 2022 fire at the Twin Parks North West building in the Bronx killed 17 people. Fire officials said it started from a space heater. Their first version would have expanded the program much further, to 150 buildings per borough. The Council passed the narrower version on May 11, 2023, and it became law in June 2023 without Mayor Eric Adams' signature. The program itself came from a 2020 law by then-Council Member Ritchie Torres.
