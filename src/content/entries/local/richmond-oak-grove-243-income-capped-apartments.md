The former Oak Grove Elementary on Richmond's Southside was replaced by a new school in 2013, and the old site then sat empty for more than ten years. In its place at 2200 Ingram Avenue are 243 apartments called Noon Ingram, which opened in 2025. The units have income limits at 50%, 60%, or 70% of the area's median income, averaging no more than 60% across the building. That allows apartments with several different income limits. If you think you qualify, you apply directly with the property.

The city did not hand the developer a lump sum. Each year that the apartments remain affordable and meet the agreement's other conditions, the developer can receive a grant based on the increase in real estate taxes created by the new building. Oak Grove's estimated annual grant was $62,316. The city therefore gives up future revenue rather than spending money already in its budget; across Richmond's first 25 approved agreements, that foregone revenue was estimated at about $7.2 million a year. Payments after year 15 also depend on required investment in the property's upkeep.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance's patron, and Council member Reva Trammell joined as a co-patron. Trammell represented the 8th District and worked with the developer and neighbors on concerns including traffic, parking, and community space. City Council approved the grant agreement on Oct. 10, 2023. Construction began in June 2024, and the city held a ribbon cutting on Oct. 30, 2025.
