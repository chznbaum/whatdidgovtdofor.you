Richmond got $155 million in federal pandemic relief from the American Rescue Plan Act, paid in two halves. This ordinance accepted the second half, $77.4 million. It put $60.5 million of that into parks and recreation building projects and the rest into the city's general budget. Under the city's spending plan, most of the second-year parks money went to four community centers: $19 million each for Lucks Field and T.B. Smith, $8 million for Southside and $7 million for Calhoun.

Three of the centers are open to the public. Southside Community Center held its ribbon cutting in March 2025 and began full-time hours in April, with a boxing studio, two gyms, dance and music studios, and an indoor walking track. The new T.B. Smith center on Ruffin Road opened that August with a computer lab, a kitchen, and outdoor courts and fields. The 30,000-square-foot Lucks Field center in the East End opened in September with indoor and rooftop basketball, study rooms and a teaching kitchen. Lucks Field and T.B. Smith can also serve as emergency shelters. As of Nov. 20, 2026, the city still listed Calhoun as closed and under construction.

Mayor Levar Stoney drew up the relief plan, and he and Council member Ann-Frances Lambert were the ordinance's patrons. City Council adopted it on November 14, 2022.
