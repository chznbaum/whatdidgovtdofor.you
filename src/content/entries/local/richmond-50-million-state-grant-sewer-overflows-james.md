Much of Richmond's sewer system is more than a century old, and in many neighborhoods the same pipes carry both stormwater and sewage. When a heavy rain overloads those pipes, the mix of storm runoff and raw sewage overflows into the James River. In a typical year that adds up to nearly 2 billion gallons. In May 2024, the city accepted $50 million from the state to keep fixing the problem. News coverage said the money would help replace and upgrade sewer pipes in Shockoe Bottom, around Chimborazo Park and in the Almond Creek area.

Residents don't have to apply for anything. The benefit is a cleaner river and less sewage running into it after storms. The grant also covers part of a bill that would otherwise fall on the city and its sewer customers. A 2020 state law requires Richmond to finish this work by 2035. The city estimates the remaining plan will cost about $650 million.

Gov. Glenn Youngkin proposed the $50 million in Virginia's 2024-2026 budget, and the General Assembly kept it in the enacted spending plan. Mayor Levar Stoney brought the ordinance to City Council, with Council Vice President Ann-Frances Lambert as co-patron. The council adopted it 8-0 on May 28, 2024. Lambert said at the time that the city was still about $350 million short of what the project needs. In 2025, the city accepted another $100 million state grant for a large storage tank.
