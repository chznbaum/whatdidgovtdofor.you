Starting in 2027, if you work construction on a large housing project that New York City helps pay for, you must get at least $40 an hour in combined wages and benefits, and at least $25 of that must be wages. The law covers projects that create or preserve 150 or more homes, cost $3 million or more, and get at least $1.5 million in city loans, grants or discounted land. Supportive housing, scatter sites, preservation projects and projects on NYCHA land are exempt, as are state-certified apprentices. Starting in July 2028, the floor will rise each year with inflation.

You don't have to apply. Developers must also make a best effort to give at least 30% of the work to city residents. The City Comptroller monitors compliance and investigates complaints. After that process, the mayor or a designee can order 1.5 times the unpaid wages or benefits plus a civil penalty. Workers can also sue on their own.

Council Member Carmen De La Rosa introduced the bill in May 2024. Backed by unions including Laborers Local 79 and 32BJ SEIU, she brought a revised version to a vote on Dec. 18, 2025, and the Council passed it. It became law in January 2026 without a mayor's signature. The real estate industry warned that the wage floor would raise building costs and could leave the city able to fund less affordable housing. Projects with smaller unit counts and supportive housing were left out. In May 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the city would implement the law as part of its housing plan.
