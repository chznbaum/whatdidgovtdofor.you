Before this ordinance, most new single-family homes in Athens-Clarke County had to contain at least 1,000 square feet. The floor was 600 square feet in the RS-5 and RM districts and 450 square feet for multifamily units. Ordinance 26-08-68 replaced those total-size floors with the minimum habitable-area rules in Section 404 of the county's adopted International Property Maintenance Code. Other building, zoning, lot and permit rules still apply.

No special application is required to use the new size standard, but owners and builders still need the permits required for their project. The change makes smaller houses and apartments legal where the old floor-area rule barred them; it does not guarantee a lower sale price or rent.

Commissioner Ovita Thornton asked Athens GICH committee members Charles Smith, Danielle Gilmer and Matthew Hall to develop zoning changes aimed at more affordable homeownership. Their memo specifically proposed removing the 600-to-1,000-square-foot single-family minimums and the 450-square-foot apartment minimum. Thornton moved the March 3 resolution that sent the recommendations to the Planning Commission, and Mayor Kelly Girtz backed and directed the review. On Aug. 4, all 10 county commissioners voted for the ordinance. At the same meeting, they rejected a separate proposal for full kitchens in secondary dwellings, so this change does not by itself legalize an independent backyard home on the same lot.
