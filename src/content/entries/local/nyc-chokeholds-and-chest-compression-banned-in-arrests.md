In New York City it's a crime to restrain someone during an arrest by squeezing their windpipe or the arteries in their neck. It's also a crime to sit, kneel or stand on their chest or back in a way that presses on the diaphragm, the muscle that lets you breathe. Breaking the law is a misdemeanor that can bring up to a year in jail, a fine of up to $2,500, or both. You don't have to be hurt for it to count, and the law doesn't take away your right to sue.

You don't need to do anything to be covered. The law took effect the day it was signed, and it goes further than the state ban Gov. Andrew Cuomo signed in June 2020, which applies only when a chokehold causes serious injury or death.

Council Member Rory Lancman first proposed a chokehold ban in 2014, after Eric Garner died in an NYPD chokehold. It passed overwhelmingly on June 18, 2020, weeks after George Floyd was killed in Minneapolis. The Council rejected an exception Mayor Bill de Blasio wanted for officers resting their weight on someone's back or chest, and de Blasio then signed the bill on July 15, 2020.

Police unions sued. A Manhattan judge struck down the entire law in 2021, finding the diaphragm language too vague, but an appeals court restored it in May 2022. On Nov. 20, 2023, the state's highest court unanimously upheld the whole law.
