If you work full time as a salaried EMT for Chesterfield County, you will receive enhanced hazardous-duty coverage through the Virginia Retirement System on Jan. 1, 2027. The county already provides that coverage to police officers, firefighters and jail officers. Each year of service will count at 1.85% of your average final pay. In general, you can retire with a full pension at 50 once you have 25 years of service, or at 60 with 5 years. After at least 20 years of hazardous-duty service, you may also qualify for the current $17,856 annual supplement until you reach Social Security's full retirement age. You do not have to apply to have your job class covered: the county made the election for the position, and it pays the employer's share of the cost.

The Board of Supervisors approved the change 5-0 on July 22, 2026, on a motion by Kevin Carroll that Jim Ingle seconded. The county said demand for emergency medical care keeps growing as more older residents stay in their homes, and that the better benefit will help it hire and keep first responders.

In the same vote, the board authorized staff to hire a consultant to establish a separate retirement savings plan for 911 dispatchers, animal control officers and juvenile detention youth counselors effective Jan. 1, 2027. State law does not let those jobs receive the hazardous-duty benefit.
