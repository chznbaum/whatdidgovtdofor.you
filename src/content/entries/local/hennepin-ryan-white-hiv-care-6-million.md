If you live with HIV in the 13-county Minneapolis-St. Paul area and qualify for Ryan White services, this federal grant can help pay for care and support from local providers. For March 2026 through February 2027, the grant totals $6,010,733. It funds 12 clinics and community groups that provide 13 services to about 3,000 low-income people with HIV.

The grant pays for early treatment, outpatient visits, medical case management, mental health and substance use treatment, nutrition therapy, and health care at home. It also pays for help that keeps people in care, including housing assistance, food shelves and delivered meals, peer support, health education, and legal aid. Minnesota's Department of Human Services determines eligibility through a centralized system, and people can apply for Ryan White services, including medication assistance, through the state.

Hennepin County became eligible for Part A funding for the metro area in 1995. A community council decides how the money is split among services, and at least 33% of its members must be people with HIV who are eligible for services and unaffiliated with a funded provider. The county board accepted $1,680,991 in April 2026. On July 14, 2026, it accepted the remaining $4,329,742 so services could continue for the full grant year.
