In Columbus, some 911 calls about a mental health crisis can bring a three-person team designed for that work. The Care Two unit combines an advanced EMT or Fire Medic from Columbus Fire and EMS, a Columbus police officer, and a certified mental health clinician from Valley Healthcare. The police dispatch center can route the unit to a call.

You do not sign up for the service. The team evaluates people where they are, can request more treatment resources, and can arrange ambulance transportation when needed. The city designed the unit to offer alternatives and reduce unnecessary trips to hospital emergency rooms or jail. Its original plan called for eight-hour weekday service, with 24-hour coverage as a future goal.

Columbus Fire and EMS developed the unit as part of its broader Columbus Correct Care program under Fire Chief Sal Scarpa. On Nov. 19, 2024, Columbus Council voted 10-0 for Resolution 450-24, authorizing the agreement among Fire and EMS, police, and Valley Healthcare. Council renewed the partnership through Resolution 142-26 on June 9, 2026, when the eight members voting all supported it.
