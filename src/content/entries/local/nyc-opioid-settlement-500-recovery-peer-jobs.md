New York City is paying for more "peer specialists": people who have been through substance use and recovery themselves and are trained to help others do the same. The $12 million pays to certify and employ them, sends outreach workers and vans into neighborhoods in all five boroughs, and funds training scholarships. Over four years, the city expects the money to create about 500 new peer jobs.

The work runs through seven nonprofits, among them Phoenix House, Odyssey House, the Fortune Society, Samaritan Daytop Village and Community Health Action of Staten Island. You don't sign up with the city. You get help through these groups' recovery centers and street outreach, and you can call or text 988 at any hour for a referral.

The money comes from settlements with drug companies over the opioid crisis, not from city taxes. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced it on May 7, 2026, at Phoenix House in Brooklyn. Health Commissioner Alister Martin presented the plan to put 500 more peers to work. It is part of the city's goal to cut overdose deaths by a quarter by 2030. Overdose deaths in the city fell from 3,056 in 2023 to 2,192 in 2024.
