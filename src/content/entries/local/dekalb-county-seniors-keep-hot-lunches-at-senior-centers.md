If you're 60 or older and live in DeKalb County, you can join one of the county's four neighborhood senior centers, which serve hot lunches. The county also requires members to be able to get around independently and have no cognitive impairments such as dementia or Alzheimer's. Once executed, the board-approved contract will fund those lunches for a three-year term. It also covers shelf-stable meals, food that keeps without a refrigerator, for seniors who qualify. Meals on Wheels Atlanta will plan, deliver and track the meals for the county's Department of Human Services.

There is no membership fee, though some classes may charge a small supply fee. The county also gives members rides to the senior center in their community. To sign up, call the Neighborhood Centers office at 770-322-2950.

DeKalb County's Purchasing and Contracting Department, led by Chief Procurement Officer Michelle Butler, recommended Meals on Wheels Atlanta after three proposals were submitted. The nonprofit scored 96.2 out of 100; the next-highest responsive proposal scored 55.64, and the third was nonresponsive. The contract can cost up to $1,885,529 over three years and is paid from an Atlanta Regional Commission grant fund. Presiding Officer Chakira Johnson chaired the Board of Commissioners when it approved the award on Oct. 27, 2026, and authorized the county's chief executive to execute the contract.
