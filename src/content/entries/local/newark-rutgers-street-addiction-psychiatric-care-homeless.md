If you're homeless in Newark and struggling with drugs or alcohol, the Rutgers team can bring care to you in the field. Newark is continuing the program for 2026 with two psychiatric nurse practitioners, two recovery community coordinators and a medical director. They provide comprehensive evaluation, treatment and medication management, along with support during behavioral or medical crises.

The Rutgers staff work with outreach, housing, hospital and law-enforcement partners to reach homeless residents with substance use disorders. The resolution does not specify whether residents contact the team directly or are connected through one of those partners. The contract covers January 1 through December 31, 2026.

The city is paying up to $535,000 from its opioid trust account. Newark's state-filed opioid settlement report says its mobile street-medicine initiative dates to fiscal 2024; a prior council record shows the Rutgers contract was extended and expanded for 2025. Because Rutgers is a state university, state law allowed the city to award the contract without competitive bidding. Council President C. Lawrence Crump led the Municipal Council when it approved the 2026 contract 8-0 on June 17, with Michael Silva absent. The vote also ratified the work back to January 1.
