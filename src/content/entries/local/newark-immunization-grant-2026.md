Newark's health department runs a program to protect children from diseases that vaccines can prevent. This year the state is paying for it with a $388,000 grant, which covers July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. The money pays for giving shots, storing and handling vaccines safely, reaching out to families, and following up with children who need care. The city adds none of its own money.

The program serves Newark residents from birth through age 18. Free vaccines through the state's Vaccines for Children program are for eligible children, including those who are Medicaid-eligible, uninsured, or American Indian or Alaska Native. During the 2026 back-to-school season, eligible Newark youth up to age 17 could get free shots at 110 William Street without an appointment.

The department's director applied to the New Jersey Department of Health in May 2026, and the state sent a letter of intent to fund the request in July. On Sept. 9, 2026, the Municipal Council voted 9-0 to approve the application and accept the money. Council President Luis A. Quintana presided over that vote. The resolution ratified the city's acceptance of the grant back to July 1.
