New York City law gives you a right to be free from unreasonable searches and seizures by police, and from excessive force even when no search or arrest is happening. If an NYPD employee or a special patrolman violates that right, you can sue them and their employer in any court with authority over the case within three years. The officer can't use qualified immunity, the court-made rule that often ends police misconduct cases before they reach a jury.

If you win, you can recover your actual damages, and possibly punitive damages, or choose a flat $1,000 instead. The court can also make the city pay your lawyer's fees and can order the officer to stop further violations. Lawsuits under federal law still face qualified immunity. The city's Law Department must post details of these lawsuits online twice a year.

Council Member Stephen Levin sponsored the bill, and Speaker Corey Johnson's Council passed it 37–10, with two members abstaining, on March 25, 2021, as part of a package of police accountability bills. Mayor Bill de Blasio said he supported it but returned it unsigned, and it became law on April 25, 2021. It applies to searches and uses of force from that date on.
