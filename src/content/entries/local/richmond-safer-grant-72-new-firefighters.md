Richmond's fire department had been shrinking for decades while its workload grew. Thirteen fire companies had been disbanded since 1970, even as calls rose more than 700 percent. The fire chief said that some neighborhoods could wait up to 20 minutes for a ladder truck. Firefighters were also being ordered to work long stretches of overtime, which their union said was hurting their health.

In 2022, Richmond Fire applied for a federal grant from FEMA's Staffing for Adequate Fire and Emergency Response (SAFER) program and won $13.7 million. The grant paid for 72 new firefighter positions, enough to rebuild four 18-person fire companies: three ladder trucks and one engine. You don't have to do anything to benefit. The new crews answer calls across the city.

Mayor Levar Stoney and Council Member Ann-Frances Lambert carried the ordinance that accepted the first $4.54 million and set up a fund for it. Fire Chief Melvin Carter led the grant application. The City Council adopted the ordinance on Jan. 9, 2023. A record class of recruits graduated that September, and the department began bringing the four companies back. When the grant ran out, the city's FY 2026 budget added $6.6 million so the city could keep paying for the 72 firefighters itself.
