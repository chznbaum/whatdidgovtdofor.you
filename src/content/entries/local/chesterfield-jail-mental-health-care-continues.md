If you or someone in your family is held in the Chesterfield County Jail and has a mental illness, the jail's mental health programs are funded for another year. The state says these programs assess people in jail, make treatment plans, provide care during incarceration and connect participants to community services after release.

On August 26, 2026, the Board of Supervisors authorized the county administrator to accept and spend the fiscal 2027 grant from the Virginia Department of Criminal Justice Services. The minutes don't give the dollar amount. The year before, the same grant was $320,350, and the county didn't have to put in any money of its own. The county said that FY2026 grant would cover continued housing and medication help, data collection and training for program staff and administrators.

Chesterfield has run the program for about a decade. In 2016, the state chose the Chesterfield County Sheriff's Office, led by Sheriff Karl Leonard, as one of six jails in Virginia's first mental health pilot. The Virginia Department of Criminal Justice Services now lists Chesterfield among five jails receiving this state funding.
