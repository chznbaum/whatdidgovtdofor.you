Duluth's Lakewalk runs along the Lake Superior shore. Its busiest stretch is being rebuilt so it can stand up to the lake. Storms in 2017 and 2018 tore up pavement, threw boulders onto the path and ate away the shoreline. That damage led to state and federal emergency declarations. The project runs from Endion Station in Canal Park to about 27th Avenue East. It moves the trail back from the water where it can and rebuilds the rock barrier that protects the shore. It also removes barriers for wheelchair users, widens the trail so bikes and walkers have their own space, improves access to the water, and adds new signs.

The Lakewalk is a public trail, and you don't need to apply for anything. Parts of the trail near Leif Erikson Park are closed during construction, with posted detours. The trail reopens over the winter. Work resumes in April 2027, and the city expects to be mostly done by the end of 2027.

The City Council authorized the federal grant application in February 2023. Sen. Amy Klobuchar helped secure the $8.2 million award and announced it in Duluth that July. A $2.05 million state grant covers the 20% nonfederal match. The city is adding about $2 million of its own money. The council formally accepted the federal grant on July 27, 2026.
