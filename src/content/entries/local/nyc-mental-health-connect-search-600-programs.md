If you or someone in your family needs mental health or addiction care in New York City, you can now look for it in one place. NYC Mental Health Connect, at mentalhealthconnect.nyc.gov, lists more than 600 providers. These include outpatient clinics for adults, youth and families, peer support, care coordination, crisis services, and programs for people with developmental disabilities. You can filter by the kind of help you need, the languages spoken, the ages served, the insurance accepted, and how soon you can get an appointment. A short questionnaire suggests options, including in-person and online care.

The site is free to use. It shows free and sliding-scale programs and which providers take Medicaid. It isn't a crisis line: if you're in crisis, call or text 988 any time. Providers can also use it to find services for their clients.

The city built the site because many people didn't know where to turn. In a 2024 city report, 24% of teens surveyed said they needed or wanted mental health care but didn't know where to get it. The New York State Office of Mental Health, led by Commissioner Ann Sullivan, partnered with the city in developing the site and provided $5 million in one-time funding for development and launch. The NYC Health Department, led by Commissioner Alister Martin, co-launched the site and keeps its listings up to date. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration launched it on Oct. 9, 2026.
