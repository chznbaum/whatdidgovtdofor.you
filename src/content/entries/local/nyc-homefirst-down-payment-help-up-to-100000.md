If you're buying your first home in New York City, HomeFirst can give you up to $100,000 toward your down payment or closing costs. It works for a one- to four-family house, a condo, or a co-op in any of the five boroughs. The money comes as a forgivable loan with no monthly payments. You have to live in the home as your main residence for 10 or 15 years, depending on the loan's size and source, or you may have to pay some of it back.

To qualify, your household can earn up to 120% of the area median income. In 2026, that's $203,520 for a family of four. Before the expansion, the limit was 80%. You also need to take an approved homebuyer class, apply through an HPD-approved counseling agency, and put in at least 3% of the price from your own savings.

The City Council called for the expansion in its City for All housing plan in November 2024. Under Speaker Adrienne Adams, the Council won it during talks over the City of Yes housing zoning changes. The agreement added $41 million over five fiscal years, doubling the program's City funding to $82 million. Mayor Eric Adams and HPD Commissioner Adolfo Carrión Jr. announced the expansion on January 30, 2025. HPD runs the program with nonprofit partners. The city expects the expansion to help 1,000 more households buy homes.
