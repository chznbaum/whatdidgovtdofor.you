Jacksonville is spending $5.28 million from its opioid settlement fund on 15 local programs that work to prevent addiction, treat it, and help people stay in recovery. The programs are run by 13 organizations, and the grants cover October 2026 through September 2027. An opioid abatement manager in the Jacksonville Fire and Rescue Department oversees how the money is used.

About $1.8 million pays for prevention, mostly for young people, through groups like the Boys and Girls Clubs of Northeast Florida, Hubbard House and Volunteers in Medicine. About $1.74 million pays for treatment, including Gateway Community Services' hospital bridge program and treatment at the I.M. Sulzbacher Center for the Homeless. Another $1.74 million pays for recovery support, including programs at the City Rescue Mission and Trinity Rescue Mission. You don't apply to the city for this help. You go to one of the recipient organizations for its services.

A city committee scored the grant applications. Council President Nick Howland (R) introduced the ordinance. The council's Finance Committee approved it 8-0, and the full council passed it 16-0 on Sept. 22, 2026, with Kevin Carrico, Reggie Gaffney Jr. and Ju'Coby Pittman abstaining. The money comes from opioid settlements, not local taxes.
