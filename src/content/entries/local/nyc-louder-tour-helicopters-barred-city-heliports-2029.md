New York City can't control the airspace over its streets, but it does own two Manhattan heliports, at East 34th Street and downtown near Wall Street. Starting December 1, 2029, this law bars non-essential helicopters from taking off or landing there unless they meet the FAA's strictest noise standard, called Stage 3. Non-essential flights include sightseeing tours and commuter trips. Military, fire, ambulance, police, news and some commercial production flights are exempt.

You don't have to do anything. The rule will be written into the city's contracts with the companies that run the heliports. Covered city contracts executed beginning in 2026 must require the city's contracted economic-development entity to report each year what percentage of flights by helicopters that fail the standard were non-essential. The restrictions can't be enforced until the airport operator completes a required federal process that includes public comment.

Majority Leader Amanda Farías introduced the bill in February 2024. Council members Keith Powers and Shahana Hanif joined her in pushing it, along with more than two dozen colleagues. Farías said 311 took more than 50,000 helicopter noise complaints in 2023. The Council passed the bill 46–1 in April 2025, two weeks after a sightseeing helicopter crash in the Hudson River killed six people. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature on May 24, 2025.
