Richmond is paying for a substance use treatment program run by Health Brigade, the free clinic once known as the Fan Free Clinic. The city granted the clinic $508,857 to build out care for Richmond residents with opioid use disorder who are at risk of overdose, including people who also have other mental or physical health conditions.

The program covers medication for opioid use disorder, therapy, peer recovery support, and recovery groups. It also offers case management, help reaching other services, and links to detox and inpatient care. Street-based teams bring these services to the neighborhoods with the most overdose harm. Health Brigade has been a state-certified harm reduction site since 2018 and had already served more than 6,000 people, and the city said this money would let the clinic reach more.

The money comes from Richmond's share of the national opioid settlement with drugmaker Janssen, so no local tax dollars are involved. Mayor Danny Avula's administration proposed the grant. The City Council approved it 9–0 on June 2, 2025, and the city lists it among its current settlement-funded investments.
