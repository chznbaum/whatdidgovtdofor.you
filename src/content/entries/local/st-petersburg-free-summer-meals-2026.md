When school let out, kids in St. Petersburg could still get a free breakfast and lunch. The city's Summer Food Service Program ran from early June to early August 2026 at 17 sites around the city. Depending on the site, it served breakfast, lunch or both. To pay for the meals, the city took a state grant of up to $536,000 and put that money in the Parks & Recreation budget.

Any child 18 or younger could eat. Families didn't need to register, fill out an application or show proof of income. Kids just came to a site during meal hours. The program is part of the federal Summer Food Service Program, which the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services runs in Florida. The city runs it through Parks & Recreation, its Healthy St. Pete division and Summer BreakSpot.

The City Council approved the grant 8-0 on May 28, 2026, as part of its consent agenda. Council Chair Lisset Hanewicz led the meeting. The resolution let the mayor sign the grant agreement and moved the grant money into the summer meals project for the year.
