Naloxone, often sold as Narcan, can reverse an opioid overdose within minutes. New York City law now requires the Education Department to keep it in every public school building that has an assigned school nurse. Before the law, the city had started putting it in high schools. The law extends it to elementary and middle schools too. Each year the department must tell the mayor and the Council Speaker how many buildings have it and how many times it was used.

Families and students don't need to do anything. By the summer of 2024 the city said every public school had naloxone and every school nurse was trained to give it. The city has also trained hundreds of teachers and other staff. Opioid overdose deaths among New Yorkers ages 15 to 24 rose from 48 in 2019 to 87 in 2022, and almost all of those deaths involved fentanyl or heroin.

Council Member Rafael Salamanca of the Bronx sponsored the bill, joined by 18 colleagues. He had pushed the idea for about six years after a teacher in one of his South Bronx school districts died of an overdose. The Council passed it on December 6, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams returned it unsigned, so it became law on January 6, 2024, and took effect 60 days later.
