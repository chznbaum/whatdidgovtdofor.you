Union County added $1,422,680 to its 2026 budget for its youth job training program. The money is federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act funding, and the New Jersey Department of Labor and Workforce Development sends it to the county. It pays for free help for young people looking for a path into work: career counseling, tutoring, job readiness training, paid or unpaid work experience, leadership development, and help earning a high school equivalency diploma.

The program serves eligible in-school youth ages 14 to 21 and out-of-school youth ages 16 to 24 who face barriers to finishing school or finding work. Federal rules require that at least 75% of non-administrative youth funds serve young people who are out of school, and at least 20% pay for work experience. You can start by calling or walking in to a One Stop for Youth center in Plainfield or Elizabeth. Staff check eligibility under state and federal rules and may ask for supporting documents.

Commissioner Kimberly Palmieri-Mouded brought the resolution. The Board of County Commissioners approved it 8-0 on July 30, 2026, with Commissioner Mirabella absent.
