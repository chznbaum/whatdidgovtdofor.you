By October 2024, New York City had counted 222 lithium-ion-battery fires that year, after 224 by the same point in 2023. Officials said uncertified e-bike equipment posed a particular risk. This law required the city to run a trade-in program. In the pilot the city ultimately ran, a selected worker surrendered an operable uncertified e-bike or illegal moped and its batteries, then got a certified e-bike and two certified batteries for free.

The city's Department of Transportation ran the program in 2025 with $2 million. Food delivery workers who were at least 18, lived in the city, had earned at least $1,500 from food delivery in the preceding 12 months, and owned and used an eligible device for deliveries could apply. DOT said delivery workers often need two batteries to complete a full day. Handouts started in June 2025, and the final distribution on Sept. 18 brought the total to 402 workers. Applications are closed, and DOT says the program ended in 2025.

Majority Leader Keith Powers introduced the bill in March 2023, and the Council passed it that September. Mayor Eric Adams did not sign it, so it became law without his signature on Oct. 15, 2023. His administration funded the program, and the Transportation Department under Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez carried it out. The law expired on Sept. 1, 2025, but DOT completed the distribution later that month. The city said any future phase would depend on additional funding.
