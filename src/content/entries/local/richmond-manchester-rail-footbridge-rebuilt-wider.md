In Manchester, a narrow footbridge crosses the Norfolk Southern railroad tracks and leads to the T. Tyler Potterfield Memorial Bridge, the walking and biking crossing over the James River. Richmond is rebuilding the top of that footbridge and widening the path from 6 feet to 10 feet, with 2-foot shoulders. The extra room lets people on foot and on bikes share it, and the bridge will also serve as a spur of the Fall Line Trail. The project is expected to cost about $6.3 million.

You don't have to do anything to use it. The bridge will stay free and open to anyone once the work is done. Design is still in its early stages. The city expects construction to start in summer 2028 and take 12 months to two years, because crews have to work around the railroad. While the bridge is closed, people will reach the river crossing from the Floodwall Walk, and the city says it is trying to keep the closure short.

Then-Mayor Levar Stoney's administration sent the agreement to the City Council, and council members Stephanie Lynch and Ellen Robertson co-sponsored it. The council adopted it on Jan. 22, 2024. The ordinance let the city sign a deal with the Central Virginia Transportation Authority, which pays for road, transit, walking and biking projects across the Richmond region, to fund the work.
