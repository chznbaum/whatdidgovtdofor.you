Richmond households that had fallen behind on their water and sewer bills could get federal help paying them off. The money came from the Low Income Household Water Assistance Program, a temporary pandemic-era program that Congress funded through the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021 and the American Rescue Plan Act. Virginia's Department of Social Services ran it with Promise, a payments company. This ordinance let Richmond's Department of Public Utilities sign on so that payments could go straight onto customers' accounts.

To qualify, a household needed a past-due water or sewer balance and income below 150% of the federal poverty level. Households whose water had been shut off, or was about to be, went to the front of the line. Customers applied online or by phone. People already enrolled in LIHEAP, SNAP, TANF, SSI or certain veterans programs didn't have to send separate proof of income. In Virginia, the one-time payment covered past-due bills up to $2,500.

Council member Ann-Frances Lambert and Mayor Levar Stoney carried the ordinance, and the City Council passed it 9-0 on Nov. 14, 2022. Virginia kept accepting applications until its funds were depleted in November 2023. The federal program closed nationally in fiscal 2024, and no new awards are expected.
