Richmond's Gap Grant gave a one-time $1,200 payment toward rent or a mortgage to lower-income city residents who spend 30% or more of their income on housing. To qualify, you had to live in Richmond, have a current lease or mortgage, and earn under HUD's low-income limit for the area, which was $61,800 for one person or $88,250 for a family of four. You applied with proof of income and housing costs, and grants went out first come, first served.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the grant in October 2024 as part of his RVA Stay housing relief package. He pitched it as a way to help renters as well as homeowners, as an alternative to cutting the real estate tax rate. Council President Kristen Nye worked with the administration on the proposal, and seven other council members joined as co-patrons before the ordinance passed unanimously on Nov. 12, 2024. The council set aside $3.9 million from surplus city funds, not from service cuts.

The program struggled. Of the 3,931 applications the city received, more than half lacked required documents, and a backlog built up. The city paused the program in January 2026. By the end of March it had paid 610 households, or $732,000 in all. In April 2026, Mayor Danny Avula's administration and the City Council agreed to end it. On July 27, the council adopted Avula's ordinance moving the remaining $3.168 million to four other housing-relief programs.
