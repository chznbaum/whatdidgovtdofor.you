When school lets out, kids who count on free or reduced-price school lunch can go hungry. In summer 2024, Hampton's Department of Parks, Recreation and Leisure Services ran meal sites across the city from June 17 to Aug. 23. The meals were meant to match what kids get at school through the National School Lunch Program.

The meals were for all children 18 and under, plus people over 18 enrolled in school programs for people with disabilities. Families didn't need to apply: meals at the listed sites were free and served on a first-come, first-served basis. The city expected more than 1,500 children to take part.

Hampton has run this federal program since 1969. USDA reimbursed the city for direct program costs based on how many meals were served. For 2024, the city expected that reimbursement to reach as much as $176,619, though fewer meals would mean a smaller payment. The parks department covered planning and overhead. If the federal reimbursement fell short of the program's cost, the city set aside up to $17,662 from its general fund to make up the difference.

The Hampton City Council accepted the grant and added it to the city's budget on July 10, 2024, while the summer's meals were already being served.
