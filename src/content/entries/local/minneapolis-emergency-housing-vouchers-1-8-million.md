Minneapolis added $1.8 million to its 2026 budget for the Emergency Housing Voucher program. The city pays for the program itself and modeled it on a federal voucher program that is ending. The Minneapolis Public Housing Authority runs it. It helps with rent for up to three years and gives each household up to $3,500 to get past obstacles to moving in, while Hennepin County and its providers offer case management and help finding a place.

You don't apply directly. Hennepin County assesses households that are homeless or at risk of homelessness and refers them through its coordinated entry system, and the housing authority approves them. The program can serve up to 100 households, with a goal of 50 individuals and 50 families.

Council Members Robin Wonsley and Jamal Osman created the program in the city's 2025 budget. Mayor Jacob Frey left it out of his proposed 2026 budget. Wonsley then led the effort to restore it, and the council turned it into a three-year pilot. In April 2026 the council approved the housing authority's agreement to run it. Wonsley wrote this November resolution adding $1.8 million. The council passed it 13–0 on Nov. 12, 2026, and Frey signed it on Nov. 16.
