When New York City helps pay for a new apartment building, some of the apartments now go to people coming out of homelessness. The rule covers new rental buildings with at least 41 units that receive city money, such as loans, grants, tax breaks, or city land sold below its value. At least 15% of the apartments with income limits must be set aside for homeless individuals and families. Renovations of existing buildings and benefits any builder gets automatically don't count.

Homeless New Yorkers get permanent apartments in new buildings instead of staying in shelters. HPD fills these apartments through referrals from the city shelter system. The agency enforces the set-aside and reports each year to the mayor and Council Speaker how many units in each covered project were reserved for homeless households and whether the project is supportive housing.

Council Member Rafael Salamanca Jr. introduced the bill in October 2018. Nathylin Flowers Adesegun, VOCAL-NY and the House Our Future NY campaign rallied, marched and testified for it. Mayor Bill de Blasio opposed it at first, then backed it after a compromise, and Speaker Corey Johnson helped get it to a vote. The Council passed it 38–3 on Dec. 19, 2019, and it became law in January 2020 without the mayor's signature. It took effect July 1, 2020. Advocates estimated it could create about 1,000 additional apartments a year for homeless New Yorkers.
