If an app treats you as an independent contractor and offers restaurant deliveries in New York City, it must let you set a maximum direct distance between the first pickup and final drop-off. That keeps the trip’s end within the distance you chose, but it does not cap the total miles you may travel on a multi-stop trip. You can also refuse trips over all bridges or tunnels, or over specific ones. The app cannot offer trips outside those limits or punish you for using them. Before you accept a trip, it must show the pickup and drop-off locations, estimated time and routed distance, the route used for the estimate, any tip, and your pay.

Covered apps also have to pay at least once a week and offer a no-fee payment option. After six food deliveries, you can request a free insulated bag. You do not file a government application; request the bag from the app.

The protections began with bills the City Council passed in September 2021 after Los Deliveristas Unidos, a campaign of Worker's Justice Project, organized for better conditions. Council Member Justin Brannan sponsored the distance, disclosure and bag bills, which passed 40-3. Council Member Carlos Menchaca sponsored the weekly-pay bill, which passed 41-2. Mayor Bill de Blasio returned the bills unsigned, so they became law without his signature. The protections started April 22, 2022. The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, led by Commissioner Vilda Vera Mayuga, then adopted rules effective July 9, 2022.
