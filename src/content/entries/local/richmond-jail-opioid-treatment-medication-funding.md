If you take medication for opioid addiction and end up in the Richmond City Justice Center, the jail can keep you on it instead of cutting you off. The city's new $400,000 pays for the treatment program and the registered nurse who runs it. You don't apply. You qualify if you arrive with a valid prescription from a community provider. Staff screen people for substance use at intake, coordinate with their outside doctors, teach them about their treatment, and plan how they'll stay on their medication after release.

This matters most right after release. The city's memo cites studies finding that people just out of jail are up to 40 times more likely to die of an opioid overdose in their first two weeks. That risk drops a lot when people stay on their medication. The sheriff's office must also report twice a year on people who use drugs but aren't in the program and, subject to availability and applicable rules, offer them naloxone when they leave.

The pilot started with $300,000 in national opioid settlement money that the City Council approved in late 2024. Mayor Danny Avula's administration proposed this transfer to keep it going. The council adopted it on its consent agenda on Sept. 28, 2026. The money comes from the city's share of national opioid settlements with drug companies, not from local taxes.
