If you have a Citi Bike membership, an e-bike ride of up to 45 minutes will cost no more than $3.65 starting January 19, 2027. Standard members now pay 27 cents a minute, or $12.15 for 45 minutes; Reduced Fare members pay 14 cents a minute, or $6.30. The existing $5.40 cap covers only rides into or out of Manhattan. Shorter rides keep their lower price. The cap includes Reduced Fare Bike Share members, including NYCHA residents and SNAP recipients eligible for $5 monthly memberships. Nonmembers' prices do not change.

You don't need to do anything; the cap applies automatically. Annual membership, member e-bike and overage fees are frozen through May 26, 2029. The cap replaces annual members' 60 free e-bike minutes, though existing minutes last through the current membership. The city will help Lyft begin installing chargers at more than 130 stations in early 2027.

In return, Lyft can sell ads on station panels, shares revenue with the city only above a $5.25 million yearly threshold, and no longer reimburses lost parking-meter revenue. The city says it will spend no additional money and estimates the cap would have saved riders $10.9 million in one year at prior usage.

Council Member Lincoln Restler introduced a fare-cap bill in 2024. Transportation Alternatives organized a $3-cap petition, and StreetsPAC advocated for a cap for years. Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Deputy Mayor Julia Kerson and Transportation Commissioner Mike Flynn negotiated the agreement with Lyft and announced it November 23, 2026.
