New York City's businesses used to hire any of about 90 private trash companies. Trucks from many of them crisscrossed the same neighborhoods every night, driving about 23 million miles a year. A 2016 city study estimated that zones could cut those miles by 49 to 68 percent. Drivers under pressure ran red lights and sped. In 2017 and 2018, two people were killed by the trucks of a single company.

This law divides the city into 20 zones. In each zone, no more than three carters, picked by the Department of Sanitation, collect curbside business trash under 10-year contracts. Five carters are also authorized to serve large containers citywide. Carters can charge no more than the maximum rates in their contracts. Covered drivers, laborers and helpers working on the street must get at least 40 hours of free safety training. When a carting company changes hands, the new owner must keep eligible workers for 90 days. When a zone launches, a business signs up with one of its carters, and the city assigns one to any business that doesn't choose.

Council Member Antonio Reynoso sponsored the bill as chair of the Sanitation Committee. Speaker Corey Johnson co-sponsored and backed it. Sanitation Commissioner Kathryn Garcia led the department's 2018 zone plan. The Transform Don't Trash NYC coalition spent six years advocating for and helping craft the law. The Council passed the bill on Oct. 30, 2019, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on Nov. 20. The first zone, Queens Central, was fully running on Jan. 3, 2025.
