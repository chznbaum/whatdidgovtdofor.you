If you see an ad for a concert, show or game at a New York City venue and it lists a price, that price has to be the full cost of the ticket. Every fee and tax you must pay to buy it has to be included. An ad can't say tickets "start at $20" unless you can really get one for $20 all in. The rule covers venues and their ticket sellers, plus the sites that sell or resell tickets.

You don't have to do anything. The city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection enforces the law. A first violation gets no fine. A second can cost up to $250, and a third or later violation can cost up to $500. The law doesn't cap fees. It only makes sellers show them up front. A 2022 state law already required sellers to show the ticket price with mandatory fees before you select a ticket, and this city law extends that requirement to ads.

Council Member Justin Brannan introduced the bill in February 2022. The Broadway League opposed it, saying the state law was enough. The City Council passed it 49–0 on April 11, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature on May 12, 2023. It took effect 180 days later, in November 2023.
