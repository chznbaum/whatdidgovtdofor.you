Crystal Beach is low-lying, and its streets and yards flood in seasonal rains and big storms. Some residents keep sandbags by their garage doors almost all year. The county's grant application said the area had flooded at least three times in five years. The county also said Hurricane Ian's 2022 storm surge added urgency to fixing the area's flood vulnerabilities.

Current plans call for rebuilding the neighborhood's drainage, including inlets, pipes, swales and ditches. Proposed planted bioswales would replace deep ditches and are designed to drain within 72 hours. Plans also include repaving roads, fixing and adding sidewalks, building an 8-foot trail and upgrading drinking water lines. The work covers the area from Crystal Beach Avenue to Florida Boulevard, between St. Joseph Sound and Avery Bayou. It should also cut flood risk at five sewage pump stations and keep stormwater out of sewer manholes. Residents don't need to apply for anything.

The county drew up preliminary plans in 2008, but said rising infrastructure costs kept the project from being fully funded. On June 16, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Dave Eggers, voted unanimously to accept a $6.16 million state Resilient Florida grant. The grant requires the county to put up the same amount, which comes from Penny for Pinellas, the county's voter-approved sales tax for infrastructure. Design is under way, and the county plans to build from 2029 to 2031.
