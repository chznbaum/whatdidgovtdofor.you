Catch basins are the storm drains at the curb that carry rainwater into the sewer system. New York City has more than 150,000 of them. Much of the sewer system was designed more than a century ago for less intense rainfall. When catch basins clog or cannot keep up, the risk of street flooding rises. The city is spending $108 million over the next decade to replace 6,708 older basins that no longer meet modern standards.

Work was originally set to begin in Queens in July 2026, with Brooklyn, Staten Island and the Bronx next. DEP solicited a Queens replacement contract in June and awarded it in November; a construction start was not publicly confirmed. The city says it will prioritize basins using inspection data, flood sensors and 311 complaints. You don't need to apply. If the basin on your street is clogged, report it to 311. The city says it resolves those complaints within three days, faster than the eight business days the law allows.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Environmental Protection Commissioner Lisa Garcia announced the plan on March 31, 2026. It builds on other work by the department: about $1.5 million a year to upgrade existing basins with better grates and covers, and $20 million for 40 new cleaning trucks.
