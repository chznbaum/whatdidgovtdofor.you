The Cypress is a planned eight-story apartment building in Fort Lauderdale, east of I-95 and north of East Cypress Creek Road. It will have at least 330 rentals, with one, two and three bedrooms. Under its deal with Broward County, at least 136 of those apartments must go to households under set income limits. About 34 are for households earning up to the area median income, and about 102 are for households earning up to 120% of it. The limits stay in place for 30 years after the building opens.

These apartments are meant for working households that earn too much for traditional subsidized housing but still struggle with South Florida rents. Renters will apply through the building and must meet the income limits. The rest of the building rents at market rates.

The county isn't paying for this up front. Once the building is finished and its owner has paid the year's property taxes, the county gives back half of the new county property tax the building generates. The payments last up to 30 years and can't add up to more than $5,845,558. The project is expected to generate about $502,000 in new county property tax in its first year; the county returns half and keeps half. The City of Fort Lauderdale agreed to a similar deal worth up to $8.6 million. The county commission, chaired by Mayor Mark Bogen, approved the agreement 9-0 on Sept. 22, 2026.
