Brickell Bay Drive sits right on Biscayne Bay, and Miami is rebuilding the waterfront to hold back flooding. The project covers the stretch from SE 14th Street to SE 15th Road. It includes a new seawall and bay walk, a rebuilt road, new drainage that lets stormwater soak into the ground, sidewalks, curbs, and ramps for wheelchairs and strollers. The city estimates the cost at about $32.7 million and counts 787 residential properties that the project affects.

On May 28, 2026, the Miami City Commission voted 5-0 to accept up to $5,687,711 from the Florida Department of Environmental Protection's Resilient Florida Program. The city is matching that amount with money it had already budgeted. Commissioner Damian Pardo sponsored the resolution. Residents don't need to do anything.

A design-build team started work in late 2025, and design was moving ahead in 2026. The city expects construction to run from February 2027 to January 2029. It plans to close some lanes along the way and to schedule them so residents are disrupted as little as possible.
