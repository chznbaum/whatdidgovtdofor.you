In a pre-1960 apartment building, if a child under 6 lives with you or regularly spends at least 10 hours a week there, the landlord generally cannot wait for turnover. Lead paint on the rubbing surfaces of doors and windows must be removed or permanently covered; window channels or slides can be replaced instead. The owner must also fix other lead hazards and make bare floors, windowsills and window wells smooth and cleanable. If the child resided there on January 1, 2025, the deadline is July 1, 2027. If a child begins to reside there later, the owner has three years, unless the apartment turns over sooner. Failure can result in a class C immediately hazardous violation.

Shared spaces are covered too. Owners had until August 9, 2025, to test common areas such as halls and stairways. During a complaint-based lead-paint inspection in an apartment, HPD also checks common-area paint along the inspector’s path. Peeling lead paint in a common area where a young child resides is a class C violation. You do not need to apply, but tenants must tell the owner when a child under 6 begins residing in the unit. To report peeling paint, call 311; immigration status does not affect a complaint.

Council Member Diana Ayala sponsored the door and window law, and Council Member Carlina Rivera sponsored the common-area law. Both passed in summer 2023 and became law without Mayor Eric Adams’s signature. HPD adopted implementing rules effective March 17, 2025.
