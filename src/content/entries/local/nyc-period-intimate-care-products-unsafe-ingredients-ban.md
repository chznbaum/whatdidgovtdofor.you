New York City is banning the sale of menstrual and intimate care products made with harmful added ingredients. The law covers tampons, pads and menstrual cups, along with douches, wipes, sprays, powders, washes, suppositories and lubricants used in connection with sexual health. Its list of restricted substances includes lead, mercury, formaldehyde, triclosan, toluene, talc, two phthalates, several parabens, PFAS "forever chemicals" and added fragrance.

You don't need to do anything. Stores, sellers and distributors in the city carry the responsibility, and each product line and each business that sells it can be fined up to $250. The city Health Department formally designates the banned ingredients by rule, and it can add to the list. The law also creates a second ban for products containing restricted substances at or above state-set limits, even when they were not added on purpose. But a 2026 state amendment removed the requirement for New York State to set those limits, so that second ban and the city's product list will not start unless state law changes.

Council Member Amanda Farías introduced the bill in May 2024, and 35 Council members of both parties signed on. The Council passed it 46–0 in September 2025, and it became law that October without Mayor Eric Adams's signature. It took effect on October 10, 2026. The Health Department proposed its rule naming the restricted ingredients in August 2026 and is still finalizing it.
