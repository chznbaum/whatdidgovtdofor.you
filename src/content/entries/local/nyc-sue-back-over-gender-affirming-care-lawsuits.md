If someone sues you, in any state, over gender-affirming care that was lawfully provided in New York City, city law now lets you sue them back. If you win, the court must award you $10,000 and may also award litigation costs when it finds that appropriate. This covers anyone the suit targets, including the person who got the care, the clinician who gave it, and anyone who helped. You don't need to apply for anything. You use this right by bringing your own case in court. It does not apply to a patient's own claim for harm, or a contract party's claim, when New York State or city law recognizes a similar claim.

In 2022, the city created this right to sue back for abortion and other reproductive care, plus hormone (endocrine) care. This law widens it to cover all forms of gender-affirming care. It took effect right away.

Council Member Shahana Hanif sponsored this expansion, known as the AFFIRM Act, and helped write it with Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso. Public Advocate Jumaane Williams cosponsored it. The City Council passed it on April 24, 2025, as part of a package that responded to federal moves against transgender health care. Some New York hospitals had paused care or canceled appointments after a presidential order. The bill drew five no votes. Mayor Eric Adams neither signed nor vetoed it, so it became law without his signature on May 24, 2025.
