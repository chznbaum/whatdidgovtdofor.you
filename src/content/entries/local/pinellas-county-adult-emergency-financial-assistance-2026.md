If you live in Pinellas County, have no minor children in your household, and face a qualifying financial emergency, First Contact can pay up to $4,000 directly to a landlord, mortgage company, utility or another vendor. The money can stop an eviction or foreclosure, keep utilities on, cover emergency transportation to work or a doctor, or pay a sudden work-related expense. Assistance is available no more than once every 12 months.

You can apply if your household earns at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, which is $31,920 a year for one person or $43,280 for two. To start, call 2-1-1 from any phone in the county. Screenings run on weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 6 p.m. You'll need proof of income, citizenship, Pinellas residency and the emergency itself. Requests over $1,000 also need proof of assets.

The nonprofit First Contact, formerly 211 Tampa Bay Cares, was the highest-ranked of two bidders after the county requested proposals in April 2026. On Sept. 10, the county commission, chaired by Dave Eggers, approved a new five-year contract worth up to $7.26 million. The ceiling covers both administering the program and paying assistance. The first year's funding comes from the county's General Fund budget for Human Services; funding for each later year depends on the commission's annual budget vote.
