New York City's Rent Freeze Program sets an approved renter's payment at the prior rent or one-third of monthly household income, whichever is higher, and protects them from most future increases. The city gives the landlord a property tax or payment-in-lieu-of-taxes credit for the difference. Certain otherwise eligible Battery Park City and former Mitchell-Lama tenants used to be excluded because of how their buildings are owned and regulated. Local Law 109 brought qualifying units into the program.

To qualify, you must be named on the lease or have succession rights, be 62 or older for SCRIE or at least 18 and receive qualifying disability benefits for DRIE, have household income of $50,000 or less, and spend more than a third of it on rent. In Battery Park City, the property must be under a Battery Park City Authority lease or sublease and have rent limits in a contract or regulatory agreement. In a former Mitchell-Lama building, your unit must be covered by a regulatory agreement, and you or your qualifying successor must have a signed agreement with the landlord limiting increases for the life of the tenancy. You have to apply to the city.

State Sen. Brian Kavanagh sponsored the Senate bills that authorized these changes. Assembly Members Deborah Glick and Yuh-Line Niou carried Assembly versions for former Mitchell-Lama buildings and Battery Park City, respectively, and Gov. Kathy Hochul signed them in December 2022. Council Member Justin Brannan then sponsored the city law, with Council Member Christopher Marte as a co-sponsor. The Council passed it in June 2023, and it became law in July without Mayor Eric Adams's signature.
