Since the 1960s, a stretch of Atlantic Avenue in Crown Heights and Bedford-Stuyvesant was zoned for industry and storage, and the rules largely banned new homes there. A rezoning approved in May 2025 now allows apartment buildings with shops and workspace on about 21 blocks between Vanderbilt and Nostrand avenues. The city expects about 4,600 new homes and 2,800 permanent jobs.

About 1,900 of the new homes are to be permanently affordable. Roughly 1,000 will come from a rule that requires private builders to set aside income-capped apartments: on average they're for households at 60% of the area's median income, and 400 are for those at 40%. About 900 more will be in fully affordable buildings on seven city, state and nonprofit sites. Most applicants will use the city's Housing Connect lottery as buildings are finished; homes reserved for formerly homeless New Yorkers are generally filled through agency referrals.

The plan grew out of Community Board 8's M-CROWN effort, which began in 2013; board member Gib Veconi helped facilitate it for 11 years. Council Member Crystal Hudson led the final community process, with Council Member Chi Ossé co-leading for Ossé's district. Mayor Eric Adams's Department of City Planning, led by Dan Garodnick, ran the study. Land Use Chair Rafael Salamanca Jr. sponsored the Council resolutions. Hudson secured more than $215 million in commitments; the mayor's office put the total at about $235 million, including an Atlantic Avenue redesign, six rebuilt parks and playgrounds, tenant legal help and anti-harassment programs.
