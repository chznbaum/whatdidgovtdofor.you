In hard rain, water runs across Prospect Park instead of into its 60-acre lake. The lake has limited capacity in intense storms, so runoff floods homes and streets south of the park and strains sewers. The city's fix is a Bluebelt: ponds and plantings that catch and slow stormwater. New York manages Bluebelts in three other boroughs. This will be Brooklyn's first.

The plan will upgrade lake drainage so the water level can drop in under 36 hours instead of three weeks, leaving room for a storm. A pond and rain gardens along West Drive will catch water that runs toward Ditmas Park, Kensington, Prospect Park South and Windsor Terrace. A pond north of the zoo will catch runoff from Flatbush Avenue. The zoo closed after a record storm in September 2023. No application is needed.

Brooklyn Community Board 14, then chaired by Karl-Henry Cesar, had pushed for flood protection for more than a decade. Mayor Eric Adams's administration announced the plan in December 2025. It described its investment as $68 million: up to $50 million from DEP for the Bluebelt, plus the mayor's $18 million share of a separate $20 million lakeshore restoration; the City Council supplied the other $2 million. The Bluebelt followed a year-long DEP study with NYC Parks and the Prospect Park Alliance. DEP Commissioner Rohit Aggarwala led the study and design. Former Deputy Mayor Meera Joshi and Deputy Mayor Jeff Roth also helped advance it. Construction is expected from 2029 to 2032.
