Students at 16 public schools in the South Bronx and Central Brooklyn can see a therapist without leaving school. Each school has a small clinic run by NYC Health + Hospitals, the city's public hospital system. It offers individual, family and group therapy and connects students to outpatient or telehealth care when they need more. Clinic staff also train teachers, which helps schools handle a student's crisis without an unnecessary 911 call. More than 6,000 students attend these schools. Another 34 schools can send students straight to Health + Hospitals clinics through fast referrals.

The city chose schools with high needs: those with no mental health services on site and high rates of suspensions and chronic absence.

Mayor Eric Adams announced the clinics in April 2024, and the last one opened at P.S. 35 in the Bronx on March 18, 2025. Flavia Puello-Perdomo oversaw NYC Public Schools' implementation, while Health + Hospitals, led by Mitchell Katz, staffed and runs the clinics. They cost $3.6 million from the Mental Health Continuum, a $5 million program run by the city's schools, its health department and Health + Hospitals, with help from the group Advocates for Children. New York State's Office of Mental Health added $700,000 in grants.

The program's funding isn't permanent. Adams left it out of his first budget draft each year and restored it later, after advocates and Council members pushed back. The city's November 2026 financial plan allocates about $5 million to the Mental Health Continuum for FY2027 and nothing for later years.
