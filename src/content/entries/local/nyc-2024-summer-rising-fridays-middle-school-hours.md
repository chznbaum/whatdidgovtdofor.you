Summer Rising is New York City's free summer program for elementary and middle school students. It combines academics in the morning with activities in the afternoon. Budget cuts had left middle schoolers scheduled for Monday through Thursday until 4 p.m. in summer 2024. City officials announced a $20 million restoration; the final adopted-budget summary lists $19.6 million. The money restored Fridays and late afternoons, keeping middle school programming open until 6 p.m. Families applied through MySchools, and 110,000 children received seats. At a Council hearing that fall, the youth department said providers had quickly adjusted schedules and hired staff for the restored hours.

The package also stabilized initial school budgets for 2024–25. Schools with falling enrollment, about 15% of the system, received initial allocations at least as large as their midyear 2023–24 budgets, with $75 million announced for that policy. Another $32 million replaced expiring federal COVID aid for programs including tutoring, restorative justice, computer science, and teacher recruitment.

Finance Chair Justin Brannan sponsored the budget resolution, and the Council's adopted-budget summary says the Council won the Summer Rising restoration. Speaker Adrienne Adams led the Council, while Mayor Eric Adams's administration proposed and announced the package with her before adoption. The city said better-than-expected revenue and fiscal management made the investments possible.
