Beginning Oct. 1, Miami allocated $9.29 million in federal HOME money for affordable housing programs serving lower-income households. The total combines $2.79 million in new HOME money for 2026-27, $500,000 in projected program income, and $6 million left unallocated from earlier years.

The city's published allocation table reserves $329,144 for program administration. It makes the rest available for building or rehabilitating affordable rental or homeownership housing, down-payment assistance, tenant-based rent assistance, or some combination of those uses. The resolution does not award money directly to households or guarantee that every listed use will receive funding. HOME assistance is limited to households at or below 80% of area median income; at least 90% of assisted renters must be at or below 60%.

HOME is a federal program administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, while participating cities choose eligible local uses through annual plans. Miami's housing department published its proposed plan in June. The City Commission, chaired by Christine King, approved the allocation with modifications, 5–0, on July 9, 2026. Rolando Escalona moved adoption, and King seconded the motion.
