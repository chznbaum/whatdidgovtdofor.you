People in New York City jails use institutional accounts to buy food, soap, and other commissary items. Under the current rule, someone leaving custody can collect up to $200 in cash immediately and request a check for the rest. The same options remain available at cashier windows for 120 days after release. The Department of Correction says nearly 70% of people who do not claim their funds at release have $100 or less left.

Starting July 21, 2027, the jail must tell you your balance orally and in writing, in one of the city's designated languages. It must make your full balance available when you leave. You can request cash, a check, or both; cash must be provided immediately, while a check must be provided as soon as practicable. The law bars fees. Preloaded debit cards become another option on April 21, 2028.

Council Member Crystal Hudson first introduced the bill in April 2024, with Council Member Sandy Nurse and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams as co-prime sponsors. That version died in committee at the end of 2025, and Hudson brought it back in January 2026. The Council passed an amended version on Sept. 24, 2026. Mayor Zohran Mamdani neither signed nor vetoed it, so it became law on Oct. 24.
