Richmond accepted $1 million in federal money for access and amenities at Broad Rock Creek Park. The city's current first-phase records call for permanent restrooms, a paved multiuse trail, front entrance pathways and signs. The 2024 federal award announcement also described a bridge repair, roads, water fountains, benches, kiosks and trash cans, but later city records do not give those features a construction date.

This is a public park project, so there is no application process. Once the work is finished, residents and visitors can use the new trail and facilities.

The money comes from the National Park Service's Outdoor Recreation Legacy Partnership, which is funded by the federal Land and Water Conservation Fund. The program provides matching grants for park projects in communities with limited access to parks and can pay up to half of a project's cost. Richmond allocated $1 million in city capital funding for the required match. The National Park Service announced Richmond's selection in March 2024.

Mayor Danny Avula introduced the ordinance to accept and appropriate the grant. City Council's finance committee recommended it, and the full council adopted it on June 22, 2026.
