When Richmond's main winter shelter, run by the Salvation Army on Chamberlayne Avenue, starts to fill up on a cold night, there will be a backup. CARITAS may open a surge shelter for up to 70 single adults from 5 p.m. to 8 a.m. when that shelter reaches 85% occupancy and temperatures stay at 40 degrees or below for two hours. The surge program runs from Dec. 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027. If both shelters fill and the forecast reaches 20 degrees or below for two hours, CARITAS opens an overnight warming center at Marshall Plaza for up to 60 more people. The city can also activate it for other life-safety hazards, with funding for up to 20 city-authorized activation days.

The surge shelter is not a walk-up site. Single adults seeking a bed go to the Chamberlayne shelter after 5 p.m., and staff provide rides to CARITAS if the first shelter is full. The city announces surge-shelter and warming-center openings through Richmond Ready Alerts and RVA311.

The city started using emergency overflow space after cold snaps in early 2025. That season it opened a temporary shelter four times over 28 nights, with about 72 people a night on average. Last winter it shared the cost of a CARITAS surge shelter with neighboring counties. Mayor Danny Avula's administration proposed renewing the arrangement this year, and the City Council adopted it on Sept. 28, 2026. The $844,143 came out of the budget of the Department of Neighborhood and Community Services, which runs the city's homeless services office. The city's transmittal doesn't say whether any other service was cut to cover it.
