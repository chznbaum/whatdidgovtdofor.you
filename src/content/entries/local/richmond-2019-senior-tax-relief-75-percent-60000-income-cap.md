If you own and live in your Richmond home and are 65 or older or permanently and totally disabled, the city takes part or all of your real estate tax off the bill based on household income. Starting in 2020, this law raised the exemption for incomes over $30,000 and up to $40,000 from 35% to 75%. It also raised the exemption for incomes over $20,000 and up to $30,000 from 70% to 100%. The eligibility caps rose from $50,000 to $60,000 in household income and from $200,000 to $350,000 in financial net worth, excluding the home and up to one acre.

You must apply through the Department of Finance. Participants now file a full application every three years and recertify in the two intervening years. About 2,300 residents were enrolled in 2019. The city raised the program’s fiscal 2020 appropriation from $2.7 million to $4.588 million within a General Fund budget that grew by about $26 million; projected real estate collections rose by $6.48 million.

Council member Parker Agelasto championed the change, with seven other council members as co-patrons, to help longtime residents stay in neighborhoods where rising home values were pushing up tax bills. Mayor Levar Stoney’s administration initially opposed it, citing lost revenue and administrative work. The council passed it 8-0 on Feb. 25, 2019. Later ordinances widened the program. The 75% bracket applies through 2026; Ord. No. 2026-302 raises the exemption for incomes up to $40,000 to 100% on Jan. 1, 2027.
