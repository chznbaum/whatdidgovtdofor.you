Saint Paul is putting safety fixes on the streets where people are most likely to be killed or badly hurt in crashes. The plan covers about 40 miles of road and 100 intersections in Wards 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7. The city picked them using the crash analysis in its Transportation Safety Action Plan, which mapped a "high injury network" and the places where people on foot and on bikes face the most danger. The project also pays for two planning studies, one for Como Avenue and one for White Bear Avenue.

You don't have to do anything to benefit. The work is done on public streets, and you'll see the changes as you travel through these neighborhoods.

The money comes from the federal Safe Streets and Roads for All program, which the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law created. Saint Paul announced the award in 2024. On Oct. 21, 2026, the City Council passed a resolution from Council President Rebecca Noecker that added $14.15 million from the Federal Highway Administration to the city's capital budget. It also accepted $3.93 million from the Minnesota Department of Transportation. That state money covers the local match, so Saint Paul does not have to supply city matching funds. The city's Long-Range Capital Improvement Budget Committee recommended approval. Mayor Kaohly Her's office signed the resolution on Oct. 25.
