If you own and occupy a home in Chesterfield County and you are 65 or older or totally and permanently disabled, the county can cut your real estate tax based on household income. The board created a new 15% tier with a base range of $65,401 to $80,000 in 2026 dollars. Because the county adjusts the limits annually and Social Security announced a 3.6% increase for 2027, that tier will be roughly $67,800 to $82,900 when it takes effect Jan. 1, 2027.

The current tiers provide full relief up to $39,600, 60% relief up to $49,100, and 35% relief up to $65,400. Applying the same adjustment gives approximate 2027 cutoffs of $41,000, $50,900, and $67,800. The asset limit likewise rises from $514,000 to about $532,500. You must occupy the property as your sole home. Relief is not automatic; apply through the Commissioner of the Revenue online or return the form by mail or in person.

The Board added $5 million in FY2027 for the combined elderly, disabled, and veterans tax-relief program, bringing its projected cost to $29.2 million. The general fund grew by $49 million from FY2026 and also added money for schools and public safety. On Aug. 26, Jim Ingle moved to adopt the ordinance and LeQuan Hylton seconded it. The board approved it 5-0. One resident urged the county to advertise the program more.
