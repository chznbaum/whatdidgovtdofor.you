Union County now has $373,010 in state money for its Childhood Lead Exposure and Prevention program. The New Jersey Department of Health told the county about the grant, and the county added it to its 2026 budget so it could spend the money.

Even small amounts of lead can harm a young child's growth and learning, and old paint is a common source. New Jersey requires every child to get a blood lead test at 12 and 24 months. This is not cash that families apply for. Children are tested through health care providers or labs, and local health departments offer free testing to uninsured or underinsured children. When a test reaches the state's action level, local health departments arrange home visits, coordinate follow-up testing, order lead hazards removed, and connect families with help. The grants also support nurse case management, environmental investigations, health education, prevention, and training.

Commissioner Kimberly Palmieri-Mouded sponsored the resolution. The Board of County Commissioners approved it 8-0 on July 30, 2026, with Commissioner Alexander Mirabella absent.
