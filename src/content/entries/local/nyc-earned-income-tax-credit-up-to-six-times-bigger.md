If you live in New York City and qualify for the federal earned income tax credit, the city adds its own credit on top. For nearly 20 years, that city credit was just 5% of your federal one. Starting with 2022 tax returns, it pays 10% to 30%, and the lowest earners get the most. You claim it on your state tax return (Form IT-215), so there's no separate application.

The difference is real money. The city says a single parent with one child earning $14,750 saw the city credit grow from $187 to $933. In the first tax season under the new rates, more than 746,000 filers claimed $345 million, up from $64.8 million the year before. The average credit rose from $107 to $462.

Mayor Eric Adams campaigned on the increase, pushed for it in Albany, and committed $250 million a year in city money to cover the cost. In the Legislature, Assembly Ways and Means Chair Helene Weinstein and Sen. Diane Savino fought for a larger credit in the state budget. Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the budget on April 9, 2022. The credit is still in effect, and the state's tax-year 2026 EITC parameters continue to list the enhanced city credit.
