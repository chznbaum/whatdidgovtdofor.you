Starting Jan. 19, 2027, a Citi Bike member pays no more than $3.65 before tax in per-minute fees for an e-bike ride of up to 45 minutes. Members now pay 27 cents a minute before tax, so a 45-minute ride costs $12.15, or $5.40 if it starts or ends in Manhattan. Shorter rides that already cost less keep their current price. The cap also covers Reduced Fare Bike Share and eligible Lyft Pink All Access members. It applies automatically; riders without a membership aren't covered.

The annual membership fee, per-minute e-bike rate and charges after 45 minutes are frozen through May 26, 2029, when Lyft's city contract ends. The cap replaces annual members' one-time allotment of 60 free e-bike minutes. Existing free minutes remain until the end of the current membership. Lyft had raised prices six times from 2022 through January 2026.

Council Member Lincoln Restler spearheaded the push for cheaper e-bike rides and introduced a fare-cap bill in 2024. Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Transportation Commissioner Mike Flynn negotiated the agreement with Lyft. Transportation Alternatives ran a petition for a $3 cap.

The city says the deal requires no additional city spending, but it made concessions. It will speed approvals for Lyft to add chargers at more than 130 stations, allow ads on all station panels, raise the revenue-sharing threshold to $5.25 million a year with inflation adjustments, and stop requiring Lyft to repay lost parking-meter revenue.
