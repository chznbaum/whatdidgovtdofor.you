Streets in a low-lying St. Petersburg neighborhood near the 54th Avenue N canal flood in heavy rain. The storm pipes there were laid in the 1950s and are only 15 to 18 inches wide, too small for the storms the area gets now. This project replaces them with a larger drainage system along 50th Avenue N, from 4th Street N to 7th Street N, and along 7th Street N up to the canal. It includes a 42-inch pipe at 50th Avenue and 4th Street N. Crews will also raise road profiles, add devices that catch sediment before it reaches the water, and replace speed humps with traffic-calming islands and better sidewalks.

Residents don't need to apply for anything. The work covers whole streets, curb to curb, so expect detours while it's underway. The city plans to talk with neighbors before construction, which is expected to start in early 2027 and take about a year. Part of the project is the city's first planted "HydroPlanter" boxes, which filter stormwater through soil before it reaches the canal.

The city held public meetings about the project in 2021, including one with the Acadia Gardens Neighborhood Association. On Sept. 3, 2026, the City Council, chaired by Lisset Hanewicz, voted 8-0 to approve up to $6,064,229 for contractor Kamminga & Roodvoets to build the first part. A $2.7 million grant from the Southwest Florida Water Management District helps pay for the work.
