Newark's health department is getting more federal money to help people with HIV in Newark and Essex County. On October 21, 2026, the Municipal Council accepted another $3,131,050 from the federal Health Resources and Services Administration's Ryan White HIV/AIDS Program. Combined with $679,332 accepted in April and $2,042,112 accepted in July, the city's award now totals $5,852,494, and the city pays none of it.

The money is part of the federal Ending the HIV Epidemic initiative. It pays for two things: getting people with HIV into treatment quickly so the virus is kept under control, and moving fast when an outbreak starts so prevention and care reach the people who need them. The program's goal is to cut new HIV infections by 75% within five years. People get services through the city's Department of Health and Community Wellness and the programs it funds, not through an application to the council.

The department applied for the grant, and the council adopted the resolution with no recorded objection. The services are funded, but their future is uncertain: the House's FY2027 health spending bill would eliminate the Ryan White Ending the HIV Epidemic initiative. That bill has not become law.
