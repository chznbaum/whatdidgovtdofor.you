Free 3-K for 3-year-olds and the city's special education pre-K classes now have money set aside in New York City's budget every year. They no longer depend on a new deal each spring. The city's 2025–26 budget "baselined" $112 million for 3-K and pre-K and $55 million for the special education pre-K classes that opened the year before. Baselined money is built into each year's budget unless the city takes it out.

Families don't need to do anything new. You apply for 3-K and pre-K through NYC Public Schools as usual. If a child's education plan calls for a special class, the family works with the city's preschool special education committee to find a placement; the regular 3-K and pre-K application does not place children in those classes. In spring 2025, more than 600 preschoolers were still waiting for a special-class seat.

For years these programs ran on federal pandemic aid and one-year city money, so they were at risk of cuts each budget season. The City Council, led by Speaker Adrienne Adams and Finance Chair Justin Brannan, pressed the administration to restore $197 million for 3-K and pre-K, while Education Chair Rita Joseph pushed for preschool special education funding. Mayor Eric Adams then proposed baselining $167 million in his April 2025 executive budget. The Council passed the budget 51–0 on June 30, 2025. The next year's budget, adopted in June 2026, kept the funding and added more for special education pre-K.
