Richmond will keep its downtown family shelter open. The city rents the former HI Richmond Hostel at 7 N. Second St., across from the main library. The nonprofit HomeAgain runs it there as a 50-bed, year-round emergency shelter for families. The city's lease was set to end on Nov. 30, 2026. The new amendment adds one guaranteed year, and the city and the building's owner can agree to renew it for up to three more years, one year at a time.

You can't walk in or apply directly. Families get a bed through the region's Homeless Crisis Line, which the Greater Richmond Continuum of Care runs. The shelter is non-congregate, which means families get their own space instead of sleeping in one big shared room.

The shelter opened on Dec. 1, 2023. That spring, the mayor and City Council had declared a housing crisis, and the city's plan to end homelessness found Richmond about 150 year-round shelter beds short. Mayor Danny Avula's administration proposed the lease extension. The council's Education and Human Services Committee recommended it, and the full council adopted it on Oct. 13, 2026. The city's request listed no new fiscal impact.
