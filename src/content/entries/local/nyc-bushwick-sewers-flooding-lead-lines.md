Knickerbocker Avenue sits lower than the streets around it, so rain runs downhill and floods it, and the sewers under it date to the early 1900s. The city will replace nearly three miles of sewers under Knickerbocker, Wilson, Central and Evergreen avenues with large box sewers and upgrade every catch basin in the area. In parts of the area, the sewers will be able to carry about 1.9 billion gallons a day instead of about 226 million, which cuts flood risk across about 2,300 acres of Bushwick.

The work also replaces almost a mile of old cast-iron water mains. Where a privately owned water service line turns out to contain lead, the city will replace it during construction at no cost to the property owner, with no application needed. The Department of Transportation is also redesigning Knickerbocker Avenue to make it safer for people walking and biking.

Council Member Sandy Nurse's team won the $390 million for her district while the City Council was negotiating the City of Yes for Housing Opportunity zoning plan with Mayor Eric Adams's administration. The Department of Environmental Protection is paying $335 million and the Department of Transportation the rest. Construction is expected to start in 2029.
