Streets in Bay Pines Estates, an unincorporated neighborhood near Seminole, flood in moderate and heavy rain. The county says the drainage there is minimal and too small for the job. The area is also exposed to tidal flooding. This project will improve the stormwater system for about 37 acres around 62nd Avenue North, 59th Avenue North, and 98th and 100th Way North. Water will drain to a county-owned pond and then to Long Bayou. The work also includes road repairs and replacement of county-owned utility pipes. The goal is to shorten how long roads stay flooded after storms and to send cleaner water to the bayou.

Residents don't need to apply; the work is a public infrastructure project. Pinellas County is doing the work. The design phase runs through summer 2028, and construction is planned from spring 2029 to spring 2030.

The state grant comes from Resilient Florida, a flooding and storm surge program created in 2021. It covers $3,887,800 of design and construction costs. The county is matching that amount with money from Penny for Pinellas, the local sales tax for capital projects. In its capital plan, the county has budgeted about $13.7 million for the whole project. The county commission approved the agreement on June 16, 2026, with Commission Chair Dave Eggers presiding.
