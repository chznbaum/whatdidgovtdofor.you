Miami authorized up to $428,500 for Sunshine for All, a local nonprofit, to continue its senior meals program for low-income, homebound older residents in District 4. Sunshine for All says residents apply through their city district. Applicants must be at least 62 and provide a state-issued ID and a Social Security or SSI letter.

The money comes from Miami's Anti-Poverty Initiative, which the city created in 2014 to help lower-income residents. The city divides that money among the commission districts based on each district's poverty rate. This grant uses District 4's share. District 4 includes Flagami, West Flagler, Little Havana, Coral Gate, Shenandoah, Golden Pines and Silver Bluff. In 2023, the district gave Sunshine for All $373,750 for senior meals, and that grant served 220 people.

Commissioner Ralph Rosado represents District 4. He won the seat in a June 2025 special election after Commissioner Manolo Reyes died. The city listed Rosado as the grant's District 4 sponsor, and he moved its approval. On Sept. 10, 2026, after a public hearing, the commission waived competitive bidding and approved the grant 5-0.
