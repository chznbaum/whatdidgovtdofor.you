When you apply to New York City for SNAP food benefits or cash assistance, the Department of Social Services now has to tell you in writing that it got your application. Within 48 hours, it must send a notice with the date you applied and what it received. The notice must also say whether your application is complete, and if it isn't, what's still missing.

Every document you hand in after that gets a receipt within 5 days. The receipt shows the date the city got it, what kind of document it was, and whom it covers. If a document can't be read, the city has to tell you and ask for a new copy. You don't need to request any of this. Starting in June 2027, you'll also be able to see electronic copies of the documents you submitted.

Council Member Tiffany Cabán introduced the bill in December 2024. She called it the Receipts Act, and her reason was that people too often left benefits offices with no record of where their case stood. The Council passed it on May 28, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature that June. It took effect 180 days later. The law also requires the city to study how to extend these receipts to other benefit programs.
