Every year, the city picks the apartment buildings with the worst records of hazardous code violations and emergency repair bills and puts them under closer watch. The 2026 list has 250 buildings with 7,038 homes and 54,909 open violations. Owners already owed the city nearly $4.5 million for emergency repairs it had made.

Tenants don't have to apply. The owner must post a sign in the building saying it's in the program. HPD inspects more often. Owners have four months to meet the initial discharge requirements: fix all heat and hot water violations and at least 80% of other serious ones, pay outstanding charges or arrange a payment plan, and file a current property registration. If they don't, HPD issues an order to correct, can hire contractors to do the work or replace broken building systems, and bills the owner. Unpaid bills can become a lien on the property. Owners also start paying fees of up to $1,000 per apartment, and HPD can take them to Housing Court. Once a building leaves the program, HPD keeps watching it for at least a year.

The program dates to a 2007 city law, and this is its 19th round. HPD, led by Commissioner Dina Levy, selected the 2026 buildings; Levy and Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the list. Tenants should report repair problems to their landlord first, then to 311.
