The law requires a traffic control device at every intersection directly next to a New York City school that provides instruction to at least 250 students at or below grade 12. By Sept. 30, 2022, the city Department of Transportation had to list every covered intersection that didn't have one. By Sept. 30, 2024, it had to install a device at each of them. DOT chooses the device for each corner—a stop sign, traffic signal or other appropriate device—using federal traffic standards and its engineers' judgment.

Nobody has to apply. The law covers streets around qualifying schools, where students walk to class. When the Council voted, it said there were about 6,800 intersections next to city schools overall, and only about half had traffic signals. We couldn't find a public DOT report saying whether every listed intersection got its device by the deadline.

Council Member Inez Barron introduced the bill in January 2018 and called it one of the first bills she filed when her term began. It then sat for nearly four years while the de Blasio administration resisted it. The Council passed it on Dec. 9, 2021. Mayor Eric Adams took office weeks later and returned it unsigned instead of vetoing it, so it became law on Jan. 9, 2022. DOT said it would carry out the law and keep following federal guidelines.
