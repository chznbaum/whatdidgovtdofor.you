Young kids pick up lead dust and paint chips in hallways and on stairs, not just at home. New York City already treated peeling lead paint inside an apartment with a young child as a serious problem. This law does the same for shared spaces. If a child under 6 lives in the building, peeling lead paint, or lead paint on rotting wood or rusting metal, in a hallway, stairwell or lobby is now a Class C "immediately hazardous" violation. That's the city's most urgent category of housing violation.

The law also requires owners of older rental buildings to test painted surfaces in common areas for lead and to keep the results for the city. The deadline was August 2025. When a city inspector comes to check an apartment for lead, they must also look at the paint in the common areas they walk through. You don't have to apply. If you see peeling paint in your building and a young child lives there, you can report it to 311 for free, whatever your immigration status.

Council Member Carlina Rivera sponsored the bill in April 2022, and 42 colleagues signed on as cosponsors. The city's housing agency pushed back over how much inspectors would have to do, so the Council narrowed that part before passing the bill on July 13, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams neither signed nor vetoed it, and it became law on August 13, 2023. It took effect in June 2024.
