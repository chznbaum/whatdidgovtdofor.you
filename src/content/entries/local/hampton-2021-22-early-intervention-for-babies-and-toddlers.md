Some babies and toddlers develop more slowly than other children or have a health condition that affects how they grow. Early intervention helps them while they're young. In Hampton and Newport News, the Infant & Toddler Connection offers speech and hearing services, physical and occupational therapy, special instruction, family training and counseling, assistive technology and a service coordinator. This money paid for those services from July 2021 through June 2022.

Children can get services until their third birthday. Parents can call the local Infant & Toddler Connection themselves, and doctors and hospitals can also refer children. Virginia's program serves eligible children no matter what their family can afford. Hampton's grant pays for services after the program works out what a family can pay. It also funds staff and public awareness training.

The $914,997 came through Virginia's Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Services: $676,384 in state money and $238,613 from the federal disabilities education law. The city didn't have to put up any match. It had received this award for about 20 years. The City Council approved it 6–0 on the consent agenda on July 14, 2021. Vice Mayor Jimmy Gray presided because Mayor Donnie Tuck was absent.
