Every year Jacksonville receives money from the federal HOME Investment Partnerships Program. This ordinance puts that money to work: $3,379,921.46 from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, plus $246,055.30 in program income. The total is $3,625,976.76. The city can use it to repair homes, build new affordable ones, and give direct help to renters and to people buying a home.

The help is for low- and very low-income households. Federal HOME rules say a household it helps can earn no more than 80% of the area's median income. Most people don't get a check straight from this ordinance. Help usually comes through a specific city housing program or an affordable home or apartment built with the money, and each one has its own application.

The mayor's office asked for the ordinance as part of Jacksonville's yearly round of federal housing grants. Council members Tyrona Clark-Murray, Jimmy Peluso and Rahman Johnson signed on as co-sponsors. The council's Neighborhoods, Community Services, Public Health and Safety Committee approved it 7-0, and the Finance Committee approved it 6-0. The full council passed it 17-0 on July 28, 2026.
