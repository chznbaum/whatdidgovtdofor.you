If you're pregnant and apply for shelter at PATH, the city's family intake center in the Bronx, you may be offered help to move straight into a home. The pilot, called CRIB, gives some eligible applicants a CityFHEPS voucher, which pays much of the rent on an apartment in their own name. Others are assigned to Pathway Home, which pays the household they move in with about $1,200 a month for up to a year.

You do not file a separate application to be considered for CRIB. Staff screen pregnant applicants at intake. Household gross income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, households eligible for NoVA domestic-violence services are excluded, and the underlying housing programs have other requirements. People assigned to Pathway Home must identify an eligible host household and complete program paperwork. Not everyone who qualifies gets CRIB housing help: a random draw assigns households to the CityFHEPS group, the Pathway Home group, or a control group that receives neither CRIB benefit. That lets the city measure whether the help cuts shelter stays and births in shelter.

Mayor Eric Adams set the goal of no child born into a city shelter in his January 2025 State of the City address and promised this pilot. Social Services Commissioner Molly Wasow Park announced the pilot and explained its design to the City Council. HRA adopted the rule, and DSS runs the program. The city put $8.5 million in its 2026 budget for the pilot and started it on Sept. 15, 2025.
