In 2020–21, this grant paid for homeless services across the Virginia Peninsula: emergency shelter, rapid rehousing (which helps people, veterans included, get back into a home quickly), help for families about to lose their housing, and one central intake line where anyone in the region could ask for help. It served Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, and James City and York counties.

People did not apply for the grant itself. During the grant year, people in a housing crisis reached the services through the region's central intake. Hampton's Department of Human Services managed the money for the region's partners, which included the Hampton-Newport News Community Services Board, The Salvation Army Peninsula, Menchville House, LINK and Transitions.

The $1,060,591 came from Virginia's Homeless Solutions Program, which combines state money with the federal Emergency Solutions Grant. For 2020–21, $940,512 was federal money and $120,079 came from the state. Hampton didn't have to put in any money of its own. The city had won this grant every year since 2014–15, and 2020–21 was the first year of a new multi-year award. The City Council, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, approved it 7-0 on July 8, 2020.
