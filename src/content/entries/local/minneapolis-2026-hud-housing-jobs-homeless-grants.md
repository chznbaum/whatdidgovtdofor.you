Every year Minneapolis gets a share of four federal housing grants from HUD, and this vote set how the city will use the 2026 money. That's about $17.4 million, plus about $1.8 million that past grant-funded loans and programs paid back. The city expects 80% of its Community Development Block Grant to benefit people with low and moderate incomes.

The block grant puts $2.88 million into the city's fund for building and fixing up affordable rentals, aiming at 339 homes. It also pays $1.35 million for job training and placement for 150 households, plus homeownership help, lead cleanup and summer jobs for young people. The $966,000 Emergency Solutions Grant pays for shelters, street outreach and quick moves into housing: the goal is overnight shelter for 1,000 people and new homes for 90 households. The $3.15 million HIV housing grant helps about 175 households in the Minneapolis area pay rent.

You don't apply to the city for these grants yourself. The help reaches people through shelters, nonprofits, housing developers and city programs.

Council Vice President Jamal Osman authored the resolutions. The Business, Housing & Zoning Committee approved them after a public hearing on May 12, 2026, and the full council passed them 13–0 on May 21. Mayor Jacob Frey signed them the next day, and the grant year began June 1.
