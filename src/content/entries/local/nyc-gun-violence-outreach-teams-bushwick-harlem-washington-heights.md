New York City's Crisis Management System uses local groups to stop shootings before they happen. Outreach workers, often called credible messengers, settle conflicts, help people after violent incidents and connect them to jobs, school, mental health care and legal help. The network is expanding into three more parts of the city.

In Bushwick, Southside United's Wick Against Violence will build on its work at Woodhull Hospital and Bushwick Houses. In Harlem, Living Redemption will expand its outreach and mediation. Washington Heights and Inwood will get a new team from Grow Up Grow Out Jr., run by United Way. This is the first time the network has covered that part of northern Manhattan. Each area gets $1.6 million for fiscal 2027. That pays for 10 credible messengers plus a program manager, a community coordinator, an education and jobs specialist and an administrative assistant. You don't have to sign up. The teams work on the street and reach out to people.

Bushwick's expansion followed five or six years of advocacy. Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso said he began the push while on the City Council and Council Member Jennifer Gutiérrez continued it. Mayor Zohran Mamdani made a broader CMS expansion a campaign commitment and announced this plan on Oct. 20, 2026, using money already in the city's fiscal 2027 budget. Deputy Mayor Renita Francois led the administration's work, and Commissioner Ayesha Delany-Brumsey's Office of Community Safety helped make it possible. The city hasn't set a start date because the groups still have to hire and train staff.
