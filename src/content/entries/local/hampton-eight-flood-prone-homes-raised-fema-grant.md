Hampton received grant funding to lift eight homes subject to repeated flooding. Elevating them is meant to reduce future flood damage, repeat claims to the National Flood Insurance Program, and demand on city public-safety staff during disasters. It also protects the health and safety of residents receiving help.

The project budget is $3,152,564. The Federal Emergency Management Agency provides $2,852,319.80: $2,702,197.80 for the project and $150,122 for management costs. The Virginia Department of Emergency Management provides a $300,244.20 state match. The award requires no local cash or in-kind match. That does not necessarily make participation cost-free for the households: Hampton says elevation participants may still have to cover pre-existing damage, some code work, and temporary housing.

Homeowners cannot apply directly to FEMA. For Hampton elevation grants, the city contacts flood-prone areas, willing homeowners respond, and the city evaluates FEMA eligibility and risk factors. Hampton City Council accepted and appropriated this award 7–0 on February 28, 2024. In April 2024, the council accepted a separate FEMA grant intended to raise seven additional homes.
