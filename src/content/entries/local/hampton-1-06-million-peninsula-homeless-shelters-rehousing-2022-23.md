From July 2022 through June 2023, a $1,060,922 grant paid for help for people on the Virginia Peninsula who had lost their homes or were about to. The money went to emergency shelters, to rapid rehousing that moves people, veterans among them, from shelter back into a place of their own, to help for families on the edge of homelessness, and to one central intake system so people knew where to turn. The program aims to keep people from becoming homeless, get them housed faster, and keep them from losing housing again.

Hampton's Department of Human Services, led by Director Tamara Temoney-Porter, ran the grant for the whole region, so the help reached people in Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County as well as Hampton. Local partners, including shelters and service groups, delivered it. People get help by calling their area's homeless crisis line rather than applying for the grant.

Virginia's Department of Housing and Community Development awarded the money. $941,627 came from the state's general fund and $119,295 from the federal Emergency Solutions Grant. The city didn't have to put in any of its own money. Hampton has won this grant every year since 2014–15. City Council approved it 6–0 on Aug. 10, 2022, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding.
