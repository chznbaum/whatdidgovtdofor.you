Ramsey County has more money to help people keep their homes. On August 4, 2026, the county board accepted $5.28 million in added state money for its homelessness prevention program. That brings the county's grant for October 2025 through September 2027 to about $9.3 million.

The money goes to local groups, including the American Indian Family Center, that work with the county. They can stop an eviction by paying back rent, cover short-term rent or a utility bill, and help with move-in costs. They also offer case management and cleanup help when a home has been condemned. You don't apply to the state. You go through the county's program and the groups it funds, and they decide who qualifies. The Legislature directed the state to deploy this round of money by December 31, 2026.

Gov. Tim Walz proposed the $40 million statewide investment and included it in his budget recommendation. Rep. Michael Howard and Sen. Lindsey Port carried the 2026 state housing law that included it. The House passed the law 105–28, and Walz signed it. The law didn't use additional general fund dollars. It paid for this money with unspent funds the state had set aside for Tyler v. Hennepin County settlement claims, plus interest. Minnesota Housing then split the $40 million among 24 local programs, Ramsey County's among them.
