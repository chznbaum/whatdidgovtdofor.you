In November 2023, mid-year city budget cuts ended Sunday hours at New York City's public libraries and paused some programs. Saturday service was at risk next. The 2024–25 city budget put back $58.3 million for the New York Public Library, Brooklyn Public Library and Queens Public Library. The city also restored funding for cultural institutions.

Library users didn't need to do anything. Seven branches went back to seven-day service on July 14, 2024: Parkchester, Todt Hill-Westerleigh and the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library in the NYPL system; Central and Kings Highway in Brooklyn; and Flushing and Central in Queens. Ten more were due to reopen on Sundays by early August. The city also committed to keep part of the money in future budgets, so the libraries could plan more than a year ahead.

The money came back through the City Council's budget talks with Mayor Eric Adams. Council Speaker Adrienne Adams and Finance Chair Justin Brannan negotiated the deal, announced June 27, 2024. Libraries Committee Chair Carlina Rivera pushed for it, and library leaders thanked all three. The Council adopted the budget 46–3 on June 30, 2024. In 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani's budget made another $31.7 million a year for the libraries permanent.
