Federal disaster aid does not always cover homeless residents' housing needs after a storm. HUD's Rapid Unsheltered Survivor Housing program, called RUSH, fills that gap. It can pay for emergency shelter, rapid rehousing with up to two years of rent help and move-in costs, help to keep people housed, and outreach. St. Petersburg has now accepted $2,328,301 in a second round of this money, on top of the $1 million HUD allocated to the city in January 2025 after Hurricanes Helene and Milton.

You don't apply to the city directly. The city is choosing local nonprofits to run the services. Its request for proposals sets aside $456,486 for rapid rehousing, $346,000 for new non-congregate shelter beds for families with children, $300,000 for short- to medium-term rent help, and $200,000 for shelter and services for people with serious mental illness. The city's RFP says providers must serve St. Petersburg residents experiencing homelessness who lived in a federally declared disaster area and still have needs unmet by FEMA's Transitional Sheltering Assistance and other federal disaster programs.

The City Council, chaired by Lisset Hanewicz, voted 8-0 on June 11, 2026 to accept the grant and add it to the Housing and Community Development Department's budget. The city plans to bring the nonprofit contracts to the council by Dec. 10, 2026. The money can be spent through April 2028.
