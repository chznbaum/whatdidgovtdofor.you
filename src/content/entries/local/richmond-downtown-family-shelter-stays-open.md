Richmond's family shelter at 7 N. Second St., in the old hostel across from the downtown library, will keep running. City Council voted to extend the city's sublease with HomeAgain, the nonprofit that runs it. The extension adds one guaranteed year and allows up to three more one-year renewals if both sides agree.

The shelter has 50 beds, is open all year, and serves families, so parents and children can stay together. Families don't walk in. They are placed through coordinated entry run by the Greater Richmond Continuum of Care, the region's network of homeless services.

The shelter opened in December 2023. Earlier that year, Mayor Levar Stoney and City Council had declared a housing crisis, and the city counted a shortage of 150 year-round emergency shelter beds. In 2024, a donation from Henrico County cut the city's costs under the agreement by 25%. Mayor Danny Avula's administration asked for this second extension, and the city lists no new fiscal impact for it. Council adopted it 9–0 on Oct. 13, 2026.
