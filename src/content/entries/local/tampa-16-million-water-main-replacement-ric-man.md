Tampa is paying to replace old water mains, the pipes under the street that carry water to homes and businesses. On Sept. 3, 2026, the City Council approved an agreement with Ric-Man Construction Florida to design and build the work for a guaranteed maximum price of $15,970,763.14. The same day, the council moved about $15.97 million into the city's Citywide Water Main Replacement Project to cover it. The money comes from the water department's debt-funded capital fund.

You don't have to do anything to benefit. New mains mean fewer leaks and breaks, steadier water pressure, and better water flow for fire hydrants. Under this contract, about 9,400 feet of old water mains in North Ybor and 11,200 feet in Oakford Park will be replaced. The pipes range from 2 to 8 inches wide. Crews are expected to work weekdays, and the project site says any water shutoffs will be short and announced ahead of time.

The city's records don't say which council member brought the item. Council Chair Alan Clendenin presided, and the council passed the resolution unanimously.
