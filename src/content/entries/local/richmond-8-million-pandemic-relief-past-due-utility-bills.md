If your Richmond gas, water or sewer bill fell behind during the pandemic, part of that debt may have been paid for you. This ordinance accepted $8.1 million and split it across the city's three utilities: about $4.17 million for gas, $2.15 million for wastewater and $1.79 million for water. All of it went to residential customers whose accounts went delinquent during COVID-19.

You didn't have to apply. Under the state program, a city utility that took the money had to credit it to residential accounts that were more than 60 days behind for service between March 12, 2020, and Aug. 31, 2021. If the money couldn't cover every dollar owed, the utility had to divide it fairly among eligible customers. Businesses weren't eligible.

The money came from the federal American Rescue Plan Act. In a 2021 special session, Virginia set aside $120 million of its share for customers of city- and county-run utilities, and the state housing department sent the money out to local systems that applied. Mayor Levar Stoney brought the ordinance to accept Richmond's share. Council President Cynthia Newbille presided when the City Council unanimously adopted it on Jan. 10, 2022.
