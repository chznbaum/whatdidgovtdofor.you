Ninety-five properties along Pine Hills Road, between Alhambra Drive and Golf Club Parkway, no longer run on old septic tanks. Orange County Utilities laid new sewer pipe, replaced 22 manholes, and connected 30 homes and 65 businesses to the county's sewer system. The owners paid nothing. A county utilities official said it's the only one of the county's septic-to-sewer projects that has been free; in the others, owners pay a share.

All of the properties were connected by June 2026. Getting the tanks out matters beyond those lots: the area sits in the Wekiwa Spring and Rock Springs watershed, where septic tanks are the biggest source of nitrogen pollution.

Florida's Department of Environmental Protection, federal American Rescue Plan money, and the county paid for the work. The state's first grant, signed in 2023, was for up to $4.29 million. In March 2025 the state picked the project for another $3.615 million from its Springs Restoration Grant Program. On Sept. 15, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners, which Mayor Jerry Demings presides over, approved adding that money to the grant agreement, for a total of up to $7.905 million.
