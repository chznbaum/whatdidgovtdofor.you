Families in the Old Oaks community in Crozier relied on private wells until a resident noticed their water smelled and tasted wrong. Tests found gasoline in it. In the end, seven wells were confirmed contaminated and 18 more were considered at risk. The fix was to connect the neighborhood to public water by extending the nearby Aqua Virginia Crozier Waterworks system.

On Feb. 5, 2019, the Goochland County Board of Supervisors approved a $1,512,400 contract with Fielder's Choice Enterprises to build a new water line, storage tank and pump station, creating 25 new water connections. The board also appropriated $1,634,670 to finish the project. County staff had bargained the lowest bid down from $1,571,550 so the project would fit its budget.

The Virginia Department of Environmental Quality paid the construction cost, with Goochland acting on the agency's behalf. The board, chaired that year by Supervisor Manuel Alvarez Jr., approved the contract 5-0. The county reports that the project was completed in fiscal year 2020 and the new infrastructure was conveyed to Aqua Virginia in February 2020. The Crozier system remained on Virginia's active-waterworks list in June 2026.
