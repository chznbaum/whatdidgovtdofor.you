UF Health Jacksonville is the city's main hospital for people who are uninsured and can't pay. Under a long-running contract, the hospital treats low-income Duval County residents, and the city pays part of the cost. This ordinance authorizes $56 million in city indigent-care funds for the 2026-27 budget year, which runs through September 2027. Part of the money can go through the state to a program that pays for training doctors at the hospital.

If you live in Duval County and get care at UF Health's Jacksonville locations without insurance, the hospital asks you to apply through its "City Contract" process. You'll need to show your income and household size. Contact UF Health's financial eligibility office to enroll.

The city's payment has grown fast. It was $40 million a year in 2022-23 and 2023-24, and in 2024 the City Council's auditor warned that the cost of care for poor patients was overwhelming the hospital. Even $56 million doesn't cover it all. A local health leader put the hospital's cost at $64 million in 2025.

Mayor Donna Deegan asked for the ordinance and kept the $56 million in her proposed budget. The City Council passed it 19-0 on Sept. 22, 2026. The current contract runs until March 2028.
