DeKalb County is putting up to $1,316,927 into its response to homelessness. The biggest piece, $469,000, pays for street outreach to people living outside. The rest goes to coordinated entry, the single front door that matches people with shelter and housing help ($139,720); managing a day center project ($149,038); running and overseeing shelter contracts ($359,360); warming and cooling centers during dangerous weather ($99,359); and the shared database that tracks who needs help and who has been served ($100,450).

You don't apply for the money itself. It pays for the county staff and operations that reach people on the street, in shelters, and at warming and cooling centers. The county's Community Development Department, which coordinates the homelessness response, said its staffing and operating capacity wasn't big enough for rising demand.

The money comes from the Affordable Housing Fund that the Board of Commissioners created on Nov. 18, 2025. It follows recommendations in the county's Unhoused Support and Services Plan, which the board received in January 2026. Community Development Director Allen Mitchell outlined the staffing and operating proposal. The board held the item in October and then approved a revised version 7–0 on Nov. 10, 2026.
