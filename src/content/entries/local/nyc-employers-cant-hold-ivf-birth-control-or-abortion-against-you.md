In New York City, an employer can't refuse to hire you, fire you, or cut your pay because of a decision you made about your sexual or reproductive health. That includes fertility treatment like IVF, testing and treatment for sexually transmitted infections, birth control, emergency contraception, sterilization, pregnancy tests and abortion. The same rules apply to employment agencies, unions, job ads, interview questions and training programs such as apprenticeships. The law also prohibits bias-motivated threats or violence that interfere with someone exercising legal rights.

You don't need to sign up for anything. The protection covers employers with four or more workers. If you think you've been treated unfairly, you can file a complaint with the NYC Commission on Human Rights (call 311) within one year, or sue in court within three years. Employers that break the law can owe damages and face civil penalties of up to $250,000 when the violation is willful. The law doesn't require employers to offer any particular health benefit.

Council Member Jumaane Williams introduced the bill in April 2018. The Committee on Civil and Human Rights amended it and approved it that December, and the full Council passed it on Dec. 20, 2018. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law on Jan. 20, 2019. It took effect that May 20.
