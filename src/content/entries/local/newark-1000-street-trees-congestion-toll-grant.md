Newark is planting 1,000 new street trees and cutting 1,000 new tree pits along busy corridors near bus stops, schools, homes and heavy traffic. The trees are meant to shade sidewalks, cool the street and filter some of the pollution from passing cars and trucks. Residents don't need to apply. The city's Office of Sustainability, Resilience, and Community Transformation will choose the sites and plant the trees between September 2026 and August 2029.

The $3.325 million comes from the Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority, the MTA agency that collects New York City's congestion tolls. The federal environmental review of the toll program found that some nearby communities could see more traffic, and Newark was one of 13 places promised money to offset it. Newark's share is $5.7 million in all. The program covers air filters in schools near highways, park improvements and roadside planting, and this grant is the planting part. Toll revenue pays the whole cost, so the city puts in nothing.

The city's business administrator brought the grant to the Municipal Council. The council, led by President Luis A. Quintana, accepted it 7-0 on October 7, 2026, with two members absent.
