The Little Apple is New York City's first child care center for its own workers, and it's free. It sits on the ground floor of the David N. Dinkins Municipal Building at 1 Centre Street and cares for about 40 children, from 6 weeks old up to age 3. It's open weekdays from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., all year. Imagine Early Learning Centers, an employee-owned provider, runs the classrooms. The city says the free care could save a family up to $20,000 a year.

It's open to parents who work full time for the Department of Citywide Administrative Services, wherever they work, and to full-time city workers assigned to 1 Centre Street. Families applied last spring, and because there were more families than seats, children were picked at random. Families not picked went on a waitlist.

Mayor Eric Adams and DCAS Commissioner Louis Molina announced the pilot in October 2025. They set aside $10 million to build the 4,000-square-foot center, which was first meant only for DCAS staff. In March 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and DCAS Commissioner Yume Kitasei opened it to every city worker based in the building. DCAS finished the renovation, and the center opened in September 2026. It's a pilot, and the city plans to judge it partly by whether parents take less leave and stay in their jobs after having a baby.
