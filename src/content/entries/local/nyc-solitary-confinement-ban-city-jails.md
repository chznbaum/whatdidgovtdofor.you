This law bans solitary confinement in New York City's jails. After someone causes physical injury or poses a specific risk of imminent serious injury, staff could place that person alone in de-escalation confinement for no more than 4 hours in 24 hours. Staff would have to observe them visually and audibly every 15 minutes and keep working to de-escalate the situation. Placement in restrictive housing generally would require a hearing first, with the right to a lawyer or advocate. The law also limits restraints and generally bars their use on people under 22, with exceptions for transportation and certain escorted movements.

No one has to apply. The rules would cover everyone held in city jails, including Rikers Island, and would guarantee at least 14 hours a day out of the cell. The main protections are not in force, however. A federal court order stays the ban and other central provisions while the court-appointed monitor, remediation manager and jail officials test whether they can be implemented safely.

Public Advocate Jumaane Williams sponsored the bill, and 38 Council members became sponsors. The Council passed it in December 2023. Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it, and in January 2024 the Council, led by Speaker Adrienne Adams, overrode the veto 42–9. Mayor Zohran Mamdani later directed the city to work toward implementation, and officials began planning pilot projects in 2026, but lifting the stay requires further action from the federal court.
