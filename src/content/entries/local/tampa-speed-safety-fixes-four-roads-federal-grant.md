Higher speeds make crashes more likely to kill or seriously injure someone, so Tampa is putting about $3.25 million into quick-build speed-safety work on four roads: Rowlett Park Drive, Palm Avenue, East Sligh Avenue and East Osborne Avenue. All four rank as urgent priorities in the city's mobility plan; three have posted speed limits above the city's target speeds. Tampa originally selected North 34th Street but switched to East Osborne Avenue after resurfacing on North 34th added many of the planned safety features.

Possible treatments include new striping and signs, signs that flash your speed back at you, new pedestrian crossings, and paint-and-post bulb-outs that extend the curb line and shorten crossings. The city is working with the University of South Florida's Center for Urban Transportation Research to choose the treatments. You don't need to apply. The changes will appear on the street as the pilot work is designed and installed.

Mayor Jane Castor's administration applied for and won the $2.6 million federal grant in 2023 as part of its Vision Zero traffic safety work. The money comes from the Safe Streets and Roads for All program in the 2021 federal infrastructure law. On Sept. 17, 2026, the City Council, chaired by Alan Clendenin, voted unanimously to approve the grant agreement and the city's $650,000 share.
