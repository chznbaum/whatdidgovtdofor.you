Route 22 is a busy highway with bus stops in its center median, so it's a dangerous road to cross on foot. Since 2007, Union County has run a free shuttle that stops at the NJ Transit bus stops along the highway in Union, Kenilworth and Springfield. It's meant for workers at the stores, offices and warehouses along the road who don't drive, and anyone else can ride too. Vans loop the route about every half hour, seven days a week, and connect with NJ Transit bus routes 65, 66, 94, 114 and 117.

You don't need to apply or pay. Just wait at a stop on the route. You can call 908-241-8300 by 3 p.m. the day before to ask the shuttle to go up to half a mile off its route.

NJ Transit's Job Access Reverse Commute program pays for transportation that helps people with low incomes get to work, and county records say the Route 22 shuttle is the service its NJ-JARC money funds. Chairman Joseph C. Bodek sponsored the county's May grant application, which sought $415,000 and required a dollar-for-dollar county cash match. On July 30, 2026, commissioners voted 8-0 to add the $402,000 award to the 2026 budget under a resolution sponsored by Commissioner Kimberly Palmieri-Mouded. Commissioner James E. Baker Jr. sponsored a separate resolution that day increasing the county's MPC Bus transportation contract by the same $402,000. Commissioner Alexander Mirabella was absent.
