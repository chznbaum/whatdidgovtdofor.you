In St. Petersburg, some 911 calls don't get a police officer. When the call is about mental health, substance use, a child's behavior, truancy, homelessness or a neighbor complaint, and nobody is in danger or breaking the law, the city can send someone from CALL instead. The Community Assistance and Life Liaison program sends trained social workers and licensed counselors. They don't go to calls where a caller says there's a weapon.

You don't sign up for CALL. It is part of how the city answers 911 and police calls. Gulf Coast Jewish Family and Community Services runs it with the St. Petersburg Police Department. Since it started in 2021, responders have handled nearly 20,000 nonviolent emergencies, and about 95% of them needed no police support.

The city's last three-year, $5 million contract ran through Sept. 30, 2026. On Aug. 27, 2026, the City Council voted 8-0 to approve a new three-year agreement worth $5,965,113. Council Chair Lisset Hanewicz presided. Mayor Ken Welch's administration asked the council to skip the usual bidding process and give the contract straight to the current operator, citing what it called CALL's "overwhelming success."
