Some older Richmond homes get their water through a lead pipe that runs from the city's meter into the house. Since the early 1980s, the city has treated its water to keep lead from leaching into it and has replaced the public part of these lines. The private part was the property owner's responsibility. This ordinance accepted $500,000 in loan principal forgiveness through Virginia's drinking water revolving fund to pay for private-side work, making full replacements possible.

The money went to the Department of Public Utilities' Lead Service Line Replacement Grant Program, which entered its second phase in October 2019. Residential property owners applied to the city and chose a plumber from its certified list. The city paid the plumber actual, reasonable costs up to $2,500; the owner was responsible for anything above that amount. Homes had to be occupied or under active renovation.

Mayor Levar Stoney's administration brought the ordinance to City Council, and the utilities department under Director Calvin Farr Jr. administered the program. The council adopted it on September 23, 2019. The $500,000 replaced private lead lines at 176 homes. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency later recognized Phase II as a 2022 AQUARIUS project for excellence in creative solutions. With other grants and federal pandemic-relief money, the broader program later reached nearly 600 replacements.
