New York City offers free 3-K, a public preschool program for 3-year-olds. In many neighborhoods, though, more families wanted a seat than local providers could hold, so some children were placed far from home or in programs their parents didn't pick. For the 2026-27 school year, the city is adding 2,000 free 3-K seats in all five boroughs. They are aimed at 56 ZIP codes where demand was highest, and most will be at community child care centers that contract with the city.

Families applied through MySchools. Everyone who applied by the application deadline got an offer on May 19. About 700 of the new seats were added after the April 24 application-update period closed, so they were made available through waitlists. The city said the process would prioritize family choice, whether a sibling already attended, and how close the family lived. Families who already had an offer could use the waitlist to look for a program closer to home.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced more than 1,000 new seats in March and doubled the plan in May. The expansion draws on more than $100 million that he and Gov. Kathy Hochul committed in January to improve 3-K. That money is part of a larger state child care commitment of $1.2 billion. Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels's department runs enrollment.
