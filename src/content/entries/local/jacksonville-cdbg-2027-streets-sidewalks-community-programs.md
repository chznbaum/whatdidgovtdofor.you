Jacksonville is spending $8.17 million in federal Community Development Block Grant money this year: about $7.09 million in new funds from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, plus $1.08 million left over from earlier grants. The money became available with the city's fiscal year on Oct. 1, 2026.

Most of it goes to neighborhoods. About $4.8 million pays for street resurfacing and stormwater upkeep, and $500,000 builds sidewalks. Another $200,000 renovates Edward Waters University's Schell-Sweet Center, and $200,000 adds a porch to Atlantic Beach's Marsh Oaks Community Center.

Another $1.06 million goes to 15 programs run by the city, the Beach cities and nonprofits. You don't apply for the grant itself; you sign up with the program. The list includes recreation at Jacksonville Beach's Carver Center, independent living and older-adult programs from city Parks, Neptune Beach's senior center, and Lutheran Social Services' help for seniors. It also funds outreach to homeless people downtown, Family Promise's help for homeless families, Youth Crisis Center counseling, Jacksonville Urban League victim services, and self-sufficiency programs at the Jacksonville Housing Authority.

The ordinance was filed at Mayor Donna Deegan's request. Council member Jimmy Peluso co-sponsored it and carried the floor amendment that set the final funding schedule. Council members Tyrona Clark-Murray and Rahman Johnson also co-sponsored it. The council passed it 16-0 on July 28, 2026, with two members abstaining.
