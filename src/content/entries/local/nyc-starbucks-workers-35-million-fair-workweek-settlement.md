New York City's Fair Workweek Law gives fast-food workers steady, predictable schedules. City investigators found that Starbucks broke it more than 500,000 times at more than 300 stores starting in 2021. Most workers never got regular schedules. Starbucks also routinely cut employees' hours by more than 15% and passed workers over for extra shifts while continuing to hire new people. That made it hard to count on a paycheck or plan around child care, school, or a second job.

Under the settlement, Starbucks agreed to pay more than $35.5 million in restitution to over 15,000 workers. It also pays $3.4 million in penalties and costs, for $38.9 million in all. Most people who worked hourly at a New York City Starbucks between July 4, 2021 and July 7, 2024 get $50 for every week they worked. For example, 78 weeks of work comes to $3,900. You don't have to apply. Workers began reporting checks in the mail in February 2026. If your violations happened after July 7, 2024, you can file a complaint with the department online or through 311. You can file anonymously, and it's illegal for your employer to retaliate.

The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, headed by Commissioner Vilda Vera Mayuga, opened the case in 2022 after dozens of workers complained. It later widened the investigation to Starbucks stores across the city. Starbucks also agreed to follow the law from now on.
