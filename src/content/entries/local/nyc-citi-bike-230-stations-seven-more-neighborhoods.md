Citi Bike now has stations in seven more neighborhoods: Norwood and Riverdale in the Bronx; Brownsville, East New York, Kensington and Bay Ridge in Brooklyn; and Forest Hills in Queens. More than 230 stations with over 3,700 docks went in. The system now covers 86.5 square miles, and 62% of New Yorkers live inside its service area. In Bay Ridge, where the first stations opened in September 2025, riders took more than 75,000 trips in the first year.

Anyone can use the new stations. If you live in NYCHA housing or get SNAP and are 16 or older, you can sign up for Reduced Fare Bike Share. It costs $5 a month, with unlimited 45-minute rides on classic bikes and lower e-bike rates. At the new Norwood stations, more than half of all trips are on reduced-fare memberships.

The city's Department of Transportation and Lyft, which runs Citi Bike, agreed on the expansion in late 2024. Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez and Mayor Eric Adams announced it that December. Then-City Council staffer Kayla Santosuosso helped shape the Bay Ridge rollout. It used docks still uninstalled under the city's earlier agreement with Lyft, and Streetsblog reported that the city wasn't paying to subsidize the system. DOT worked with each community to pick station sites, and Lyft installs and maintains the stations. The rollout began in September 2025 and finished in East New York in October 2026, under Commissioner Mike Flynn.
