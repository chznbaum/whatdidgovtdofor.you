Hennepin County will spend $2 million more in federal Ryan White money on people living with HIV. The money comes from the Minnesota Department of Human Services, which receives the state's share of Ryan White Part B funding. The county had already received about $6.2 million under the agreement since 2024. This raises the total to $8.2 million and keeps it running until June 30, 2027.

Ryan White pays for HIV care and support for people with low incomes. Hennepin County Public Health uses this money to buy services from local providers, including medical visits, early intervention, case management, rides to appointments, food shelves and home-delivered meals, nutrition therapy, outpatient addiction treatment, and help finding housing and paying rent. These services reach people across Minnesota, not only in Hennepin County. You can apply through Minnesota's centralized eligibility system on your own or with an HIV provider; DHS decides whether you qualify.

The county board approved the amendment on June 11, 2026, after its Health Committee advanced it on June 2. It also added $1 million to the county's 2026 public health budget so the money could be spent this year. This is a different stream from the federal Part A money Hennepin County manages for HIV care in the Twin Cities area.
