Newark is getting $4.03 million in federal money to make part of Broad Street safer for people on foot and on bikes. The project covers the stretch from the Route 21 ramps to Emmett Street. It's meant to make crossings easier and more accessible, slow down traffic, and give more of the street to people walking. Newark doesn't have to put in any of its own money.

You don't need to apply for anything. Everyone who uses that part of Broad Street benefits once the work is built. The city has until September 30, 2032 to spend the money.

The money is a federal earmark in the 2024 transportation budget. Rep. Donald Payne Jr., who represented Newark in Congress, requested it in the House, and Sens. Cory Booker and Bob Menendez requested it in the Senate. It became law in March 2024, when President Joe Biden signed the spending bill. The New Jersey Department of Transportation passes the money to Newark. The Municipal Council, led by Council President Luis Quintana, voted 9–0 on May 20, 2026 to accept it.
