Miami-Dade's Mental Health Center at 2200 NW 7th Avenue in Allapattah was built but sat empty because no one paid to run it. This vote pays for that. The first phase has 75 beds: 10 for crisis stabilization and detox, 20 for short-term residential treatment, and 45 for longer residential treatment. The center will treat both mental illness and addiction, and it adds outpatient care so people can keep getting treatment after they leave. The county says it has lost 259 crisis beds since 2018.

The center is aimed at people who keep cycling through crisis, arrest, jail and homelessness. Many will be referred from the courts as an alternative to jail. Two nonprofits will run it: WestCare's Village South, with contracts of up to $27.2 million, and The Advocate Program, with contracts of up to $4.2 million. The county must seek an agreement for Jackson Health System or its designee to independently review operations and care twice a year. Part of the money comes from the county's share of opioid settlement funds, under a spending plan that runs through September 2031.

Commissioner Raquel Regalado sponsored the earlier operating proposal and helped carry the plan before cosponsoring the final substitute. Chairman Anthony Rodriguez sponsored the final resolution and added oversight and reporting rules. Retired Judge Steve Leifman had fought for the center for more than 20 years, after starting the Criminal Mental Health Project in 2000. Mayor Daniella Levine Cava's team negotiated the contracts. The commission passed it 12–0. How the center will be paid for after the initial contracts remains undecided.
