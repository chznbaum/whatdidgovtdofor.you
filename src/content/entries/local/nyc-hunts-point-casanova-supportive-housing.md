A six-story building with 96 homes is going up at 658-674 Casanova Street in Hunts Point, in the Bronx. The site held an empty commercial building. Construction started on Aug. 3, 2026.

Fifty-eight apartments are supportive housing for formerly homeless people, both individuals and families, who live with serious mental illness, substance use disorders or disabling medical conditions. These tenants come through the city's 15/15 supportive housing program, with city agencies helping to place them. Of the other 38 rent-stabilized apartments, 37 will be open to lower-income New Yorkers through the city's housing lottery, NYC Housing Connect, and one is reserved for a live-in superintendent. Everyone in the building gets free broadband. Tenants can also use case management, adult education, job training and help finding work, all provided on site by The Doe Fund.

The Doe Fund's real-estate executive John McDonald said he and his father, founder George McDonald, bought the site in February 2020. The pandemic held the project up. McDonald said the building happened because of the city housing department, HPD, led by Commissioner Dina Levy. HPD backed it with tax credits and a loan from its Supportive Housing Loan Program, and private lenders later joined in. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration plans to spend $1 billion over two years to create and preserve supportive housing, and this building is part of that push. No completion date has been announced.
