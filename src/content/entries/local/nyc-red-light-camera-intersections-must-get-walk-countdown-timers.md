A pedestrian countdown display shows how many seconds you have left to cross. City law requires the Department of Transportation to put one at every intersection that has a red light camera. The department can skip a location only if it finds that a display there would put people in danger, wouldn't fit its countdown guidelines, or isn't practical. You don't have to do anything to get this; the timers are part of the signal.

A red light camera usually goes where drivers run lights, and those are dangerous places to cross. In 2011, a city study found that countdown signals helped keep people from getting caught in the middle of the crosswalk when the light changed. By January 2017, countdowns were up at more than 7,500 of the city's 12,800 intersections with traffic lights, but no rule said that intersections with cameras had to get one. The number of camera intersections grew from 150 to 600 in 2026, so the rule now covers four times as many.

Council Member Steven Matteo, a Staten Island Republican, introduced the bill in January 2018. Cosponsors included Justin Brannan, Kalman Yeger, Ydanis Rodriguez, Chaim Deutsch, Peter Koo and Ben Kallos. The Council passed it on Feb. 13, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature on March 16, 2019. It took effect 120 days later.
