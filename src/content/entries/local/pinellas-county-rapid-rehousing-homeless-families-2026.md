If you're homeless in Pinellas County, rapid rehousing can help you get back into a home of your own quickly. A caseworker helps you find a place and stays with you after you move in. A shared fund then helps pay rent, utility deposits and utility bills, with payments going directly to landlords and utility companies. The program serves people on their own and families with children under 18.

You don't apply to the county directly. You get referred through the coordinated entry system run by the Homeless Leadership Alliance of Pinellas, the same front door used for other homeless services in the county. Under the new agreements, St. Vincent de Paul CARES handles housing search and case management. 211 Tampa Bay Cares, also called First Contact, manages the assistance fund and pays landlords and utility companies.

The county commission, chaired by Dave Eggers, approved the agreements on Sept. 10, 2026. They're worth up to about $4 million over three years, including roughly $600,000 a year in direct assistance, and services started Oct. 1. The county and the City of St. Petersburg contribute to the fund together. The county's first-year share comes from its FY2027 General Fund budget; later years require annual budget approval. Five firms bid for the work, and the county picked the two top-ranked ones, allowing the existing partnership to continue.
