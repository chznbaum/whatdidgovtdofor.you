Newark is buying, renovating and planning to operate a community resource center where residents can get help with housing, health care and work. On May 27, 2026, the New Jersey Department of Community Affairs notified the city that it had awarded an additional $1,316,188. That raised the grant from $6,732,267 to $8,048,455, with no city money required. The grant includes federal Capital Projects Fund money from the American Rescue Plan.

New Jersey's Capital Projects Fund report says the state is partnering with Newark, the North Jersey Community Research Initiative and Bridges Outreach to develop the center near Newark Penn Station. The original federal award was described in March 2024 as expanding services from NJCRI's Crossroads center for homeless adults and Project WOW for LGBTQIA+ youth into a multipurpose downtown facility. Plans include housing navigation, health and workforce services, meals, showers, laundry and free high-speed internet. The facility will be open to the public, with a focus on unsheltered Newark residents, especially people experiencing chronic homelessness.

The Council of the Whole sponsored the grant amendment, and the Municipal Council adopted it 8-0 on Aug. 5, with one member absent. Council President Luis Quintana led the body. On Oct. 7, the council extended the center's renovation contract through Nov. 30 because the project still needed to be completed. The grant runs through Dec. 31, 2026.
