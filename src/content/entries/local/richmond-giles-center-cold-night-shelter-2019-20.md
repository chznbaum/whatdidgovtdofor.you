In the winter of 2019–20, Richmond opened the Annie Giles Community Resource Center at 1400 Oliver Hill Way, once the Conrad Center, as an overnight shelter. It opened on nights when the outdoor or wind chill temperature was forecast to drop to 40 degrees or below. Doors opened at 7 p.m., and people had until 9 p.m. to register. Opening announcements document nights in November, December and January; the seasonal authorization ended April 15.

People could not just walk in. Single adults needing overnight shelter were told to call the Homeless Crisis Line for a referral. Richmond residents who could not use an existing shelter or were told its beds were full were directed to Commonwealth Catholic Charities during daytime hours for a referral to the overflow shelter. The shelter was basic. People slept on mats in one large shared room, and Council Member Stephanie Lynch later called it "not a humane space."

City code requires the administration to hold public meetings and present a written operating plan before any city-owned building can serve as a cold weather shelter, and the City Council has to approve it. Mayor Levar Stoney's administration did that work and introduced the ordinance in September 2019. The council amended it and passed it on Oct. 14, 2019. The approval ran out on April 15, 2020.
