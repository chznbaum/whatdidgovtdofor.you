If you work for the Columbus Consolidated Government, your pension is set to get better. The council changed both of the city's plans, General Government and Public Safety. Workers will vest sooner, which means fewer years on the job before they've earned a pension they keep. Service can count for up to 40 years, so long-serving employees can keep building their benefit. A rule that made new members of the Deferred Retirement Option Plan wait through a three-year suspension period is gone.

The biggest change in take-home pay is for people hired after July 1, 2012. Since a 2012 overhaul, those employees have had to pay into the plan and wait 10 years to vest. Their required contribution drops from 8% of pay to 6%, starting Jan. 1, 2027, or sooner if the city manager approves. Employees don't have to apply, because the change is built into the plans. When council considered the changes, city staff said both pension plans were funded above 100% for fiscal year 2027.

Councilor Glenn Davis brought the ordinance. At its Aug. 25 first reading, he secured the amendment setting the date for the contribution cut. On Sept. 8, the council adopted the ordinance unanimously, 10–0.
