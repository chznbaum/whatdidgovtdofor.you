New York City cuts property taxes for homeowners who are 65 or older, or who have a disability, and have modest incomes. The two programs are the Senior Citizen Homeowners' Exemption (SCHE) and the Disabled Homeowners' Exemption (DHE). Homeowners normally have to send in a renewal form on schedule, and anyone who misses it can lose the break.

This law carried SCHE and DHE benefits from the 2021–22 tax roll over to the 2022–23 roll, so most recipients did not have to reapply. The Department of Finance could still ask for a form if it had reason to think a home had changed hands, the owner had moved or died, or someone new had been added to the deed. Owners whose income had dropped could still apply on their own for a bigger break. The city had done the same thing the year before, during the pandemic. The Council's fiscal statement projected no added spending and a $2 million revenue increase because Finance could review properties that appeared no longer eligible.

Council Speaker Adrienne Adams sponsored the city's bill, Council Member Tiffany Cabán cosponsored it, and Justin Brannan's Finance Committee approved it the same day the full Council passed it, March 10, 2022. Gov. Kathy Hochul's Executive Orders 11.1 and 11.3 allowed local governments to skip 2022 renewals. The Council acted because an estimated 7,100 homeowners had not renewed with the March 15 deadline close. Mayor Eric Adams did not sign or veto the bill, so it became law without his signature in April 2022.
