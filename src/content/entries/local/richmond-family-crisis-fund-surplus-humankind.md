Richmond's Family Crisis Fund helps city residents through sudden money emergencies that could cost them their home or get their power shut off. HumanKind, a local nonprofit, administered it from January 2023 through fiscal year 2026. Its initial 2023 funding included federal pandemic aid and city money. By that October, it had provided more than $700,000 to about 500 residents and was running low.

This ordinance kept the fund going. It took 10% of the city's calculated 2022–23 general fund surplus—the most its rules allowed for a one-time special-purpose reserve—and put it in a new HumanKind budget line “for families of the city of Richmond experiencing crisis.” The ordinance used year-end surplus rather than taking money from another budget line. Under HumanKind, trained partner groups referred residents, and approved payments went directly to landlords, utilities or other creditors.

Council member Stephanie Lynch spearheaded the program and pressed the city to replenish it. Mayor Levar Stoney then committed the surplus money, and his administration identified HumanKind to continue administering the fund. Lynch, Stoney, Ellen Robertson, Cynthia Newbille and Ann-Frances Lambert were patrons of the ordinance. Council adopted it on Dec. 11, 2023. The city continued funding the program afterward; ACTS replaced HumanKind as administrator for the remainder of fiscal year 2027.
