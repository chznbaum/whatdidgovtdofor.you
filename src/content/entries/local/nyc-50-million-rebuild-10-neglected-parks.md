New York City is spending $50 million to fully rebuild 10 neighborhood parks and playgrounds that have gone without major upgrades for years. The sites are in neighborhoods with high need: Mott, Fountain of Youth and Morris Mesa playgrounds in the Bronx; Van Dyke, Roebling and Elizabeth Stroud playgrounds in Brooklyn; Vladeck Park and the 133rd Street playground in St. Nicholas Park in Manhattan; Corona Health Sanctuary in Queens; and Kaltenmeier Playground on Staten Island. The three Bronx sites alone get $19.5 million.

You don't need to apply for anything. Before each design is drawn, NYC Parks holds community meetings where neighbors say how they use the park and what they want, such as new play equipment, seating, courts and more green space. Full rebuilds usually take three to four years.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Parks Commissioner Tricia Shimamura announced the money in March 2026 as part of the city's fiscal 2027 capital spending. It runs through the Community Parks Initiative, which has finished 70 park rebuilds over the past decade. With these 10, the program has 57 active projects. Advocates noted that the mayor's overall parks budget still fell short of his campaign pledge to give parks 1% of city spending.
