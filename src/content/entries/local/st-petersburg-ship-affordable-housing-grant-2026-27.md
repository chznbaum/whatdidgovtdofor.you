The St. Petersburg City Council authorized the mayor or his designee to accept $1,851,747 from the Florida Housing Finance Corporation for the 2026–27 year of the State Housing Initiatives Partnership, known as SHIP. The state program gives cities money to help eligible people and households afford a place to live.

Much of the city's homebuyer help runs on SHIP money. Its SHIP-funded down payment program provides a 0% second mortgage worth up to 20% of the sales price, plus $5,000 for closing costs, with a cap of $75,000. For buyers earning 80% of the area median income or less, the loan is forgiven after 10 years in the home. When applications are open, buyers do not apply directly: their lender submits the application through the city's online portal. Priority goes to lower-income households, people with special needs, and essential workers such as teachers and nurses. Buyers also take an 8-hour homebuyer class. As of Nov. 15, 2026, the city says new citywide SHIP down payment applications are closed because funding is limited; the separately funded South St. Petersburg CRA program remains open.

The City Council approved the resolution 7-0 on Sept. 10, 2026, as part of its consent agenda, with Councilmember Gina Driscoll absent. Council Chair Lisset Hanewicz presided.
