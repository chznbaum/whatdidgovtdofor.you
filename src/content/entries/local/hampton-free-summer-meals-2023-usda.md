When school lets out, children who count on school meals can lose food they rely on. In summer 2023, Hampton's Department of Parks, Recreation and Leisure Services ran free meal sites around the city from June 13 to Aug. 25. The program covered children 18 and under, plus older people enrolled in qualifying school programs for people with disabilities. Hampton expected more than 1,500 children to take part.

At open meal sites, families did not submit individual income applications; children went to the site during meal hours. Under federal rules, open sites generally qualify by being in areas where at least half of children are eligible for free or reduced-price school meals. The summer meals were intended to meet the standards used for school lunches.

Hampton has received USDA Summer Food Service Program funding since 1969. For 2023, the council authorized up to $165,228 in expected federal reimbursement. The final payment depended on how many meals were served, so the resolution did not establish that Hampton received the entire ceiling. USDA was expected to cover the program's direct costs, while the parks department covered coordination and indirect costs.

Hampton City Council, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, voted 7-0 on Aug. 9, 2023, to accept the grant and add it to the city budget.
