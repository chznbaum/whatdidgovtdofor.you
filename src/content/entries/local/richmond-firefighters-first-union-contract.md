If you fight fires, run medical calls or answer emergency calls for the City of Richmond, your job now comes with a union contract. It runs from July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2027. It sets starting firefighter pay at 102% of the average in nearby counties, rising to 104% by the last year, and the raise carries through every step of the pay scale. You get at least two hours' pay if you're called back for overtime and 30 minutes if you're held over. The contract also adds yearly cancer screenings, bars lie-detector tests for hiring and promotion, and creates a labor-management committee that advises the fire chief. Workers don't have to apply: the contract covers everyone in the Fire and Emergency Services unit. The city set aside about $29 million over three years for it.

The contract was a long time coming. In 1977, the Virginia Supreme Court held that local governments lacked authority to bargain collectively without express permission from the state. That restriction held until a 2020 state law let localities allow bargaining. Richmond City Council voted in 2022 to let city workers unionize. Councilmember Reva Trammell, who had pushed for bargaining since 1998, was the lead patron of those ordinances, and Council President Kristen Nye co-led the effort. IAFF Local 995, led by Keith Andes, then bargained with Mayor Levar Stoney's administration. Stoney introduced the contract, and the council approved it 8–0 on May 13, 2024, along with first contracts for police and administrative workers.
