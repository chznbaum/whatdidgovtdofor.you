Lead can get into drinking water from the service line that runs from the water meter to a building. Richmond's utility already had funding to replace the public portion, but it was not authorized to spend utility money on private infrastructure. This state-administered grant helped cover that private portion. It earmarked $500,000 for approved replacements at residential properties, schools and day cares, as well as administration and outreach. The remaining $250,000 funded development of an interactive service-line inventory map required by the Virginia Department of Health.

Property owners hired private plumbers certified by the city. At the time of the ordinance, 21 plumbing companies were certified to participate, and grant funds could reimburse approved replacement costs. The ordinance did not guarantee money for every property; it funded approved work through participating plumbers.

Mayor Levar Stoney and Council Member Stephanie Lynch were the ordinance's patrons. The City Council adopted it 8-0 on Dec. 11, 2023; Lynch had left the meeting before the vote. Richmond later reported more than $1.5 million in total financial assistance to property owners across its lead-line program, not solely from this award. In September 2026, the council accepted $7.3 million for a later phase, so the broader replacement program continues beyond this one-time grant.
