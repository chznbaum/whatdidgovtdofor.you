Richmond owned a former nursing home at 1900 Cool Lane, just across the line in Henrico County, that had sat empty and flood-damaged since 2008. In July 2021, the city moved $600,000 of its unspent federal pandemic aid, Community Development Block Grant money from the CARES Act, toward turning the building into 86 apartments for people affected by COVID-19. Virginia Supportive Housing, now called SupportWorks Housing, did the conversion.

The $24 million conversion opened in January 2024, and tenants began moving in that month. All residents earn half the area's median income or less, and 28 apartments are reserved for people leaving homelessness. Rent is capped at 30% of household income. Thirteen apartments are fully accessible, and the building has staff and services on site. Henrico, the state housing agency and PlanRVA also put money into the project. In 2026, officials said 97% of residents had not returned to homelessness.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the funding shift, and council members Ann-Frances Lambert, Ellen Robertson, Cynthia Newbille and Andreas Addison joined him as patrons of the ordinance. The council adopted it on July 26, 2021. The money came from federal relief the city had already received but not yet assigned, so no other service was cut to pay for it.
