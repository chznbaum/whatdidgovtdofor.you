Riverland floods even when the rain isn't extreme. The neighborhood has no full drainage system, so water sits in streets and yards after moderate to heavy storms. Fort Lauderdale has now approved a contractor to design and build one: new drainage pipes, stormwater pump stations, and other equipment that moves rainwater out of the neighborhood.

The City Commission approved $82.6 million for the project. That includes a $79.6 million design-build agreement with David Mancini & Sons, Inc. and $3 million for city project management and construction inspection. The city expects the work to be mostly done about two years after it gives the contractor the second go-ahead to start. Residents don't need to apply or do anything. The project is public infrastructure in their neighborhood.

The project started as an unsolicited proposal from the contractor. Under Florida's public-private-partnership law, the city opened a 21-day window for competing proposals but received none, then negotiated the agreements with David Mancini & Sons. Commissioner Pam Beasley-Pittman, whose district includes Riverland, moved to approve them on Oct. 20, 2026, and Commissioner Steven Glassman seconded. That night the commission also approved a separate, roughly $94.8 million drainage project for nearby Melrose Manors.
