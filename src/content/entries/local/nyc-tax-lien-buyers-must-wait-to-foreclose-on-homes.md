When New York City homeowners fall behind on property taxes or water bills, the city can sell that debt to private buyers in a yearly lien sale. Buyers then add interest and fees, and they can eventually foreclose. Under this law, if you own and live in a small home and your debt is sold, the buyer must wait at least a year before trying to foreclose. Even then, the debt must reach $70,000 or 15% of your home's market value, whichever is less.

The buyer must also send you a notice every three months. It has to show how much you owe, what steps they've taken to collect, and your options for paying it off. You don't need to apply; the city must write these terms into every lien sale. The law also lets the city sell liens to a new public land bank instead of private investors. After 2028, that's the only kind of sale it allows.

Speaker Adrienne Adams sponsored the bill, and Council Members Sandy Nurse and Justin Brannan helped lead the reform package. The Council passed it in December 2025. Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it on his last day in office, and on January 29, 2026, the Council overrode him by at least a two-thirds vote. Mayor Zohran Mamdani paused lien sales in March 2026. The city plans its next sale for mid-2027, and this law will cover it.
