Henrico County is getting more than $1.7 million in opioid settlement money for the 2026-27 fiscal year. It's the most the county has received in one year. The biggest change is to the county's mobile treatment van. It now runs about 6 to 8 hours a week. With four new full-time staff, it will run more than 40 hours a week, including weekends. The van gives out free naloxone (Narcan), fentanyl and xylazine test strips, and kits for getting rid of old medicines. It also connects people with a clinician, a nurse practitioner, and a peer support specialist. Overdose data from the fire department will guide where the van goes.

The money also pays for a pilot program. Firefighters at an overdose will be able to reach a doctor in real time, and the doctor can start treatment with medicines like Suboxone on the spot. Other programs help pregnant and parenting women, and more libraries and social services offices are getting free naloxone cabinets. Residents can connect with treatment through the van without first visiting a county office, or pick up free naloxone from a cabinet.

The money comes from national settlements with companies that made, shipped, and sold opioids. Del. Charniele Herring and Sen. George Barker carried the 2021 law that created the Virginia Opioid Abatement Authority to give that money out. The Empower Henrico Task Force planned the 13 programs, the authority approved the fiscal 2027 funding on June 15, and the county was hiring staff as of early November.
