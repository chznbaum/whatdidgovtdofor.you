Richmond put $1,966,278 from the U.S. Justice Department toward keeping young people away from gun violence. The largest share, $1,094,716, went to youth gun violence work in the city's Department of Justice Services. Another $551,040 went to We Matter RVA, a city youth program, and $95,470 went to the city's Gun Violence Prevention Steering Committee to plan the work. The last $225,052 was set aside for Virginia Commonwealth University's Survey and Evaluation Research Lab to conduct a three-year independent study of whether the programs work.

The money went into city-run programs, including the rollout of Youth LIFT Messengers, so young people and families joined through those programs rather than applying for the grant. The city told the Justice Department that Richmond had 90 homicides in 2021.

Mayor Levar Stoney's administration applied for the grant. Richmond was one of six city, county, or tribal governments nationwide to win a grant to expand existing violence-prevention work. Stoney was the ordinance's patron, and the City Council adopted it on December 12, 2022, which let the city accept and spend the money. The federal grant period was later extended and ran through September 2026.
