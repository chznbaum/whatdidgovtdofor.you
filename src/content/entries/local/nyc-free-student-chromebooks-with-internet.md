In the 2025–26 school year, New York City public schools began giving students new Chromebooks with built-in T-Mobile cellular service. Because the internet came with the laptop, students could do schoolwork at home even if their family had no broadband. Students could take the laptops home and keep them until they graduated. Schools with devices more than five years old, or with too few devices for every student, got them first. Students in temporary housing came next, then high-poverty schools.

The city bought 350,000 of the Chromebooks and sent them to schools. By December 2025, 71,000 had reached students, the Education Department said. It hasn't given a later count.

Mayor Eric Adams launched the program in September 2025. The Education Department said Adams and Chief Technology Officer Matthew Fraser started it with NYC Public Schools, and Fraser's technology office held the T-Mobile service contract. Adams credited First Deputy Mayor Randy Mastro and Deputy Mayor and Chief of Staff Camille Joseph Varlack with putting the package together. Schools Chancellor Melissa Aviles-Ramos set the rollout order. The city planned to spend about $327 million over four years, and most of that was for cellular service. Adams said part of the cost would be covered by savings from making T-Mobile the city's main wireless carrier.

The free internet lasted one school year. The city was paying T-Mobile a flat $3.5 million a month however many students used it. In August 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration ended the service and called it a duplication of existing resources. Schools can now pay $10 a month per student for Verizon service for students who need it.
