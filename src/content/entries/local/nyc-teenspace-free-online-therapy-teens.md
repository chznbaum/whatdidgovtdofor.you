If you live in New York City and you're 13 to 17, you can talk with a licensed therapist for free through NYC Teenspace. Messaging is unlimited, and you can schedule one 30-minute live audio or video session each month. You keep the same therapist from session to session. You don't need insurance, payment or a doctor's referral. The service also has self-guided courses on coping skills, and teens who need more intensive help are referred to psychiatric or in-person care.

To sign up, a teen enters their address and birthdate online and answers a few questions about how they're doing. A parent or guardian gets an email to give consent, as New York law requires for most minors. Teens are matched with a therapist within days.

Health Commissioner Ashwin Vasan brought the idea to Mayor Eric Adams, who embraced it and announced it in his March 2023 mental health plan. Executive Deputy Commissioner Deepa Avula helped deliver it, Schools Chancellor David Banks championed it, and Youth Commissioner Keith Howard helped publicize it. The city launched the program in November 2023 through a Health Department contract with Talkspace estimated at $26 million. The neighborhoods with the most early sign-ups included Brownsville, East New York and Morrisania. The Health Department is evaluating the program and hasn't said whether it will continue past November 2026.
