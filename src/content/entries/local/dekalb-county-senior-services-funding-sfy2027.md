DeKalb County's Office of Aging, part of the Human Services Department, runs the county's main senior services. It offers rides, case management, information and referral, group meals at senior centers, meals delivered to homebound seniors, homemaker and personal care help at home, and respite care so family caregivers can take a break. The office has served DeKalb seniors for more than 23 years as the county's designated aging and nutrition provider.

In July 2026, the county accepted about $2.47 million in federal and state funds for these services. The money comes through the Atlanta Regional Commission and covers July 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027. The county adds a $172,933 match from money it had already set aside for seniors, which brings the total to about $2.65 million. The county calls that a planning estimate: the final amount could rise or fall depending on what the regional commission receives. The resolution also lets the county pay the outside providers that deliver some of the services.

The Board of Commissioners approved the funds 7-0 on July 14, 2026. Presiding Officer Chakira Johnson chaired the meeting. To find out about services, older residents and their families can contact the county's Human Services Department.
