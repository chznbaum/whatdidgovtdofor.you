Miami-Dade County owns a small vacant lot in Wynwood that no county department said it needed. The county's property appraiser valued it at $3.45 million. Land costs that high make it hard to build apartments that working families can afford. On June 16, the county commission authorized transferring the lot to Cipres RE for $10 after required due diligence. In exchange, the developer must build 14 three-bedroom apartments with income and rent limits.

Two apartments are reserved for households earning up to 80% of the area's median income, four for households earning up to 100%, and eight for households earning up to 120%. All 14 will have rent ceilings based on no more than 120% of the area median income, adjusted for family size. A rental agreement recorded on the property will keep the limits in place for 30 years after the first certificate of occupancy. Before leasing begins, the developer must submit its resident application and tenant-selection policies to the county housing department. The developer has three years after the deed is recorded to build and rent the apartments, or the county can take the land back. Plans also include ground-floor shops, and the developer agreed to give $800,000 toward a scholarship fund for District 3 students.

Commissioner Keon Hardemon, whose district includes the lot, sponsored the resolution. The county commission approved it 12-0 on June 16, 2026.
