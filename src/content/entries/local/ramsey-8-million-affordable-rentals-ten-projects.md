Ramsey County is putting $8.19 million into 10 projects that build or repair affordable rental housing. The largest award, $1.86 million, goes to Saint Paul Public Housing for renovations at the Wilson Hi-Rise. Outlaw Development gets $1 million toward Alfond Apartments, a new building. The other awards, from $345,000 to $1 million each, help owners buy and fix up older apartment buildings or repair the ones they already run, so rents there stay within reach.

You don't apply to the county for this money. The county manager can put the awards into loan or other agreements with the developers and owners. Each agreement will require a promise recorded on the property's title to keep its rents affordable for 30 years, and the county will check every year that the owner maintains the rent limits.

The money is Ramsey County's share of Minnesota's Local Affordable Housing Aid, which the state pays eligible Twin Cities-area counties and cities each year for housing. County staff invited proposals in April 2026, received 45 eligible applications, and scored each one. The county board approved the 10 winners 6–0 on Aug. 4, 2026, on a motion by Commissioner Rena Moran, seconded by Commissioner Kelly Miller. Commissioner Garrison McMurtrey was excused. The year before, the board put $11.9 million of this aid into housing.
