When COVID-19 hit New York in 2020, many older and medically vulnerable people couldn't risk a trip to the store. Through the city's GetFoodNYC program, they could have free meals brought to their door. It was meant for people who couldn't get food themselves, had no one to bring it and couldn't afford private delivery. They signed up online or by phone and answered a few questions to see if they qualified.

The city paid taxi and for-hire drivers, whose fares had dried up, to make the deliveries. Drivers initially earned $15 an hour plus $5 an hour for expenses. Starting April 26, the city paid $53 per route: $40 in base pay and $13 for expenses. About 11,000 drivers signed up in the first weeks. From March to October 2020, TLC-licensed drivers delivered more than 65 million meals and earned about $40 million.

Sanitation Commissioner Kathryn Garcia ran GetFoodNYC after Mayor Bill de Blasio named her the city's food czar in March 2020. TLC Commissioner Aloysee Heredia Jarmoszuk proposed using idle TLC-licensed drivers and developed that part of the program. NYC Emergency Management Commissioner Deanne Criswell issued the rule formally establishing the program in May and continued it under a final rule in September. Transportation Commissioner Polly Trottenberg issued a supporting rule allowing drivers to park for up to 20 minutes during deliveries. De Blasio approved both emergency rules. Most GetFoodNYC funding came from the federal government through FEMA.
