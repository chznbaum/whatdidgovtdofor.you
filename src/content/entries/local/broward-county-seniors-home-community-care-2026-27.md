Older Broward residents who qualify for care at home or in their community will keep getting it for another year. The county will pay up to $9 million to the providers that deliver this care from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027. The county's Elderly and Veterans Services Division renewed agreements that pay those providers a set rate for each client the county refers to them.

The money covers clients in five programs. Three are state-funded: the Alzheimer's Disease Initiative, Community Care for the Elderly, and Home Care for the Elderly. The fourth is the county's own effort to shorten the Community Care for the Elderly waitlist. The fifth is an adult day care program paid for by the Areawide Council on Aging of Broward County. You don't sign up with a provider yourself. You enroll in one of the programs, and the county refers you to a provider and pays it.

Broward's Human Services Department sent the renewal to the County Commission. The commission approved it 8-0 on June 9, 2026, with Commissioner Nan Rich abstaining. Mayor Mark Bogen chaired the meeting. The vote also lets the county administrator approve contract changes that don't add to the county's costs.
