Union County now has $2,454,429 more in federal money to keep nine more local homelessness programs going. The money comes from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development's Continuum of Care program, and the county awarded it to agencies that run the programs. The county's contract notice says the money pays for tenant-based, sponsor-based, and scattered-site rental assistance, along with program operations and supportive services for homeless individuals and families.

People receive those services through the organizations operating the programs rather than applying for a share of the grant. In May, the county approved a spending plan of up to $3,100,929. This change raised the most it can spend to $5,555,358.

The money came after a hard year for the federal program. In late 2025, HUD's attempt to rewrite the competition was challenged in court. Congress then told HUD to renew Continuum of Care projects whose grants had expired, so programs wouldn't lose their funding.

Commissioner Stanley J. Neron brought the resolution. The Board of County Commissioners approved it 6–0 on June 25, 2026. Three members were absent, including Neron.
