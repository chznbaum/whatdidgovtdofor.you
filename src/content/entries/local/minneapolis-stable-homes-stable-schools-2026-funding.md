Stable Homes Stable Schools helps Minneapolis Public Schools families who are homeless or close to losing their homes. Families who can still stay where they are can get short-term emergency help, like overdue rent or a car repair needed to keep a job. Homeless families can get monthly rent help for up to three years, with a year to transition off. They also get help finding a place on their child's school bus route and support from the YMCA. In June 2026, the City Council added $5 million to the city's agreement with the Minneapolis Public Housing Authority, which runs the program, to set its 2026 budget.

You can't apply on your own. A social worker at a participating elementary school decides whether a family qualifies and makes the referral. Charter and private school students aren't eligible. Immigration status doesn't affect eligibility. Since the program began in 2019, MPHA says it has helped more than 8,045 kids and 2,880 families.

The money comes from the city's 2026 budget. It set aside $3.6 million a year for the program plus $1.4 million one time. Mayor Jacob Frey proposed raising the yearly funding by $1.4 million after MPHA warned that its reserves were nearly gone. Council Member Robin Wonsley led the amendment that added the one-time $1.4 million. The council approved the contract 13-0 on June 11, 2026, and Frey signed it on June 15.
