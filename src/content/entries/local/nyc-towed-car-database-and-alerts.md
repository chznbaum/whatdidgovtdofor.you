Right now, if your car is towed in New York City, finding it can mean searching several city tow databases or calling the local police precinct, especially when a private tow company took it. This law replaces that with one public online database. It covers nonconsensual tows by city agencies and private tow operators, including operators working without a required license. Each record must list the plate number (or the VIN if the plate is missing, concealed or obscured), the date and time, where the car was taken from and where it went, who towed it, and how to reach them.

Tow operators must enter a car within 2 hours of towing it, or within 6 hours if it has no plate. You don't have to sign up to search, but you can opt in to get a text or email as soon as your car shows up in the system. The Department of Finance must create the database, and the mayor can designate agencies to implement other parts of the law. Evidence tows by local law enforcement, state or federal agency tows, and tows from Port Authority property aren't covered.

Council Member Inna Vernikov carried the bill and had sponsored versions of it since 2023. She said the old process ended in "confusion and miscommunication." Twelve colleagues from both parties cosponsored it. The Council passed it on Oct. 8, 2026, and it became law on Nov. 7, 2026, without the mayor's signature. It takes effect in November 2027.
