A sidewalk that stops partway down a block, or a corner with no ramp, can push people into the street. That's a bigger problem if you use a wheelchair, walk with a cane, or push a stroller. Orange County hired two contractors to fix those spots. The crews will build curb ramps that meet the Americans with Disabilities Act where they connect to existing sidewalks, pour concrete to fill missing sidewalk sections, and add the bumpy warning mats that tell people with low vision they've reached a street. The work includes the drainage pipes needed to keep the new sidewalks clear.

The county's Public Works Department runs the program, and the work covers all of the county's commission districts. Residents don't need to apply. Crews will work on locations the county picks over the five-year contracts.

The Board of County Commissioners approved the contracts on Sept. 15, 2026, at a meeting chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings. The county took ten bids and chose the two lowest qualified bidders. RMS Constructors Group's contract is estimated at about $6.99 million and W.T. Comp's at about $7.40 million, for a combined total of about $14.4 million. Both amounts are estimates of how much work the county will order, not fixed prices.
