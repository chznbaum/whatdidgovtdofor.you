A mobile farmers market now sells fresh produce, eggs, dairy and other local food around DeKalb County, with a preference for food grown in Georgia. It stops three days a week at nine places, including senior centers, recreation centers, a YMCA and an elementary school, and its operator says it reaches every county district. The market started in November 2025 at sites in South DeKalb that the county had marked as low-food-access areas. It added a third day and more stops in June 2026.

You don't need to sign up. Just show up at a stop. The market takes cash, cards and SNAP/EBT. If you pay with SNAP, local produce is half off. Shoppers 65 and older and students also get 10% off, though these discounts can't be combined.

DeKalb CEO Lorraine Cochran-Johnson launched the market as part of a package to help families during SNAP disruptions. Commissioner Ted Terry helped advance and champion the early investment in 2025, and the CEO's office brought the 2026 contract to the Board of Commissioners. Retaaza, a Georgia social enterprise, runs it. On Oct. 27, 2026, the board approved paying up to $378,000 from the county's 2026 budget. Commissioner Nicole Massiah moved approval and Commissioner Robert Patrick seconded. The contract requires Retaaza to report to the county on the market's results every quarter and every year.
