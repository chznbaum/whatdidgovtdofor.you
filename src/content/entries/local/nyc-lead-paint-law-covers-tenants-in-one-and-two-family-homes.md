New York City's main lead paint law, Local Law 1 of 2004, used to apply only to buildings with three or more apartments. That left out renters in small houses, where old paint can be just as dangerous to young children. Since February 2021, the law has also covered rented units in one- and two-family homes built before 1960. Units lived in by the owner or the owner's family are still exempt.

If you rent one of these units, your landlord now has the same duties as the owner of a big apartment building. They must ask whether a child under 6 lives with you. If a child under 6 lives there or regularly spends at least 10 hours a week in the unit, the landlord must inspect for peeling paint at least once a year, use trained workers and safe methods for repairs, and pay for the fixes. Before a new tenant moves in, they must clear lead hazards from doors and windows. You don't need to apply: return your landlord's yearly notice, let them in for the inspection, and call 311 if you see peeling paint.

Council Member Stephen Levin introduced the bill in May 2018. The Council passed it on Jan. 23, 2020, as part of a package of lead bills, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on Feb. 11, 2020. It took effect a year later. Over that year, the Department of Housing Preservation and Development, led by Commissioner Louise Carroll, wrote its rules to cover these homes.
