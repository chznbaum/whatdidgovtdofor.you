A new 60-apartment building is under construction at 1401–1407 Hull Street in Richmond's Manchester neighborhood. The city's grant agreement requires the apartments to remain income- and rent-restricted for at least 30 years. Each apartment will have its own income designation, and those designations must average 60% of the area median income. Some units may have higher limits if they are balanced by units with lower limits.

The city estimates the grant at $127,206 a year. After construction is complete, Richmond can return 97% of the increase in property taxes above the site's existing tax bill for up to 30 years, subject to annual appropriation. The cost is foregone future revenue: the city keeps the existing base taxes and 3% of the increase. If the owner violates the affordability or maintenance terms, the city can terminate the grant after notice and an opportunity to fix the default.

The building is not leasing yet, and the developer has not posted application details. When leasing opens, applicants will need to meet the income limit assigned to the available apartment.

Mayor Levar Stoney was listed as the ordinance's patron, and City Council adopted it on January 13, 2025. The Economic Development Authority's board unanimously authorized execution of the agreement on February 27, 2025. The agreement requires the project to be finished within three years after construction commences.
