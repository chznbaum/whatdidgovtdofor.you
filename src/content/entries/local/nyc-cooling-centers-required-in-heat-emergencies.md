During a heat-related emergency, NYC now has a legal duty to designate cooling centers. The city had run cooling centers for years, but no law required this. Where practicable, it must designate at least 7 centers per 100,000 residents. It must consider where vulnerable people live and the heat vulnerability index, and post health alerts and the locations and hours of centers that are open. The law does not guarantee every designated site will open during every emergency; if centers are unavailable or insufficient, the city's yearly plan must explain how vulnerable people will get cooling instead.

You don't need to sign up. During a heat emergency, you can walk into any open center, such as a library, an older adult center, or a public building. Find the nearest one on the city's Cool Options map or by calling 311. City officials say more than 500 New Yorkers die early from heat-related illness each year, and about 1 in 10 New Yorkers have no air conditioning at home.

Council Member Keith Powers wrote the bill, and 45 other Council members and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams signed on. The Council passed it in August 2025. Mayor Eric Adams's office said much of it was already city practice, and he let it become law without his signature. The law also requires the city to write a yearly cooling plan and report on how it reaches the public. The first plan came out in May 2026.
