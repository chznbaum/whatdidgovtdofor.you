DeKalb County authorized seven housing programs under a planned $155 million bond program. HomeStart will give qualified first-time buyers up to $20,000 for a down payment, eligible closing costs and prepaid escrow expenses. Households at or below 100% of area median income can get $20,000; those from 101% to 120% can get $15,000, and eligible county employees can get $5,000 more. The zero-interest loan is forgiven after 10 years if the buyer keeps the home as a primary residence.

BrighterHome will provide up to $30,000 for critical repairs to income-qualified homeowners who have owned and occupied their home for at least 10 years. Seniors, veterans, people with disabilities and 15-year homeowners get preference. People who also qualify for a separate county repair program can receive up to $14,000 more. Other bond programs preserve apartments, fill financing gaps for affordable construction, redevelop blighted property, expand transitional housing and support nonprofit and faith-based developers.

The programs are still rolling out. HomeStart applications were not available on Nov. 14, 2026; Decide DeKalb said it would finalize the application process in December. BrighterHome applications are planned for the first quarter of 2027.

CEO Lorraine Cochran-Johnson proposed the housing initiative, and Chief Housing Officer Alan Ferguson's office designed and coordinates the programs. On a motion by Commissioner Ted Terry, seconded by Commissioner Michelle Long Spears, the Board approved them 7–0 on July 14, 2026. The county will repay the bonds from its general fund. The broader 2026 budget raised the general-fund property-tax rate by half a mill—an estimated $65 a year on a $350,000 home—to fund housing and other initiatives without drawing down reserves.
