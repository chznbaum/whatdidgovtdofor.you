Jacksonville approved $479,997 so Trinity Rescue Mission can renovate its day center and offer daytime services to people experiencing homelessness in Duval County. Up to $359,997 pays for operations, and up to $120,000 pays for one-time capital expenses. The money comes from the city's Homelessness Initiatives Special Revenue Fund. The contract runs from Oct. 1, 2026, through Sept. 30, 2027, with oversight by the Jacksonville Fire and Rescue Department.

You don't apply for a grant. The center, which has a peak capacity of 55 people, is meant for people who have nowhere to go during the day. That includes people who sleep at Trinity's shelter and have to leave in the daytime, which leaves them outside in extreme heat or cold. The ordinance lets the city contract with Trinity directly instead of running a competitive award process.

Ron Salem chaired the City Council's DOGE committee, which in March 2026 called a day center for people leaving Trinity during the day a key need. Salem said people were "just hanging out there with nothing to do" once the building emptied out. Mayor Donna Deegan's administration requested the ordinance in July, and Council Member Jimmy Peluso co-sponsored it. Three council committees approved it, and the full council passed it 18-0 on Aug. 25, 2026.
