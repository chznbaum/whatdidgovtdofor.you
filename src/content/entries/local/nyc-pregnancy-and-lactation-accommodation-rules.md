If you work in New York City for an employer with at least four employees and you’re pregnant, recovering from childbirth, or pumping milk, these rules spell out your accommodations. Domestic workers also get pregnancy accommodations even when they are the only employee. A stool, extra bathroom or water breaks, a looser uniform, lifting limits or light duty are generally reasonable unless the employer proves undue hardship. You don’t need a disability, and a doctor’s note generally isn’t allowed for small changes.

Your employer has to talk with you in good faith and must start the talk when it knows or should know you need help. If it says no, you get the decision in writing. It should first keep you in your job, then consider a vacant equivalent job before a noncomparable one. Leave is a last resort. If you’re pumping at a covered workplace, you get reasonable break time and a private lactation room that isn’t a bathroom. The rules also protect choices like birth control, fertility treatment or abortion.

The City Council wrote these protections into law in 2013, 2018 and 2019. The Commission on Human Rights, led by Chair Carmelyn Malalis, wrote the detailed rules, held a public hearing in November 2020, and voted unanimously to adopt them on Feb. 24, 2021. They took effect that April 2. A clerical amendment effective July 1, 2021 moved them from §§ 2-07 and 2-08 to the current §§ 2-09 and 2-10.
