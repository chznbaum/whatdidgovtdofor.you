CityFHEPS is New York City's rent voucher for people leaving or trying to avoid homeless shelters. Until June 2023, single adults and families had to stay in a city shelter for 90 days before they could qualify, even if they had a job and an apartment lined up. This rule ended that wait, so a household can qualify for a voucher as soon as it meets the other rules.

The rule lowered the work threshold from 14 to 10 hours a week for families qualifying through employment and set the same 10-hour threshold for single adults. Work is not the only way to qualify: households may also qualify based on disability, age, caregiving responsibilities, military service, or a city referral. Income generally must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, with an exception for some single adults working full time at minimum wage. Your shelter's case manager or housing specialist helps you apply, and the voucher pays part of the rent on a new home.

Deputy Speaker Diana Ayala had pushed to end shelter stays as a condition of the voucher, and her bill passed the City Council in May 2023 as part of a broader package. Mayor Eric Adams opposed the rest of that package, but he signed this emergency rule on June 16, 2023, to end the 90-day rule right away. The city said shelters were nearly full and wanted to free up beds. Commissioner Molly Wasow Park's Human Resources Administration made the change permanent in November 2023.
