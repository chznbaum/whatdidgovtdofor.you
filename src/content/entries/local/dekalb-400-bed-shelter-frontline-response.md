DeKalb County is paying the nonprofit Frontline Response to run an emergency shelter with 400 beds for men, women and families. The shelter is at 2585 Gresham Road. The contract also pays for food and case management. People get rides to the shelter and to medical care, behavioral health care and job interviews. Frontline will also help county public safety crews with outreach, prevention, rescue and relocation.

You don't apply to Frontline directly. The county's Community Development Department runs Coordinated Entry, which handles every referral to the shelter and oversees the contract. The county will pay up to $800,000, which covers one full fiscal year of services.

The contract continues work that began under a 2025 contract. In the county's words, that contract gave DeKalb more shelter space and was "well received" by homeless residents and the community. Community Development brought the item forward, and the board's planning and community services committee recommended it on Nov. 10. After one deferral, the Board of Commissioners approved it 5–1 on Nov. 17, 2026, with Presiding Officer Chakira Johnson in the chair. Commissioner Ted Terry voted no, and Commissioner Michelle Long Spears was absent.
