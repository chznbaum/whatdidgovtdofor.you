Getting through addiction treatment is hard. Going straight from intensive treatment to living on your own can make recovery harder. Broward County is putting $3 million toward transitional recovery housing: safe, structured places to live for people recovering from opioid addiction, including those with co-occurring substance use or mental health conditions. Residents also get peer support and case management while they work toward living on their own. The county says the program is meant to increase treatment engagement and reduce hospital stays.

The housing runs through Broward Behavioral Health Coalition, which already runs the county's mental health jail diversion services. The new money raises the county's agreement with the coalition from $1.5 million to $4.5 million for October 2026 through September 2027, and the county calls the housing program recurring. The county's documents don't say how many people it will serve or how to get a spot.

The money comes from Broward's share of opioid settlement funds, which companies paid to settle lawsuits over the opioid crisis. It doesn't come from local taxes. The amount was built into the 2026–27 county budget, and the Human Services Department's Community Partnerships Division, which began managing part of the settlement money in April 2026, brought the item forward. The County Commission approved it 8–0 on Oct. 20, 2026, with one commissioner abstaining.
