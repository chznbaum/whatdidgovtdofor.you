In 2012, lightning sparked a seven-alarm fire at 665 New York Avenue in Brooklyn. Vacate orders followed, and hundreds of rent-stabilized tenants lost their homes. The 117-apartment building has been uninhabitable since. Construction is now underway to rebuild it with 134 homes, with completion expected in 2028.

Of the 134 apartments, 90 will have income limits: 12 for households at 50% of area median income, 22 at 80%, and 56 at or below 105%. Eighteen of the 90 are expected to go to former tenants exercising a right to return, and 14 are reserved for formerly homeless New Yorkers. The city has not published dollar rents or an application date. The other apartments are 43 market-rate homes and one superintendent's unit. The building will be all-electric.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani proposed the revolving loan in his Block by Block housing plan. HDC President Eric Enderlin recommended this project, and HDC's members approved up to $12.87 million from its reserves or Battery Park City Authority funds. The loan is due back with interest from project cash flow, refinancing, or sale, so the city can reuse the money for housing. Council Member Linda Lee sponsored a 40-year Article XI tax exemption after HPD requested it, and the Mayor's Office credits Council Member Rita Joseph as a partner. The owner pays no property tax during construction and 1.63% of gross rent after completion; the 90 income limits remain permanently under an HPD agreement.
