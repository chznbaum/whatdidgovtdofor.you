The state set aside $13 million to design and improve publicly owned sanitary sewer infrastructure under West 7th Street in Saint Paul, through one of the city's oldest areas. Half the money, $6.5 million, covers the stretch from Kellogg Boulevard to St. Clair Avenue. The other half covers St. Clair Avenue to Interstate 35E. Residents and businesses don't need to apply; the city manages the project. The sewer work will be coordinated with a larger street reconstruction that MnDOT, Ramsey County and Metro Transit plan to begin as early as 2029.

Sen. Sandy Pappas, who chaired the Senate Capital Investment Committee, and Rep. María Isa Pérez-Vega, both St. Paul Democrats, were the chief authors of companion bills that sought $80 million for the project. The final bonding law set aside $13 million. Lawmakers passed that law 122–11 in the House and 60–7 in the Senate, with support from both parties, and Gov. Tim Walz signed it on May 27, 2026. The appropriation is financed with state general-obligation bonds.

To receive the grant, Saint Paul must submit the required information and enter an agreement with the Minnesota Public Facilities Authority. Council President Rebecca Noecker sponsored the resolution authorizing those steps. The council adopted it on Nov. 4, 2026, and Mayor Kaohly Her signed it a week later.
