In September 2019, Goochland County agreed to help Central Virginia Electric Cooperative pay for a fiber network to its customers in the county. The co-op planned to bring high-speed internet to the 804 homes and businesses on its power lines in Goochland, mostly served from its Cartersville and Shannon Hill substations. The county said a very large number of those members lacked high-speed broadband. The co-op estimated it would invest $2.5 million in Goochland.

The county doesn't pay anything up front. For the first five years after the fiber in each substation area is finished, it gives back the new taxes the co-op owes on that equipment, up to $125,000 in total. To get the money, the co-op has to document what it spent on construction and new equipment. The co-op sells internet service through Firefly Fiber Broadband. In August 2023 the co-op said it had finished its fiber build and that all of its roughly 38,000 members could get service.

The co-op first asked Goochland for help in July 2018. County records don't name a supervisor who brought the agreement forward. The Board of Supervisors, chaired that year by Manuel Alvarez Jr., approved it 5-0 on Sept. 3, 2019. That same month the county started its broader push for internet access in every part of Goochland.
