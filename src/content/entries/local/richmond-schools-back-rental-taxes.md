This summer, Richmond Public Schools found itself $8.9 million short for the 2026-27 school year after the state's late budget sent less money than the district had planned on. To close the gap, the district looked at cutting all of its year-round substitute teachers and leaving more teaching jobs empty. The city stepped in with $4.78 million, which covered about half of the hole.

The money was Richmond's early share of hotel and short-term-rental tax revenue held by the Greater Richmond Convention Center Authority. It followed the city's collection of $5.6 million in outstanding taxes from short-term-rental operators. The authority covers regional convention-center costs and normally sends remaining revenue back to localities each December. On July 17, 2026, Mayor Danny Avula asked it to release Richmond's share early so the money could support the schools. The budget amendment added anticipated revenue rather than taking money from another city budget line.

Once the city promised the money, the school board passed its budget unanimously on July 23. The district kept its year-round substitutes and mental health services, and the superintendent said there would be no more layoffs or pay cuts. Some cuts still happened: the district left five teaching jobs unfilled, which will push some middle and high school classes from 21 students to 24. City Council made the transfer official on Nov. 9, 2026. Families don't need to do anything to get this help.
