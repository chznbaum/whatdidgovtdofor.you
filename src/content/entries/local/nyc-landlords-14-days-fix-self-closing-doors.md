A self-closing door keeps a fire in one apartment from filling the halls and stairs with smoke. In New York City, doors to apartments, hallways and stairwells must swing shut and latch on their own. When HPD, the city's housing agency, finds one that doesn't, it issues its most serious class of violation. This law gives landlords 14 days to fix it instead of 21. Since 2023, HPD must go back and reinspect within 20 days after that deadline, even if the owner has filed a certificate saying the door is fixed.

The law also added fines of $250 to $500 for a broken self-closing door, plus $250 for each day it stays broken past the deadline. Owners who falsely certify repairs face larger penalties. Tenants don't have to apply for anything. The rules apply to every building HPD inspects.

Council Member Oswald Feliz wrote the bill after the Jan. 9, 2022, fire at the Twin Parks North West tower in the Bronx, which killed 17 people, eight of them children. Smoke inhalation caused every death. Bronx Borough President Vanessa Gibson asked for the bill, and 30 other council members signed on. The Council passed it on May 19, 2022, as part of a package of fire safety bills, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it on June 1.
