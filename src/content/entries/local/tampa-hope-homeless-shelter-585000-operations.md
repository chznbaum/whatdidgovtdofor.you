Tampa Hope is a shelter for people experiencing homelessness, run by Catholic Charities on its own land in Tampa. People sleep in small cottages or single-occupancy tents. They get three meals a day, clothing, medical care, and mental health and addiction treatment. In July 2026, Tampa City Council added up to $585,000 in city Housing Program money to cover the shelter's operations. That raises the city's agreement with Catholic Charities to as much as $1,585,000.

Tampa Hope has grown since it opened in December 2021. The city reports 215 cottages and 116 tents, enough for more than 352 people, and says the shelter served 402 people in 2025. The average stay is about 90 days. Outreach teams from the city, Tampa Hope, and Tampa police visit areas in and around downtown. They screen people on site and drive those who qualify to the shelter.

The council adopted the amendment 5–0 on its consent agenda on July 30, 2026. Council member Lynn Hurtak chaired the meeting as chair pro tem because Chair Alan Clendenin was absent. Bill Carlson was absent for the vote. Mayor Jane Castor later signed the first amendment. On Oct. 1, the council extended the agreement through Dec. 31, 2026, without adding funding.
