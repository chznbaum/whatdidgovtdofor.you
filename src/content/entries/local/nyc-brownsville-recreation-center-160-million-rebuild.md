The Brownsville Recreation Center has been a city facility since 1955. It will be torn down and rebuilt from the ground up. New York City set aside $160 million for the work in its 2024–25 budget. In 2025, the city added $31 million for higher construction prices and a second regulation-size basketball court, bringing the overall project budget to $191 million. The plans call for a new center of more than 74,000 square feet, with an indoor pool and two gym/basketball courts.

The rebuild follows years of delay. The city announced a $20 million partial renovation in 2016, but work did not begin until 2022. Crews found serious water and structural damage in 2023, and the indoor pool and gym closed. As of September 2026, the Golden Age Center was expected to remain open until at least 2028, with classes continuing there and outdoors on the adjacent field. The city dropped two temporary-space options and redirected $8 million to the permanent building.

Mayor Eric Adams and the City Council, led by Speaker Adrienne Adams, added the money in the budget deal they reached in June 2024. The Council passed the budget 46–3 on June 30. Mayor Adams credited Parks Commissioner Sue Donoghue with rallying and pushing for parks investments. DDC shortlisted design-build teams in September 2026. Its latest timeline calls for notice to proceed in the third quarter of 2027, construction to start in the third quarter of 2028, and completion in the third quarter of 2031.
