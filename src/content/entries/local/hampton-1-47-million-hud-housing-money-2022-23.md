Every year, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development awards Hampton Community Development Block Grant and HOME funding. CDBG can pay for housing, public facilities, services, code enforcement and other community development; HOME focuses on affordable housing. Hampton's plan covered July 2022 through June 2023 and set how the city would use that program year's grants.

HUD's final 2022 awards to Hampton were $903,077 in CDBG money and $570,404 in HOME money, $1,473,481 in all. The adopted plan covered housing improvements, neighborhood revitalization and help for new homebuyers. The programs principally serve people with low or moderate incomes. Residents do not receive the two grants as lump sums. Depending on the activity, the money pays for city or partner programs; someone seeking housing rehabilitation or homebuyer help has to qualify and apply through the responsible program.

On April 27, 2022, Mayor Donnie Tuck presided as the City Council held the required hearing and adopted the plan 7–0. No one spoke at the hearing. The official minutes also make clear that workforce development was not funded from these grants that year: the earlier WorkOne position had moved into city funding, and employment training used separate CARES Act money.
