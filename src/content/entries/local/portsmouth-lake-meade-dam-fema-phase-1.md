Lake Meade Dam sits in Suffolk but belongs to Portsmouth, and it holds back one of the lakes that supply the city's drinking water. Lake Meade and Lake Cahoon together supply more than 36,000 residential and commercial accounts, including Norfolk Naval Shipyard and Portsmouth Naval Medical Center. City engineers rate Lake Meade Dam as high-hazard. Studies found its spillway capacity and foundation strength inadequate for an extreme storm, raising the risk of failure and flooding within a zone containing more than 80 occupied homes and almost 30 businesses.

The planned repairs will strengthen the dam, upgrade its spillways and protect its sides from erosion. Water customers don't need to do anything. The work is meant to keep the water supply reliable and the people and property below the dam safer.

In 2023, FEMA announced that it would award $24.21 million for the project, pending further review. The Trump administration canceled pending grants under the program in April 2025. U.S. Sens. Mark Warner and Tim Kaine and U.S. Reps. Bobby Scott and Jennifer McClellan wrote to the Department of Homeland Security asking it to reverse the cancellation. A coalition of states sued, and a federal judge ordered FEMA to restore the program. FEMA approved $5.2 million for the Lake Meade project in July 2026. On Sept. 22, 2026, Portsmouth City Council voted 6–0 to accept the exact $5.226 million award for Phase 1.
