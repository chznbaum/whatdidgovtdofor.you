New York City's civil-rights law lets a person injured by a crime of violence motivated by gender seek damages and other court relief from a party that committed, directed, enabled, participated in or conspired in the violence. The law does not require a police report or criminal prosecution. Claims ordinarily must be filed within seven years—not nine. Local Law 21 changed a narrower rule: if injury or disability caused by the violence, or childhood, kept someone from suing when the claim arose, the person has nine years after that barrier ends, up from seven.

The law also revived claims whose deadlines had already passed. Survivors could file from March 1, 2023, through March 1, 2025, even if the violence happened long before. It also expanded the text beyond the person who committed the violence to parties that directed, enabled, participated in or conspired in it.

Council Member Carlina Rivera was the prime sponsor, and Selvena Brooks-Powers was the co-prime sponsor. Rivera said she passed the bill in coalition with survivors and advocates. The Council approved it on Dec. 9, 2021, and it became law Jan. 9, 2022, without the mayor's signature. After a court dismissed 464 lawsuits against the city in 2025, Brooks-Powers sponsored Local Law 50 of 2026. That separate law reopened claims through July 29, 2027, over Mayor Eric Adams's veto. A court has restored a claim under the 2026 law but stayed it while New York's highest court considers whether state law preempts the city's revival window.
