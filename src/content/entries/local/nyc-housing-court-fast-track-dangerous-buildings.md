Some NYC repair cases now move to the front of the line in Housing Court. They include buildings where a vacate order covers at least one-third of the apartments, at least one-third have immediately hazardous violations for loss of heat, hot water, electricity, gas or water, or every elevator in a building or section is out and documented by an immediately hazardous Buildings Department violation. A judge is assigned the day the case is filed. For these emergency HP cases, the court date must be no more than five days after a reasonable period to complete service. Later appearances follow an expedited schedule.

The fast track also covers Article 7-A cases where tenants or HPD ask a judge to appoint an outside manager for a building with an open immediately hazardous HPD Class C or Buildings Department Class 1 violation. City Hall expects the fast track to reach 40 to 50 buildings a year.

The Legal Aid Society had raised concerns about delays for years and helped develop the policy with HPD. Commissioner Dina Levy said HPD brought the idea forward after consulting legal advocates. Civil Court Administrative Judge Shahabuddeen Ally issued DRP-227 on July 16, 2026, and Housing Court Supervising Judge Jack Stoller collaborated on its implementation. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced it on Aug. 25. Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani and tenant-protection director Cea Weaver also helped push the initiative forward.
