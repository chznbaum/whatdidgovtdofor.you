If you walk across the Queensboro Bridge, you now use a dedicated path on the south side, where cars once drove. If you bike across, the north-side path is for cyclists. Before May 2025, walkers and cyclists shared one 11-foot path in both directions. The city counted about 2,785 people walking and 7,568 people biking across the bridge each day. Nothing to sign up for: both paths are public.

Transportation Alternatives began pushing to open the south roadway to people in 2016. Council Members Jimmy Van Bramer and Ben Kallos championed the conversion for years and even offered city funding for it; State Sen. Michael Gianaris also supported the campaign. Mayor Bill de Blasio announced the plan in January 2021, with an initial 2022 opening target.

The deadline slipped repeatedly. Starting in 2022, Council Member Julie Won pressed DOT for a schedule and an earlier opening as crashes continued on the crowded shared path. The walkway was ready by March 2025, but City Hall canceled the planned opening, saying Mayor Eric Adams had not been fully briefed. In April, Won and Gianaris joined about 200 advocates in walking across the finished path. Adams and DOT Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez announced the change on May 13, and it opened May 18. DOT repainted the bike path after two riders died in a May 2026 crash; as of November 2026, both paths remain in use.
