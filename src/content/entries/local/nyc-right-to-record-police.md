You have the right to record New York City police and peace officers while they are on the job, as long as you film from a safe distance. City law spells that out. An officer can't stop you from recording, try to scare you into stopping, take your phone, or stop, search, ticket, or arrest you because you were filming. The one defense the city gets is that your recording physically got in the way of lawful police work.

You don't have to apply for anything. If an officer violates your right, you can sue the city in state court, and a judge can award punitive damages. The police commissioner must also report every three months on arrests and summonses issued to people who were recording police.

Public Advocate Jumaane Williams carried the bill, which his office says was first introduced in 2016. Council Member Helen Rosenthal co-sponsored it. The Council passed it on June 18, 2020, during the protests after George Floyd was killed, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on July 15, 2020.

In June 2026, New York's highest court ruled unanimously that this law and the state's version don't cover filming inside police station lobbies. They still protect you everywhere else they apply.
