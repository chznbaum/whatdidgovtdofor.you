Newark set up a program that helps property owners pay to create a legal accessory dwelling unit (ADU), a small extra apartment such as a carriage house, a unit above a garage, or an addition to the home. The money can cover new construction, converting existing space, repairs, bringing a unit up to code, utility hookups, design and permit costs, and accessibility upgrades. It can come as a grant, a forgivable or deferred loan, or a reimbursement.

The city says the program is meant to help low- and moderate-income homeowners earn legal rental income while adding safe, affordable homes. The Department of Economic and Housing Development runs it and sets the rules, including income limits, owner-occupancy requirements, and how long a unit must stay affordable. Owners who take part may have to sign a deed restriction. Work on an existing unit can include code-compliance improvements, but projects still need any required zoning, permits, and code approvals.

The money comes from a federal Pathways to Removing Obstacles to Housing (PRO Housing) grant from HUD. Mayor Ras Baraka announced the program in his March 2026 State of the City address. He said $1.4 million of the grant would go to it, at up to $40,000 per single-family homeowner. The Municipal Council, led by President Luis Quintana, adopted the ordinance on July 15, 2026.
