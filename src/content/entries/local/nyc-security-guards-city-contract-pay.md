If you are a covered security guard for a private employer, your employer must match city-contract guard pay starting January 1, 2027. New hires and early-tenure unarmed guards must get at least $18.87 an hour. Unarmed guards meeting the schedule's experience, certification and training requirements get $22.20, and armed guards $33.70; those rates rise in May. Matching paid time off starts in 2028 and supplemental benefits in 2029.

The law excludes government and Port Authority guards and work already covered by listed prevailing-wage laws. For guards under a valid union contract—or another qualifying pay or benefits agreement made by October 30, 2025—the law applies when the agreement ends. A union contract can waive the law only if its combined pay, leave and benefits meet or beat the law's package.

You do not need to apply. DCWP posts the rates and enforces the law. You can complain to the department or sue. An employer that underpays can owe twice the underpayment as damages on top of back pay. Discipline within 90 days of exercising a protected right is presumed retaliation.

Named for Aland Etienne, a guard killed at 345 Park Avenue in July 2025, the law was introduced by Council Speaker Adrienne Adams and Council Member Crystal Hudson as prime and co-prime sponsors, with 32BJ SEIU's support. Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it after passage. In January 2026, new Speaker Julie Menin led the 46–5 override. A federal suit to invalidate the law remains pending; oral argument is set for December 8.
