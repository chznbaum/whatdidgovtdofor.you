Superstorm Sandy flooded the Lower East Side in 2012. The city is building a 2.4-mile flood barrier along the East River, from Montgomery Street to Asser Levy Playground. It uses floodwalls, berms, 18 gates, and a rebuilt East River Park on higher ground. It is designed for the storm expected once every 100 years by 2050 and can be raised another two feet later. More than 110,000 residents live behind it, including 28,000 in public housing. You don't need to apply.

The first section, from East 15th Street to Asser Levy Playground, cost $163 million. It was finished in October 2024, two months early and $10 million under budget. Full protection requires finishing the southern section. The city expects the system to come online in 2027.

The project costs $1.45 billion, including $335 million from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development; the city funds the rest. In November 2019, Council Members Carlina Rivera, Margaret Chin and Keith Powers negotiated an agreement with Mayor Bill de Blasio before the Council approved land-use changes. Land Use Chair Rafael Salamanca Jr. and Landmarks Subcommittee Chair Adrienne Adams sponsored the zoning approval resolution. The city agreed to reduce noise, test air and soil, make 30% of new hires low- or very-low-income residents or residents of HUD-assisted housing, plant 1,000 street trees and improve more than 17 nearby parks. Construction began in November 2020. DDC Commissioner Thomas Foley and Mayor Eric Adams's administration finished the first section in 2024.
