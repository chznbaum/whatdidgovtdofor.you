In New York City, your height and weight are now protected under the city's Human Rights Law, alongside traits like race, gender and disability. An employer with four or more workers can't refuse to hire you, pass you over for a promotion or harass you because of your body size. A landlord can't turn you away or give you worse lease terms, and a store, restaurant or other business open to the public can't refuse you service. Sometimes a business has to look for a practical fix instead, such as stocking uniforms in more sizes or offering other seating.

Narrow exceptions let employers use size criteria when a law requires it, core job duties leave no alternative, or normal operations reasonably need them. Public accommodations have similar exceptions; housing has none. The city says stereotypes and guesses about health or safety don't count as reasons. If you face this kind of bias, you can report it to the Commission on Human Rights at 212-416-0197 or online, and you can do it anonymously. Penalties run as high as $250,000 for willful discrimination.

Council Member Shaun Abreu introduced the bill in April 2022. The Council passed it 44–5 on May 11, 2023, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it on May 26. It took effect on November 26, 2023.
