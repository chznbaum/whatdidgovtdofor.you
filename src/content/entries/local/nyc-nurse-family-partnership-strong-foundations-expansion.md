If you're pregnant in New York City and are eligible for Medicaid or WIC, you can get a registered nurse who visits you through pregnancy and until your child turns 2. This is the city's Nurse-Family Partnership. It's free, and you can get it whatever your age or immigration status. Until this year it was mostly for first-time parents who signed up early in pregnancy. Now parents who already have a child can join, and so can people who enroll later in pregnancy. Ask your doctor, email nycnfp@health.nyc.gov, or call 311 to sign up.

The $20 million over three years also adds staff at the city's perinatal and early childhood mental health clinics. It pays for mental health certification for 20 practitioners a year and starts a fellowship for new graduates. Families also get supplies like pack and plays, diapers and toys. With increased staffing, the nurse program could serve up to 3,000 families, including up to 1,500 families newly eligible under the expansion.

ACS began researching the plan in January 2024 and developed it with the Health Department through May 2025, under ACS Commissioner Jess Dannhauser. Mayor Zohran Mamdani put the funding transfer in his preliminary budget and announced the initiative in February 2026. Health Commissioner Dr. Alister Martin's department rolled it out. The money comes from the child welfare agency's budget, where it had been set aside for an infant program that ended. Some family advocates worried the link could bring child welfare into people's homes. The agency said it has no role in running the program.
