If you own a one- or two-family home in much of New York City, you can now legally add one more apartment of up to 800 square feet. It can go in the attic, basement, cellar or garage, in an addition attached to the house, or in a small building in the backyard. An owner has to live on the property when the new apartment is first occupied, and the apartment needs its own entrance.

This law wrote the building and fire safety rules for these apartments. It requires sprinklers in most of them, fire separation from the main home, and their own controls for heat, power and gas. Basement and cellar units must have enough ways out, and they aren't allowed in areas at high risk of flooding. Backyard units are also barred in flood zones, historic districts and some low-density areas. You apply for a permit through the Department of Buildings, which started taking applications on Sept. 30, 2025. In 2026 the city added pre-approved designs. It also reopened Plus One ADU financing, which offered eligible homeowners up to $395,000 and technical help; that intake ran through June 12, 2026.

Council Member Pierina Ana Sanchez, who chairs the Housing and Buildings Committee, sponsored the bill. Mayor Eric Adams's administration asked for it as part of his City of Yes housing plan. The Council passed it on Dec. 5, 2024, the same day it approved the City of Yes zoning changes, and Adams signed it on Dec. 18. The law took effect June 16, 2025.
