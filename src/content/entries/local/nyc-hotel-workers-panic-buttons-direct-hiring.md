Every hotel in New York City now needs a city license. Core employees whose duties take them into occupied guest rooms must get panic buttons at no cost. Hotels must keep the front desk covered around the clock, although a trained security guard may substitute overnight. Hotels with more than 400 rooms need a security guard whenever a guest room is occupied, and core employees must receive human-trafficking recognition training. Occupied rooms must be cleaned daily unless the guest declines.

Hotels with 100 or more rooms generally must directly employ housekeeping, front desk and front-service workers instead of using third-party staffing. Some older contracts are temporarily exempt: fixed-term contracts until 30 days after they end, and contracts without a fixed end date until December 1, 2026. Workers do not have to apply. They can report violations or retaliation through 311 or nyc.gov/workers, including anonymously. Civil penalties run from $500 for a first violation to $5,000 for a fourth or later violation of the same offense within two years.

Council Member Julie Menin sponsored the Safe Hotels Act. Hotel and Gaming Trades Council members rallied, testified and urged Council members to pass it. The Council passed it 45–4, with one abstention, in October 2024, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it on November 4. Most provisions took effect May 3, 2025. A hotel-owner suit remains pending; it challenges parts of the city's implementing rules, not Local Law 104 itself.
