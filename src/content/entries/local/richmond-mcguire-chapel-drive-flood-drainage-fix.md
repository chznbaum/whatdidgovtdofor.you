Streets and yards in Richmond's McGuire, Chapel Drive and Hopkins Road neighborhoods flooded again and again because the drainage there couldn't handle heavy rain. This ordinance let the city accept a $1.1 million state grant and add it to the stormwater utility's budget to rebuild that drainage. Residents didn't have to apply for anything: the city did the work on the streets and the drainage system.

The money came from Virginia's Community Flood Preparedness Fund, which the state paid for with 45% of what it earned from selling carbon pollution allowances to power plants. The 2021 grant application estimated the full project at $11.5 million and said the city would cover the balance through its stormwater utility, funded by monthly fees on developed properties. Gov. Ralph Northam's administration announced the Richmond award in October 2021. It was one of 19 local projects that shared $7.8 million in the fund's first round.

Council Member Kristen Nye—then Kristen Larson—requested the 2021 resolution that directed city administrators to prepare a flood-fund application; she and Stephanie Lynch sponsored it. The Department of Public Utilities submitted the application. Mayor Levar Stoney then sponsored the ordinance accepting the award, with Ann-Frances Lambert as a co-patron. City Council adopted it on January 24, 2022. The city's stormwater program now lists the McGuire and Chapel Drive project as completed, and its completion notice is dated March 2026.
