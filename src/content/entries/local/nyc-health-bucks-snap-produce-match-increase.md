If you use SNAP at a New York City farmers market or farm stand that takes EBT, you get Health Bucks: coupons for fresh fruits and vegetables. For every $2 you spend with your EBT card, you get $2 in Health Bucks. Today the most you can get in one day is $10. Starting in January 2027, the limit rises to $14, which is 40% more.

You don't sign up separately. Use your EBT card at a participating market or stand and you get the coupons on the spot. Health Bucks work at more than 100 locations around the city, and the city expects to give out about $2.3 million in them in 2027. That money is also spent with local farmers and vendors.

The NYC Health Department has run Health Bucks since 2005 and has given out more than $20 million in coupons. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the higher limit in October 2026. City money and a new $5.7 million federal grant from the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Gus Schumacher Nutrition Incentive Program will pay for it.
