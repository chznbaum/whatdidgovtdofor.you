No. 4 heating oil is a thick, dirty fuel that some New York City boilers still burned for heat and hot water. Its smoke carries fine soot, sulfur and nitrogen pollution, which can worsen asthma and heart and lung disease. This law moved the city's general ban from Jan. 1, 2030 to July 1, 2027. Boilers used to generate electricity or steam had to stop by Jan. 1, 2025, and boilers at city properties and public schools by July 1, 2025. Remaining boilers face the July 1, 2027 deadline.

You don't have to do anything. The rules fall on building owners. Since June 30, 2024, the city has stopped issuing or renewing permits for boilers that burn No. 4 oil, and burning it after the applicable deadline brings fines of $2,500 to $10,000. Owners facing unreasonable hardship can petition for a variance, but it cannot run past Jan. 1, 2030. The city's free NYC Accelerator program helps owners switch fuels.

When the law was signed, about a quarter of public schools still burned No. 4 oil, and the city said about 72% of the buildings using it were in environmental justice communities. Compared with waiting until 2030, the city estimated the faster deadline would keep about 2 million pounds of fine-particle pollution out of the air.

Council Member James Gennaro sponsored the bill, and he said 40 Council colleagues signed on. The Council passed it on Feb. 16, 2023, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it on March 6, 2023.
