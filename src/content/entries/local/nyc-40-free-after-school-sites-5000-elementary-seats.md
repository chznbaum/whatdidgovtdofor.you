Starting in September 2025, 40 more New York City schools began offering free after-school programs for kids in kindergarten through fifth grade. The new sites added 5,000 seats: 37 at public schools and 3 at charter schools. By borough, the Bronx got 14 sites, Queens 12, Brooklyn 10, Manhattan 2 and Staten Island 2. The programs run after the school day ends, so working parents have a safe place for their kids to stay later, at no cost. Families apply through the program provider at their school, the school office or discoverDYCD.

Before the expansion, only about one in five elementary students could get a city after-school seat, compared with two in five middle schoolers. That's why the new seats went to younger kids.

Mayor Eric Adams proposed the After-School for All plan in April 2025. Deputy Mayor Ana J. Almanzar championed the expansion, calling it a top priority. The plan called for 20,000 new seats over three school years and for after-school spending to reach $755 million a year. The first $21 million, for these 5,000 seats, was in the city's 2026 budget. Finance Chair Justin Brannan sponsored the budget resolution, which the City Council passed 51–0 on June 30, 2025. The Department of Youth and Community Development, led by Commissioner Keith Howard, opened the sites. COMPASS remains free for families in the 2026–27 school year.
