If you lose your home in Orange County and end up at the Coalition for the Homeless of Central Florida's shelter, this money pays for the people whose job is to get you back into housing. The one-year contract, which started Oct. 1, 2026, pays for three housing specialists, two case managers and a part-time assistant director inside the shelter. They write housing plans with residents that cover problem-solving, money skills, job searching and staying housed once they move.

Any unhoused Orange County resident who needs shelter and help finding a home can use the program. On average, about 1,400 people or households enter the Coalition's shelter each year. The program puts the most vulnerable people first for shelter beds, and it aims to keep stays short by moving people into permanent homes. Its staff also connect people to other help and support the county's response to homeless encampments.

The Coalition is the region's largest shelter provider and has worked with the county since 2017, most recently under an earlier contract. The county pays the $522,614 from its general fund. On Aug. 25, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, approved the contract 7-0 on its consent agenda. It also gave the county authority to renew the contract for up to four more years.
