If you've survived domestic or gender-based violence in New York City and your household earns at or below 300% of the federal poverty guidelines, you can get a grant to help keep a safe place to live. The grant can cover rent and other housing costs, moving, transportation, legal help, medical bills, a phone, or a safety plan. You don't need documents or an order of protection, and your credit, criminal record, and immigration status don't count against you.

The money is part of Home+, a free city program run by a community nonprofit in each borough, with help to stay housed. Funds come as checks, gift cards, or payments made straight to a landlord or other vendor. From July 2024 through June 2025, 426 survivors got a grant. The average was $2,054 and the median $2,000.

Council Member Tiffany Cabán sponsored the law, inspired by a 2019 comptroller report that found domestic violence was the leading cause of family homelessness. Cabán introduced it with Shahana Hanif, Farah Louis, Diana Ayala, Sandra Ung, and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams. The Council passed it 44-6, and Mayor Eric Adams signed it in November 2022. The grants didn't start until July 2024. Advocates said the $1.2 million fiscal 2025 budget was not enough. The budget for fiscal 2027 added $1.4 million to the program's $2.3 million base.
