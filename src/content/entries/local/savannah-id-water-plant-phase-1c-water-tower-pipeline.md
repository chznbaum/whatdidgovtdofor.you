Savannah is rebuilding its I&D water plant, which opened in 1946. The plant treats river water and is the city's main source of drinking water for the growing region. Fast growth, both residential and industrial, along with state limits on pumping groundwater, has pushed demand well past what the old plant was built for. City staff call this the biggest upgrade in the plant's history.

This $65.5 million phase replaces a critical section of the pipeline that carries untreated river water to the plant. It also builds a new elevated water tower, rebuilds the basins where dirt settles out of the water, automates the filters, adds a system to manage dissolved oxygen, and finishes dredging a sludge pond. You don't need to do anything to benefit. The work is meant to keep clean water flowing reliably to the roughly 350,000 people the city system serves.

The City Council, with Mayor Van Johnson presiding, approved the work 9-0 on Sept. 10, 2026. An earlier $69.9 million phase began construction in January 2026. City staff say the project is paid for mainly through the Georgia Environmental Finance Authority, which is distributing a state regional water allocation through grants and zero-interest loans. Savannah's share is $146 million.
