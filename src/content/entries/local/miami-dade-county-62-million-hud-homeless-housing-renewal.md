Miami-Dade County accepted $62.5 million from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development. The money comes from HUD's Continuum of Care program, which pays for housing and support services for people who are homeless. The vote lets the county mayor take in and spend the money, sign renewal grants, and pass funds through sub-grants to the groups that run the programs. If more federal money becomes available, the county can take that too.

These are renewals, so the money keeps existing programs running instead of starting new ones. The county passes funds to nonprofit providers and select cities that operate the projects; the resolution does not create a separate application for residents. Most renewal periods were already underway by Nov. 16, 2026, while eight listed projects start Dec. 1.

The renewals followed a national fight. HUD tried to rewrite the rules for this funding in late 2025. A federal court put that on hold, and in February 2026 Congress passed a spending law requiring HUD to renew expiring grants for 12 months. HUD announced the renewal awards in rounds from March to May 2026. Commissioner Marleine Bastien sponsored the resolution, and the commission passed it 12-0 on July 21, 2026, with Commissioner Hardemon excused. The county waived its usual requirement that non-county parties sign agreements before board approval, allowing the subrecipient agreements to be finished after the vote.
