Apartment and hallway doors in New York City buildings with three or more apartments must swing shut and latch by themselves, so a fire's smoke stays in one place. In January 2022, a door in the Bronx's Twin Parks building failed to close. Smoke spread through the building and killed 17 people, eight of them children.

This law has the city look for broken doors instead of waiting for complaints. Each year, the Department of Housing Preservation and Development picks 300 apartment buildings that look most at risk, based on complaints, past violations, and Fire Department data. Inspectors check the doors in hallways and common areas, plus apartment doors they can get to. A door that won't close and latch is the most serious class of violation, and the owner has 14 days from receiving the violation notice to fix it. If the owner doesn't, the city can make the repair, bill the owner, and seek fines in Housing Court. The agency also has to publish a yearly report listing each building it inspected and what it found. Tenants don't need to do anything to be covered, and anyone can report a bad door by calling 311.

Council Member Nantasha Williams sponsored the bill, and the Council passed it 50–0 on June 2, 2022, four months after the fire. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in July 2022. The inspection rules took effect in September 2023.
