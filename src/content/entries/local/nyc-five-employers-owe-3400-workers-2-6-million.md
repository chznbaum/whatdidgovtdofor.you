Five employers agreed to pay more than $2.6 million in restitution to New York City workers after breaking city workplace laws. The settlements cover more than 3,400 workers, and each company must follow the laws from now on.

Most of it comes from three stores that broke the city's Fair Workweek Law. Burlington Coat Factory owes more than $1.3 million to 865 workers at one Bronx store. The company didn't give workers their schedules 72 hours ahead, added hours without asking, and used on-call shifts. Hot Topic owes more than $135,000 to 64 workers at one Staten Island store for late schedules, added hours and shifts canceled on short notice. Starbucks owes more than $112,000 to 48 workers at one Brooklyn store, partly for failing to pay premium pay. Two other employers broke the Paid Safe and Sick Leave Law by not letting workers use their sick time. The home care company Elara Caring owes more than $913,000 to more than 2,200 workers, and Tavern on the Green owes more than $150,000 to 301 workers.

The city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection investigated all five employers and won the settlements under Commissioner Vilda Vera Mayuga. The companies also owe the city civil penalties.
