Peeling lead paint is one of the main ways young children get lead poisoning. In 2018, nearly 4,000 New York City children were found to have elevated blood lead levels. This law makes the city look for paint hazards where young kids spend their days. Public schools and day care sites must survey rooms used by young children every year, and city schools must check those classrooms at least three times a year. The results are posted online and sent to every child's parent or guardian. You don't have to ask for them.

The law also has a rule for renters. Landlords in older buildings must do lead paint work when a unit changes tenants, including removing lead paint from places where doors and windows rub. If a city inspector finds that this work wasn't done for a tenancy that began after August 2004, the city must issue a violation. A landlord who can't produce records of the work is presumed to have skipped it and can be fined up to $1,500.

Council Member Margaret Chin introduced the bill in May 2018. The Council passed it with a package of lead bills on January 23, 2020, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on February 11, 2020. It took effect a year later.
