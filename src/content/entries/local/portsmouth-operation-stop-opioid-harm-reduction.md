Portsmouth runs a harm-reduction program that aims to keep people who use drugs alive and connect them to care. It has a protocol for responding to overdose "hot spots," a syringe exchange and a mobile harm-reduction unit, and it refers people for STI and hepatitis testing. Under the grant rules, the money can also pay for naloxone, the overdose-reversal drug, and for test strips that detect fentanyl and xylazine.

The program is paid for by Operation STOP! (Specifically Targeted Overdose Prevention), a state grant for seven cities and counties where opioid overdose deaths disproportionately affected Black residents. Portsmouth was one of those localities. From 2019 to 2023, 172 Black residents died of opioid overdoses, and the death rate rose 300%.

The Virginia Opioid Abatement Authority, which manages a large share of the state's settlements with opioid makers and distributors, awarded Portsmouth $500,000 in June 2025 as a single grant that could cover up to two performance periods. For fiscal 2027, the authority approved Portsmouth's request to carry forward the unused $500,000 balance; it did not award new money. On Sept. 22, 2026, Portsmouth City Council voted 6-0 to accept and appropriate that carryforward to the city's behavioral health budget for the fiscal year ending in June 2027. The money comes from opioid settlements, not local taxes.
