New York City is lowering the speed limit to 15 mph on streets at its schools. This year, about 700 school locations that already had 20 mph limits are dropping to 15 mph, and about 100 locations now at 25 mph are getting new 15 mph zones. By September 2026, more than 200 of these zones were in place. The goal is every eligible location, about 2,300 sites holding roughly 3,200 public, private, parochial and charter schools, by the end of 2029.

You don't have to apply for anything. The Department of Transportation picks locations based on crash data and other planned street work, and it gives the local community board 60 days' notice before each change. At the most dangerous spots, it's also adding speed humps and other physical fixes. The city says a person hit at 25 mph is more than three times as likely to be seriously hurt as one hit at 15 mph.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced this school-zone rollout on March 16, 2026, and Transportation Commissioner Mike Flynn's department is installing it. The administration calls the effort an expansion of Sammy's Law, named for 12-year-old Sammy Cohen Eckstein, who was killed by a driver in Brooklyn in 2013. But New York already allowed NYC to set 15 mph school speed limits before that 2024 law. Sammy's Law instead broadened the city's authority to set 20 mph general limits and 10 mph traffic-calmed slow zones.
