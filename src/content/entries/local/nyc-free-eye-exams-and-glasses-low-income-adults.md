If you're an adult in New York City and money is tight, you can get your eyes checked for free and, if the exam shows you need them, a free pair of glasses. A 2023 city law directed the Health Department to set up the program. It covers residents 18 and older whose household income is at or below 250% of the federal poverty level, and it has to offer each person a test at least once every four years.

The program, called Vision for All, opened in June 2025. The Health Department runs it with Community Healthcare Network, a group of nonprofit health centers. It offers mobile eye care vans and fixed sites in four boroughs, and appointments are booked through Community Healthcare Network. A November 2025 report said it serves people who are uninsured, on Medicaid, or earning up to 250% of the poverty level, about $39,125 a year for one person. By then, more than 200 New Yorkers had been helped.

Council Member Justin Brannan of Brooklyn led the effort. In 2022 he asked for budget money for a free mobile eye care pilot, and he introduced the bill that March. The full Council passed it on June 8, 2023. Brannan said no one should be too poor to see. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in July 2023. The law says the program runs only as long as the city funds it.
