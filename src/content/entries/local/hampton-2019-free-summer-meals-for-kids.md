In summer 2019, Hampton served free meals through the federal Summer Food Service Program. Federal rules covered every child 18 and under. People over 18 also qualified if a state or local education agency determined that they had a disability and they attended a public or nonprofit private school program for people with disabilities. The grant period ran from April 1 through Sept. 1, 2019.

The city's Department of Parks, Recreation and Leisure Services ran sites around Hampton. At an open site, families did not have to prove each child's income eligibility. Instead, the site qualified using local data showing that at least half of children in the area were eligible for free or reduced-price school meals. Hampton expected more than 2,500 children to take part.

USDA funded the program's direct costs, while the parks department supplied coordination and other indirect costs. The council accepted and appropriated an initial $320,983.66, with no local cash match required; the resolution said the final award could increase or decrease as participation changed. Hampton has received money from the program since 1969. On Aug. 14, 2019, the City Council, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, voted 7–0 to accept the grant as part of its consent agenda.
