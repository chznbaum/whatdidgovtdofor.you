Sometimes you have to break a lease because of a new job, a family emergency, or rent you can no longer afford. If you vacate a New York City apartment in violation of your lease, this law limits what your landlord can recover to get the unit ready for the next tenant. The amount cannot exceed the fair market cost of that work, and your landlord must give you an itemized list showing how it was calculated.

You do not have to apply. The protection applies to residential leases entered into on or after June 22, 2022. A 2019 state law already requires landlords to take reasonable steps to re-rent a unit instead of leaving it empty and charging the former tenant rent for the rest of the lease. But that state law did not cap fees for preparing the unit to rent again. This city law closes that gap. You may still owe lost rent until a replacement tenant's lease begins, but the landlord has to try to re-rent the unit.

Council Member Kevin Riley introduced the bill in May 2021, joined by Council Members Keith Powers, Mark Treyger and Helen Rosenthal. The Council approved it 46-2 on November 23, 2021. Mayor Bill de Blasio returned it unsigned, and it became law on December 24, 2021.
