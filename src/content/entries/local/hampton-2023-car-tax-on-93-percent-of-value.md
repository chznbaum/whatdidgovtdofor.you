Hampton taxes cars each year based on their fair market value, generally using a recognized pricing guide. After the pandemic, shortages of workers, parts and computer chips reduced the supply of new cars and pushed up demand and prices for used ones. For the 2023 tax year, Hampton applied a 93% assessment ratio to most cars and trucks under 2 tons instead of using their full fair market value. The tax rate stayed at $4.50 per $100 of assessed value. For example, a car valued at $20,000 had a tax base of $18,600, producing a tax of $837 before state car tax relief instead of $900.

You didn't have to apply. Commissioner of the Revenue Ross Mugler recommended the ratio and his office applied it when setting the June and December 2023 bills. The relief covered only the 2023 tax year. It was smaller than in 2022, when Hampton used a 75% ratio, because vehicle values had begun to fall but remained above 2021 levels.

City Council approved the resolution on March 22, 2023, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding. Hampton estimated the ratio would provide $2.4 million in relief and incorporated the lower revenue into its FY24 budget. The city said strong revenue growth allowed it to provide tax relief without cutting services; the approved budget also included 5% staff raises, a $15 minimum wage for city employees and 7.4% more local funding for schools.
