Lead can harm a young child's brain and growth for life, and much of it comes from old paint and dust in older homes. Elizabeth is getting $335,147 from the New Jersey Department of Health to keep its childhood lead poisoning prevention program going for a full year, from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027.

State grants like this one pay local health departments to screen children's blood for lead. When a child's level comes back high, a nurse helps the family and inspectors look for the source of the lead in the home. New Jersey now flags lower lead levels than before, so health workers can step in earlier. Families don't apply for this money. Help comes through the city's health services when a child's test shows elevated lead.

The City Council voted 9-0 on June 9, 2026 to let city officials apply for the grant and accept it if awarded. Council President Manny Grova Jr. presided over the meeting. The state pays for the program, not Elizabeth taxpayers.
