In New York City, landlords must keep apartments warm from October through May. This program targets buildings with repeat heat violations. HPD selects 50 apartment buildings each year using violations over the previous two years and whether it received heat complaints from more than one apartment. Owners must install an internet-connected temperature sensor in one living room of each apartment by Oct. 1, unless a tenant opts out. Each sensor measures the temperature at least once an hour, and owners must send readings to HPD every month during heat season. From Oct. 1 through Jan. 31, HPD inspects each selected building at least twice a month without waiting for a complaint. Inspections continue after Jan. 31 for buildings with open inadequate-heat or heat-sensor violations.

Tenants don't need to apply. If your building is selected, HPD posts a notice and the owner must notify you. You can choose which living room gets the sensor or refuse one in writing. The owner cannot charge you for acquiring or installing it. Buildings stay in the program for up to four years unless they meet HPD's rules for an early discharge.

Council Member Ritchie Torres introduced the original citywide sensor proposal in 2016 in partnership with Brooklyn Borough President Eric Adams. Torres introduced the narrower repeat-violator bill in 2018. The Council passed it on Dec. 19, 2019, and it became law on Jan. 19, 2020, without Mayor Bill de Blasio's signature. Council Member Pierina Sanchez sponsored a 2023 law that, beginning in July 2024, changed selection from 50 buildings every two years to 50 every year and required owners to submit sensor data to HPD during every 30-day period of heat season.
