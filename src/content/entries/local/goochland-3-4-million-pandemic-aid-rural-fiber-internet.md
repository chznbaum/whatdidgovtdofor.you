In large parts of rural Goochland, homes and businesses couldn't get high-speed internet. In 2021, county planning counted 2,765 locations without at least basic broadband speeds. On March 1, 2022, the Board of Supervisors put $3,437,500 from the federal American Rescue Plan into the county's capital fund for broadband expansion. That way, local taxes didn't have to cover the county's share of the fiber build.

Goochland is one of 13 Central Virginia counties working with Firefly Fiber Broadband, an electric co-op's internet company, on its Regional Internet Service Expansion (RISE) project. The project also uses state grants, along with lines from Dominion Energy and Rappahannock Electric Cooperative. In late 2021, the county pledged up to $3,637,500 toward the build. You don't apply for the county money: once Firefly's fiber reaches your road, you sign up for service with Firefly. Firefly is waiving the $100 installation fee in areas still being connected.

Susan Lascolette and Neil Spoonhower had publicly called broadband the county's top priority, and Lascolette moved the funding resolution. The board approved it 5-0 after a public hearing, with Chair Spoonhower presiding. Firefly now lists most Goochland areas as in service. Several areas are still being connected, and Maidens is set to start getting connections in fall 2026.
