If you ride the bus on Rice Street or Robert Street, a faster line is on the way. The 11.5-mile METRO G Line will run from Little Canada Transit Station through downtown Saint Paul to the Dakota County Northern Service Center in West St. Paul. It will become the corridor's main service. Route 62 is expected to be discontinued when the full line opens, while Route 68 will continue on a restructured route. Buses will run frequently all day and most of the evening.

Riders will pay at the station before the bus arrives and can board through any door. Buses will get priority at traffic lights and make fewer stops. Stations will be well lit, with security cameras, emergency phones, real-time departure screens, benches and bike parking. Low-floor buses will have ramps for people using mobility devices. You do not need to sign up; you just ride.

The council minutes credit Transportation Committee Chair Deb Barber's leadership in seeing the project through. She recused herself from the contract vote. On Aug. 26, 2026, the Metropolitan Council approved a contract of up to $20.7 million with Meyer Contracting. Station construction is underway at Rice and Maryland, Rice and Cook, and Rice and Front. Phase 1 is expected to open in late 2027 and phase 2 in late 2028. At contract approval, the project had a $104 million authorized budget: $50 million from the state, $33 million from federal funds and $21 million from local and regional funds.
