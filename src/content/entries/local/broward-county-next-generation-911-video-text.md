Broward County is replacing the network under its 911 system with a Next Generation 911 platform that runs over the internet. The upgrade will let dispatchers receive photos and live video from a caller's phone, alongside voice and text, and use geospatial routing for more accurate location. It will serve the county's regional and nonregional 911 answering points.

You don't need to sign up for a separate program. These changes are part of the 911 infrastructure. The county's current project page says kickoff and planning are underway and lists Q3 2027 as the implementation date.

The County Commission approved the Motorola agreement unanimously on June 9, 2026, with Mayor Mark Bogen presiding. The contract allows up to $12.23 million for the initial five-year term before optional services and up to $25.68 million over 10 years if renewed; the ten-year ceiling includes $2 million for optional services. The county says the agreement will use money allocated from Florida's state-administered E911 fee.
