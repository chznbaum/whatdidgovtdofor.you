Minneapolis accepted $2,538,028 in federal money to help residents find work and build job skills. The money comes from the U.S. Department of Labor under the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act and passes through Minnesota's Department of Employment and Economic Development. It comes as three grants: $891,971 for adults, $457,402 for workers who were laid off, and $1,188,655 for young people.

The city's employment and training office, part of its Community Planning and Economic Development department, runs these programs, and they are free for Minneapolis residents. They offer career counseling, skills checks, job training and certifications, workshops, and help with resumes. Laid-off workers can qualify if they lost their job through no fault of their own, are eligible for unemployment benefits, and are unlikely to go back to their old job or field. Staff check eligibility when you apply.

Council Member Jamal Osman is listed as the author of the item. The Business, Housing & Zoning Committee approved it on June 16, 2026, and the full City Council adopted it 12-0 on June 25. Council Member Jamison Whiting was absent. Mayor Jacob Frey signed it on June 30.
