When you order food or groceries through a delivery app in New York City, the app must offer you a way to tip the worker. At least one selectable tip option must be 10% or more of the price of your order, and you can also enter your own amount. A companion law makes apps show the tip option before or when you place the order, not afterward. The rules cover apps like DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub and Instacart, and they started January 26, 2026. Workers don't have to apply.

The law answers what happened after the city set a minimum pay rate for delivery workers in 2023. Some apps moved the tip option to after checkout and raised their fees. Bloomberg analyzed 2024 data the companies gave the city and found customers tipped 64% less. By mid-2025, tips had fallen from about half of workers' hourly earnings to 13%. The city's consumer and worker protection agency says these design tricks cost workers more than $550 million in tips.

Council Member Shaun Abreu sponsored the bill, and 20 colleagues signed on as cosponsors. The Council passed it on July 14, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams returned it unsigned, and it became law on August 13, 2025. DoorDash and Uber sued, saying the rule violates their free speech rights. So far, the courts have refused to block it, but the companies' appeal is still pending.
