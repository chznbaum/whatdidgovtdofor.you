When someone in Minneapolis calls 911 about a mental health crisis and no weapon is involved, dispatchers can send unarmed behavioral crisis responders instead of police, or with them. The teams work 24 hours a day. They talk people through the crisis and offer a ride to care, but only if the person wants one. In August, the city added $2.23 million to the contract with Canopy Roots, the Minneapolis company that runs the teams, and extended it through the end of 2026.

You don't need to sign up or apply. Call 911 in an emergency. For a crisis that isn't an emergency, call 612-348-2345. The program takes an average of about 10,000 calls a year that would otherwise go to police.

Minneapolis started the program in 2021, after George Floyd's killing. Council President Elliott Payne presided over the 10–0 vote on Aug. 13, 2026. The amendment had cleared the Public Health, Safety & Equity Committee, chaired by Jason Chavez, on Aug. 5. Mayor Jacob Frey signed it the same day as the vote. The city is still deciding who runs the program after 2026. On Nov. 12, the council approved a 10-month procurement waiver for another Canopy Roots extension while it considers in-house or county-partnership models; the waiver itself did not create a 2027 contract. A city audit found weak oversight of the contract's billing.
