If you rent in a New York City apartment building, the owner must get the last four years of rents for your apartment from the state housing agency, NYS Homes and Community Renewal, and give them to you. The state keeps these records when owners register rents each year, which mostly happens for rent-stabilized apartments. When the state has no record for a unit, the rule doesn't apply. You don't have to apply for anything: the duty is on the owner. The state's online system lets owners print certified rent histories to meet the requirement.

The rent history shows what past tenants were charged. That lets you check whether a big jump in your rent is legal or whether you may be overpaying. If you think you're being overcharged, you can file a complaint with the state. You can also ask the state for your own apartment's history at any time.

Council Member Mark Levine introduced the bill in November 2018. He said too many tenants didn't know their legal rent and were overpaying landlords by hundreds or thousands of dollars a year. The City Council passed it on May 8, 2019, as one of 17 tenant protection bills. Council Members Brad Lander, Alicka Ampry-Samuel, Rory Lancman, Diana Ayala, Ben Kallos, Helen Rosenthal and Carlina Rivera cosponsored it. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in June 2019. It took effect 120 days later.
