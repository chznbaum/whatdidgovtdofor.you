If you survived gender-motivated violence in New York City, such as a sexual assault, before Jan. 9, 2022, you can file a civil lawsuit now even if the old deadline passed. The window opened on Jan. 29, 2026 and runs for 18 months, to about late July 2027. You can sue the person who attacked you. You can also sue anyone who directed, enabled, took part in, or conspired in the violence, such as an institution that covered for an abuser. A court can award damages, attorney's fees, and other relief.

The city first opened a lookback window from 2023 to 2025. In 2025, courts read that law narrowly, and hundreds of people who said they were sexually abused in city juvenile detention had their cases thrown out. If you filed a claim between March 1, 2023 and March 1, 2025, the new law lets you amend or refile it.

Majority Whip Selvena Brooks-Powers sponsored the fix, and the City Council passed it 48–0 in November 2025. Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it on Dec. 24, 2025, citing what lawyers' fees would cost the city. The Council overrode his veto on Jan. 29, 2026.

The new window remains in effect, but there is an active legal threat. The New York Court of Appeals is deciding whether state revival laws preempt the city's earlier window. In August 2026, a state court said that ruling may control whether claims under the 2026 law can proceed and stayed one restored claim. The Court of Appeals had not ruled as of Nov. 13, 2026.
