New York City's rent freeze programs let older and disabled tenants in rent-regulated apartments keep paying the same rent even when the landlord raises it. The city gives the landlord a property tax credit to cover the difference. The Senior Citizen Rent Increase Exemption (SCRIE) is for tenants 62 and older. The Disability Rent Increase Exemption (DRIE) is for tenants 18 and older who get disability benefits such as SSI or SSDI. In both programs, the household must spend more than a third of its income on rent.

In 2014 the city raised the household income limit to $50,000. For seniors, that was up from $29,000. The higher limit was due to expire in June 2020. This law extended it through June 30, 2022, and applied back to July 1, 2020, so the higher limit legally covered the gap after the prior authorization expired. Tenants generally apply through the city's Department of Finance. Seniors in certain HPD-supervised Mitchell-Lama, Redevelopment Company, and HDFC housing apply for SCRIE through HPD.

Council Member Margaret Chin, who sponsored the 2014 increase, was the bill's prime sponsor, and Mayor Bill de Blasio's administration asked for it. The Council passed it on October 15, 2020, and it became law on November 15 without the mayor's signature. Later state laws kept the $50,000 ceiling in place through June 2026. On October 22, 2026, the Council passed a bill to raise it to $75,000 retroactive to July 1, but as of November 20 that bill was still awaiting mayoral action and the city's eligibility page still listed $50,000.
