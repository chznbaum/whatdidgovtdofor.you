When cars park right up to a corner, drivers turning in can't see someone about to step off the curb, and parked cars can hide a child completely. Daylighting fixes this by clearing the parking spaces next to the crosswalk. A New York City law requires the Department of Transportation to daylight at least 100 intersections every year, starting in 2025. Where the department finds barriers practical and useful, it must also add them, such as planters or bollards, so drivers can't park there anyway. DOT has to report each year on where it added or removed daylighting. You don't need to do anything to benefit.

The law also made DOT study whether daylighting works. In its January 2025 study, DOT found that daylighting with physical barriers reduced pedestrian injuries. Daylighting marked only by signs showed no clear benefit. DOT can end the yearly requirement on or after Jan. 1, 2030, but only if it finds that more daylighting wouldn't make streets meaningfully safer.

Council Member Selvena Brooks-Powers, who chaired the Council's transportation committee, sponsored the bill. Council Members Julie Won and Tiffany Cabán co-sponsored it and joined Brooks-Powers in pressing for daylighting along Newtown Road after 7-year-old Dolma Naadhun was killed at an intersection there in 2023. DOT first opposed the bill. It supported the bill once the start date moved to 2025 and barriers were added. The Council passed it 40–7 in April 2023, and it became law that May without Mayor Eric Adams's signature.
