Minneapolis is helping convert part of the historic Grain Exchange, the office complex at 4th Street and 4th Avenue South near City Hall, into 232 apartments. Under the city's plan, 210 of them will be for households earning no more than 60% of the area median income, and the other 22 will rent at market rates. The developer, Sherman Associates, calls it a $120 million project that will also keep office, retail and event space in the buildings.

The City Council approved three pieces of financing. It authorized up to $39 million in tax-exempt housing revenue bonds for the project. It adopted a tax increment financing plan that creates a housing district for the site. And it approved a pay-as-you-go note of up to $1.775 million, which is paid from the new property taxes the project brings in.

The apartments still have to be built. When they open, renters will need to meet the income limits to get one of the 210 income-capped units.

Council Member Jamal Osman is listed as the author of the measure. The Business, Housing & Zoning Committee approved it on July 7, 2026, and the full council passed it 12–0 on July 16, with Council Member Aisha Chughtai absent. Mayor Jacob Frey signed it on July 23.
