If you're a parent in Richmond with a low income and no car, and your job is somewhere the bus doesn't go, the city's Free Rides to Work program can drive you there for free. A van picks you up at home and takes you to work or school. Most trips stay within about 20 miles, reaching places like Short Pump, Midlothian and the West End.

To qualify, you need at least one dependent child, an income under 200% of the federal poverty level, and eligibility for TANF, the federal-state cash assistance program for families. You sign up through the city's online form or by calling 804-646-3513 to join the waitlist. Demand has been high: about 400 people applied after the program launched, and about 50 were riding in early 2025.

Richmond started the program in 2022 under Mayor Levar Stoney. It's run by the city's Office of Equitable Transit and Mobility, and local van companies drive the routes. All of the money comes from grants from the Virginia Transit Association, which passes along federal TANF funds, so city taxpayers don't pay to match it. Mayor Danny Avula's administration asked the council to accept a new $275,000 grant, and the council approved it 8–0 on April 27, 2026.
