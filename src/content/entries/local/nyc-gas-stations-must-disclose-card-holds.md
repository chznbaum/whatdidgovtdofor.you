When you pay at the pump, a gas station often freezes more money on your card than you actually spend, sometimes more than $100, until the sale settles. On a debit or benefit card, that can tie up money you need for days. New York City now requires every gas station that sells fuel for cars or boats to post a notice where you pay. The notice must say whether the station places these holds, and if it does, when the hold starts, how much it is, and how you can avoid it.

You don't have to do anything. The notice should be posted where you pay, so you can choose another way to pay before you fill up.

Council Member Selvena Brooks-Powers sponsored the bill in September 2024. At a hearing in February 2025, she described a constituent who bought $20 of gas with a benefit card and got a $150 hold that lasted three days. The hold didn't lift until after the benefits had expired, so the money was lost. The City Council passed an amended version 48–0 on November 25, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign or veto it, so it became law on December 25, 2025, and took effect 180 days later.
