For decades, most of the 42 blocks between West 23rd and West 40th streets, from Fifth to Eighth avenues, were zoned for industry, and new homes largely were not allowed. The Midtown South plan changes that. Builders can now put up apartments there and turn old office buildings into homes. The city expects more than 9,500 new homes over time.

The plan maps Mandatory Inclusionary Housing across the area for the first time. Most projects with more than 10 homes or more than 12,500 square feet of residential space must make 20% to 30% of that space permanently affordable; some projects of up to 25 homes may pay into an affordable housing fund instead. More than 2,800 affordable homes are expected. When affordable rental apartments are built, eligible renters generally apply through the city's housing lottery. The agreement also includes a commitment to create a car-free busway on 34th Street and more than $120 million to support garment businesses.

Council Members Keith Powers and Erik Bottcher, who represented the area, ushered the rezoning through the Council. Mayor Eric Adams's administration and the Department of City Planning, led by Dan Garodnick, developed and formally proposed the plan. Land Use Chair Rafael Salamanca Jr. and Zoning Subcommittee Chair Kevin Riley sponsored the two approval resolutions. The Council approved them 44–0 on August 14, 2025.
