Hampton accepted $1,499,000 in federal and state money to resurface six worn stretches of city roads. The biggest share, $421,000, covers Wythe Creek Road from Commander Shepard Boulevard to the Back River bridge. Three sections of Shell Road get a combined $611,000, covering the stretch from Melville Road to LaSalle Avenue. Mallory Street from Sargeant Street to Seaboard Avenue gets $266,000, and $201,000 covers a 0.31-mile stretch of Power Plant Parkway south of Queen Street.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. Anyone who drives, rides the bus, or bikes on these roads gets a smoother surface once the work is done.

The money comes from Virginia's State of Good Repair program. Under state law, the program pays to rebuild failing bridges and repair worn pavement on major roads, including main roads that cities maintain themselves. The Commonwealth Transportation Board set the year's funding split in June 2023, and Hampton applied for and won money for these six projects. On Sept. 27, 2023, the City Council, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, voted 7-0 to accept the money, add it to the city's capital budget, and ask VDOT to schedule the projects.
