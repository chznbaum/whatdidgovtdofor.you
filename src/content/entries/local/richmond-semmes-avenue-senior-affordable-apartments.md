Commonwealth Catholic Charities plans a three-story building at 1903-1905 Semmes Ave., just south of the Lee Bridge. It will have 20 one-bedroom apartments for seniors above a ground-floor shop, plus a community room. Every apartment will have an income limit. Two are for households earning up to 40% of the Richmond-area median income, eight for those up to 50%, and ten for those up to 60%.

Richmond's grant works as a tax refund. Once the building opens, the city will give back the increase in property taxes that the new building brings. The first-year grant is estimated at $84,824, but the actual amount will depend on the property's assessment when the grant begins. It is paid only if the rents stay affordable and the building is kept up. The grant lasts 30 years. It uses new property-tax revenue the project creates, not existing city funds; the tradeoff is that Richmond gives up that future revenue during the grant period.

The site was surplus city land. The city passed it to the Maggie Walker Community Land Trust, which picked Commonwealth Catholic Charities to build there. Mayor Danny Avula sent the grant agreement to City Council, which adopted it on March 24, 2025. The nonprofit won tax credits for the project, but as of November 2026 it still listed the development as being planned.
