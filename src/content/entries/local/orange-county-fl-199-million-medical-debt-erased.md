Orange County pays a nonprofit, Undue Medical Debt, to buy up residents' old medical bills from hospitals, providers and collection agencies and then cancel them. Bought in bulk, the debt often costs a penny or less on the dollar. In October 2026, the county's fourth round erased $199 million in medical debt for more than 112,000 residents. Since 2024, the program has cleared $758.2 million in medical debt for about 429,000 people.

You can't apply, and you don't need to do anything. Your debt can qualify if your household earns up to 400% of the federal poverty level (about $100,000 a year for a family of three) or if it equals at least 5% of your household income. People whose debt is canceled get a letter in an envelope from Undue Medical Debt, and they owe no taxes on the canceled debt.

The program began with a 2023 campaign by Central Florida Jobs With Justice, a coalition of labor, faith and student groups. Commissioners approved the first $4.5 million in August 2024. On Aug. 4, 2026, with Vice Mayor Kelly Martinez Semrad chairing the meeting, the board added $2 million and extended the contract through October. The money is unspent federal American Rescue Plan funding that had to be used by the end of 2026, so no county services were cut to pay for it.
