Virginia Beach voters authorized the city in 2021 to borrow up to $567.5 million for 21 flood protection projects. The plan was intended to compress work that could have taken roughly 40 years into about a decade. At the time of the vote, the higher real estate taxes expected to repay the bonds were estimated at $10 to $14 a month for an average-priced home.

The projects include tide gates, pump stations, larger drainage pipes, deeper canals and new places to store stormwater. Residents do not apply for anything; protection reaches each area as its projects are completed.

By late 2023, the city had finished a small retention pond and drainage work in Windsor Woods, new Lake Pleasure House outfall pipes and preparatory drainage work at Cape Henry. The Windsor Woods tide gate, which can block high tides and create storage space in Lake Windsor before storms, was completed in December 2025. Major construction remains ahead, and the city’s current capital plan schedules some work beyond the original 10-year goal.

The City Council unanimously put the borrowing question on the ballot. Mayor Bobby Dyer publicly supported it and directed city staff to draft the referendum. Voters approved it with 72.72% of the vote.
