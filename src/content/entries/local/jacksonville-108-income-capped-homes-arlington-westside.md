Jacksonville is lending $3.8 million to help build 108 homes for renters with lower incomes. They are going up on three empty lots. In Arlington, 35 small one-bedroom apartments are on Tracy Road and 35 more are on Justina Court, near Jacksonville University. On the Westside, 38 three-bedroom townhomes are on Ricker Road, off 103rd Street. The city funds the loan after substantial completion. The developer then makes interest-only payments at 1% for 20 years, with the principal balance due at the end.

The homes are for households earning no more than 60% of the area median income, roughly $45,000 for one person or $65,000 for a family of four. They must stay at that level for 50 years. The developer expects rents of about $800 for a one-bedroom and about $1,400 for a three-bedroom. Parkwood Commons South on Tracy Road began accepting applications in November for an expected February 2027 opening. You apply through the property manager; the other sites are expected to be completed in 2027.

Council members Ken Amaro and Tyrona Clark-Murray introduced the ordinance, and Ju'Coby Pittman co-sponsored it. The council approved it 13-1 on June 9, 2026, with Mike Gay voting no. The city loan sits alongside federal housing tax credits, bonds issued through the Jacksonville Housing Finance Authority, and other loans that together pay for the project.
