If you sell food or goods on a New York City sidewalk, a broken vending rule no longer counts as a misdemeanor. Before this law, setting up too far from the curb could bring a fine of up to $500 and up to 30 days in jail. Now those where-and-how rules carry a civil fine of up to $250. Vending without a license used to be a misdemeanor that could mean three months in jail. It's now a violation, a lesser offense, with a fine of up to $250 and no jail time; separate civil penalties can reach $1,000 plus daily penalties.

That matters because a misdemeanor conviction creates a criminal record. A record can stand in the way of a job, housing, school or an immigration case, and nearly all of the city's street vendors are immigrants. Police wrote 3,662 criminal vending summonses in 2025. Vendors don't need to apply; the change applies to tickets written since March 9, 2026. Police kept writing criminal tickets after the law took effect. Unlicensed vending may still be charged as a violation, but later reporting found at least 32 tickets under rules whose criminal penalties the law removed. Krishnan said he would investigate the NYPD's protocol.

Krishnan wrote the bill, and the Street Vendor Project, a vendors' organization, pushed for it for years. The City Council passed it 40–8 in June 2025. Mayor Eric Adams vetoed it that July, and the Council overrode his veto with 35 votes on September 10, 2025.
