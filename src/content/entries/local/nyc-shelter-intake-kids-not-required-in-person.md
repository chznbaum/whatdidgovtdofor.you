If your family has children and needs a New York City shelter, the Department of Homeless Services can't make your kids come in person when you apply or reapply. The children can be seen by video instead. Families can still bring them in if they want to.

There are two exceptions. The city can require a child to come in if staff ask to see the child by video and can't within 24 hours. It can also require it if the child hasn't checked into the assigned shelter by curfew the day after placement. You don't have to request this protection. It applies to every family that goes through intake.

The change matters because intake takes a long time. DHS officials told the Council that families spend six to seven hours on average at the Bronx intake center, which is hard on small children. The city had let children appear remotely during the pandemic. DHS said it already exempted children from the first intake meeting, but this law makes the protection permanent and extends it to reapplications.

Deputy Speaker Diana Ayala sponsored the bill and has talked about waiting for hours at an intake center with her own young child. The Council passed it 44–0 in August 2024. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in September 2024.
