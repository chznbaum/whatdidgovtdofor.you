Streets in East Flagami, on the western side of Miami, flood during heavy rain. The city is fixing that with new and larger storm drains and pipes, underground trenches that let water soak into the ground, and a stormwater pump station at NW 37th Avenue and NW 4th Street. Crews are also rebuilding and repaving streets and adding curb ramps for people with disabilities. The work covers streets in Flagami and Auburndale between roughly NW 2nd and NW 7th streets.

The city says the project will affect 765 properties, 553 of them residential. Residents don't need to apply for anything. The city estimates the whole project at about $80.6 million. As of May 2026, it expected the first section to finish in January 2027 and the second in June 2027, though those dates could slip.

On May 28, 2026, the Miami City Commission voted 5-0 to accept up to $5,970,718 from Florida's Resilient Florida program, which the state Department of Environmental Protection runs. The required city match is up to the same amount and comes from funds already budgeted for capital project 40-B223801, including Miami Forever Bond money. Commissioner Ralph "Rafael" Rosado sponsored the resolution.
