If you pay your Richmond personal property tax bill, which includes the tax on your car, after the June 5 due date but before the first of the next month, the late penalty is now 2% of what you owe, or $10, whichever is more. It used to be 10% from the first day late. On a $500 bill paid a few days late, that's a $10 penalty instead of $50.

Interest waits longer, too. It used to start the day after the due date. Now the 10% annual interest starts only if the bill is still unpaid on the first of the following month. The penalty also rises to 10% at that point. You don't have to apply for any of this: the lower penalty is applied automatically when you pay.

Chief Administrative Officer Lincoln Saunders proposed the change in January 2024 as part of a package of tax-collection fixes. Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance's first-listed patron. Council members Ellen Robertson, Kristen Nye, Ann-Frances Lambert, Stephanie Lynch and Katherine Jordan were the other patrons. City Council adopted it on Feb. 26, 2024. The ordinance took legal effect July 1, 2024, so the first annual bills it covered were due in June 2025.

The city estimated it would collect about $565,000 less in penalties and interest beginning in fiscal 2025. Richmond's adopted fiscal 2025 budget used no unassigned reserves; overall General Fund revenue and spending still rose by about $50 million from the prior adopted budget.
