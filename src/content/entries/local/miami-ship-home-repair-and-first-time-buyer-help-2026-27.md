Miami accepted $2,211,782 from Florida's State Housing Initiatives Partnership (SHIP) for 2026-27. The city's funding notice identified four resident-facing programs for the allocation: full home rehabilitation, emergency repairs, replacement homes, and help for first-time buyers.

Households must meet income limits set by the state, and the home must be within City of Miami limits. First-time buyers can receive up to $130,002, depending on bedroom count, toward a home worth up to $494,000. Owner-occupants can receive up to $150,000 for full rehabilitation, though that program already has a waitlist, or up to $75,000 for emergency repairs. A home that is beyond repair and unsafe to live in may qualify for up to $375,000 toward replacement.

The assistance comes as 0% deferred loans secured by a note and mortgage. No payments are required while a loan remains in good standing. Repair loans are forgiven after 10 years; first-time-buyer and replacement-home loans are forgiven after 30 years. Selling, renting out the property, losing its homestead status, or moving out during the term can trigger repayment. Applications became available Sept. 14, 2026, and are handled first-come, first-ready, first-served until the money runs out.

The Miami City Commission approved accepting the money 5-0 on July 23, 2026. Mayor Eileen Higgins chaired the commission when it voted.
