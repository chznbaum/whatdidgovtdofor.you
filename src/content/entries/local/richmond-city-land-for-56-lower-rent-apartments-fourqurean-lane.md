Richmond agreed to sell about 2.27 acres of city land at 1111 Fourqurean Lane, in Northside near Highland Park, to Commonwealth Catholic Charities' housing arm for $10,000. The ordinance says why: to help build about 56 apartments for lower-income renters on the church land next door, behind St. Elizabeth Catholic Church.

That project is now Greenway Village. It has 14 buildings with studio to three-bedroom apartments, a community room, a playground, and a walking trail to the nearby elementary school. The apartments are for households earning 40% to 60% of the area's median income. Commonwealth Catholic Charities expected a studio to rent for about $675, much less than the area's fair-market rent. Renters apply through the property, and the charity also offers residents financial and job help.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance's sole original patron. Council Vice President Ellen Robertson, whose 6th District includes the site, and Council President Cynthia Newbille were added as patrons before adoption. The council adopted it on Dec. 13, 2021, after the Planning Commission backed it. Construction began in 2025 with about $22 million from close to 10 sources, including federal low-income housing tax credits and a city affordable housing grant. On Nov. 19, 2026, the property manager still listed the apartments as coming soon.
