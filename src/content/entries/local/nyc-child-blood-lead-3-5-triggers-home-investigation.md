Lead can harm a child's growing brain, and there's no safe amount. In New York City, a blood test showing 3.5 micrograms of lead per deciliter or more must be reported to the Health Department within 24 hours. The department then has to track down where the lead is coming from. Before this change, a child had to reach 5 before the city was required to step in.

You don't have to apply. When your child's doctor orders a blood lead test, the lab sends the result to the city. If the level is 3.5 or higher, the Health Department investigates. That means inspecting the apartment where your child lives and other places where they regularly spend a lot of time. If inspectors find a lead paint hazard, the department can order it fixed.

The change follows the CDC, which lowered its benchmark for children's blood lead to 3.5 in October 2021. The New York City Coalition to End Lead Poisoning petitioned the Board of Health for the lower threshold. Under Commissioner Ashwin Vasan, the Health Department began serving children at the new level in March 2022 and proposed amendments to make it a legal requirement. The board adopted the amendments on Oct. 25, 2022, and they took effect Dec. 5, 2022.
