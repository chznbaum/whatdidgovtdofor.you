Richmond set aside $619,950 from Virginia's opioid settlement fund to prevent overdoses. The money comes from Operation STOP!, a state grant for cities and counties where overdose deaths have hit Black residents especially hard. The ordinance appropriated $594,950 for local groups and $25,000 for VCU's Wright Center to study what works and help plan the city's next steps.

The city's plan says how the money will be used. Health Brigade is funded to run street and online outreach and distribute harm-reduction supplies where fatal overdoses are rising. Atlantic Outreach Group and the Daily Planet are funded to manage scholarships covering recovery-housing bed fees for Richmond residents who show need. ChildSavers is funded to offer trauma-informed counseling to children under 18 whose parent or caregiver has overdosed. The Trauma Healing Response Network is funded to add supplies to its Community Safe Zones and hold quarterly programs. Residents will seek help through these groups rather than applying to the city.

Mayor Danny Avula sponsored the ordinance as part of a package of substance use and recovery measures. City Council adopted it on Feb. 9, 2026. The state grant covers the cost, so the city does not pay for it from local taxes. In June 2026, the Opioid Abatement Authority carried Richmond's full $619,950 award into fiscal 2027.
