CityFHEPS is New York City's rent voucher for people leaving shelter or facing eviction. Until 2021, an apartment rented with it could cost no more than $1,265 a month for a single adult or $1,580 for a family of three or four. Few apartments rent that cheaply in New York, so many families with vouchers stayed stuck in shelter. Now the maximum rent is tied to the payment standard the New York City Housing Authority uses for Section 8. In 2021, that was about $1,945 for a one-bedroom and $2,217 for a two-bedroom, and it changes when that standard changes.

You still must qualify for CityFHEPS. The higher rent ceiling is built into the program, and your shopping letter shows the current maximum for your household and apartment size. HRA pays a subsidy toward the rent after accounting for your required household share.

Council Member Stephen Levin introduced the bill in 2018. Homeless New Yorkers and advocates pushed for it for three years, and Council Speaker Corey Johnson championed it and brought it to a vote. The Council passed it 46-2 in May 2021. Council fiscal staff projected a net cost of $74.7 million in fiscal 2022 after estimated shelter savings, paid from the general fund, with costs growing in later years. Mayor Bill de Blasio had resisted the increase and let the bill become law without signing it. After Shams DaBaron organized dozens of demonstrations, de Blasio and HRA, under Commissioner Steven Banks, applied the higher limits to September rentals, months before the law required them. HRA's rules still tie the maximum rent to NYCHA's Section 8 standard.
