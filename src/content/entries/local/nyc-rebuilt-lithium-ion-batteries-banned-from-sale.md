In New York City, nobody may build or recondition a lithium-ion battery using cells taken out of used batteries, and nobody may sell or offer one for sale. These "second-use" batteries are cheap rebuilds, often sold for e-bikes, and Council members called them among the most dangerous on the market. Sellers who break the rule face a civil penalty. The law also has the Fire Department reach out to shops that repair e-bikes and scooters.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. The ban applies to sellers and repair shops, so a battery you buy in the city shouldn't be a risky rebuild. That matters for delivery workers who ride all day and for anyone whose neighbor charges a battery in the hallway. The FDNY investigated 220 lithium-ion battery fires in 2022, and those fires killed six people.

Council Member Gale Brewer introduced the bill in October 2022 after an oversight hearing on battery fires. Council Member Joann Ariola, who chaired the Fire and Emergency Management Committee, cosponsored it alongside more than a dozen other members. The Council passed it on March 2, 2023, along with a separate bill that bans selling batteries that aren't safety-certified. Mayor Eric Adams signed both on March 20, 2023.
