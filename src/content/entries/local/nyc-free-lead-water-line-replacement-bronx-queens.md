In New York City, the pipe that brings water from the street main into your house belongs to the property owner. In older homes that pipe is often lead or galvanized steel, which can leach lead into drinking water, and replacing it can cost more than $10,000. The city's Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) now replaces these lines with copper for free in targeted neighborhoods. A contractor does the work in a day or two, and DEP fixes the sidewalk and hands out a water filter afterward.

You can't simply apply. DEP picks low-income neighborhoods that carry a heavy pollution burden and sends letters to eligible owners there, and those owners sign up online. Replacements have been completed in Bronx neighborhoods including Mount Eden, Fordham Heights and Belmont, and work is under way in Mott Haven, Williamsbridge and Flushing, among others. Work will expand to Soundview and Borough Park beginning in 2027. More than 900 homeowners have had their lines replaced so far, and the program expects to reach up to 7,000.

DEP launched the program in the Bronx in July 2024 under Commissioner Rohit Aggarwala, and Mayor Eric Adams extended it to Queens that October. Under Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Commissioner Lisa Garcia, it has $96 million in state and federal money, including $24 million added this year. Most of the federal share comes from the 2021 infrastructure law's lead pipe fund. The state Environmental Facilities Corporation, led by Maureen Coleman, passes the money to the city, backed by Gov. Kathy Hochul's state loan forgiveness program.
