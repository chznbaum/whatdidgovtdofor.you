A severe allergic reaction can close a child's throat within minutes, and a shot of epinephrine is the first treatment. Under this law, every school building in New York City has to keep an epinephrine device on site and in working order. That covers city public schools, charter schools and private schools. Each child care facility regulated by the city Health Department has to keep at least two.

Parents don't need to do anything. The device is there for any child who has a reaction, including a child who has no prescription on file. Before this law, state law only allowed schools to stock epinephrine, and the teachers union says public schools with a full-time nurse were supposed to keep it for emergencies. The law makes it a requirement for every school building. City-regulated child care programs have had to keep two auto-injectors under the city Health Code since 2018, and that rule is now in city law too.

Council Member Julie Menin, now the Council Speaker, introduced the bill in May 2024. Health Committee chair Lynn Schulman was among its 17 sponsors, who came from both parties. The Council passed it on December 4, 2025. It became law on January 3, 2026, without a mayor's signature, and it took effect 120 days later.
