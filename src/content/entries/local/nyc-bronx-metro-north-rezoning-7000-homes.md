Metro-North is building four new stations in the East Bronx, at Parkchester/Van Nest, Morris Park, Hunts Point and Co-op City. This plan rezoned about 46 blocks around the Parkchester/Van Nest and Morris Park stations. Apartments can now go up in places that had been zoned only for stores, offices and industry. The city expects about 7,000 new homes. Under the city's Mandatory Inclusionary Housing rules, about 1,700 of them must stay income-restricted for good. As part of the deal, the administration also committed to develop up to 500 homeownership units.

You don't apply for anything yet. As buildings go up, the income-restricted apartments will be offered through the city's housing lotteries. The plan also comes with $498.5 million in city commitments, including about $119 million for parks and playgrounds, $12 million for schools, street and sewer work to curb flooding, and repairs to the 49th Precinct station house.

The Adams administration's Department of City Planning, led by Dan Garodnick, drew up the plan. Council Majority Leader Amanda Farías won changes on affordability for Parkchester. Republican Council Member Kristy Marmorato, who represented much of the rezoned area, won height limits, kept parking requirements and secured over $300 million for her district. Land Use Chair Rafael Salamanca Jr. and Zoning Chair Kevin Riley helped carry the modified plan through the Council. The full Council passed it 44–0 on Aug. 15, 2024.
