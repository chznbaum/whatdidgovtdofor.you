If you own and live in your home in Richmond and you're 65 or older or permanently and totally disabled, you may owe less real estate tax, or none. The city offers two kinds of relief. An exemption cuts your bill by 25% to 100%, depending on your household income. A freeze holds your bill at the amount assessed for the tax year before the year you first qualify. Both come with income and net worth limits, and your home doesn't count toward net worth.

This ordinance gave people more time to sign up. Before it, applications and annual recertifications were due March 31 of the tax year. Starting Jan. 1, 2023, the deadline moved to Dec. 31. For the 2026 tax year, you can still file any time up to Dec. 31. You do need to apply, through the Department of Finance at City Hall or one of its community sites. The city asks people to file by Sept. 30 so approved relief is reflected on the next year's tax bill.

The ordinance was listed under the full City Council's name, not a single member's. The council approved it on July 25, 2022, as part of its consent agenda, with Council President Cynthia Newbille presiding. It changed only the deadline; the age, disability, income, and worth rules stayed the same in 2022. A later ordinance, approved Nov. 9, 2026, expands some exemption income limits and percentages but shortens the application and annual recertification deadline to June 30 beginning in 2027.
