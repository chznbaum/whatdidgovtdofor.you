Rapid rehousing gets people out of homelessness and into a home of their own quickly, then helps them keep it. In Pinellas County, the Pinellas Rapid Re-Housing Collaborative places single adults and families with children in permanent housing and covers up to six months of rent, utility deposits and utility bills. St. Vincent de Paul South Pinellas handles the case management. First Contact, the agency also known as 211 Tampa Bay Cares, makes every payment to landlords and utility companies for the households in the program.

You can't apply directly. Referrals come only from the Pinellas Homeless Leadership Alliance's housing priority list, which ranks people by how vulnerable they are. To get on the list, you have to be homeless, for example living on the street or in a shelter, and be screened by a local shelter or street outreach team.

On Oct. 15, 2026, the St. Petersburg City Council voted 8-0 for a five-year agreement that funds First Contact's role in the program. The city will pay up to $400,000 a year, as long as the council approves the money in each year's budget. Council Member Richie Floyd moved the agreement and Council Member Corey Givens Jr. seconded it. The city and Pinellas County Human Services fund the program and set its policies.
