On Aug. 26, 2026, the Metropolitan Council awarded 100 Bring It Home rent vouchers to units in five existing apartment developments. The vouchers are assigned to particular units rather than tenants. An eligible household living in one of those units receives assistance after Metro HRA confirms eligibility, the unit passes inspection, and a payment contract is signed. When a unit becomes vacant, Metro HRA fills it through its waiting list or referrals rather than through a direct application to the building.

To qualify, a household must be a Minnesota resident, earn no more than half the area's median income when assistance begins, be paying more than 30% of its income toward rent or be unhoused, and not already receive federal Section 8 assistance. Metro HRA also requires adult household members to pass a criminal background check and have citizenship or eligible immigration status. Families with children earning 30% or less of the median get priority. Metro HRA serves Anoka, Carver, Hennepin and Ramsey counties, excluding Minneapolis, Saint Paul and several suburbs with their own housing authorities.

Bring It Home is the statewide rent-assistance program the Legislature created in 2023. Rep. Michael Howard and Sen. Lindsey Port were the chief authors of the housing law that created and funded it. The law pays for the program through state appropriations and a new quarter-cent metro-area sales tax dedicated to rental assistance and local housing aid. Metro HRA Senior Manager Chong Lao prepared and presented the award recommendation. Council Member Toni Carter moved the award, and the motion carried.
