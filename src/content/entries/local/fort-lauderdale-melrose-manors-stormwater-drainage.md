Heavy rain regularly floods streets in Melrose Manors because its existing swales and roadside drains cannot adequately clear the water. Fort Lauderdale has approved a $91.8 million design-build agreement with Man-Con for a comprehensive drainage system. The plan includes improved swales, storm drains and underground trenches that temporarily hold water while it soaks into the ground. A new pump station will send collected rainwater through underground pipes to the North Fork of the New River.

Residents do not need to sign up. Most of the work will happen in streets and other public areas, and the system is intended to reduce flooding of roads and nearby properties. The city's current project page says final plans, permit reviews and negotiations for a drainage easement are still underway. Construction begins after the city issues its notices to proceed, so the neighborhood is not protected by the new system yet.

The project is part of Fortify Lauderdale, the flood-prevention program launched after an April 2023 rainstorm flooded low-lying neighborhoods and temporarily closed the city's airport. Commissioner Pam Beasley-Pittman, who represents Melrose Manors, moved to approve the agreement on Oct. 20, 2026. Vice Mayor Ben Sorensen seconded it, and the commission voted 5–0. The city says the project is partially funded by a Florida Department of Environmental Protection grant. The broader flood program relies heavily on borrowing, and the Sun-Sentinel reported that stormwater rates would likely rise to repay it.
