Part of the money from Manhattan's congestion toll is going to Bronx children with asthma. Of the $20 million, $11.1 million expands the city's Asthma Case Management Program. It gives students close support at school, including help taking medicine during the day and lessons for children and families on controlling asthma. Fifteen more Bronx schools are joining. The other $8.9 million funds the Bronx Asthma Program, which will bring asthma services and education into the community.

Families do not apply for the funding. Students at participating schools receive the school-based help through their schools. The money also supports an electronic system to replace paper forms parents submit so schools can administer asthma medicine. The city expected that system before the 2026–27 school year. Contractor-run community case management is scheduled to start in July 2027.

The MTA offered the asthma funding during congestion pricing's federal environmental review after the toll plan projected more traffic and pollution in parts of the Bronx. Rep. Ritchie Torres negotiated over mitigation, and Council Member Amanda Farías pushed for action. Under MTA Chair Janno Lieber, the proposal became part of the agency's mitigation program. Health Commissioner Alister Martin's department is developing the programs and hiring staff and contractors. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the funding split in May 2026. South Bronx Unite later reported higher pollution readings after tolling began and argued that the investment was too small.
