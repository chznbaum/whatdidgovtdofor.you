Rain from the north end of Central Park drains into combined sewers serving Harlem and East Harlem. In a hard storm, that runoff can overwhelm sewers and flood nearby streets. A new system will recirculate and filter water among the Harlem Meer, the Loch and the Pool. Before a big storm, it will lower the Meer to make room for rainwater. Once finished, it should cut stormwater flowing from this part of the park into city sewers by 90%, or about 132 million gallons a year.

The closed loop will reduce how much city drinking water the park needs to refill the three water bodies, saving an estimated 175 million gallons a year. You do not need to do anything to benefit. Work began in September 2026 and should be finished in fall 2028.

The city is paying more than $19.6 million. The Mayor’s Office of Climate & Environmental Justice, led by Louise Yeung, helped secure a $10 million state grant with community and agency partners. The state Environmental Facilities Corporation, led by Maureen Coleman, awarded it in 2024 from the 2022 Environmental Bond Act, which Gov. Kathy Hochul championed. Council Speaker Julie Menin added $2 million, Manhattan Borough President Brad Hoylman-Sigal $500,000 and Council Member Gale Brewer $100,000. Central Park Conservancy donors gave $3.5 million. Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s administration broke ground in October 2026.
