Supportive housing in New York City is affordable housing with on-site services, mostly for people who were homeless. If your provider has a contract or similar agreement with a New York City agency, it must give you a written notice of your rights. You get it at your interview, when you move in, each time your lease or program agreement is renewed, and any time you ask. You do not have to file a separate application for the notice.

The notice spells out rights you already have under state and city law. They include safe, livable housing, the right to go to housing court to force repairs, a receipt for your rent, reasonable accommodations for a disability, and the right to form or join a tenants' association. The notice also tells you whether your unit is rent stabilized or its building gets a tax break, which agency runs your program, how to file a grievance, your provider's eviction policies, and where to get help with back rent or a lawyer. If your provider won't give you the notice, you can complain through 311. A provider that gets a summons is fined $250 for each violation unless it hands over the notice within 14 days.

Council Member Stephen Levin introduced the bill in December 2020 and carried it to passage. The tenant-led group Supportive Housing Organized and United Tenants advocated for it, and Levin said the bill grew from years of work by tenants, advocates and providers. The Council passed it unanimously on Dec. 9, 2021. It became law on Jan. 9, 2022, without Mayor Bill de Blasio's signature, and took effect that May. In the first weeks, some tenants said their providers didn't know about the law. Advocates also said the city hadn't notified providers that held state contracts.
