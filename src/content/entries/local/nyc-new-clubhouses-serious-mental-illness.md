If you're 18 or older in New York City and have a primary psychiatric diagnosis of serious mental illness, you can join a clubhouse for free. Clubhouses aren't residences or clinics. They're community centers run with their members, offering peer support, help with school and work, temporary job opportunities, and connections to health care. To enroll, you need an application and either a psychiatric evaluation or an attestation signed by a licensed mental health professional. Find a location on the Health Department's clubhouse page or call 311.

A 2023 city law required the city to establish and maintain at least five new clubhouses, all operating by Dec. 1, 2025. It also required locations to be posted online and a report on costs and results by Dec. 1, 2026. The Health Department put $30 million in city and state money toward clubhouses, double the prior funding. In 2025, seven new sites opened and six existing sites received new contracts, with a goal of serving 6,600 members by 2027. The procurement also excluded seven smaller clubhouses; the Council later funded five of them to keep them open.

Council Member Kevin Riley prime-sponsored the bill. It was part of the Council's Mental Health Roadmap, unveiled by Mental Health Committee Chair Linda Lee, Speaker Adrienne Adams and Majority Leader Keith Powers. The Council passed it on July 13, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in August 2023.
