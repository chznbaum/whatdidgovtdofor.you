New York City's Newborn Home Visiting Program offers eligible parents free in-person or virtual visits during the first 12 weeks after a birth. Social workers, nurses and lactation professionals can help with feeding, safe sleep, bonding, health insurance, vaccines, mental health and other services.

Today, first-time parents in listed TRIE ZIP codes qualify. Families with a baby up to 3 months old also qualify if they live in a Department of Homeless Services shelter, are engaged with the Administration for Children's Services, or live in NYCHA housing in a TRIE neighborhood. Age and immigration status do not affect eligibility. Staff contact eligible families; if they do not contact you, call 311.

The program had served about 9,500 families by December 2023. This law requires the Health Department to maintain it and expand it into priority neighborhoods, which the law defines as areas with significant health and economic gaps: 75% by December 2028 and all of them by December 2030. The department must report where the program is available as it grows.

Council Majority Whip Selvena Brooks-Powers introduced the bill in December 2024 with Council Members Lincoln Restler and Nantasha Williams as original cosponsors. The Council passed it 47-0 with support from both parties in October 2025 as part of a maternal health package shaped by the steering committee that Speaker Adrienne Adams convened earlier that year. Mayor Eric Adams did not sign or veto it, so it became law on Nov. 28, 2025.
