If a one- or two-year rent-stabilized lease starts between October 1, 2026 and September 30, 2027, the annual guideline increase is 0%. That includes renewal leases and leases for newly occupied stabilized apartments. A two-year lease carries the 0% guideline for both years, and you don't have to apply. If you have paid more than the order allows, the extra must be credited against your next month's rent.

The Rent Guidelines Board sets the maximum guideline increase for the city's roughly 1 million stabilized apartments every year. Last year it allowed 3% on one-year leases and 4.5% on two-year leases. The board froze one-year leases three times under Mayor Bill de Blasio, but it had never frozen two-year leases before.

Board Chair Chantella Mitchell moved to adopt the freeze, and on June 25, 2026, the board approved it 7–1. One owner representative voted yes; a public member reappointed by former Mayor Eric Adams voted no. Mayor Zohran Mamdani had campaigned on a rent freeze and appointed six of the board's nine members earlier that year.

A group of landlords has sued to overturn the decision. A Manhattan judge said he lacked authority to halt the freeze or impose last year's increases while the case was pending. The case remained unresolved as of November 14, 2026.
