If you live in a rent-stabilized apartment and your lease starts between October 1, 2026 and September 30, 2027, your landlord can't add the annual guideline increase when you renew. That's true whether you pick a one-year or a two-year lease, so a two-year renewal carries a 0% guideline increase for both years. You don't have to apply. If you've already paid more than the order allows, the extra must be credited against your next month's rent.

The Rent Guidelines Board sets the maximum guideline increase for the city's roughly 1 million stabilized apartments every year. Last year it allowed 3% on one-year leases and 4.5% on two-year leases. The board froze one-year leases three times under Mayor Bill de Blasio, but it had never frozen two-year leases before.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani campaigned on freezing the rent and appointed a majority of the board's members. On June 25, 2026, the board voted 7–1 for the freeze. One owner representative voted yes; a public member reappointed by former Mayor Eric Adams voted no.

A group of landlords has sued to overturn the decision. A Manhattan judge said he lacked authority to halt the freeze or impose last year's increases while the case was pending, and said he would rule before the end of 2026.
