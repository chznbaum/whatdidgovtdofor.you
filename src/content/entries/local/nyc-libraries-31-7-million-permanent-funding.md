New York City's three library systems are Brooklyn Public Library, Queens Public Library and the New York Public Library, which serves the Bronx, Manhattan and Staten Island. Together, they now have an additional $31.7 million a year baselined in the city budget. The money supports day-to-day operations at more than 200 branches, so the systems can plan, hire staff and keep programs running. Council Member Crystal Hudson said it includes support for more than 30 branches to stay open on Sundays.

You don't need to apply for anything. If you use your local branch for free internet, job help, English classes or children's programs, this money helps keep it staffed and open.

For years, part of the libraries' operating money was left out of the city's first budget plan each year, and libraries, workers and advocates had to fight to win it back. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's February 2026 preliminary plan left the prior year's one-time operating support out, putting fiscal 2027 funding $31.7 million below the fiscal 2026 adopted level. In its April budget response, the City Council asked to restore and baseline more than $30 million. In May, Mamdani's executive budget added $31.7 million to the baseline. Speaker Julie Menin and the Council kept that money in the budget adopted on June 30 and added $1 million for seven-day service. Library advocates say the adopted total, about $538 million, still falls short of Mamdani's promise to spend 0.5% of the city budget on libraries.
