Some employers use software to screen applicants. For New York City-based jobs—or when the staffing agency is in the city—the rule covers machine-learning, statistical, data-analytics, or AI tools when their output is the only factor, outweighs every other factor, or overrides other conclusions. Employers and staffing agencies cannot use a covered tool without an independent bias audit from the past year. The audit compares results by sex, race and ethnicity, and combined sex-and-race categories; a summary must be posted online. NYC residents must get 10 business days’ notice before a covered tool screens them. You do not need to request protection. Complaints go to the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, and subsequent violations can carry $1,500 penalties.

The law does not set a passing score or make employers stop using a tool because of the results, though anti-discrimination laws still apply. A December 2025 state comptroller audit found weak enforcement: from July 2023 through June 2025, the city received only two complaints and found one issue among 32 companies, while state auditors found at least 17 possible violations at the same companies. The department generally agreed with the recommendations and said it would adopt most of them.

Council Member Laurie Cumbo was the bill’s prime sponsor. The Council passed it in November 2021, and it became law that December without Mayor Bill de Blasio’s signature. The consumer and worker protection department, led by Commissioner Vilda Vera Mayuga, adopted the rules in April 2023 and began enforcing them that July.
