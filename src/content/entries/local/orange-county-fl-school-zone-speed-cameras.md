Speeding near schools puts kids and crossing guards at risk. Orange County plans to install cameras in the school zones of 12 schools in unincorporated parts of the county, two in each commission district. They include Dr. Phillips, Oak Hill, Rock Springs and Palmetto elementaries and Meadowbrook and Discovery middle schools. A county study found that at least 20% of traffic in every chosen zone violated the speed limit. About 32 more schools are planned over the following two years.

The cameras can enforce the reduced school-zone limit around arrival and dismissal and the regular posted limit during the rest of the school session. The sheriff's office reviews each potential violation. For the first 30 days, vehicle owners get warnings. After that, an owner whose vehicle is recorded going more than 10 mph over the applicable limit gets a $100 notice. County leaders say fines, not taxes, fund the program. Florida law directs $60 of each fine to the county for camera administration and other public safety, $12 to the school district, $5 to crossing guards and $23 to state funds.

Commissioners authorized the program in August 2025. On June 30, 2026, the board, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, selected RedSpeed and authorized staff to negotiate and execute a 10-year contract with a $103,075,200 ceiling. Commissioner Michael Scott helped choose Oak Hill Elementary as a District 6 site and later defended RedSpeed as the best price. The cameras were expected to become active in January 2027.
