If you work in New York City and believe you faced discrimination, harassment or violence on the job, the city's Human Rights Law gives you time to act. You have three years to sue in court. You have one year to file most complaints with the city Commission on Human Rights, or three years for gender-based harassment. Some employers had put clauses in job contracts and arbitration agreements that cut those deadlines short, sometimes to just months, so workers could lose their claims before they knew they had them.

This law makes those clauses void. You don't need to do anything. If your contract has one, it can't be used against you, and the rest of the agreement still stands. The law covers the city's Human Rights Law only, not deadlines under state or federal law.

Council Member Lincoln Restler sponsored the bill. Civil and Human Rights Committee Chair Nantasha Williams was its first co-prime sponsor and held a hearing on it in the prior Council session, then another after the new session began. Employment lawyer Anne Clark brought the problem to Restler's office and helped guide the bill's drafting. Restler's chief of staff, Molly Haley, pulled the measure together. The Council passed it on April 11, 2024. Mayor Eric Adams neither signed nor vetoed it, so it became law and took effect on May 11.
