If you live in Pinellas County, are between 18 and 64, have no health insurance, and can't get Medicaid or Medicare, the county's health program can give you a regular doctor. It covers checkups, mental health and addiction care, cancer screenings, lab work and imaging like X-rays and mammograms, prescriptions, and dental referrals. You have to meet federal income guidelines. To apply, go to pinellashsapp.org or call (727) 464-4200. The program doesn't cover emergency room visits or hospital stays.

The same agreement pays for Health Care for the Homeless. That program runs the Bayside Health Clinic in Clearwater, a mobile medical unit with weekly stops in Clearwater and St. Petersburg, and a street medicine team that brings care to people where they are. Homeless patients with incomes at or below the federal poverty line pay nothing for primary care. Those earning up to twice that amount pay on a sliding scale.

The Florida Department of Health in Pinellas County provides the care. On Sept. 24, 2026, the county commission, chaired by Dave Eggers, renewed the agreement from Oct. 1, 2026, through Sept. 30, 2028, at up to about $12.5 million a year. The money comes from the county's general fund and a federal grant. The renewal also added a $67,000 grant for HIV services. The county can end the agreement with 30 days' notice or if it doesn't budget the money.
