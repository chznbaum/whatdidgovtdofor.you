If you deliver food or groceries through a covered app in New York City, the new protections start after your first 30 days on that app. Starting Jan. 17, 2027, it can deactivate you only for just cause, for a genuine economic reason, or because the law requires it. For most problems it must use progressive discipline first, and it can skip that step only for serious misconduct.

When an app cuts you off for cause, it has 72 hours to tell you why in writing. If it cuts workers for economic reasons, it must give 120 days' notice. You don't need to sign up for anything. If you think you were cut off unfairly, you can work it out with the app or ask the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection to investigate. If the city finds the deactivation was wrongful, you can get your account back and be paid for lost earnings. The final law doesn't cover temporary lockouts that block you from logging in.

Council Member Justin Brannan introduced the bill in July 2025. Delivery workers organized by Los Deliveristas Unidos rallied for it, saying they had filed more than 1,000 deactivation complaints in four months. The Council passed it 40–8 in December 2025, and it became law in January 2026 without the mayor's signature. Uber, Instacart and Amazon have sued to stop it.
