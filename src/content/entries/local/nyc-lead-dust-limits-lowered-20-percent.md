Lead-based paint and the dust it creates are the most common sources of lead exposure among New York City children. The city now counts dust as lead-contaminated at 4 micrograms per square foot on floors, 32 on windowsills, and 80 in window wells, each 20% below the previous limit. A reading at the limit counts as contaminated.

You do not have to apply. The floor and windowsill limits apply when the Health Department investigates a location and during required clearance testing after covered lead-paint work. The window-well limit applies only to clearance testing. A work area cannot be cleared for permanent re-occupancy until every sample is below the applicable limit. Owners must give occupants copies of the clearance results, and laboratories analyzing the samples must have both federal NLLAP recognition and New York State ELAP certification.

The Health Department proposed the change in May 2026 after the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency tightened its dust standards. City law allows the Board of Health to set stricter limits and requires lower thresholds when federal standards fall. No one testified at the July public hearing, and the city received six written comments. The Board adopted the rule on Sept. 17, 2026, and it took effect Nov. 5.
