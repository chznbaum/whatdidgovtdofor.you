McGuinness Boulevard in Greenpoint now has a parking-protected bike lane in each direction from Meeker Avenue, under the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway, to the Pulaski Bridge into Queens. Cars get one travel lane each way instead of two, with parking and loading along both curbs. The city says the new layout shortens crossings for people on foot and slows turning cars. Nobody has to sign up for anything: the changes are on the street now.

The boulevard is a main bike route between Brooklyn and Queens. More than 4,000 people ride it each day in summer, and those trips are now separated from car traffic.

The push started in 2021, after teacher Matthew Jensen was killed by a hit-and-run driver while crossing McGuinness. Council Member Lincoln Restler, Assemblymember Emily Gallagher, and the neighborhood group Make McGuinness Safe, co-founded by Bronwyn Breitner, pressed for a full redesign. DOT drew up that plan in 2023, but the Adams administration scaled it back. The road diet went in only on the southern part, and north of Calyer Street the boulevard kept two car lanes each way.

On Jan. 3, 2026, days after taking office, Mayor Zohran Mamdani committed to building the original plan. Commissioner Mike Flynn's Department of Transportation started construction that May and finished it on Nov. 13, 2026.
