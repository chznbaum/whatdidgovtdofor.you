New York City law gives most workers paid safe and sick time. This law gives that right teeth. If your employer won't let you use your time, doesn't pay you for it, or punishes you for taking it, you can now sue in court. You used to have only one option: a complaint to the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, and then you waited.

You have two years from when you knew or should have known about the violation to sue. You don't have to go to the city first, and you can still file a city complaint if you want. A court can award damages for each violation. If your sick days went unpaid, you get three times the wages owed or $250, whichever is more. Each wrongful denial is worth $500. A firing over sick time is worth $2,500 plus lost pay, and you can ask to get your job back. You can also recover your lawyer's fees.

Council Member Gale Brewer introduced the bill in July 2022, and 19 colleagues signed on. The Council passed it on Dec. 20, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams sent it back without signing it, so it became law on Jan. 20, 2024, and took effect that March. The city expanded its leave law again in 2025, but the right to sue did not change.
