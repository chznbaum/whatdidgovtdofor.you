New York City made four employers pay back workers whose rights they broke. Walgreens owes more than $1.6 million to more than 570 workers at three Brooklyn stores. The company didn't post schedules 72 hours ahead and made people work extra hours without notice or their written consent. That broke the city's Fair Workweek Law, which covers retail and fast-food jobs. Calzedonia, which owns the Intimissimi store in SoHo, owes more than $154,000 to more than 50 workers for similar schedule violations, including shifts it canceled on short notice.

The other two cases were about paid time off. Allstar Security & Consulting owes $270,000 to more than 900 workers, and Kinship Coffee owes more than $67,000 to more than 90 workers at three shops in Astoria. Both broke the Protected Time Off Law, which gives most city workers up to 40 or 56 paid hours off a year.

Workers named in the settlements don't need to apply. The money goes to them directly, and Kinship is paying in installments. The companies also owe more than $218,000 in penalties and costs. The city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, led by Commissioner Sam Levine, investigated the companies and reached the settlements. If you think your employer broke these laws, you can file a complaint at nyc.gov/workers. You can stay anonymous, your immigration status doesn't matter, and your employer can't retaliate.
