Many older Minneapolis homes still get their water through a lead pipe, called a service line, that runs from the water main under the street to the house. Lead can get into drinking water. In Minneapolis the property owner owns that whole pipe, so replacing it could cost a lot. Instead, the city's program uses state and federal money to replace lead lines at no cost to owners.

In May 2026, the City Council accepted the year's project orders for lead line replacement and authorized the city to carry them out. It also moved the money into the Public Works water fund that pays for the work. You don't apply: the city picks project areas under the state's funding rules, then contacts eligible owners before contractors start. The city expects more than 1,800 replacements in 2026, over 1,200 of them in the Northside Green Zone, where it plans to finish replacing every lead line by the end of the year.

Council Member Robin Wonsley carried both resolutions. The council adopted them 13-0 on May 21, 2026, and Mayor Jacob Frey signed them on May 26. Minnesota aims to replace every lead service line in the state by 2033, and federal rules require it by 2037.
