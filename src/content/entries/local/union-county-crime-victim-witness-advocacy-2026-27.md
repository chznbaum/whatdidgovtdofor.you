If you're the victim of a crime or a witness in a case prosecuted in Union County, the county's victim-witness advocacy office is there to help you through the court process. This grant supports that office from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. The total project cost is $525,855. Of that, $420,684 is federal Victims of Crime Act money that the New Jersey Attorney General passes on to the county, and the rest is a required match of $105,171.

State law sets out what these offices must do when you ask. They keep you updated on the case and tell you ahead of time about court dates, plea deals and sentencing. They can give you a waiting area away from the defendant, send someone with you to court if you feel threatened, and help with rides or child care so you can attend. They help you apply for victim compensation and restitution, and help you write a statement about how the crime affected you. They also tell you when the offender is released or escapes. Victims of sexual assault get help arranging medical testing and care.

The Union County Board of County Commissioners approved the grant 9–0 on May 28, 2026. Chairman Joseph C. Bodek presided. New Jersey awards VOCA funds to victim-witness advocacy offices in all 21 counties.
