If you're uninsured or your insurance doesn't cover much, Orange County is helping pay for your care at two community health center networks: Community Health Centers, Inc. and True Health. The county doesn't pay the clinics directly. It sends $1.3 million to Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration. The state uses that money as its share to draw federal Medicaid funds, then pays the clinics through the Low Income Pool, a Medicaid program that covers care for people who are low-income, uninsured or underinsured.

Of the county's money, $1,020,314 goes toward payments to Community Health Centers, which runs clinics in Apopka, Pine Hills, Winter Garden and other parts of the county. Another $284,457.50 goes toward payments to True Health's clinics. Patients do not apply to the Low Income Pool; they arrange care with the clinics, and this funding helps cover eligible uncompensated care.

According to the county, it has done these transfers every year for 26 years to support its Primary Care Access Network, a partnership of clinics, hospitals and social service groups that serves the uninsured. The Board of County Commissioners approved this year's agreements on Sept. 29, 2026, at a meeting led by Mayor Jerry Demings. They cover the state fiscal year that began July 1, 2026.
