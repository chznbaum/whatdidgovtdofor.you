Orange County is paying the Coalition for the Homeless of Central Florida $1,466,486 for a year of homelessness outreach and prevention, running from October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027. Outreach workers go out to find people living outside and connect them with help. When they can, they keep people from needing a shelter bed at all. Residents facing eviction or already homeless can also get brief, one-time help with housing costs.

You don't need a referral to get this help. Outreach workers come to people, and people facing eviction or homelessness can contact the Coalition. Its outreach team helped more than 1,800 people across Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties last year. The Coalition says the work has gotten harder since Florida's camping ban took effect, because people are now less willing to be found.

The Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, approved the agreement on September 15, 2026. The board also let the county renew it for up to four more one-year terms. The county's Mental Health and Homelessness Division will oversee the work.
