More and more city services are moving online, which can leave out older New Yorkers who don't use a computer or smartphone. This law requires NYC's Department for the Aging to offer everything it provides online in two more ways, by phone and on paper, and to make those versions work just as well as the online ones.

By phone, you'll be able to start and finish an application, find out what documents you need, check on an application you've already sent, and ask for forms to be mailed to you. Paper versions also have to be available at the city's older adult centers. You don't need to sign up for anything to get this. The requirement takes effect January 7, 2027.

Council Member Linda Lee sponsored the bill, and 11 colleagues signed on as cosponsors. The Committee on Aging approved an amended version 6–0 on June 10, 2026, and the full City Council passed it 50–0 the next day. The mayor didn't sign it or veto it, so it became Local Law 116 on July 11, 2026. The law also cleans up the city code by replacing several repeated definitions of "older adult center" with a single one.
