The Cleveland neighborhood on Minneapolis's north side floods badly in hard rains, and some streets there go under in most years. Residents have pictures of themselves standing in waist-high water near Xerxes Avenue. Floodwater has pushed trash bins down alleys and blown off manhole covers, and it has worn away garages, retaining walls, and front steps. People have been asking the city to fix it since the 1990s.

The city is now putting in larger storm sewers and more street drains on 34th and 35th avenues north, roughly from Washburn to Queen, and on Washburn between 35th and 36th. The work also moves sanitary sewer lines, adds rain gardens and wider boulevards, narrows the streets, and adds traffic circles, curb bump-outs, and accessible curb ramps. Most mature trees will stay, but one lane of parking will go away on 34th and 35th. You don't have to apply for anything. The city says the design won't send more water into Crystal Lake or make flooding worse downstream.

On June 25, 2026, the City Council voted 12-0 to give the $17.9 million construction contract to Minger Construction, the lowest bidder. Council President Elliott Payne presided, and Council Member Jamison Whiting was absent. Mayor Jacob Frey signed the approval on June 30. A $5 million grant from the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency helps pay for construction, which started in 2026 and is planned to finish in 2028.
