If you own a home or a small business and have an old, unpaid city violation that became a court-style judgment, you can settle it for less until January 10, 2027. The "Debt Demolisher" amnesty covers eligible Environmental Control Board judgments from violations written by city agencies including the Sanitation, Buildings, Transportation, and Environmental Protection departments, such as improper trash disposal, dirty sidewalks, or building code problems.

If the judgment came from a default, meaning nobody answered the ticket, you pay only the original base fine, with default penalties and interest dropped. If you lost at a hearing, you pay 75% of the imposed fine and no interest. If you've already paid part of it, you may owe little more; even if your payments already reached the required amount, you must pay $1 to resolve the judgment. For a judgment tied to an order requiring correction, the issuing agency must confirm that the problem was corrected before the program ends.

Some debts don't qualify, including judgments entered after July 15, 2026, violations from citizen complaints like noise or idling, and debts already turned into tax liens. You apply through the Department of Finance at nyc.gov/debtdemolisher, by calling 311, or at a Finance business center.

Council Member Linda Lee sponsored the law at Mayor Zohran Mamdani's request. The Council passed it on August 13, 2026. It took effect on September 12 after the mayor neither approved nor disapproved it within 30 days and was returned unsigned on September 14.
