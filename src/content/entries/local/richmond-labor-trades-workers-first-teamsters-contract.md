Richmond's labor and trades workers, including crews in the Department of Public Works and the Department of Public Utilities, now work under a union contract. The city negotiated it with Teamsters Local 322, and its pay and working conditions apply to everyone in the bargaining unit. Workers don't need to apply. The city estimated the contract would cost about $11.38 million over its first three years. It took effect July 1, 2025, and runs through June 30, 2028.

Getting there took years. Virginia let cities bargain with their own employees starting in May 2021. In July 2022, Richmond City Council passed an ordinance that set up collective bargaining for city workers, who had gone about 45 years without it. In October 2023, the labor and trades workers chose the Teamsters in a runoff election. The union said the vote brought 611 city employees under its representation. After that, the union and the city negotiated.

Mayor Danny Avula's administration sent the finished agreement to City Council. The council's Governmental Operations Committee recommended it, and the full council approved it on April 28, 2025. City rules require the council to approve each union contract before it takes effect.
