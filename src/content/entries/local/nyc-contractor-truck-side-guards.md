Side guards are panels or rails that fill the open space between a truck's front and rear wheels. In a side-impact crash, they keep a person on foot, on a bike, or on a motorcycle from falling under the rear wheels. This law covers trucks with a gross vehicle weight rating over 10,000 pounds that work at least weekly on a city agency contract worth $2 million or more. For contracts registered starting January 1, 2023, guards are due within 12 months after notice to proceed if the contract uses 10 or fewer covered trucks, or within 18 months if it uses more than 10. You don't have to do anything: the contractor must equip its trucks.

A 2015 law already required side guards on the city's own trucks and on private trash-hauling trucks by 2024. This law moved that deadline up to January 1, 2023. By early 2023, about 4,000 vehicles in the city had side guards, the largest program of its kind in the country. Some trucks can get exemptions when guards are impractical, for example because of built-in toolboxes or fuel tanks.

Council Member Ydanis Rodriguez introduced the bill in 2019, and Council Member James Gennaro also sponsored it. The City Council passed it on September 9, 2021. Mayor Bill de Blasio did not sign or veto it within the charter's 30-day window, so it became law without his signature on October 10; the Council recorded it as returned unsigned on October 12.
