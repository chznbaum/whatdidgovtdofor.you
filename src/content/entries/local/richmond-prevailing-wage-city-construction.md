If you work construction on a Richmond city project, your employer has to pay you at least the area's going wage. The requirement took effect July 1, 2025, for city construction contracts worth more than $250,000. It binds the main contractor and every subcontractor. The Virginia Department of Labor and Industry sets rates from wage surveys conducted every three years; no rate can fall below the federal Davis-Bacon rate. Federally covered contracts are exempt from the city rule.

You don't need to apply. Contractors must post rates at the work site, certify pay scales under oath and keep payroll records for six years. If you think you were underpaid, you can file a claim with the state labor department. An underpaying contractor owes missing wages plus 8% annual interest and cannot bid on public contracts until workers are repaid. A willful violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor.

A 2020 state law let Virginia localities adopt prevailing-wage rules for local contracts. Councilor Stephanie Lynch, Council President Kristen Nye and Councilor Ellen Robertson requested Richmond's ordinance; Mayor Levar Stoney was also a patron. IBEW Local 666 and other building-trades unions lobbied for it. The city said construction prices and administrative costs were likely to rise but could not estimate the amount; no budget amendment was required. After a September amendment, the council passed it unanimously, 7-0 among members present, on Oct. 15, 2024.
