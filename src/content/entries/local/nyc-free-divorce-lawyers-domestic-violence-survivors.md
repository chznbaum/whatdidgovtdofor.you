Leaving an abusive marriage can require a divorce lawyer many survivors cannot afford. New York State appoints lawyers for financially eligible people in Family Court domestic-violence proceedings, but not in a separate divorce case. Local Law 137 fills part of that gap. Subject to yearly funding, the Office to End Domestic and Gender-Based Violence must offer free brief advice or full representation through the final disposition of a divorce.

To qualify, you must be a domestic violence survivor, 18 or older, living in New York City, with household income no more than 250% of the federal poverty guidelines. You must seek help, and funding limits mean not everyone who qualifies is guaranteed a lawyer. A separate pilot authorized under Local Law 5 of 2022 received $2 million in the fiscal 2026 budget and began before Local Law 137 took effect. In January 2026, the Legal Aid Society began taking cases for survivors who live in Manhattan or have cases there (212-298-3200). For fiscal 2027, the Council allocated $1,333,334 to Legal Aid and NYLAG.

Council Member Julie Menin, now Council Speaker, was the prime sponsor. The Council Women’s Caucus advocated for the program, then-Speaker Adrienne Adams advanced the bill, and Deputy Speaker Diana Ayala cosponsored it and promoted its funding. The Council passed it on September 10, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams let it become law without signing it on October 10. It took effect on April 8, 2026, and the city must report yearly on requests, services, and unmet need.
