Saint Paul is putting $1.44 million from the state into making sidewalks and street crossings easier and safer to use, especially for people who use wheelchairs, walkers or strollers. The Minnesota Legislature set aside the money for accessible walkways in the public right of way, and the City Council added it to four projects.

Most of it, about $934,000, goes to Payne Avenue on the East Side. There the city is rebuilding 11 intersections with new curb ramps, bumpouts that shorten the walk across the street, and medians that give people a safe place to stop. The rest pays for sidewalks on Cleveland Avenue from Summit to Marshall and on Marshall Avenue from Snelling to Albert, and for crossing improvements at Fourth and St. Peter streets downtown. You don't need to apply for anything. These are public sidewalks, open to everyone.

Council President Rebecca Noecker sponsored the resolution. The city's capital budget committee recommended it on May 11, and the City Council adopted it on May 20, 2026. Mayor Kaohly Her signed it on May 26. Work on Payne Avenue began in late August 2026. The city plans to finish the remaining intersections in 2027.
