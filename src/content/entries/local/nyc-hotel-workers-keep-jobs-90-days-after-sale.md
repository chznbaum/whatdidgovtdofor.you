When a New York City hotel changes ownership, management, or a service contractor, the successor must offer jobs to eligible staff from the prior year and former workers with recall rights. The written offers must cover at least 90 days at no less than old wages. Workers cannot be fired without cause, but if fewer are needed, the employer may cut staff by seniority and experience within each job. After 90 days, satisfactory workers must get continued job offers. Managers, supervisors, and confidential employees are excluded, as are employers that agree to specified union contracts with discharge or layoff terms.

You do not need to apply; the successor has to make the offer, and it must stay open at least 10 business days. A worker illegally fired or denied retention can sue in state court for reinstatement, back pay plus an equal amount in damages, lost benefits, and legal fees.

Since January 26, 2021, hotels have also had to notify existing guests and reservation holders within 24 hours after learning of a covered disruption, unless timely notice is impracticable. Covered disruptions include prolonged strikes, excessive construction noise, untreated pests, and certain amenity, accessibility, or utility outages. Guests who learn after booking can cancel without a fee.

Council Member Mark Levine was the prime sponsor, and 29 colleagues joined as co-sponsors. Hotel and Gaming Trades Council President Rich Maroko also championed the measure. The Council passed it 45–4, with one abstention, on September 23, 2020. Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it five days later.
