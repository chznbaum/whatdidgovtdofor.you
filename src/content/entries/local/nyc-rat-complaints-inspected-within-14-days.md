If you call 311 about rats in your building, or about things that draw them like garbage or holes, the city's Health Department now has a deadline. It must make its best effort to inspect the building within 14 days. If inspectors can't get inside or can't finish, it must try again within 20 days. There are a few exceptions. The department can skip a new visit if it inspected the building for the same problem in the last 90 days, or if it's already doing pest control there.

You don't have to do anything beyond filing the complaint. Within 30 days of each inspection, the department must post the results online: the date and address, whether the building passed or failed, how many rat violations or cleanup orders it issued, and what the inspector saw. That makes it easier to check out a building before you rent there, and to see whether your landlord was cited.

Council Member Susan Zhuang of Brooklyn introduced the bill in March 2025, saying families were living in rat-infested apartments. Her first version set a 10-day deadline, which was changed to 14 days before the Council passed it 47–0 in December 2025. Mayor Eric Adams didn't sign or veto it, so it became law in January 2026, and it took effect that July. The Health Department's rules defining what counts as passing or failing an inspection were still only proposed as of November 2026.
