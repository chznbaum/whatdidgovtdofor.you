Richmond-Henrico Turnpike is being rebuilt for about 1.15 miles, from Laburnum Avenue north to Hummingbird Road. Today it's a two-lane road with no median. When the work is done, it will have four lanes with a raised median, a 5-foot sidewalk on the west side, and a lighted 10-foot path for walking and biking on the east side. Crews are also putting in enclosed drainage, two stormwater ponds, a new culvert where the road crosses Horse Swamp Creek, sewer lines, and new water mains.

You don't need to sign up for anything. The sidewalk and path will be open to anyone once they're built. The road is set to be finished in 2028.

Henrico County voters approved the project in a 2016 bond referendum. County officials say spending that local bond money first helped them win state funding for this southern section. On July 28, 2026, the Henrico Board of Supervisors gave Abernathy Construction Corp. a $40.3 million contract for the road, path, drainage, water and sewer work. Fairfield District Supervisor Roscoe D. Cooper III, who chairs the board, said the project had been more than a decade in the making.

The county also uses the dug-up road to lay the first mile of a new 13-mile water main for eastern Henrico. Officials said the 2025 water crises pushed them to build that line sooner.
