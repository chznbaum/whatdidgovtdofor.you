Newark is getting $6,527,010 from the federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act this year. The money comes through the New Jersey Department of Labor and Workforce Development and pays for job training and job-search help at the city's One Stop Career Center, run by NewarkWORKS. It covers July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2028, and the city puts nothing in from its own budget.

The money is split three ways: $2,728,630 for young people, $2,629,716 for adults, and $1,168,664 for workers who lost their jobs to layoffs. The programs are meant for people who face serious barriers to steady work. They also help local employers hire and keep skilled workers. Under federal rules, adult training money goes first to people on public assistance, people with low incomes, and people who need help with basic skills. Veterans get priority too. To get help, you sign up through the One Stop Career Center.

Council members Amina Bey and Donna Jackson sponsored the resolution accepting the money. The Newark Municipal Council adopted it on July 15, 2026. The city didn't have to apply: the state awards this money to Newark every year. Most of the adult allocation now faces a federal threat: H.R. 9260 would rescind the October 2026 advance that supplies $2,140,421 of Newark's adult-program money.
