If you work for Orange County government, you aren't covered by a union contract, and you're meeting your job's performance standards, you get a 4% salary increase beginning Oct. 11, 2026. The county adds it to your base hourly rate and/or pays it as a lump sum where appropriate based on the maximum for your pay range. You don't need to apply.

The county also moved its pay scales. The lowest and highest pay in specified ranges of the regular, administrative and mayor and commission staff pay plans went up by at least 3%. That raises starting pay for new hires and gives workers at the top of a range more room to grow. The same vote approved the county's Personal Leave Sell Program for employees.

The county's Human Resources Division asked for the raises to keep county pay in line with the market. It pointed to national surveys showing employers were planning raises of 3% to 4% for 2026–27, and the 4% was already in the county's proposed budget. The Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, approved the plan on Sept. 29, 2026. Employees in bargaining units are outside this pay plan.
