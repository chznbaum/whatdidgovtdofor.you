New York City's lead-paint law turns on whether a child under 6 "resides" in an apartment. In 2016, a court held that a girl who spent about 50 hours a week at her grandmother's apartment did not. Since Jan. 1, 2020, city law defines "resides" as routinely spending 10 or more hours a week in a dwelling unit. In covered tenant-occupied apartments—generally buildings with at least three units built before 1960, plus some built through 1977 with known lead paint—that triggers annual notices, inspections and safe repairs. Report the child on the landlord's yearly form.

If a person under 18 has an elevated blood lead level, the Health Department must investigate possible sources, including every dwelling where the person routinely spends 10 or more hours a week. Local Law 64 also strengthened day-care rules. After the department orders a lead hazard corrected, the operator must post notice near the entrance. The law requires immediate remediation; after 21 days without compliance, the department must ask a city agency to do the work. The department may extend the deadline for hardship if the operator shows a good-faith effort and keeps interim protections in place.

Council Member Daniel Dromm introduced the bill in February 2018. Laurie Cumbo, Ben Kallos, Brad Lander, Diana Ayala, Helen Rosenthal, Bill Perkins, Margaret Chin, Stephen Levin and Carlina Rivera cosponsored it. The Council passed it on March 13, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio did not sign or veto it, so it became law on April 14, 2019.
