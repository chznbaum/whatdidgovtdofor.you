N. Armistead Avenue between Thomas Street and Convention Center Boulevard floods often. The city's coastal resilience engineer counted 15 floods on the eastbound lanes in a single year. This money helps rebuild that half mile so it drains better and has walkways people with wheelchairs can use on both sides of the road. You don't need to apply for anything. The benefit is a safer, drier street for anyone who walks, rolls, or drives it.

The rebuild entered Virginia's SMART SCALE funding process in 2018 and wasn't due to start until the 2024 budget year. Hampton asked to move it up so crews could do it together with a separate project that raises the same stretch of road, which the city said would save money. The Virginia Department of Transportation agreed. On Oct. 13, 2021, the Hampton City Council, led by Mayor Donnie Tuck, accepted $670,695 from VDOT. It also moved $480,000 in city stormwater bond money to the project as the city's share. VDOT pays its part back after the city spends the money.

The combined work costs about $20 million. It raises the road about three and a half feet and adds a shared path that connects to the trail around Lake Hampton. Construction began in August 2026, and the city says the work will take about two years to complete.
