Broward County approved $25.75 million in gap loans for four projects covering 712 income-capped apartments. The Gem of Hallandale gets $6.75 million for 150 apartments. Residences at Pompano Beach gets $7 million for 234 of its 244 apartments, and Village at Oakland Park gets $7 million for 164 of its 174. Federal Apartments in Fort Lauderdale gets $5 million for 164 apartments. The county program covers households earning up to 80% of the area's median income; Federal Apartments' current financing plan targets households earning 30% to 50%.

Federal Apartments is a redevelopment of an existing 164-unit, scattered-site Section 8 development. Its largest site, with 88 apartments, will be demolished and rebuilt with 164 apartments as the scattered homes are consolidated. The county housing authority's relocation plan says those 88 households will move temporarily, the owner will pay relocation costs, and residents can return to suitable apartments after construction.

The gap loans cover the last piece of each project's financing. They carry 0% interest for at least 30 years. County affordability restrictions last at least 50 years, and the developers committed to 99 years. The county documents do not give leasing dates or application instructions yet.

The money comes from the county's Affordable Housing Trust Fund. Eleven eligible applications were ranked after a February 2026 request. On May 26, the County Commission, led by Mayor Mark Bogen, approved the loans 9-0. It also put Village of the Arts first in line for $5 million in 2027 if trust fund money is available and it closes construction financing by June 30, 2027.
