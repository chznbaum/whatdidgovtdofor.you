Gwinnett County's senior services will keep running through June 2027. The county took $2,447,083.50 in federal Older Americans Act money through the Atlanta Regional Commission and added $163,224.21 of its own from the General Fund, for about $2.61 million in total. The grant reimburses the county for what it spends on these programs from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027.

The money pays for breakfast and lunch at senior centers, frozen meals delivered to seniors who can't leave home, care managers, homemaking, short breaks for family caregivers, recreation, and rides. These services help older residents stay in their own homes rather than move into a nursing home. Gwinnett residents age 60 and older, and their spouses, can get help by visiting any senior center or calling 678-377-4150. A care manager does an assessment first. Some services have waiting lists, and people with the greatest economic or social need, or who are frail, may get priority. Delivered meals are free, with donations optional. Caregiving, homemaking and some rides have a cost share based on income or mileage.

The Board of Commissioners, led by Chairwoman Nicole Love Hendrickson, approved the grant 5-0 on July 14, 2026.
