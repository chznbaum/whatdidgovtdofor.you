If you rent in Richmond and your landlord files an eviction case, the city funds free legal representation through Central Virginia Legal Aid Society for eligible tenants. You have to apply through Legal Aid and meet its eligibility rules; the pilot expands the number of lawyers available but does not legally guarantee a lawyer in every case. In January 2025, the City Council moved $500,000 set aside for the program to Legal Aid. That money was expected to support three new housing attorneys and one paralegal and help about 450 tenant families in a year.

Legal representation is linked to better results. Richmond has one of the highest eviction rates in the country, and a 2021 RVA Eviction Lab report found that 40% of its eviction hearings lasted less than a minute. In a study of cases in Richmond, Henrico and Chesterfield cited by the city's resolution, tenants without lawyers received a favorable outcome 2.4% of the time. Represented tenants did so 18.4% of the time.

Councilmember Andreas Addison proposed the pilot after watching rushed eviction cases in court. In September 2024, the council passed his resolution calling evictions without legal representation a public crisis. Mayor Danny Avula was the lead patron of the ordinance that sent the money to Legal Aid, and seven council members cosponsored it. The council passed it 9–0 on January 27, 2025. The city added $700,000 in a second round of funding. On July 27, 2026, the council appropriated another $700,000 to keep the program operating through the fiscal year ending June 30, 2027.
