Every year Richmond gets federal grants from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to spend on housing and neighborhoods. For the year that ran from July 1, 2019 to June 30, 2020, the city took in $7.48 million from four HUD programs and put it into its budget.

The biggest share, about $4.46 million, was a Community Development Block Grant. Richmond uses that grant for home repairs, neighborhood projects and public services. Another $1.46 million in HOME money paid for repairs for homeowners, help with down payments and affordable rentals. About $377,000 went to homeless shelters, rapid rehousing and street outreach. And $1.19 million paid for housing and support for people living with HIV/AIDS. The city runs that program for the whole Richmond metro area, not just the city.

People didn't get this money directly from the city. They got help through the city's programs and the nonprofits it funded, and each program had its own income rules and way to apply.

Mayor Levar Stoney's administration wrote the plan and sent it to the City Council. The council adopted it on July 22, 2019, which let the city send its plan to HUD and spend the grants.
