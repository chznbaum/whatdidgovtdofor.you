Fulton County is building a second behavioral health crisis center, this one in North Fulton. Adults in a mental health or addiction crisis will be able to get stabilizing care close to home instead of going to an emergency room or jail, or driving to the county's only crisis center on Metropolitan Parkway in south Atlanta. The new center is planned to have 24 beds and will sit on the county's health and human services campus at 4700 North Point Parkway in Alpharetta.

The state is paying for the center. Georgia set aside $9.4 million a year for it, and in July 2026 the county accepted a $9.48 million state grant to plan and design it. Because state money arrives in installments and the project is estimated at about $24 million, the county needed a way to pay builders on time. The Aug. 19 vote approved a contract under which the Fulton-DeKalb Hospital Authority will issue bonds for the construction, with the county's general fund pledged to cover its payments.

The Board of Commissioners, chaired by Robb Pitts, approved the deal 5–0; Commissioner Marvin Arrington Jr. did not vote. County Manager Dick Anderson has said the center will also serve Forsyth and nearby counties. As of Nov. 16, 2026, Fulton County was seeking a design-builder, with proposals due Dec. 4.
