Every year Jacksonville gives grants to local nonprofits that serve people who need help. This ordinance sets aside $7.2 million for the 2026-27 budget year. Of that, $6,984,000 goes to three categories the city calls acute needs, prevention and diversion, and self-sufficiency and stability. Another $216,000 is for micro-grants to smaller groups. You don't apply to the city yourself. Nonprofits compete for the grants and then serve residents with the money. The city's grants compliance division oversees the contracts. Before any micro-grants go out, the council has to approve how applications are scored.

The Finance Committee wanted to hold back 35% of the money until after the Nov. 3 vote on Amendment 3, a statewide cut to homestead property taxes. If voters passed it, that money would have gone to reserves. On Sept. 22, Council Member Jimmy Peluso won a floor amendment that made the full $7.2 million available for grants. The council then passed the ordinance 17-1, with one abstention. Council President Nick Howland introduced it, and Mayor Donna Deegan's administration had put the grants in its budget.

Voters did pass Amendment 3. Council auditors expect it to reduce the city's property-tax revenue by about $300 million in fiscal 2029, and the mayor will present a smaller budget for the year starting in October 2027. That could shrink future grant rounds, but this year's money has already been approved.
