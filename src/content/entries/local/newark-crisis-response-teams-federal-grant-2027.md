When someone in Newark has a mental health emergency, the city can send a co-responder team instead of relying on police alone. The project adds social workers to teams with Newark police and coordinates crisis services across city offices, 988, Rutgers and community groups. That includes improving the response for people who are homeless. This grant pays to strengthen the teams through Sept. 29, 2027.

You don't apply for anything. The help comes when a crisis call is answered. The money comes from the federal Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA), and the city adds nothing of its own.

Newark's Office of Homeless Services applied in 2022 for $2,906,654 over four years. Annual continuation awards depended on available federal money, the city's progress and its compliance with the grant terms. The first installment was announced in 2023. On July 31, 2026, SAMHSA awarded $727,087 for the project's fourth and final year. SAMHSA required council approval before releasing the money. With Council President Luis Quintana presiding, the Municipal Council voted to accept it on Oct. 7, 2026.
