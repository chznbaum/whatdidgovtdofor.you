The Pulse bus-only lanes on Broad Street are now bright red. They run from I-195 to 3rd Street, and right-turn lanes get red striping that tells drivers to merge behind the bus. Drivers had often driven or parked in the bus lanes. The red makes it plain where cars don't belong, so buses can stay on schedule. It also warns people crossing Broad Street that traffic moves differently there. Riders don't have to do anything to get the benefit.

The idea came after a Pulse bus struck and killed Richmond resident Alice Woodson in 2019 as she crossed Broad Street. Mayor Levar Stoney said the city began working on a grant proposal shortly after her death. Dironna Moore Clarke's Office of Equitable Transit and Mobility won a state grant from the Virginia Department of Rail and Public Transportation. The state covered more than $1.6 million, and the city added $413,452 from its regional transportation fund.

City Council, led by President Cynthia Newbille, approved the money on April 26, 2021. Crews began painting in June 2022 with a red coating. They finished the lanes in July 2022, about four years after the Pulse began running.
