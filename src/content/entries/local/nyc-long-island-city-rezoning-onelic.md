The plan rezoned 54 blocks in the Hunters Point and Dutch Kills parts of Long Island City, Queens. Some of them are industrial lots where new homes weren't allowed before. The city expects the change to bring about 14,700 homes, the most from a single neighborhood rezoning in at least 25 years.

For the first time, the area falls under Mandatory Inclusionary Housing. On private sites, 20 to 25 percent of new apartments must stay affordable permanently. The city also plans about 1,000 affordable homes on city-owned land, with at least half going to households with very low or extremely low incomes. That includes 320 homes on a Transportation Department site on 45th Avenue. In all, about 4,350 homes will be income-restricted. They will open over the coming years as buildings go up.

The deal also includes more than $200 million in repairs at the Queensbridge Houses: new plumbing, kitchens and bathrooms, and 80 elevators replaced or repaired. It pays for two new elementary schools with 1,094 seats and a nearly two-mile waterfront path.

Mayor Eric Adams's Department of City Planning, led by Dan Garodnick, drew up the plan after two years of public meetings. Council Member Julie Won, who represents the area, pushed for more than the planning commission's version offered and secured $1.97 billion in city commitments. The Land Use Committee, chaired by Rafael Salamanca Jr., approved it with changes, and the full Council approved it unanimously on Nov. 12, 2025.
