Lead can get into drinking water through lead service lines between a water main and a building. This ordinance let Richmond accept $500,000 from the Virginia Resources Authority and add it to the water utility's 2021–22 budget for replacing those lines at residential, school and day-care properties.

The money came through the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund as loan principal forgiveness, so Richmond did not have to repay it. EPA says this Phase II funding replaced private lead service lines at 176 homes. Homeowners selected one of 12 city-certified plumbing companies to do the work. Richmond still operates a broader Lead Free Water program, but its January 2026 FAQ says customer-initiated grants accept only very limited applications under strict criteria. Residents can call 804-646-8600.

The ordinance was introduced on April 11, 2022. City Council adopted it on its consent agenda on April 25, 2022, with Council President Cynthia Newbille presiding. The council's record doesn't name a sponsor.
