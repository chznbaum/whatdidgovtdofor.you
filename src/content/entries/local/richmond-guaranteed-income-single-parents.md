Richmond is paying 42 working families $500 a month for two years, or $12,000 per family. The money comes with no strings attached, so families can spend it on rent, bills, child care or paying down debt. This fourth group of the Richmond Resilience Initiative is for single parents and other primary caregivers, including guardians, stepparents and foster parents. It's aimed at households that earn too much to qualify for most public benefits but too little to afford living in the city.

Richmond residents could apply from May 27 through June 30, 2025. The city says payments began in September 2025.

The city started the program in 2020 and had served 94 households in its first three groups when this contract was proposed. Mayor Danny Avula was the patron of the contract ordinance, and the Office of Community Wealth Building, led by Caprichia Spellman, runs the program. City Council approved the contract with the nonprofit UpTogether 8-0 on June 23, 2025. The city supplied $500,000 already appropriated in its fiscal 2025 operating budget, and UpTogether added $60,300 of its own philanthropic funding. The contract permits up to $50,000 for administration and makes $510,300 available for participant payments, including survey payments. UpTogether also collects data to help evaluate how families fare, avoiding the cost of a separate outside evaluator.
