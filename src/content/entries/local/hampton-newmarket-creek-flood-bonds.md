Hampton financed three projects to hold stormwater in the Newmarket Creek watershed, where heavy rain and high tides often flood streets. At Lake Hampton, the city converted an old detention pond into a stormwater park with higher berms, smaller ponds and wetlands. The Big Bethel Blueway is turning a drainage canal into a planted channel that slows and stores water. On North Armistead Avenue, an evacuation route that floods often, the city is raising a half-mile of road and planting the median and shoulders to soak up runoff. Together, the projects are projected to add more than 8.6 million gallons of storage.

Nobody has to apply. If you live near Newmarket Creek or drive North Armistead, the goal is fewer flooded streets and yards. The projects also add walking and biking paths.

Hampton resiliency officer David Imburgia led development of Virginia's first environmental impact bond. The City Council approved the bond resolution 7-0 on Sept. 23, 2020, and Mayor Donnie Tuck signed it. Hampton issued the $12 million bond that December and must measure and publicly report how much water the projects store. In 2023, the three-project package was estimated to cost about $34 million, with state and federal grants helping pay the rest. Lake Hampton was completed in December 2024. As of September 2026, Big Bethel Blueway and North Armistead Avenue were under construction, with completion expected in December 2027 and December 2028.
