New York City's Fair Workweek Law gives fast-food and retail workers a right to predictable schedules. Fast-food employers generally have to post schedules 14 days ahead, obtain consent for added work, and pay premiums for late changes or back-to-back closing and opening shifts. Retail employers must post schedules 72 hours ahead, cannot cancel shifts inside that window, and need workers' agreement to add hours on shorter notice. The city found that two employers broke these rules, and in March 2026 it made them pay the workers.

Salz Management runs 24 Dunkin' and Taco Bell locations in Manhattan and Queens. It agreed to pay more than $1.5 million to over 760 workers, plus more than $155,000 in penalties and costs. Investigators found that the company didn't post schedules 14 days ahead, changed shifts without consent, and skipped the extra pay owed for back-to-back closing and opening shifts. The clothing retailer Theory agreed to pay more than $277,000 to over 60 workers at two Manhattan stores.

Workers do not need to file a complaint or claim to receive payment. Payments go by check or direct deposit to the address or bank account the employer has on file. Theory workers receive two checks beginning in April 2026. Salz workers get two payments, around August 2026 and January 2027.

The city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection spent two years on the Salz case. The investigation began under Commissioner Vilda Vera Mayuga, and the settlements were announced after Sam Levine succeeded her as commissioner.
