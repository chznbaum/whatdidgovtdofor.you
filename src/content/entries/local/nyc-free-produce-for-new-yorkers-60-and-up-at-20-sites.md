If you're 60 or older and live in New York City, you can pick up a free bag of fresh fruits and vegetables at a city pop-up this fall. There are 20 sites, including older adult centers, food pantries and community centers in every borough. Each site holds pickups on set days from Nov. 16 to Dec. 11, 2026. The city does not list a program-wide application or income test, but some locations have their own check-in rules. Krakus Senior Center, for example, asks for a valid senior center ID. Check the schedule at nyc.gov/aging or call Aging Connect at 212-AGING-NYC before you go.

The city's Department for the Aging, known as NYC Aging, started Pop-Up Produce in August 2026. That month, eight neighborhood groups held more than 60 distribution events, and NYC Aging said the first round prepared about 24,000 bags of produce. The agency funded the events.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration doubled the program from 10 to 20 sites for this second round, timed for the holidays. The city tied the expansion to rising food costs and to federal SNAP changes that raised the age limit for some work requirements from 54 to 64, which could cost some older adults their food benefits. NYC Aging Commissioner Lisa Scott-McKenzie's agency runs the program.
