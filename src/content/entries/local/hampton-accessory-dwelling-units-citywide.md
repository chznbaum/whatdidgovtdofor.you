If you own a legally permitted one-family home in Hampton, you can now add one small extra home on your lot. This kind of extra home is called an accessory dwelling unit, or ADU. It can be a cottage out back or an apartment built into your house. It can be up to 800 square feet, or half the size of your main home if that's smaller. People can use one to keep an aging parent close, give a grown child a place to start out, or bring in rent from a long-term tenant. Until this change, Hampton allowed these units only in the Buckroe Bayfront districts. The new rules apply to eligible one-family properties citywide.

Owners need a zoning administrator permit and a building permit. After inspection, the unit also needs a certificate of occupancy. The owner has to live on the property, either in the main house or in the unit, and both homes cannot be offered for rent at the same time. The ADU cannot be used as a short-term rental. A detached unit generally can be no taller than 16 feet or the main house, whichever is lower, and has to sit at least 5 feet from the side and rear lot lines.

City work on the rules began with research in September 2024. Jonathan McBride of the city's housing division was part of the group spearheading the effort, and 363 residents answered a city survey. The City Council adopted the rules 7–0 on Nov. 12, 2025, on a motion by Councilwoman Hope Harper. They took effect Dec. 31, 2025.
