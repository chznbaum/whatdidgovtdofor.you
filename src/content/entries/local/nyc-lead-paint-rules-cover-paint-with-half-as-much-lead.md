Lead paint can poison young children and harm how they grow and learn. New York City's lead law requires landlords to find and fix lead paint hazards in homes where a child under 6 lives or spends 10 or more hours a week. This law changed what counts as lead paint. Paint now qualifies at 0.5 milligrams of lead per square centimeter, half the old level of 1.0. The city's housing agency, HPD, called it the strictest standard in the nation.

You don't have to apply. The stricter standard applies when HPD inspects for lead and when landlords test, repair, and turn over apartments. HPD expected about 15% more surfaces to test positive for lead paint. That means more violations landlords must fix, more lead-painted windows and doors replaced or repaired when tenants move out, and fewer buildings that qualify to be exempt from the rules.

The law also tightened the city's limits on lead dust and lowered the blood lead level that triggers city action to 5 micrograms per deciliter, matching the CDC's level at the time. Both have since been lowered again. Council Speaker Corey Johnson sponsored the bill, joined by 45 colleagues, as part of a 10-bill lead package the Council passed in March 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio returned it unsigned, and it became law on April 14, 2019. The lower paint standard took effect on Dec. 1, 2021, once the federal government had approved a testing device that could measure lead at that level.
