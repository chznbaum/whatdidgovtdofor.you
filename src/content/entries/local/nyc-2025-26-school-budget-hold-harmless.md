New York City pays its district schools mostly according to how many students they enroll. When a school loses students, its next starting budget usually shrinks. For 2025–26, the city budgeted $128 million so that no K–12 or District 75 school would start with less total funding than its comparison budget from the prior year. The Education Department ultimately distributed about $127 million to roughly 450 schools.

Schools did not have to apply. On June 4, 2025, the department issued the allocation with schools' initial budgets. For regular district schools, it compared the prior year's initial allocations, including adjustments made through May 30, with the new initial allocation. Individual funding categories could still change, but the covered total could not be lower. District 75 schools, which serve students with disabilities, were also protected.

Mayor Eric Adams included the money in his Fiscal Year 2026 Executive Budget, and Schools Chancellor Melissa Aviles-Ramos's department distributed it. The protection used city tax-levy money. The city first protected schools this way during the pandemic, using federal relief funding, and later continued the policy with city funds.

In November 2025, the city separately let schools that enrolled fewer students than expected keep more than $250 million it otherwise would have taken back during the year. In June 2026, Chancellor Kamar Samuels said schools' starting budgets for 2026–27 would be protected again.
