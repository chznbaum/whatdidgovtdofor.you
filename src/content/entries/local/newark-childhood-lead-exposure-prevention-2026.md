Newark has $1,775,025 from the New Jersey Department of Health for its Childhood Lead Exposure Prevention program. The money covers July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. It supports work addressing children's blood lead levels of 3.5 to 4.9 micrograms per deciliter and outreach to families in Newark and surrounding areas. Even low lead levels can harm children's growth and development, so finding exposure and helping families stop it matters.

The city's Department of Health and Community Wellness runs the program. Families do not apply for the grant itself. The health department provides childhood lead prevention services and works with families after children are identified through blood lead testing.

Health Director Ketlen Baptiste-Alsbrook submitted Newark's application for the state grant. Council Members Dupré L. Kelly and Louise Scott-Rountree sponsored the resolution that accepted the money on Sept. 9, 2026. Because Newark was still operating under a temporary budget, the council also had to approve an emergency appropriation before putting the grant into the budget. State law required a two-thirds vote of the full council, which adopted the appropriation on Sept. 23 under Council President Luis Quintana.
