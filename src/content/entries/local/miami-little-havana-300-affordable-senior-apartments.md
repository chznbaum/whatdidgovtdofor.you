Miami authorized up to $4 million toward a 300-apartment project for seniors in Little Havana. The resolution says eligible households across the development are projected to average no more than 60% of area median income; it does not make 60% a ceiling for every tenant. No application process has been announced because the apartments are not open yet. Commissioner Rolando Escalona said he expected rents to range from about $300 or $400 to $800 a month, depending on a tenant's income.

The project is planned for two pieces of city land along Southwest 8th Street. In 2025, the commission approved a 99-year ground lease for $1 a year through a no-bid resolution sponsored by then-Commissioner Joe Carollo. The city retains ownership of the land. Escalona, who now represents the district and had criticized the arrangement as a land giveaway, sponsored the May reimbursement-grant resolution and made its funding subject to review by the city's Housing and Commercial Loan Committee.

The City Commission approved the grant 5-0 on May 28, 2026, with Christine King chairing the meeting. The loan committee later approved project funding. At its Sept. 30 meeting, it approved a funding swap that left the project with $8 million in city-administered support: about $5.63 million in federal HOME funds and $2.37 million in District 3 public-benefit funds. The buildings had not yet been constructed.
