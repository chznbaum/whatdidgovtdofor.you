For nearly 40 years, New York City capped full-term permits for food carts and trucks at 3,000. NY1 reported estimates of 10,000 to 20,000 street vendors of all kinds working in the city. Some vendors who couldn't get a food-cart permit worked without one and risked tickets, while others rented permits from legacy holders on an underground market for thousands of dollars a year.

This law authorized up to 445 additional full-term food-vending permits a year for 10 years, starting in July 2022. It created a new supervisory license, and a supervisory-license holder must work on site whenever a cart or truck with one of the new permits is operating. Vendors receive application offers based on waiting lists kept by the Health Department. The law also created an enforcement office and an advisory board of vendors, businesses, and city agencies to monitor the rollout.

Council Member Margaret Chin introduced the bill in 2018, with Carlos Menchaca, Brad Lander, and Debi Rose as its original co-sponsors and the Street Vendor Project leading vendor advocacy. The Council passed it 34–13 in January 2021, and it became law without Mayor Bill de Blasio's signature. The rollout lagged: by 2023 the city had missed the first two deadlines. In 2026, Local Laws 54 and 59 expanded the system to at least 2,200 supervisory-license applications a year for five years.
