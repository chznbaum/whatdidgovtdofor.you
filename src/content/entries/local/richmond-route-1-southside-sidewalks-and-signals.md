Route 1, also called Richmond Highway, is being rebuilt and widened between Chesterman Avenue and Brinser Street in Richmond's Southside. The work adds new sidewalks with curb and gutter, turn lanes, and new traffic signals. The project is combining the separate Hopkins Road and Harwood Street intersections into one intersection with signals, with a 12-foot turn lane on northbound Route 1. Crews are also laying new water mains and sewer lines and upgrading stormwater drainage.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. The new sidewalks and signals serve anyone who walks, drives or rides along this stretch. Nearby residents may have water service disruptions while service switches to the new main.

Mayor Levar Stoney brought the ordinance to City Council. It approved an agreement with the Virginia Department of Transportation to fund the design and construction of the work. The council's Land Use, Housing and Transportation Committee recommended it, and the council adopted it on Jan. 25, 2021, with Cynthia Newbille serving as council president. VDOT, which manages the project, started construction in October 2024. It estimates the cost at $12.4 million. The realigned intersection was scheduled to open with working signals on May 18, 2026; VDOT's latest project page still lists the full project as under construction and expects to finish in early 2027.
