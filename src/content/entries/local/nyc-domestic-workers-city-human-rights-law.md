If you work in a New York City home as a nanny, housecleaner, home care worker or companion, the city's Human Rights Law protects you on the job. Your employer can't refuse to hire you, fire you, underpay you or harass you because of your race, gender, religion, age, national origin, immigration status or other protected traits. They also can't punish you for complaining. You can ask for reasonable changes for a disability, pregnancy or religious practice. The city's ban on employers asking about your salary history or credit history now applies to them too.

Before this law, most of the city's job-bias protections didn't apply to employers with fewer than four workers, so most domestic workers weren't covered. Now one worker in a home is enough. You don't need to sign up for anything. If you're mistreated, you can file a complaint with the NYC Commission on Human Rights. Workers employed only occasionally, workers related to the employer or person receiving care, and workers providing companionship services who are simultaneously employed by an outside agency aren't covered by this expansion.

Council Member Debi Rose introduced the bill in January 2018. The city human rights commission, led by Commissioner Carmelyn Malalis, supported it and worked with community advocates to get the protections into law. It passed the Council in July 2021, Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on August 25, 2021, and it took effect in March 2022. New York State extended its own bias law to domestic workers around the same time.
