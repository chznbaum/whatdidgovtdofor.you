Henrico's EMTs and paramedics who work only in emergency medical services face many of the same dangers as police and firefighters: unstable scenes, traffic, and exposure to infectious disease. They aren't sworn officers, though, so until now they didn't get the county's enhanced hazardous duty benefits. On June 9, 2026, the Board of Supervisors voted to give those benefits to full-time EMS-only staff, both current and future.

The benefits run through the Virginia Retirement System. They're the stronger retirement package that local governments can choose to offer people in dangerous jobs. Depending on the position, covered workers can get a larger pension multiplier at retirement. Eligible retirees with at least 20 years of hazardous duty service can also get a monthly supplement until they reach Social Security retirement age, currently $17,856 a year. Workers don't need to apply. The coverage comes with the job.

The fire division had six full-time EMS-only providers when the board voted and planned to add five more paramedic positions. Jackson Baynard is a former fire chief who is now Henrico's deputy county manager for public safety. He had pushed for the change since the previous September. He told the board that 85% of the county's calls were EMS-related, and he called the benefits a tool to keep medics on the job.
