Unless state or federal law or regulation prohibits it, New York City's Department of Social Services must give you a web form when you apply for a benefit or service. You can type your answers on the website, sign electronically, and submit the application there. Before this law, many of the department's forms had to be printed, filled out by hand, and scanned or photographed to upload again. That was hard for anyone without a printer or scanner.

The law also says that when an application needs an interview, you can do it by phone. The department can require an in-person interview only to prevent fraud or address a safety concern. If you'd rather use paper, the department still has to accept applications you mail or bring in. You don't have to do anything to get these options. They became required on May 17, 2026.

Council Member Yusef Salaam introduced the bill, and ten Council colleagues cosponsored it. His office described the problem as a barrier for older adults, people with disabilities, caregivers, and people who don't speak English well. The Council passed it on December 18, 2025. It became law on January 17, 2026 without the mayor's signature.
