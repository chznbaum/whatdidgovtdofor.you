New York City funded 10,000 additional free COMPASS after-school seats for elementary students for the 2026–27 school year. DYCD's final contracts included 71 new elementary programs in public schools and 13 in community centers, accounting for about 9,500 seats. Programs offer homework help, recreation, arts, STEAM and other activities at no cost. The broader contract rollout experienced staffing shortages and delayed or limited openings at some sites in September.

Families can apply through the COMPASS provider at their school, their school's main office or discoverDYCD. Not every program accepts applications at the same time, so families may need to check back or contact the provider directly.

Mayor Eric Adams proposed After-School for All in April 2025, with Deputy Mayor Ana J. Almanzar championing the expansion and DYCD Commissioner Keith Howard overseeing its first phase and requests for proposals. After the city funded the plan, Commissioner Sandra Escamilla-Davies announced the final contracts in May 2026, working with NYC Public Schools under Chancellor Kamar Samuels. Added spending grows to about $331 million a year by fiscal 2028 and funds 20,000 additional seats over three school years: 5,000 in fall 2025, 10,000 in fall 2026 and 5,000 in fall 2027. The comptroller estimated that hundreds of thousands of children would still lack a publicly funded seat.
