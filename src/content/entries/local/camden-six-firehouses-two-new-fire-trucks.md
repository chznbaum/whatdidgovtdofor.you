Camden is renovating six of its fire stations and adding two new fire trucks. The money comes from a $6.4 million loan backed by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development. Residents don't need to do anything: the upgrades are to the stations and trucks that answer emergency calls across the city.

The fix was overdue. In 2024, Camden firefighters went public with repeated mechanical breakdowns in the department's trucks. William Perez, president of the firefighters union, warned that the aging trucks were delaying response times and could put lives at risk. In August 2025, Mayor Victor Carstarphen announced the HUD loan to fix the problem, with Fire Chief Jesse Flax at his side. Officials planned to start station renovations by the end of 2025 and expected the new trucks in 2026.

Camden is borrowing against its future federal Community Development Block Grant allocations to repay the loan. On June 11, 2026, with Council President Angel Fuentes presiding, the City Council voted 7–0 to authorize the loan contract with HUD.
