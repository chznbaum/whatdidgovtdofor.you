Miami-Dade County buys ready-made hot and frozen meals for older residents and serves them at adult care and senior centers and at group meal sites. It also delivers them to seniors who can't leave home, through Meals on Wheels. The meals come from a pool of nine local caterers that have already qualified to bid. That pool was set to expire on Sept. 30, 2026. The county commission extended it five more years, to Sept. 30, 2031, and allowed up to $14,125,000 more in spending. That brings the pool's total to $29,125,000.

Seniors already in the county's meal programs don't need to do anything. This vote keeps the meals coming without a gap while the county's Community Services Department keeps buying them from the vendors. The county says it spends about $208,000 a month on these meals. The new amount covers that, plus extra for rising food prices. The money is federal: it comes through the Alliance for Aging under the Older Americans Act.

Commissioner Marleine Bastien sponsored the resolution. At a committee hearing in May, Commissioner Vicki Lopez asked which seniors the vendors serve and raised concerns about meal cuts at some senior buildings in her district. The full board adopted the resolution 9-0 on June 2, 2026.
