If you work for the City of Portsmouth, you now have a formal process to choose a union and negotiate with the city over pay, benefits and working conditions. The ordinance creates three bargaining groups: police officers below lieutenant; firefighters, fire marshals and emergency dispatchers below captain; and other city employees. Managers, supervisors, temporary or seasonal workers and constitutional officers aren't covered. Virginia is a right-to-work state, so no one has to join a union or pay dues.

Bargaining doesn't start right away. Workers first have to choose representatives, and those representatives have to be certified. Local 539 had submitted a letter of intent by October 2026, but union president Tiffany Stuart said the parties still had ground rules to set and expected roughly 18 months before bargaining could begin. Council keeps final authority over the city budget.

Before May 2021, Virginia law barred local public employees from bargaining collectively. A law passed in 2020 gave cities the option to allow it. Portsmouth Fire Fighters Local 539 spent six years pressing the city to use that option. Former Local 539 president Kurt Detrick led the union during the November 2023 campaign, when council voted 4-2 to authorize bargaining—the first Hampton Roads city to do so. Stuart led the union during the final ordinance work and said the city manager's team reviewed it with union representatives page by page. Nearly three years after the 2023 vote, council adopted the rules unanimously.
