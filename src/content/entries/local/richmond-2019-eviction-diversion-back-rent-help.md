In 2019, Richmond started helping renters who were already being taken to eviction court. Through a $485,140 city grant, the nonprofit Housing Opportunities Made Equal could pay up to half of a family's overdue rent. Tenants also got free help from volunteer lawyers and money-management counseling, while landlords received the rent they were owed.

The help was for tenants whose eviction case was about unpaid rent and who otherwise had a good payment record: no more than two late payments in the past six months or three in the past year. The landlord had to agree to participate. At court, tenants had to pay 25% of the amount due. The remaining 75% was divided into three monthly installments; the program expected to pay one or two of those installments while tenants kept up with their regular rent.

Central Virginia Legal Aid Society litigation director Marty Wegbreit suggested an eviction diversion program and became a critical adviser on its design and implementation. Mayor Levar Stoney announced the plan in his 2019 State of the City address and was the patron of the grant ordinance. Housing Opportunities Made Equal helped design and then administered the program. City Council approved the grant 9-0 on Sept. 9, 2019.

The program began Oct. 1 and diverted 76 evictions in its first three months. It followed an Eviction Lab finding that Richmond had the second-highest 2016 eviction rate among large U.S. cities. A separate state court pilot authorized in 2019 for Richmond, Petersburg, Hampton and Danville began July 1, 2020.
