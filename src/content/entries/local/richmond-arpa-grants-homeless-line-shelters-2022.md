In April 2022, Richmond originally moved $1,283,288 in American Rescue Plan money from its housing department into grants for five local groups that help people keep or find housing. In December 2024, the council recaptured $80,342.95 that St. Joseph's Villa did not use, reducing its youth-outreach award from $240,000 to $159,657.05. The amended total is $1,202,945.05.

Homeward was budgeted $264,170 for the Homeless Connection Line, (804) 972-0813. In the Richmond region, people who are homeless or within three days of losing housing can call this primary access point for shelter and other help. The city lists its hours as 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. on weekdays and 1 to 9 p.m. on weekends. Daily Planet was budgeted $350,400 for medical respite care, which gives homeless people a place to recover from illness. CARITAS was allotted $350,000 for emergency-shelter upgrades, and Southside Community Development and Housing Corporation was allotted $78,718 for counseling after someone buys a home.

Council member Ann-Frances Lambert and Mayor Levar Stoney carried the ordinance as its patrons. The City Council adopted it 9-0 on April 11, 2022. The money came from the federal pandemic relief law, not from new local taxes.
