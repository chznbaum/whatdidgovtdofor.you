In spring 2020, many Hampton households lost pay and businesses lost income as COVID-19 closed or restricted businesses and the state ordered people to stay home. Real estate, personal property and business personal property taxes, including the tax on your car, were still due June 5. This resolution let taxpayers who could show they had been hurt pay those bills as late as Aug. 1, with no penalty or interest.

You had to apply. The relief covered residents and businesses that could show a financial loss or strain caused directly by COVID-19, where unemployment benefits or other stimulus aid wasn't enough to keep their taxes current. Applications were due to the city treasurer by May 15, 2020. The treasurer and the city's tax and fee collection committee reviewed each one, and approved taxpayers got written confirmation.

City Manager Mary Bunting presented the plan at an April 8, 2020 special session, which Mayor Donnie Tuck chaired. The council approved it 7-0. The city moved $500,000 from its reserve fund into that year's budget to cover the penalties and interest it gave up, so it didn't have to cut services to pay for it. The relief ran out when the Aug. 1, 2020 deadline passed.
