If you live in one of the 220 NYCHA developments in the program, you can get high-speed home internet and basic cable TV for free. The modem, router, cable box, and remote are free too. Many buildings also get free Wi-Fi in common areas. There's no income form to fill out. If you already had Optimum or Spectrum, you were signed up automatically and pay only for extras you choose. If you didn't, you can sign up with the provider for your development.

The city says a household that signs up saves more than $1,700 a year on average. Before the program, internet providers estimated that 30 to 40 percent of NYCHA residents had no broadband at home.

Mayor Eric Adams launched Big Apple Connect in September 2022. In March 2023 he expanded it to 202 developments, reaching his goal of more than 200 ahead of schedule, and it later grew to 220. Chief Technology Officer Matthew Fraser's Office of Technology and Innovation runs the program and pays Optimum and Spectrum directly, about $38 million a year. In September 2025 the city extended the deal through June 2028. The program has also drawn criticism. Reporting by New York Focus found that its connections were being used to link NYCHA security cameras to the NYPD's surveillance system, which the City Council questioned at a hearing.
