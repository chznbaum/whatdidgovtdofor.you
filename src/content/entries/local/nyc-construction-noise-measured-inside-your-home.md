If construction noise is coming through your walls and the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) conducts an inspection, you can ask the inspector to take a sound-level reading inside your home. The law covers owners, landlords and occupants in residential buildings within half a mile of the construction site. DEP must honor the request during the inspection and take the reading with windows and doors that could affect it closed.

The law also added an indoor daytime standard. From 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., sound from construction devices that is 10 dB(A) above the normal background level inside a home counts as unreasonable noise under the city code. You can report construction noise through 311.

Council Member Keith Powers first introduced the indoor-measurement proposal in July 2018. That bill expired at the end of the Council session, and he reintroduced it in October 2022. Twenty-two other Council members signed on, including Darlene Mealy, Lincoln Restler and Julie Menin. The Council passed the amended bill on December 6, 2023. Mayor Eric Adams returned it unsigned on January 5, 2024, and it became law the next day. It took effect on May 5, 2024.
