If you held an eligible permanent City of Richmond job, this ordinance raised your base pay by 3.25% starting with the pay period that began Oct. 9, 2021, subject to the top of your pay range. Employees did not have to apply. Separately, starting with the July 3 pay period, the city raised its hourly floor from $12.07 to $13 for covered full- and part-time positions. Summer youth workers, interns, special-event workers and some other special-program participants were excluded from that floor.

The ordinance also restored the full step plan for sworn police officers and firefighters on Oct. 9. That made workers eligible for the 2021 step and the step skipped in 2020 during the pandemic, although some workers at the top of the plan received fewer than two increases. It also extended through July 1, 2022, a pause that had been in place since 2010 on new applications for, or increases in, police and fire education incentives.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the $13 floor, the police and fire steps, and study-based raises for other city employees. Council Member Kristen Larson, now Kristen Nye, argued that his general-employee plan would reach only about one-third of the workforce. She proposed a 3.25% raise instead, and the council adopted it. The council amended the ordinance on May 10 and passed it on May 24, 2021.
