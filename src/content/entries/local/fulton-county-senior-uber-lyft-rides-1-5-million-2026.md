If you're 60 or older, live in Fulton County and can get around on your own, the county will set up and pay for most of an Uber or Lyft ride. On June 3, 2026, the Board of Commissioners raised the program's spending limit by up to $1.5 million to keep up with the rising cost of those rides. The money comes from a senior transportation increase that commissioners added to the county's 2026 budget.

You sign up through the Department of Senior Services at 404-613-6000. The county's page lists a $15 one-time fee and a $1 share per one-way trip, billed monthly, and you need a card on file. You can take up to eight one-way trips a month. Senior Services may approve up to two additional one-way medical trips if funding permits. Trips can go up to 15 miles for medical care and 10 miles for other errands, such as groceries, banking or the pharmacy. The cars generally aren't wheelchair accessible. The county runs a separate door-to-door van service for that.

Common Courtesy, an Atlanta nonprofit, books the trips under its county contract. The county's Senior Services department says the rides offer same-day service and help seniors stay connected instead of isolated. The board approved the increase 5-0; Commissioner Bob Ellis was absent. Chairman Robb Pitts presided, and the county's record doesn't name a commissioner who brought the item.
