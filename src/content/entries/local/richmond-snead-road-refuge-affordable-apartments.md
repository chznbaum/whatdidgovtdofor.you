Richmond is helping pay for Refuge, a new 180-apartment complex on 10 acres at 5015 Snead Road in South Richmond's Piney Knolls area. The one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments will go to households earning up to 60% of the area's median income. For 2026 that means up to $47,700 for one person or $61,320 for a family of three. The developer, Lawson Cos., is targeting early 2028 for move-ins. When leasing opens, renters apply through the property and have to meet the income limits.

The city's help is an Affordable Housing Performance Grant. After construction, the owner can receive back the portion of each year's real estate taxes above the site's fixed $2,172 baseline. The payments can continue for 30 years, but only while the owner keeps the apartments subject to the required income and rent limits. City staff said the grant has no direct fiscal cost because it uses only the new property-tax revenue generated by the development.

Mayor Danny Avula's administration brought the grant agreement to City Council, which adopted it 9-0 on Sept. 22, 2025, after months of delays. City planning staff had urged denial of the project's separate special use permit, saying the site was car-dependent and the complex was too dense next to single-family homes. Council member Nicole Jones initially opposed the project because Snead Road has no sidewalks. Lawson then agreed to build a sidewalk to J.L. Francis Elementary or pay the city $50,000 to do it. Separately from the grant, the Avula administration promised $4 million over three years from Central Virginia Transportation Authority funds for sidewalks and road work between the school and Broad Rock Boulevard.
