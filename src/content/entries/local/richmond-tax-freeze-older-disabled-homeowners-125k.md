If you own and live in your Richmond home and you're 65 or older, or permanently and totally disabled, you can freeze your real estate tax bill. When assessments rise, your bill stays at the amount from the year before you qualified. For example, a $2,000 bill that would have gone up to $2,200 stays at $2,000.

This ordinance opened the freeze to many more people. The household income limit went from $70,000 to $125,000. The limit on savings and other assets went from $450,000 to $750,000, and the value of your home and up to one acre doesn't count. The freeze also now starts in the year you apply. You file a full application through the city's Department of Finance, then recertify by December 31 in each of the next two years; after that, you apply again. Homeowners with lower incomes can choose the city's separate exemption instead, which cuts the bill by 25% to 100%, but you can't get both.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the bigger freeze in fall 2024 as part of his RVA Stay housing relief package. Council President Kristen Nye was an original patron after collaborating with the administration, and seven other council members added themselves as co-patrons before the council adopted it 9-0 on November 12, 2024. It took effect January 1, 2025. The city said it would pay for RVA Stay, about $4 million including a grant pilot for renters and owners with low incomes, from extra tax revenue in fiscal year 2025 rather than by cutting services.
