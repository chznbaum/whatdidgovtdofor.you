In July 2020, Richmond accepted $8.2 million from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development and put it in the city’s budget for the program year from July 1, 2020, through June 30, 2021. The money came through four federal programs.

The largest share, about $4.76 million in Community Development Block Grant money, had to mainly benefit people with low and moderate incomes. Richmond budgeted it for home construction and repair, down-payment help, fair-housing work, workforce and business programs, neighborhood planning, and administration. About $1.67 million in HOME money supported affordable housing. Nearly $1.4 million from Housing Opportunities for Persons With AIDS paid for housing and support for low-income people with HIV and their families across the Richmond metro area. The final $392,068 came from Emergency Solutions Grants for shelter, rapid rehousing, and homelessness prevention.

This was not a general rebate. The city assigned the money to named projects and partner organizations in its annual plan; some programs offered rental, mortgage, utility, or down-payment assistance to eligible households. In 2023, the council reappropriated $93,000 of recaptured HOME money within the same plan to complete two Habitat for Humanity homes, without adding new HUD funds.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance’s patron. The City Council, led by President Cynthia Newbille, adopted it on July 27, 2020.
