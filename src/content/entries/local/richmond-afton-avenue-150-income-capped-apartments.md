Richmond's Southside has 150 new apartments that lower-income families can afford. Pinnacle at Afton, a four-story building at 2400 Afton Avenue, was built on about 2 acres of land that sat empty. It has studios up to three-bedroom units, and households earning up to 60% of the area median income can rent them. The units have washers and dryers, and the building has a gym and community rooms. The building opened in October 2026, and move-ins were set to begin that month. You apply through the building's management.

The 2023 ordinance tied the project to Richmond's Affordable Housing Performance Grant program. When the new building raises the property's tax bill, the city's Economic Development Authority pays the increase back to the developer, an estimated $401,910 a year. That only happens while the developer keeps rents and tenant incomes at an average of 60% of the area median and invests in upkeep. Grants under the program run for 30 years. The city isn't giving up money it already collected: it refunds new taxes the building itself creates. The city's housing department also gave the project a separate $1 million grant, and the $34 million project was financed by Fulton Bank and Virginia Housing.

Councilmember Reva Trammell, whose district includes the site, shepherded the project through the city process. Mayor Levar Stoney sent the agreement to City Council, and the council adopted it on Nov. 13, 2023. Developer Genesis Properties started construction in early 2025.
