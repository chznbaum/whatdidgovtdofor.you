Orange County is paying Samaritan Resource Center, a nonprofit, $1.4 million to help people who are homeless or close to losing their homes. The money pays for short-term bridge housing, financial help, outreach workers who go out into the community, and support services meant to help people get stable. The contract runs from Oct. 1, 2026 through Sept. 30, 2027.

The help is for Orange County individuals and families who already have no home or are at imminent risk of losing one. Eligible residents can visit Samaritan Resource Center Monday through Friday for referrals and an in-person assessment; outreach workers also meet clients where they are. The county's Mental Health and Homelessness Division oversees the contract, and the nonprofit delivers the services.

The Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, approved the contract on Sept. 15, 2026. The board also let the county's purchasing office renew it for up to four more one-year terms. The county's memo says Demings committed more county money to expand homeless services and outreach in response to the state's House Bill 1365 and the county's own anti-camping ordinance. The money comes from the county's general fund.
