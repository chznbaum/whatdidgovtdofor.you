If you rent in Saint Paul and your landlord won't make a required repair, you can arrange it and take the cost off your rent. Give written notice of what's broken and your intent to deduct the cost, and also call, text, email or use the rental portal if available. The landlord has 14 days to complete the repair. If the landlord neither finishes it nor disputes it in writing and arranges an inspection, you can do the work when no license is required or hire someone, with a license when the law requires one. Give the landlord a receipt before deducting the cost; the landlord may reimburse you instead. The limit is $500 or half a month's rent, whichever is more.

If the landlord disputes the repair, you can proceed only if an inspector confirms it is required or no inspection occurs within 30 days. Landlords cannot retaliate. The law excludes deliberate or negligent tenant-caused damage, certain owner-occupied units, bed-and-breakfasts, short-term rentals and hotels. The city says it took effect Nov. 10, 2026, and is still writing procedures.

Renters did the work on this one. The Safe Homes St. Paul coalition gathered the petition signatures. In August the City Council voted 5–2 to keep the measure off the ballot, citing conflicts with state law. The group sued, and the courts ordered the question onto the ballot. On Nov. 3, voters approved it 92,045 to 24,469. Landlord groups opposed it. Duluth passed a similar law in 2025.
