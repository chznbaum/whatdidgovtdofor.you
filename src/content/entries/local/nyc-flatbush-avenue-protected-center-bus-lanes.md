If you ride the B41, B67, B45 or B103 on Flatbush Avenue between Livingston Street and Grand Army Plaza, your bus now travels in barrier-protected lanes down the middle of the street. You board from six concrete islands with shelters and benches. The lanes are open to buses around the clock, and you do not have to do anything to use them.

Before the redesign, buses on Flatbush moved at under 4 miles per hour at rush hour. The city has not published new speed figures yet. It says it will watch how the lanes work over the coming months and adjust them. The project also added about 29,000 square feet of space for people on foot, new crossing signals and streetlights, and loading zones for deliveries.

Bus riders organized for the change, and Council Members Shahana Hanif, Crystal Hudson, Lincoln Restler and Rita Joseph urged DOT in March 2025 to choose center-running lanes. Riders Alliance senior organizer Jolyse Race helped lead that campaign. Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez proposed the plan in June 2025, after public outreach that began in 2022, and the first lanes were painted that fall. Under Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Commissioner Mike Flynn led the reconstruction that began in April 2026 and opened on November 17. NYC DOT designed and built the project in-house. The city calls it the first step toward full bus rapid transit on Flatbush by 2030 and has started outreach on the next stretch, from Grand Army Plaza to Avenue H.
