New York City is building flood barriers along Red Hook's most flood-prone edges at Atlantic Basin and Beard Street. The two-mile system includes nearly 1.5 miles of floodwalls, 10 floodgates, sewer upgrades and streets raised by up to three feet. It lifts protection from about four feet above sea level at the lowest ground to 10 feet. The project also extends the Brooklyn Waterfront Greenway by 1.2 miles, rebuilds Todd Triangle and plants nearly 200 trees. Residents do not need to apply.

The barrier has limits. It is designed for a storm with a 1-in-10 chance of occurring each year, not a Sandy-sized storm. Van Brunt Street beyond Reed Street remains largely outside it. Researchers quoted by ProPublica and the New York Amsterdam News say the project falls short of the protection the city's own study found Red Hook will need as seas rise.

After Sandy, Mayor Michael Bloomberg's administration proposed a Red Hook flood system in 2013. Mayor Bill de Blasio commissioned it in 2015 after he and Gov. Andrew Cuomo announced the initial $100 million plan. Resilient Red Hook petitioned the city for flood protection for more than a decade, and Council Member Alexa Avilés pushed to get the project done. As costs rose, Mayor Eric Adams's administration and FEMA added about $100 million. FEMA has awarded nearly $80 million; the city also allocated $10 million in federal HUD disaster-recovery funds, and city capital pays most of the rest. The state homeland security agency administers FEMA's grant. Construction began in August 2025 and is due to finish in summer 2028.
