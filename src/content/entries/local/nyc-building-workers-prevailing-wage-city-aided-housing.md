Since 2012, NYC has required prevailing wages for building service workers at certain large projects receiving at least $1 million in discretionary city aid. Affordable housing and nonprofit residential developers were exempt. This law removed those exemptions for most projects. Starting April 2, 2020, most new or preserved housing with 120 or more apartments and that much aid must pay the wage and benefit rates the city Comptroller sets each year.

You don’t apply. The requirement is part of the project’s city financing agreement. Developers certify compliance to the city and Comptroller, which can investigate complaints. At qualifying preservation projects—including projects under 120 units and certain deeply affordable 120-plus projects—workers instead get a “protected wage”: no less than their wage and benefits when the city aid arrived, plus annual increases.

Council Member Rafael Espinal introduced the bill after helping set the same standard in the East New York rezoning. 32BJ SEIU campaigned for it for years: union president Héctor Figueroa started the push, and his successor Kyle Bragg led the final drive. The Council approved it on Nov. 14, 2019, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it Dec. 4. The Council estimated $46.3 million a year in added city capital spending once fully in effect. Housing groups warned about pressure on deeply affordable projects. The law excludes supportive housing and converted public housing workers; qualifying smaller and deeply affordable preservation projects get the protected wage instead.
