If you wait for a bus in New York City, there's a good chance your stop has nowhere to sit. The city has more than 15,000 bus stops. About 8,750 of them have the public sidewalk or island space for seating and still have none: nearly two-thirds of the stops where seating fits. Each eligible stop is set to get a bench or, where there's less room, a leaning bar.

Riders don't need to do anything. The Department of Transportation said the expanded installation schedule would start in November 2025. It plans to add seating at about 875 stops a year for 10 years and to maintain the seating already in place. A long wait standing up is hardest on older riders, people with disabilities, pregnant riders and parents with young children.

Mayor Eric Adams and Transportation Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez announced the $40 million program in October 2025, after New Yorkers advocated for more seating and shelters. Manhattan Community Board 4, for example, had asked DOT for more bus-stop benches and shelters. When Mayor Zohran Mamdani took office, the program continued. His July 2026 bus plan kept the goal of 875 stops a year and seating at every stop where it fits by 2035.
