When you shop for a hotel room in New York City, the price you see first has to be the real price. Under a city rule that took effect February 21, 2026, hotels and booking sites can't advertise a nightly rate and then add "resort," "destination," or "hospitality service" fees at checkout. Only government taxes and fees can be left out of the advertised total, and the business has to tell you what those are before you pay. The rule also covers New York City residents booking hotels outside the city.

You don't have to do anything to get this protection. A business that hides mandatory fees is breaking the city's consumer protection law. Fines start at $525 and reach $3,500 for repeat violations. In March 2026, the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection warned more than 760 licensed hotels to follow the rule.

A second part of the rule is still coming. Starting January 22, 2027, hotels must explain their deposit and card-hold policies up front: how much they usually hold, when they can keep it, and about how long it takes to come back to you.

DCWP proposed the rule under Mayor Eric Adams, modeled on a federal hotel-fee rule. Council Member Gale Brewer worked with the department on the issue before the rule was finalized. Mayor Zohran Mamdani then ordered a citywide junk-fee crackdown, and Commissioner Sam Levine adopted the rule in January 2026. Economists estimated that the hotel-search time saved on bookings covered by the rule would be worth $46.4 million to $67.4 million in 2026.
