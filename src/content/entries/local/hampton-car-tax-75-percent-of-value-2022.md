Car values usually go down every year. In 2022 they went up. Pandemic chip shortages left dealers short of new cars, used-car prices soared, and Hampton's Commissioner of the Revenue reported that local automobile and truck values had risen an average 44%. Virginia law requires the commissioner to start with fair market value, so car tax bills were headed for a big jump.

To head that off, Hampton taxed most passenger cars and trucks under 2 tons on 75% of their value for 2022. With the rate at $4.50 per $100, the ratio works out to about $3.38 per $100 of fair market value before state personal property tax relief. A vehicle whose value rose 44% saw its taxable value rise about 8% instead. You didn't have to apply: the cut was built into both 2022 bills for qualifying vehicles. The resolution said the lower taxable values also made the state's personal property tax relief go further.

Commissioner of the Revenue Ross Mugler approached the council and recommended the lower ratio. The City Council, led by Mayor Donnie Tuck, approved it 6-0 on March 9, 2022. Hampton's council-approved budget attributed the room for tax relief to strong revenue growth. It raised no tax or fee rates and still funded raises for city workers and school employees.
