New York City's rent freeze programs protect you from most future rent increases if you qualify. SCRIE is for renters 62 and older. DRIE is for adults who receive a qualifying disability benefit, including SSI, SSDI, certain VA or USPS disability benefits, or disability-related Medicaid after receiving SSI or SSDI. This law raises the most your household can earn and still qualify from $50,000 to $75,000. Your rent also has to be more than a third of your household's income, and you have to live in a rent-controlled, rent-stabilized, or similar regulated apartment.

You do have to apply, at nyc.gov/rentfreeze or by calling 311. The higher limit applies back to July 1, 2026. If you were turned down for earning too much after that date, the city will look at your application again. Your landlord gets a property tax credit to cover the rent increase you no longer pay. A Council report estimated the city would collect about $1.47 million less in property tax in 2027.

The $50,000 limit had been in place since 2014. In May 2026, the state budget allowed the city to raise it, after Gov. Kathy Hochul proposed the change. Council Member Farah Louis introduced the city's bill that July, and more than 30 colleagues signed on. The Council passed it on Oct. 22. It became law on Nov. 21 without the mayor's signature, under the City Charter's 30-day rule.
