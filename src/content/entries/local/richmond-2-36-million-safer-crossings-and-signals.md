This ordinance brought $2,358,999 from the Virginia Department of Transportation into Richmond's capital budget. The money comes through the federal Highway Safety Improvement Program, and it pays for five safety projects run by the Department of Public Works. The largest share, $892,636, goes to Hull Street at Belt Boulevard. The rest pays for three things. $420,475 replaces signals hung on roadside posts with signals on overhead mast arms that drivers can see better. $292,222 pays for pedestrian hybrid beacons and rapid flashing beacons that help people cross busy roads. And $753,666 pays for two phases of crossing upgrades at intersections with traffic lights.

At Hull Street and Belt Boulevard, the city said the work would add new sidewalks, accessible curb ramps, countdown signals and push buttons, and high-visibility crosswalks. It would also build part of a planned shared-use path and make it easier to reach the GRTC bus stops there. You don't have to apply for any of this: the upgrades are built into the streets.

Mayor Levar Stoney's administration brought the ordinance to City Council on Sept. 12, 2022. The Planning Commission recommended it a week later, and the council adopted it on Sept. 26, 2022. Richmond reported the signal-visibility project complete in March 2023. By March 31, 2025, city reports listed the other four projects as complete or construction complete, with administrative closeout still underway at Hull Street and Belt Boulevard.
