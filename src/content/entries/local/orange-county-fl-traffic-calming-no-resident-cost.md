If speeding is a problem on a local residential or collector street served by Orange County's program, residents no longer have to fund the installation themselves. Under a policy dating to 1994, benefiting property owners had to cover the initial installation cost for speed humps through a Municipal Service Taxing Unit, or MSTU, charged on their property tax bills. Resolution 2026-M-21 eliminated that required contribution, and the county now fully funds eligible traffic-calming improvements.

The update also adopts a broader Traffic Calming and Safety Toolbox. It includes traffic circles, chicanes, speed tables, raised intersections, curb extensions, and other treatments. The county says engineers choose among them using traffic, speed, and volume thresholds. Residents can start a request through 311 or their county commissioner's office, and Public Works staff study the street to determine whether it qualifies.

The Public Works Department, led by director Joseph Kunkel, submitted the update. Its memorandum said the old policy needed modern methods and more equitable access to safety improvements. The Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Mayor Jerry Demings, approved it 6-0 on June 2, 2026, as part of its consent agenda.
