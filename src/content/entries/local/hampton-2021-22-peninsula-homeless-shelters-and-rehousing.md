From July 2021 through June 2022, Hampton budgeted a $1,060,591 state-and-federal grant for homeless services across the Virginia Peninsula. The city later reported $750,671 in actual spending for the grant in fiscal 2022. The grant supported emergency shelter, rapid rehousing (including for veterans), help that kept people from losing their homes, and a coordinated central intake system where people could ask for help.

The grant's service area covered Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County. Hampton's Department of Human Services was the lead agency for the region's homelessness consortium and the grant's fiscal agent. Partners included the Salvation Army Peninsula, the Hampton-Newport News Community Services Board, Menchville House and the LGBT Life Center.

Of the $1,060,591 award, $951,413 came from Virginia's general fund and $109,178 was federal money. The city did not have to provide a cash or in-kind match. Hampton had received this grant every year since 2014–15. City Council, led by Mayor Donnie Tuck, accepted the 2021–22 award 7-0 on Aug. 11, 2021.
