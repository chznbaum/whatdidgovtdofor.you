Child care for a baby or toddler is the most expensive kind. In 2024, an infant or toddler spot at a New York City child care center cost about $26,000 a year on average. Through the Birth-to-2 pilot, some families get that care free. Children from 6 weeks to 2 years old can attend participating community child care centers at no cost, and seats are paid for up to 10 hours a day, year-round.

Any New York City family can apply, whatever their income or immigration status. You apply in person at a participating center near where you live. Bring proof of address, your child's birth certificate or passport, and their shot records. Seats go first come, first served. When enrollment opened, the city announced more than 200 seats at 16 sites. The City Council later reported that capacity had been reduced to 182 seats and 139 were filled by February 2026.

The City Council added the $10 million pilot to the city's 2026 budget. Speaker Adrienne Adams and Finance Chair Justin Brannan negotiated the agreement with Mayor Eric Adams, while Education Chair Rita Joseph also worked to win the early-childhood funding. The Council passed the budget unanimously. Chancellor Melissa Aviles-Ramos's early childhood division opened enrollment that December, and care began Jan. 5, 2026. In May 2026, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Chancellor Kamar Samuels continued it for 2026–27, then expected to serve more than 120 children at 10 programs. NYC Public Schools now lists 12 participating programs at schools.nyc.gov/brightstarts.
