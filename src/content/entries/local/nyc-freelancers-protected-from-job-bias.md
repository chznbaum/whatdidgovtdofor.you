If you freelance or work as an independent contractor in New York City, the city's Human Rights Law protects you the way it protects employees. A business that hires you can't discriminate against you or harass you because of your race, gender, religion, disability, immigration status, sexual orientation or other protected traits, and it can't punish you for speaking up. You can also ask for reasonable accommodations, for example for a disability, a pregnancy or a religious practice.

You don't need a formal contract to be covered. If something goes wrong, you can file a complaint with the NYC Commission on Human Rights or go to court. Contractors also count toward the four workers that bring a business under the law, so more small businesses are covered.

Council Member Brad Lander sponsored the bill and had pushed to protect freelancers since 2015, working with the Freelancers Union. The Council passed it 47–3 on Sept. 12, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law without his signature in October 2019. It took effect Jan. 11, 2020.
