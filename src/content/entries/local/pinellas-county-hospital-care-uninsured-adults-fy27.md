If you live in Pinellas County, are 18 to 64, have no health insurance and can't get Medicaid or Medicare, the county's health program gives you a regular doctor at a Florida Department of Health clinic. It also covers tests, prescriptions and mental health care. These agreements help pay for some care the clinics cannot provide. For authorized patients, they partially reimburse BayCare, Orlando Health Bayfront or AdventHealth North Pinellas for outpatient surgery, inpatient procedures or rehab coordinated with a primary care provider. The same agreements serve people in the county's Health Care for the Homeless Program.

To get this help, you have to be enrolled first. Apply online at pinellashsapp.org or call (727) 464-4200. You'll need proof of where you live, your income and your assets. The program does not pay for emergency room visits.

The county will pay the three hospital providers up to $1.73 million for the year that started Oct. 1, 2026. BayCare gets about $1.19 million, Bayfront about $482,000 and AdventHealth about $56,000, based on how much care each provided. The county used to budget $3 million a year for this. Starting in 2026 it lowered the amount after reviewing how much care clients were actually using. The money comes from the county's general fund, and each year's share has to be approved in the budget.

The Board of County Commissioners, chaired by Dave Eggers, approved the agreements on Sept. 24, 2026.
