Richmond agreed to help pay for a building of 128 apartments for seniors on Walmsley Boulevard, just west of Broad Rock Boulevard. Under the city's grant terms, renters' incomes must average 60% of the area median. The building, now called The Cascade, is part of Foundry Creek, a 344-unit complex for lower-income renters. Foundry Creek also has 216 family apartments, which the city backed with an earlier grant in 2023. The complex opened in June 2026. At the opening, about half of the senior apartments were leased.

The money comes from Richmond's Affordable Housing Performance Grant program. Instead of cutting rents directly, the city pays the owner yearly grants that offset most of the extra property tax the new building creates. That runs for 15 years, with a possible 15 more. The city estimates the grant for the senior building at about $331,000 a year. The money comes out of new tax revenue the building itself creates, so no current city service is cut to pay for it. The developer said the program made the $88 million project financially possible.

Seniors apply to rent through the building's leasing office, like any apartment. Adding the grant for the senior building raised the project from 276 planned units to 344.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the ordinance, and the City Council adopted it on June 24, 2024.
