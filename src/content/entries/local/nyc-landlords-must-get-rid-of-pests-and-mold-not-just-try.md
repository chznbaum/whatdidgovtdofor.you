Mold, mice, roaches and rats can trigger asthma attacks. New York City's 2018 asthma-allergen law, Local Law 55, set rules for landlords to deal with them, but it often asked only for "reasonable efforts" or "reasonable measures." This 2019 law took that wording out. Now a landlord must keep your home free of pests and mold, prevent problems they can reasonably see coming, and quickly fix both the problem and the defect causing it, such as a leak or a crack where mice get in.

The law covers buildings with three or more apartments. You don't apply for anything. Tell your landlord about mold or pests. If they don't fix it, or don't fix it safely, call 311 or file online, and the city's housing agency (HPD) can inspect and issue violations. The law took effect right away and applies back to the date the 2018 law started. It also lets HPD decide whether to take enforcement action when the Health Department refers an uncorrected order to it. Under the 2018 law, HPD had to act.

Council Member Carlina Rivera introduced the bill in April 2018. Council Members Margaret Chin, Laurie Cumbo, Diana Ayala and Ben Kallos cosponsored it. The Committee on Housing and Buildings approved a revised version 10–0 in February 2019, and the full Council passed it on Feb. 13, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio returned it unsigned, and it became law on March 16, 2019.
