If your Richmond home's assessment jumps, you won't have to pay the whole increase right away. Starting with the 2028 tax year, you pay up to 105% of last year's tax bill and can put off the rest. For example, if your bill goes from $1,000 to $1,200, you pay $1,050 and can defer $150. Deferred taxes don't count as late, so they won't keep you out of other city programs.

You have to apply each year by Jan. 14. To qualify, the home has to be the only place you live, its assessment can be no more than twice the city median (about $650,000 at the 2026 median), and you have to be current on your property taxes. People already in the city's tax relief or freeze programs for seniors and people with disabilities can't also use this one. This isn't forgiveness. The city puts a lien on the home and charges 2% interest or the IRS rate, whichever is lower. You owe the balance when you sell, within 10 years, or, after the last owner dies, within a year. No home can defer more than $25,000 in total.

Council member Sarah Abubaker led the push, pointing out that assessments were rising fast even though the tax rate hadn't changed since 2008. Mayor Danny Avula's administration warned about the cost and the extra finance staff the program would need, so council moved the start from 2027 to 2028 and asked for an implementation plan first. Council adopted it on June 8, 2026.
