If you deliver food or groceries through an app in New York City, the app has to pay you at least $22.13 an hour for your time, not counting tips. Apps can't use your tips to meet the minimum. The rate goes up with inflation every April. You don't have to sign up for anything; the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection enforces the rule.

Before the rule, app delivery workers earned an average of just $5.39 an hour before tips, according to the department. The city says workers have taken home an extra $1.2 billion since enforcement began in December 2023.

The City Council set this in motion in 2021 with a law led by Council Member Brad Lander that required the city to study delivery workers' pay and set a minimum. Mayor Eric Adams's administration adopted the rule in 2023, starting at $17.96 an hour, and defended it when the delivery apps sued; enforcement began after state courts ruled for the city.

In January 2026, new Council laws extended the same minimum to grocery delivery apps and added requirements for tipping options, faster payment, and itemized pay statements.
