If a store, restaurant, bar, theater, stadium or museum in New York City collects your face scan, fingerprint, eye scan, voiceprint or other biometric data, it has to tell you. A clear sign in plain language must be posted near every customer entrance. And no business covered by the law may sell, lease, trade or share that data for anything of value. Banks are exempt from the sign rule, and so are businesses that only record ordinary security video they don't run through identification software and don't share except with law enforcement. Government agencies aren't covered.

You don't have to do anything to get this protection, but you can enforce it yourself. If a business sells or trades your data, you can sue for $500 per careless violation or $5,000 per intentional or reckless one, plus lawyer's fees. For a missing sign, you first give the business written notice and 30 days to fix it; if it doesn't, you can sue for $500 per violation.

Council Member Ritchie Torres of the Bronx introduced the bill in 2018. The Consumer Affairs committee, chaired by Andrew Cohen, approved it unanimously, and the full Council passed it on Dec. 10, 2020. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, and it became law on Jan. 10, 2021. It took effect July 9, 2021.
