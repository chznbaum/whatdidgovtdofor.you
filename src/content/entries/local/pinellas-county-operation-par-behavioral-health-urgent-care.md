Pinellas County is paying for a new urgent care where people can walk in the same day for mental health or addiction needs that aren't life-threatening. Staff there will assess you, help you through a crisis, and connect you to ongoing care. The center will offer outpatient counseling, psychiatry, medication management, and medication for opioid addiction. It will also provide basic medical care and help coordinating everything else.

Operation PAR, a longtime Tampa Bay addiction treatment nonprofit, will run the center. The county will pay up to $25 million: the first year covers capital and start-up costs, and the next three years cover full service, through September 2030. A deed restriction requires the property to be used for substance use prevention, treatment, or support services through 2046. The money comes from the county's share of the national opioid settlements with drug makers and distributors, so it doesn't come out of other services.

Pinellas County Human Services ran an open competition for the money in 2025. Eleven groups applied, and Operation PAR scored highest after interviews by the county's opioid abatement advisory board. The board recommended the final deal in July 2026. Commissioner Kathleen Peters, who served on the advisory board, helped narrow a larger proposal to focus on urgent care and moved its approval. On Sept. 24, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners approved it unanimously. Money after the first year still has to be approved in each year's county budget.
