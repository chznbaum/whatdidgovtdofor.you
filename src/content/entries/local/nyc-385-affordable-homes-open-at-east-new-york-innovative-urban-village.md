The two-building first phase of Innovative Urban Village, on the Christian Cultural Center's campus in East New York, opened in October 2026 with 385 permanently affordable tenant apartments, plus one apartment for the superintendent. The tenant apartments have income limits set at 30% to 80% of area median income. Ninety-four are reserved for families who experienced homelessness because of domestic violence and receive support services on site. The buildings use all-electric heating and cooling and have solar panels and green roofs.

Of the 385 tenant apartments, 291 were offered through New York State's housing lottery. The other 94 are supportive apartments filled through referrals from homeless-service agencies. The next phase, with 453 more affordable homes and a child care center, topped out when Phase 1A opened. Another planned phase would add 599 homes, including 206 for seniors. The full plan calls for nearly 2,000 affordable homes across 10 buildings.

Rev. A.R. Bernard's church selected Gotham Organization as its developer in 2018. Council Member Charles Barron, who represented the area, spent years negotiating for more affordability and community benefits before the City Council approved the rezoning in November 2022. Council Member Farah Louis also advocated with Bernard for city housing capital. In February 2024, New York State Homes and Community Renewal, led by RuthAnne Visnauskas, approved first-phase financing. HCR provided about $47 million through the Housing Finance Agency and allocated federal tax credits expected to generate more than $115 million in equity. The city's housing agency, then led by Adolfo Carrión Jr., added about $47 million.
