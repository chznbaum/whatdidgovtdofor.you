Richmond's sworn police employees now work under a union contract covering their pay and working conditions. Ordinance 2024-122 approved the first agreement between the city and the Richmond Coalition of Police, which officers chose to represent them. The council adopted the ordinance on May 13, 2024; the agreement took effect July 1 and runs through June 30, 2027.

The city estimated the police agreement would cost about $34 million over three years. Its adopted 2025 budget included $9.1 million for police, fire and emergency services raises. All sworn Richmond police employees are in the bargaining unit, so the contract covers them without an application. The city still lists the agreement, and its adopted 2027 budget fully funds its union contracts. That budget provides an average compensation increase of about 6.73% for sworn police and fire personnel, including negotiated pay changes and step increases, effective July 2026.

Virginia enacted a law in 2020, effective May 1, 2021, allowing local governments to bargain collectively with their employees. Richmond authorized bargaining in 2022. Councilmember Reva Trammell was lead patron of the city's collective bargaining ordinances, and Council President Kristen Nye co-led the effort. RCOP then negotiated with Mayor Levar Stoney's administration, and Stoney was the patron of the contract ordinance. The council approved it 8–0 alongside the city's first contracts for firefighters and administrative and technical workers.
