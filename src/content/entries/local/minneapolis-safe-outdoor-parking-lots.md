If you're living in your car in Minneapolis, these laws create a path for safer places to spend the night. They change the city's licensing and zoning codes to allow "safe outdoor parking": existing off-street lots where people can sleep in their vehicles from 5 p.m. to 8 a.m. Staff must be there whenever a lot is open. The ordinances also aim to connect people with services and help them move indoors.

A nonprofit, a religious group, or a government agency can run a lot. Each one needs a city business license and an interim use permit, which takes a public hearing and lasts up to five years. Operators must have a safety plan approved by police and provide accessible bathrooms and trash pickup. The rules take effect Jan. 1, 2027. No lots have been chosen yet, and the city isn't required to pay for them. The idea follows Duluth's program, which runs mostly on donations and grants.

Council Member Jason Chavez wrote the ordinances with Aisha Chughtai, Aurin Chowdhury and Soren Stevenson. The City Council passed the licensing ordinance 9-4 and the zoning ordinance 13-0 on Oct. 29, 2026. Mayor Jacob Frey returned both without his signature, so they became law on Nov. 5 anyway.
