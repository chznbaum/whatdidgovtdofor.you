In New York City, a landlord generally needs a court warrant or order to evict you. The city's unlawful-eviction law generally protects occupants who have a lease or have lawfully lived in the home for at least 30 days. It covers force, threats, service shutoffs, lock changes without a key, and other conduct meant to make them leave. Since October 2025, an owner's unlawful eviction or attempt also counts as tenant harassment.

That gives protected occupants another tool. A lawful occupant can bring a harassment claim, called an HP proceeding, in Housing Court. The court can order the owner to stop and impose a civil penalty of up to $10,000 for each affected dwelling unit. You can also report harassment by calling 311. The law also required HPD to include in a report due Feb. 27, 2026, a recommendation on whether an owner's unlawful-eviction history should count in the city's "certification of no harassment" program.

Council Member Sandy Nurse sponsored the bill and introduced it in March 2024 in conjunction with Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso. It was the first of her No Lockouts Act bills to pass; Nurse said illegal eviction is abuse and should be reflected in the city's harassment definition. At a 2024 hearing, HPD agreed with the bill's goal but raised concerns. After changes, the Council passed it on Sept. 25, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams did not sign it, and it became law on Oct. 25, 2025 without his signature.
