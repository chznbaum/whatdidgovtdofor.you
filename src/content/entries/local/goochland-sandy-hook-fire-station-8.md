Goochland County is building a new fire and rescue station in Sandy Hook, on county-owned land along Whitehall, Dogtown and Sandy Hook roads. Station 8 will have three drive-through bays and living quarters for crews. When it opens, a fire engine and an ambulance will be staffed there around the clock by career and volunteer providers. It is the county's first new emergency response district in more than 60 years.

You don't need to do anything to benefit. The station is intended to put emergency crews closer to the Sandy Hook area and shorten response times. A temporary station began providing daytime ambulance service there in 2023 and expanded to 24-hour operations in February 2025. By April 2026, the county said it had answered 1,620 calls and quickly become the county's third-busiest ambulance.

The project dates to fire-rescue reports from the 1970s. The county credits former Supervisors James Bowles Sr., William Quarles Jr. and Manuel Alvarez, along with current Supervisor Neil Spoonhower, with years of advocacy. On March 3, 2026, the Board of Supervisors voted unanimously to award Gulf Seaboard a $9.45 million construction contract and approve a 10% contingency. The county had already appropriated about $12.8 million for the full project. Ground was broken in April, and substantial completion is expected in late spring or early summer 2027.
