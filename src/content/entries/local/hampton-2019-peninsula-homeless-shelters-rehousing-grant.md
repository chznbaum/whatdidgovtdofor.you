For the year that ran from July 2019 to June 2020, Hampton took in $1,047,754 from the state and federal governments to help people on the Virginia Peninsula who were homeless or about to be. The money went to the Virginia Peninsula Collaborative, a group of local agencies that covers Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County. Hampton's Department of Human Services manages the money for the group.

The grant budget put $327,029 toward rapid rehousing, which moves people who were evicted or became homeless back into housing, and $295,395 toward prevention, which helps people at risk keep their homes. Another $250,000 was budgeted for shelter operations. The rest was budgeted for street outreach, a crisis hotline and intake that sends people to the right help, regional planning, and a shared data system. To get help, people go through the region's intake process. Partners included LINK of Hampton Roads, Transitions and the Salvation Army Peninsula.

The grant comes through the Virginia Department of Housing and Community Development, which combines state money with federal Emergency Solutions Grant funds. Hampton has received it every year since 2014–15 and didn't have to put up matching money. With Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding, the City Council approved the grant 7–0 on Aug. 14, 2019.
