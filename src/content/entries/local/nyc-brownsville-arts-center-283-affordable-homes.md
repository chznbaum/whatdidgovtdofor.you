A new building is going up on a vacant city-owned lot at 366 Rockaway Avenue in Brownsville, Brooklyn. It will contain 283 homes: 282 income-restricted rental apartments and one superintendent’s unit. The rentals range from studios to three-bedrooms for households earning 30% to 70% of area median income. In 2025, that was $43,740 to $102,060 for a family of three. Forty-three apartments are set aside for formerly homeless households. The building will also have a 28,000-square-foot community arts center.

To rent an income-restricted apartment, you will need to apply when the city opens its housing lottery, generally through NYC Housing Connect. The building is expected to be finished in December 2027.

The project grew out of the city’s Brownsville Plan, and HPD selected the developers through a competitive process. Council Member Darlene Mealy pushed HPD to remove a planned 80%-of-AMI tier, keeping every income-restricted apartment at or below 70% of AMI. Former Council Member Alicka Ampry-Samuel provided $2 million in Council capital money, and Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso provided $1 million.

Financing closed in July 2025. HPD, then led by Acting Commissioner Ahmed Tigani, and the Housing Development Corporation, led by Eric Enderlin, provided $88.1 million in housing subsidies. The Department of Cultural Affairs under Laurie Cumbo provided $8.2 million for the arts center; Empire State Development under Hope Knight and NYSERDA under Doreen Harris also funded the project. Mayor Eric Adams’s administration brought the city financing to closing, and construction began that month.
