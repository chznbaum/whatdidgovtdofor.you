St. Petersburg is putting $40.85 million in federal disaster recovery money into six apartment projects across the city. Together they will create or preserve 645 apartments for households earning no more than 80% of the area's median income, and the homes must stay affordable for at least 99 years. The largest award is $15 million for JR Tower, a 12-story building on Central Avenue with 150 affordable apartments. Hartford and Saratoga Apartments, run with the St. Petersburg Housing Authority, sets aside 81 of its 109 homes for the lowest-income renters. Trailside Estates II will have 94 apartments for people 55 and older, and The Portland, an existing building downtown, will get a major repair.

The apartments are not available yet. Renters will have to meet each building's income limits when leasing begins. Construction is scheduled to finish between January 2028 and April 2029.

The money comes from HUD's Community Development Block Grant Disaster Recovery program. Congress funded it after Hurricanes Idalia and Helene, and St. Petersburg's share is about $159.8 million. The city set aside $48 million for affordable rentals and ran a competition: 34 applications sought about $246.7 million, and six were chosen. The City Council approved the awards 5–0 on Oct. 1, 2026.
