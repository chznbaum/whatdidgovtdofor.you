In heavy rain, water runs off pavement and fills the sewers until it backs up into streets. To ease this in East Flatbush and Canarsie, the city built 906 infiltration basins and 291 rain gardens along the sidewalks. Both catch rainwater and let it soak into the ground instead of pouring into the sewers. A rain garden can hold up to 2,500 gallons. An infiltration basin works the same way but has a surface you can walk on.

The city estimates that together they will capture about 122.5 million gallons of stormwater a year. The project also planted 223 trees, which NYC Parks will look after. The trees add shade in two neighborhoods the city counts among its most heat-vulnerable. Residents don't need to do anything to benefit, because the basins and gardens are already in the ground.

The $42.3 million project was carried out by the city's Department of Environmental Protection and Department of Design and Construction. DEP Commissioner Rohit Aggarwala led the green infrastructure work, while DDC Commissioner Thomas Foley's agency managed construction. Mayor Eric Adams announced that the work was complete on Earth Day, April 22, 2025, as part of the city's wider spending on stormwater infrastructure.
