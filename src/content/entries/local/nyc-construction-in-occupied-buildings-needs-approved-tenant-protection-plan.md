If your landlord plans construction, renovation or partial demolition while people still live in your New York City building, the owner needs a tenant protection plan for that specific site. The plan must lay out how tenants stay safe: clear exits, fire safety, control of dust, debris, lead and asbestos, limits on noise, and keeping heat, hot water and power on. The Department of Buildings can't issue a permit for the work until it approves the plan. The owner must also tell the department in writing before work begins.

The law also put the city on a schedule. It must inspect 10% of these sites within seven days after work begins, up from 5% before. It must follow up every 180 days until the job is finished, and it must inspect within 10 days of any complaint about the work. Tenants don't have to apply for any of this. Since the 2022 Building Code, a special inspector must also check work at occupied multiple dwellings against the plan, with limited exceptions.

Council Member Carlina Rivera introduced the bill in November 2018. It was one of 17 bills in a tenant anti-displacement package aimed at landlords who use construction to push tenants out. The Council passed it on May 8, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, so it became law on June 8, 2019, and it took effect on March 8, 2020.
