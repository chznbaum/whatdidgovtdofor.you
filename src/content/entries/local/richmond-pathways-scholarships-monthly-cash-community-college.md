If you graduate from a Richmond public high school, have financial need and plan to attend a Virginia community college, you can apply for help. Selected Pathways applicants receive a scholarship and may also receive a monthly cash allowance; participants get mentoring and other support. The assistance can go toward a career certificate, training in a skilled trade, or credits that transfer to a four-year college.

The program is run by the Great Aspirations Scholarship Program (GRASP), a nonprofit whose advisers already work in city high schools. To apply, you meet with your school's GRASP adviser, file the FAFSA or the state's VASA aid form, show financial need, and plan to enroll at a Virginia community college. The Mayor's Office, corporate donors and community partners help fund the awards. GRASP's current page lists applications as open for the spring 2026 graduating class.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed starting the program with money left over from the city's 2022 budget. The City Council, led by then-president Michael J. Jones, adopted the ordinance 9–0 on April 10, 2023, moving $1.75 million into a new line for GRASP's Pathways Program. The startup money came from the city's FY2022 fund-balance surplus and had already been set aside as a special-purpose reserve. Later that year, the program won a national policy contest run by NewDEAL, a network of state and local officials.
