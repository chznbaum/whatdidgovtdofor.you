DeKalb County treats its tap water at the Scott Candler Water Treatment Plant in Doraville, its only plant, drawing from the Chattahoochee River. County staff say its filter plant, running since 2005, often has single points of failure: when one piece breaks, nothing takes over.

This contract tears out that filter plant and builds a new pump station that sends treated water into the county's pipes. It also adds two clearwells, which are large tanks that hold clean water ready to use. Each holds 10 million gallons, so the county gains 20 million gallons of stored drinking water. That reserve can keep water flowing after a breakdown, and the site has room for another 20 million gallons later. No signup is needed. The benefit is a water supply that's less likely to fail.

Customers help pay for this work. It is part of a capital plan funded with water and sewer charges, bonds, loans and system cash; commissioners approved 10% yearly water and sewer rate increases through 2034 to support the plan and system operations.

Watershed Management Director Reginald Wells presented the contract to commissioners. They held it twice over the summer before the Public Works and Infrastructure Committee recommended approval. On Aug. 25, 2026, the full board voted 7–0 to hire Ruby-Collins, Inc., the top-scoring of two bidders, to design and build the project for up to $233.8 million. The work is still ahead, so the new storage isn't in service yet.
