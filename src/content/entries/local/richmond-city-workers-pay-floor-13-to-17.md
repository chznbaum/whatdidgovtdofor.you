In July 2022, Richmond raised its minimum pay rate for covered city jobs from $13 to $17 an hour. The rule applied to full- and part-time employees but excluded the Summer Youth Program, special-event workers, interns, and other special employment programs designated by Human Resources. It took effect with the pay period that began July 2. The same ordinance gave eligible city employees a 5% raise and moved the city's general pay ranges up 5%.

Employees covered by these changes did not need to apply. The ordinance changed the city's pay plan directly, so the raises were applied through payroll.

Mayor Levar Stoney proposed the $17 floor and the 5% raise for non-sworn city staff in the budget he presented in March 2022. Police officers and firefighters got a separate step pay plan. Legistar lists Stoney as the ordinance's patron, and the City Council, led by Council President Cynthia Newbille, adopted it 9–0 on May 9, 2022, the night it passed the 2022–23 budget.

Richmond later raised the floor again. The city set it at $20 in July 2024, and the FY 2026–27 pay plan in effect since July 1, 2026, keeps that rate for covered employees.
