Henrico County will put speed cameras in the school crossing zones at all nine of its high schools and at Tuckahoe and Fairfield middle schools. The cameras run only when a zone's warning lights are flashing in the morning and afternoon. They flag only drivers going at least 10 mph over the posted limit. The fine is $100, with no points on your license.

Each violation gets checked before a ticket goes out. A Henrico police officer has to confirm the vehicle's owner and confirm that the lights were flashing. The registered owner gets the ticket but can name the person who was actually driving. By state law, the camera data can't be sold and is deleted on a set schedule. The fine money pays for the camera program, and any excess must go to other local traffic-safety work.

Police Chief Eric English presented the ordinance and recommended its approval. The Henrico Board of Supervisors passed it on Sept. 8, 2026, after a public hearing. Police officers wrote 700 speeding tickets in Henrico school zones in the 2024-25 school year. The cameras are expected to start in early 2027. Police will first explain to the public how the cameras work, and drivers then get a 30-day warning period before fines begin.
