If you own your home in Richmond and are 65 or older, or permanently and totally disabled, the city can cut or erase your real estate tax. Starting in 2027, more people qualify, and many will get bigger cuts. The income limit for the exemption rises from $70,000 to $75,000. Households earning up to $40,000 (up from $30,000) qualify to have the whole tax exempted. People earning $40,001 to $50,000 can get 75% off, $50,001 to $60,000 can get 50% off, and $60,001 to $75,000 can get 25% off. Your net worth still has to be $450,000 or less, not counting your home and up to one acre of land.

You have to apply through the city's Department of Finance. One catch: the filing deadline moves earlier, from December 31 to June 30 of the tax year, so apply sooner than you used to. People who choose the separate tax freeze keep the same limits as before.

Councilmember Stephanie Lynch led the list of patrons. Mayor Danny Avula and six other council members signed on as co-patrons. The council approved the change on November 9, 2026. Richmond pays for this relief from its general budget. The FY2027 budget the council adopted in May set aside $7.4 million for tax relief for older adults and people with disabilities, up from $6.6 million the year before.
