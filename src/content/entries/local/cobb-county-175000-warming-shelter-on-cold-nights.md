On designated cold-weather days, people with nowhere to sleep will have a warm place to go. County commissioners agreed to pay MUST Ministries $175,000 to run warming shelter services for homeless residents on those days.

The warming shelter serves men, women and children. People are asked to arrive by 8 p.m. when it is open. MUST's shelter at 1297 Bells Ferry Road is the county's only winter weather shelter. In January 2026, MUST's CEO said it opens as a warming center whenever the temperature drops below 35 degrees. During that month's 12-day freeze, it expected about 100 people a night, with nearby churches taking in the overflow.

The new agreement gives the shelter more than last winter's $108,000. By that January freeze, the earlier money had already paid for 26 nights, and officials said it would likely run out soon. Chairwoman Lisa Cupid moved to approve the agreement and Commissioner Erick Allen seconded. The board passed it 5–0 on Oct. 27, 2026, and authorized Cupid to sign it.
