If your child qualifies for student transit in New York City, their school hands them a Student OMNY card. It's good for four free rides a day on the subway and on local, limited and Select buses, plus the Staten Island Railway, the Roosevelt Island Tram and the Hudson Rail Link. A free transfer between bus and subway is included. The card works at any hour, every day of the year, including weekends, holidays and summer. The old student MetroCard gave three rides a day, and only on school days between 5:30 a.m. and 8:30 p.m.

You don't apply. Students who qualified for a student MetroCard, based mainly on grade and how far they live from school, get a card from their school, and a lost card is replaced free. In the first month of the 2024–25 school year, students took 2.5 million trips, 36% more than the year before.

Assemblymember Harvey Epstein's Youth Council began raising the limits of student MetroCards in 2019 and pushed for longer hours, weekend use and more rides. Mayor Eric Adams's administration launched the cards with MTA Chair Janno Lieber in July 2024. The city's Education Department, under Chancellor David Banks, pays the MTA $50.5 million a year for the program, and it continues in the 2026–27 school year.
