The Mayo Bridge carries 14th Street over the James River between Shockoe and Manchester. It opened in 1913, and the Federal Highway Administration classifies it as being in poor condition. A chemical reaction is damaging concrete in at least one pier, and the city's federal grant application projected that the bridge could become unusable by 2035 without replacement. The ordinance authorized Richmond to sign an agreement putting the Virginia Department of Transportation in charge of the project. The city recorded a $103,536 match, and the agreement was executed on Jan. 17, 2023.

The scope grew after engineers studied the bridge. The original plan was to replace the deck and other parts above the piers, but structural and ground studies found that at least one pier also needed replacement. VDOT and the city decided to plan a new bridge instead. VDOT now estimates the project at $190 million. The latest concept has one car lane each way, dedicated bike lanes, wider sidewalks, river overlooks and a 25 mph speed limit. VDOT plans to build it beside the existing bridge so the old crossing can stay open for much of construction. The design and construction dates are not final.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance's original patron. Council Vice President Ellen Robertson joined as a co-patron and moved its committee recommendation. The council adopted it unanimously on Dec. 12, 2022.
