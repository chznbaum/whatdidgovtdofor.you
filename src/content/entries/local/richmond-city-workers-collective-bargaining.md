If your City of Richmond job falls into one of the ordinance's five bargaining units, you have the right to join a union that bargains with the city for you. The units cover police, fire and emergency services, labor and trades, professional, and administrative and technical workers. Managers, supervisors, confidential staff and some other categories are excluded. Each unit can bargain over wages, paid and unpaid leave, insurance, hours, retirement, and other working conditions. Temporary workers employed for more than 150 days in a row are covered too.

It has already paid off. The administrative and technical workers' first contract took effect in July 2024 after 98% of voting union members approved it. It raises pay a total of 10.5% over three years and requires the city to show a legitimate reason for discipline. The 2026-27 city budget added $22 million to honor labor deals with all five units.

A 2020 state law let Virginia localities opt in to collective bargaining. Reva Trammell, Kristen Nye, Ellen Robertson, Katherine Jordan, Stephanie Lynch and Michael Jones sponsored the council's original proposal. Trammell, Nye and Lynch then requested the compromise ordinance. Mayor Levar Stoney's administration had proposed a narrower plan covering only trades workers before joining negotiations with council and unions. The final ordinance passed 8–0 on July 25, 2022; Jordan was absent.
