People living with HIV or AIDS can have a hard time keeping a stable home, and losing one makes it harder to stay in care. This agreement reimburses Catholic Charities up to $425,000 a year for three years to operate Mercy House in Hillsborough County. The money covers transitional housing, support services, operations and administration. In the first year, Catholic Charities must provide housing services to at least 30 households.

To qualify for housing assistance, a household must live in Hillsborough County and include a low-income person with AIDS or a related disease. Catholic Charities says Mercy House serves single women and women with children who are homeless, marginally housed, at risk of eviction or leaving a residential setting. Residents pay part of their living expenses based on income. The agency lists 813-375-3933 as the program contact.

The Tampa City Council approved the agreement unanimously on Nov. 19, 2026, at a meeting chaired by Alan Clendenin. The resolution took effect retroactively on Oct. 1 and authorizes the mayor to sign the agreement. The first $425,000 is available, but the remaining $850,000 depends on future federal HOPWA funding. President Donald Trump's fiscal 2027 budget proposed eliminating HOPWA, putting that future funding at risk.
