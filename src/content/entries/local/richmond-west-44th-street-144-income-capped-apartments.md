700 West 44 is a new 144-unit garden apartment community now leasing near Richmond's Forest Hill neighborhood. It has one-, two- and three-bedroom homes. The property's application page lists income-limit tiers at 30%, 40%, 50% and 60% of the area median income, with dollar limits that vary by household size. Renters apply through the property's website or leasing office.

The 2023 grant agreement requires at least 144 apartments and restricts their rents and occupancy to an average designation of 60% of area median income for 30 years. The community has a clubhouse, fitness center, playground, dog park and bicycle storage.

The owner pays the full property-tax bill first. The city then sends its Economic Development Authority the amount above the property's $1,320 annual tax baseline, and the authority pays that amount to the owner. Richmond estimates the yearly grant at $242,605 once the project qualifies. The city keeps its existing baseline revenue but gives up the added property-tax revenue created by the buildings. Payments after the first 15 years depend on the owner documenting at least $1.44 million in capital improvements.

Mayor Levar Stoney was the ordinance's patron, and his administration brought the agreement to City Council. The council adopted it 7-0 on Nov. 6, 2023, with Councilmember Stephanie Lynch abstaining.
