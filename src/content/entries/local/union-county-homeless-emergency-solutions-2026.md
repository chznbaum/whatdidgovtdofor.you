Each year, Union County gets federal money from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development to help people who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless. The county doesn't run most of these services itself. It pays outside agencies to do the work. This plan lets those agencies spend up to $358,538 between Aug. 1, 2026 and July 31, 2027.

The county's notice says the money is meant to improve emergency shelters and transitional housing for homeless individuals and families. Its annual plan also includes street outreach, shelter nights, data work, rapid rehousing, and help with rent or utilities to prevent homelessness. If you need help, you don't apply to this grant directly. You go through the shelters and agencies the county funds.

Commissioner Stanley J. Neron brought the resolution, and the county's nine commissioners approved it unanimously on Aug. 27, 2026. The county's plan says service providers must document a dollar-for-dollar match when they seek reimbursement.
