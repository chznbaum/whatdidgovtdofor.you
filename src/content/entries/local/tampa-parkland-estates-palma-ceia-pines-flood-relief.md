Parkland Estates and Palma Ceia Pines sit in low-lying bowls in South Tampa where heavy rain overwhelms undersized drains. During Hurricane Milton, more than 325 homes and businesses flooded. Heavy storms have also made it difficult or impossible for patients, physicians and staff to reach HCA Florida South Tampa Hospital and nearby medical offices. Tampa plans a 10-by-10-foot concrete culvert under South Howard Avenue to carry water from the neighborhoods to Hillsborough Bay. The project also replaces or upgrades about 11,800 feet of water mains.

People don't need to apply. City modeling says the system would remove about 95% of flooding during a five-year, eight-hour storm and prevent structural flooding at about 96 homes and businesses. About 230 homes and businesses would avoid structural flooding in a Milton-like storm. Utility relocation was scheduled to begin in November 2026, with culvert construction starting in spring 2027 and substantial completion expected in September 2030.

The City Council approved the $92.7 million contract 4-3. Lynn Hurtak made the motion, Charlie Miranda seconded it, Alan Clendenin cast the swing vote and Luis Viera also voted yes. Brad Baird led the city's presentation. The project uses stormwater bonds and assessments, Tampa-Hillsborough Expressway Authority money and a $10.1 million state grant. That includes $11.9 million redirected from existing stormwater accounts; the city says those accounts held savings or money from canceled or superseded projects, while opponents dispute that characterization. Business owners also warn that years of construction could hurt their workers and livelihoods. Two pending lawsuits seek to stop the project.
