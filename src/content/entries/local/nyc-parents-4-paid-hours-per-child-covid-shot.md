When COVID-19 shots opened to younger kids in late 2021, New York City made sure covered private-sector workers who were parents or guardians didn't have to give up pay to get them vaccinated. For each shot a child got, a worker could take four hours of paid time. They could use it to take the child to the appointment, or to stay home with a child who had side effects and couldn't go to school or child care. Two kids who each got two doses and a booster added up to 24 hours.

The time was separate from regular sick leave, so workers didn't have to use up their sick days. It covered full-time, part-time and temporary private-sector workers. Employers had to pay it at the regular rate, and they could ask for proof of the shot within seven days. A union contract couldn't waive it.

Council Member Vanessa Gibson introduced the bill at Mayor Bill de Blasio's request. The Council passed it on Nov. 23, 2021. De Blasio returned it unsigned, and it became law without his signature on Dec. 24, 2021. It reached back to Nov. 2, 2021, so workers could get paid for shots their kids had already gotten. It ended on Dec. 31, 2022.
