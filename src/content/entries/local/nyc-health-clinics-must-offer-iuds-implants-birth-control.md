If you go to one of New York City's sexual health clinics, it has to offer every FDA-approved form of birth control that doesn't need surgery. That includes the pill, patch, ring and shot, emergency contraception (the morning-after pill), and long-acting methods like IUDs and the arm implant. If a clinic can't place an IUD or implant for you, it must quickly refer you to a place that can and tell you how to get one for free or at low cost. Clinic staff also get yearly training on contraception, bias, and the history of forced sterilization.

The clinics see anyone 12 and older without a parent's consent, whatever your immigration status, and you don't need insurance. Patients under 19 are not asked for insurance or payment. For adults, the clinic bills your plan if you use insurance, and you may owe a copay. If you don't have insurance or would rather not use it, you are asked to pay a sliding-scale fee, but you can still get care if you cannot pay. No money is collected at the clinic. IUD and implant visits are by appointment through the clinic hotline, 347-396-7959.

Council Member Carlina Rivera introduced the bill in June 2019. It got its first hearing in October 2020 and passed the Council on Dec. 9, 2021. Mayor Bill de Blasio didn't sign it, but he didn't veto it either, so it became law in January 2022. The requirements took effect a year later.
