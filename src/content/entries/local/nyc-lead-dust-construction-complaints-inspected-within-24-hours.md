Old paint in New York City buildings can contain lead. When renovation work sands or breaks it, toxic dust can spread into hallways and homes. If a complaint says construction dust is not being contained or controlled as required by a tenant protection plan, the Buildings Department must inspect within 24 hours. When inspectors find uncontrolled dust, the Buildings and Health departments must test it for lead, send samples to a laboratory and share the results. The Buildings Department may stop work after the Health Department refers unsafe lead practices.

Permit applicants whose work will disturb lead paint or paint of unknown lead content must certify compliance with lead-safe rules, and tenant protection plans must address those rules. When the Health Department issues a correction order for a common-area lead hazard found during certain investigations, it posts a notice in the lobby and the owner posts one on every floor. For blood lead screening, the Health Department must refer a person who requests help for a child who needs screening when the parent cannot obtain a covered test; the parent or guardian no longer has to make the request. A dust complaint triggers the 24-hour inspection; there is no separate application.

Council Member Margaret Chin introduced the bill in May 2018. Laurie Cumbo, Ben Kallos, Brad Lander, Paul Vallone, Helen Rosenthal, Alan Maisel and Carlina Rivera cosponsored it. WE ACT for Environmental Justice and allied advocates worked with the Council to improve and advance the bill. The Council passed it nearly three years later, on March 18, 2021. Mayor Bill de Blasio did not sign it, so it became law without his signature in April 2021 and took effect a year later.
