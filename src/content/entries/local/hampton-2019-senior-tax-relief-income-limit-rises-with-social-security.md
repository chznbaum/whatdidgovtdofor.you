Hampton lets homeowners who are 65 or older, or permanently and totally disabled, freeze their real estate tax bill or put off paying it, as long as their household income and assets stay under set limits. For at least ten years the income limit had been $50,000, even as Social Security checks went up with the cost of living. That meant a small raise could push someone on a fixed income out of the program.

The 2019 ordinance fixed that. Every year, Hampton's Commissioner of the Revenue now raises the income limit by the same percentage as the Social Security cost-of-living adjustment for the year being reported. The first increase was 2%, for applications covering July 2019 through June 2020. By 2023 the limit had reached $62,000; Hampton now lists a $67,000 limit and a $200,000 asset limit, excluding the home and up to 10 acres. You still apply through the Commissioner of the Revenue, and you have to keep qualifying to keep your freeze.

City Council approved the change on April 10, 2019, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding. The city covered the change while revenues were growing, not by cutting services: its fiscal 2020 budget plan kept service levels and general-fund tax rates steady while increasing total spending 3.55%.
