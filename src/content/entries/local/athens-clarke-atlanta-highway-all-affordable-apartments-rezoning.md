About 18.57 acres at 4320 Atlanta Highway in Athens, once an automotive salvage yard, can now be developed with housing. The county attached a binding condition to the new zoning: every residential unit built there must be rented to a household earning no more than 60% of the applicable area median income, adjusted for household size, with rents capped accordingly. The condition lasts for 30 years from the ordinance's effective date.

The commission did not build or fund the homes. The applicant submitted a nonbinding concept for 250 apartments with 673 bedrooms, and a project representative later told commissioners the development would provide more than 700 affordable beds. If housing is built, eligibility will depend on household income and size.

The developer would need to extend about 2,500 feet of gravity sewer at its own expense before the site could be served. Planning staff also raised concerns about possible contamination from the former salvage yard, the lack of nearby public transit, and access to the property. The proposed primary entrance is a driveway shared with the CarMax next door.

The Athens-Clarke Mayor and Commission, with Mayor Kelly Girtz presiding, approved the rezoning unanimously on Sept. 1, 2026. The Planning Commission had earlier recommended approval with conditions by a 7-1 vote.
