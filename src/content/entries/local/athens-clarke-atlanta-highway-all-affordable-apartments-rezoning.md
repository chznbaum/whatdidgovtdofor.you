About 18.57 acres at 4320 Atlanta Highway in Athens, once a scrap yard, can now be used for apartments. Before, the land was zoned for offices and employment. The county attached a firm condition to the new zoning: every apartment built there must go to households earning no more than 60% of the area's median income. That condition runs for 30 years from the ordinance's effective date.

The commission didn't build the homes itself. What it did was require any apartments built on the site to serve lower-income renters while the 30-year zoning condition is in force. The applicant's engineer told commissioners the project would provide more than 700 affordable beds. If the apartments open, renters will need to show their household income is under the limit.

Before anyone could move in, the developer must extend a sewer main to the site, which will also serve nearby land. Neighbors raised questions about the site's past as a salvage yard, the lack of a bus line, and a driveway shared with the CarMax next door.

The Athens-Clarke Mayor and Commission, with Mayor Kelly Girtz presiding, approved the rezoning by a unanimous vote on Sept. 1, 2026. The planning commission had earlier recommended approval.
