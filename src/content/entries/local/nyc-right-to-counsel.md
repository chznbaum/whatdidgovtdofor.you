If your landlord takes you to Housing Court to evict you, New York City law requires the Office of Civil Justice to provide access to free legal services. Tenants whose household income is at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, and tenants 60 or older, are eligible for full representation. Others can get brief legal advice. Immigration status does not matter, and the program covers every ZIP code. NYCHA residents facing termination of tenancy are covered too.

To seek help, call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline. At the courthouse, ask the clerk or court attorney about a free lawyer. Funding and staffing have not kept pace with demand, so not every eligible tenant gets one.

The program began in 15 ZIP codes and expanded citywide in March 2020, ahead of the law's July 2022 deadline. A 2023 law sponsored by Council Member Crystal Hudson made every tenant 60 or older eligible for full representation regardless of income.

Council Members Mark Levine and Vanessa Gibson introduced the bill in 2014 and pushed it for three years. Mayor Bill de Blasio agreed to fund it in February 2017, the Council passed it on July 20, and he signed it on August 11, making New York the first U.S. city to create this kind of program. In fiscal year 2025, city-funded providers fully represented more than 22,700 households in eviction cases. In the fourth quarter, 44% of eviction cases where the tenant appeared had a full lawyer.
