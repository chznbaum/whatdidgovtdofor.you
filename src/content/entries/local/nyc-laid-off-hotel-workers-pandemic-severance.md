When COVID-19 emptied New York City's hotels, thousands of housekeepers, bellhops and other service workers were laid off, and many big hotels stayed closed well into 2021. This law covered hotels with at least 100 rooms after a qualifying closure or a layoff of at least 75% of service staff in 30 days. Starting Oct. 11, 2021, each covered worker who remained laid off was owed up to $500 a week for up to 30 weeks.

Workers didn't have to apply. A hotel had to pay within five days after each week. Severance or similar pay for the same week reduced the city-law payment; severance for earlier periods did not. Payments stopped when the worker was recalled. A closed hotel could also stop them by reopening and recalling at least 25% of its March 2020 staff. Permanently converting hotels were exempt if every covered worker was offered at least 20 days' pay per year of service.

Council Member Francisco Moya sponsored the bill, with backing from the Hotel and Gaming Trades Council, the hotel workers' union. The Council passed it 40–3 on Sept. 23, 2021, and Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on Oct. 5. The union later reported that 24 hotels reopened and another 11 made weekly payments. Hotel owners sued, but a federal judge refused to block the law in March 2022. It expired on June 1, 2022, as written.
