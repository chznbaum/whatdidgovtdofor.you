Hennepin County is giving $1,286,919 to local cities, school districts and parks for things kids use. Brooklyn Center Community Schools gets $300,000 for an accessible playground, and Maple Grove gets $300,000 for a water play structure. Minneapolis Public Schools also gets $300,000 from the play-area fund, which will buy digital scoreboards for seven high schools.

Another $139,000 helps keep pools and beaches staffed. It pays to train and certify lifeguards in Bloomington and Crystal and to train and keep lifeguards at Three Rivers Park District. Eleven smaller grants, about $10,000 or less each, buy gear. Eden Prairie gets two all-terrain wheelchairs, the Minneapolis Park and Recreation Board gets adaptive water equipment, and several schools get soccer, archery and other sports equipment. Nine arts grants totaling $146,018 cover a reduced-fee art lab in Edina, theater and arts residencies in Minneapolis schools, after-school theater in Robbinsdale and lower-cost spots in a summer arts program.

Families don't apply. Local governments applied, and the county got 82 requests for about $6 million, far more than it had to give. Review panels scored the requests on how many young people they serve, how accessible they are, community and financial need, and geographic spread. The Hennepin County Board approved the awards on Aug. 4, 2026. The program is paid for with the county's ballpark sales tax, which state law allows the county to spend on youth activities.
