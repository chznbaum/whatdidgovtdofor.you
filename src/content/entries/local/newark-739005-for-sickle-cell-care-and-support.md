Newark will spend $739,005 from the state on care for people with sickle cell disease, an inherited blood disorder that can cause severe pain and organ damage. The city's Department of Health and Community Wellness runs the work, which covers medical care, support services and education about the disease for Newark and nearby areas. The money covers September 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027.

The health department applied for the grant on September 10, 2026, and the New Jersey Department of Health awarded it on September 28. The grant requires no city contribution. The Municipal Council accepted the money on October 21 and added it to Newark's temporary budget on November 5. The emergency appropriation required a two-thirds vote of the full council.

The grant is part of New Jersey's three-year Sickle Cell Disease Pilot Program. A state law approved in 2024 provided $10.2 million for grants to federally qualified health centers offering coordinated medical care, behavioral and mental health care, social support, monitoring, education and outreach. Newark was among six municipalities whose health centers received first priority.
