Minneapolis accepted about $1.75 million from the State of Minnesota to help people find work. The larger grant, $1,252,963, pays for the city's program for laid-off workers. The other grant, $500,463, comes from the Minnesota Youth Program and pays for job training for young people. The city's Community Planning and Economic Development department runs both.

If you were recently laid off, you can apply if you did not quit or get fired, qualify for unemployment benefits, and are unlikely to return to your old job or field; staff check other eligibility rules. The program provides career counseling, job-search help, counselor-approved training and support services at no cost. To get started, fill out the city's interest form or contact a counselor. The youth program is for low-income and at-risk young people ages 14 to 24. It teaches work-readiness skills and can lead to paid or unpaid work experience, skills training, and support services through local providers.

Council Member Jamal Osman authored the item. The Business, Housing & Zoning Committee approved it on July 21, 2026, and the City Council passed it 12-0 on July 30, with Council Member Robin Wonsley absent. Mayor Jacob Frey signed it the next day.
