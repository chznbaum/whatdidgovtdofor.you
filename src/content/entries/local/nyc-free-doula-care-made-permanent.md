A doula is a trained birth worker who supports you before, during and after birth. New York City's Health Department now has to keep a citywide doula program running. It offers free care to eligible residents, trains new doulas, helps maternity hospitals welcome doulas onto care teams, and reports results every year, including doula pay.

When the Comptroller audited the program in 2025, free doulas were available in all 33 high-need neighborhoods. To enroll, you must be income-eligible for Medicaid and live in one of those neighborhoods, a shelter or foster home, or be a pregnant teenager anywhere in the city. From March 2022 through June 2024, 2,158 people got at least one visit. The audit found lower rates of C-sections, preterm births and low birth weight among Black and Hispanic clients than the latest citywide rates for those groups. Those citywide data were from 2022, so the audit said a direct comparison was not possible. Doulas reported no pregnancy-associated deaths among clients during the audit period.

Council Member Jennifer Gutiérrez sponsored the 2022 law that set up the pilot and the 2025 bill that made it permanent. Council Members Julie Menin and Farah Louis and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams were among the 2025 cosponsors. The Council passed it on October 29, 2025. Mayor Eric Adams did not sign it, and it became law on November 28, 2025, taking effect immediately.
