Early in the pandemic, crowded shelters were a real danger for people with nowhere else to sleep. This grant gave the Peninsula's homeless services $800,000 to deal with that: $240,000 in state COVID relief money and $560,000 from HUD's Emergency Solutions Grant–COVID program. The city didn't have to put up any matching money.

The state money kept emergency shelter open around the clock. It also paid to move shelter guests into separate spaces, like hotels, to slow the virus's spread. The federal money paid for rapid rehousing. That covered things like a first month's rent and a deposit, plus ongoing help like budgeting and credit counseling so people could keep their new homes. Help came through agencies in the Greater Virginia Peninsula Homelessness Consortium, which covers Hampton, Newport News, Williamsburg, Poquoson, York County and James City County. Hampton's Department of Human Services was the lead agency. People got help through those local shelter and housing agencies.

It was the first year of this grant, and it covered 2020 to 2022. Hampton City Council accepted it 7–0 on Aug. 12, 2020, with Mayor Donnie Tuck presiding.
