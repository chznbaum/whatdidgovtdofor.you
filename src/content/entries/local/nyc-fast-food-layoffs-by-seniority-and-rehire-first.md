If you're a nonsalaried worker at a fast food chain in New York City and have finished any probation period, which can last no more than 30 days, your employer can't lay you off on a whim. A layoff needs a real economic reason, like closing all or part of a restaurant, or reorganizing after sales or profits dropped. The employer must have business records to prove it. At that restaurant, the newest workers are laid off first and the longest-serving workers stay longest.

If you're laid off for an economic reason, you get first call back. For 12 months, the employer must try to offer you shifts or restore your hours before hiring someone new. The most senior laid-off workers have priority. You don't have to apply for this protection. It covers chains with 30 or more locations nationwide. You can complain to the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection or take your case to arbitration. Violations can bring back pay and a $500 penalty.

Council Member Adrienne Adams introduced the bill in February 2019. It passed the Council in December 2020 alongside Council Member Brad Lander's companion bill, which bars firing fast food workers without just cause after any probation period. Fast food workers organized with 32BJ SEIU for both bills. Mayor Bill de Blasio signed them on Jan. 5, 2021, and both took effect that July. Restaurant industry groups sued, but a federal judge upheld the law in 2022 and an appeals court agreed in January 2024. The city was still enforcing it in 2026.
