Thousands of New York City cab owners, many of them immigrant drivers, were crushed by medallion loans after prices collapsed. Since 2021, the city's Medallion Relief Program and MRP+ expansion have restructured loans for more than 2,000 owners, and lenders have forgiven $476 million. Before this change, an MRP+ owner could transfer a medallion, but the restructured loan and city guaranty could not go with it. Under this rule, an owner, or their estate after death, can transfer that package to a spouse, child, parent, sibling, or other close relative. A sole owner can also move it into a company they fully own, which the TLC says could save about 730 owners thousands of dollars in taxes. Partners can also remove a partner if everyone agrees.

The lender has to approve, and the loan has to be current. The TLC's Owner/Driver Resource Center and the New York Legal Assistance Group will help with the paperwork for free, including setting up a company.

The extended-family and company changes were added after drivers, the New York Taxi Workers Alliance, and other owners asked for them at an August 2026 public hearing. The commission, led by Chair Midori Valdivia, adopted the rule unanimously on October 22, 2026. A first vote on a broader package in September fell one vote short. That package also would have dropped several fines on drivers, such as a $200 penalty for a late drug test, but the fine cuts weren't brought back for the October vote.
