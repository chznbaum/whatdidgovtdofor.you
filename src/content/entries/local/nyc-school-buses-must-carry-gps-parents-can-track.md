Every bus used to carry students under a city Department of Education contract must have an operational GPS tracker whenever it is in service. While students are aboard, it must also have a two-way radio or another device that allows communication with the driver. Authorized parents and guardians must get real-time location data, and the city must store GPS records electronically.

Families use the free NYC School Bus app, developed with Via. You sign in with your NYC Schools Account and your child's student ID. A bus appears only when its driver logs in; more than 75% of assigned drivers had subscribed when the app launched citywide in January 2024. A December 2025 audit found frequent missed K–12 driver logins and said Pre-K buses still lacked parent-facing real-time tracking. The city had not set a deadline to fix the Pre-K gap.

Skyer Law suggested the GPS bill, and Council Member Ben Kallos introduced it in September 2018. A November snowstorm that stranded children on buses added urgency. Education Committee chair Mark Treyger helped shepherd it through committee; Kallos also credited Chaim Deutsch's 18 years of work and Speaker Corey Johnson with fast-tracking the vote. The Council passed it on Jan. 9, 2019. Mayor Bill de Blasio did not sign it, and it became law on Feb. 9. The tracking requirement took effect that September, but the parent app did not launch citywide until January 2024.
