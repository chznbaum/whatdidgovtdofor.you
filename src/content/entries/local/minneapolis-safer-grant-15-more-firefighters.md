Minneapolis is preparing to add 15 firefighter positions with help from a $3.38 million federal grant. The money comes from FEMA's Staffing for Adequate Fire and Emergency Response program, known as SAFER. The program helps fire departments raise the number of trained, front-line firefighters in their communities. FEMA's list of awards shows the Minneapolis Fire Department winning the hiring grant on Sept. 28, 2026, and FEMA announced that round of awards two days later.

You don't need to apply or sign up for anything. The grant helps cover salary and benefit costs for the 15 positions over three years, but it does not cover the entire cost. FEMA's FY2025 rules require Minneapolis to pay 25% of actual costs in each of the first two grant years and 65% in the third. The positions are intended as a floating team that can cover absences across fire stations and answer emergencies.

Mayor Jacob Frey proposed using a SAFER grant for a team of 15 floating firefighters in his 2027 budget plan, and the Fire Department applied. The City Council's Public Health, Safety & Equity Committee approved accepting the grant on Oct. 7. The city's record lists no council member as its author. With Council President Elliott Payne presiding, the full council accepted the grant 12–0 on Oct. 15, 2026. Council Member Pearll Warren was absent. Frey signed it on Oct. 20. The acceptance still needed final approval from the City Attorney's Office.
