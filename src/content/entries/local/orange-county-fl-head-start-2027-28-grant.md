Orange County runs its own Head Start program, which offers free preschool to children from low-income families. Besides daily classes, children get health and nutrition services, including vision, hearing, dental and developmental screenings, plus speech and other therapy when they need it. The program serves more than 1,500 children a year at about two dozen centers, some of them on public school campuses.

Most of the money comes from the federal government. On June 16, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners approved the county's application for the second year of its five-year federal grant: $16,427,721 for the 2027–28 year. The county also committed the required $4,406,930 local match, which comes from parent volunteer hours, donated goods and services, building leases and county support.

To qualify, a family has to live in Orange County, earn at or below the federal poverty line, and have a child who turns 3 or 4 by Sept. 1. Families apply year-round by calling (407) 836-6590, and a waiting list fills open seats.

Mayor Jerry Demings chaired the meeting where the board approved it.
