The Fall Line Trail is a planned 43-mile path for walking, running and biking that will link seven localities, from Ashland through Henrico and Richmond down to Petersburg. Richmond's share is 13 miles, and it starts in Bryan Park. This ordinance set up and paid for that first city segment. Once complete, it will be a free, off-road trail open to anyone, with no sign-up needed.

The city's plans for the Bryan Park trailhead include parking, restrooms, water fountains, picnic shelters and a bike repair station. In July 2026, crews had laid a gravel base for most of the segment and paved about half of it. The project was then scheduled for completion on Aug. 21, 2026.

Then-Mayor Levar Stoney brought the ordinance to City Council. Council members Katherine Jordan, Ann-Frances Lambert, Cynthia Newbille and Ellen Robertson were also patrons, and the council adopted it on March 25, 2024. The $3 million came from the Central Virginia Transportation Authority, the regional body that is the trail's main funder. That means city taxpayers didn't have to cover the cost from Richmond's general budget. Some neighbors objected to running the path through the middle of the park, but the city kept that route.
