If you live in New York City and owe a hospital or doctor money you couldn't pay, the city may already have cleared it. The city pays Undue Medical Debt, a nonprofit, to buy unpaid medical bills from local hospitals and health care providers at a steep discount and then cancel them. By October 2025, nearly $135 million in debt had been wiped out for more than 75,000 New Yorkers.

You can't apply, and you can't ask to have a specific bill cancelled. The nonprofit checks which debts qualify. If yours is one of them, you get a letter saying you owe nothing, and you won't owe tax on the cancelled amount. You qualify if your household earns no more than four times the federal poverty line, or if your medical debt equals at least 5% of your household's yearly income.

Mayor Eric Adams and Health Commissioner Dr. Ashwin Vasan launched the program in January 2024. The city committed $18 million over three years, with a goal of erasing more than $2 billion in debt for up to 500,000 people. In 2025 the city also opened free financial counseling at eight public hospitals to help patients avoid new medical debt. As of October 2026, the program was still running under Mayor Zohran Mamdani's administration and was set to continue into 2027.
