If you live in New York City and owe a hospital or doctor money you couldn't pay, the city may already have cleared it. The city pays Undue Medical Debt, a nonprofit, to buy bundled, qualifying debts from participating hospitals, health care providers, and debt buyers at a steep discount and then cancel them. By October 2025, nearly $135 million in debt had been wiped out for more than 75,000 New Yorkers.

You can't apply or ask to have a specific bill cancelled. Undue identifies eligible debts in the portfolios that providers agree to sell. Under the city's rule, your household income can be no more than 400% of the federal poverty line. If it is above 400% but below 1,000%, your medical debt must equal at least 5% of your yearly household income. The rule excludes debt owed to NYC Health + Hospitals and debt for FDNY ambulance rides. If your debt is selected, you get a letter saying you owe nothing on it, and the cancellation is not taxable income.

Mayor Eric Adams and Health Commissioner Dr. Ashwin Vasan announced the program in January 2024. The city committed $18 million over three years, with a goal of erasing more than $2 billion in debt for up to 500,000 people. In 2025 the city also opened free financial counseling at eight NYC Health + Hospitals locations. As of November 2026, the city still listed the medical debt program, and NBC New York reported that it was available until 2027.
