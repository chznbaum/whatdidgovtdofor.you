This grant paid for homeless services across the Virginia Peninsula from July 1, 2023, to June 30, 2024. The money went to emergency shelter, rapid rehousing (help getting into a home and paying for it at first, including for veterans), help for people about to lose their homes, and a central line where people can ask for help. The goals were to keep people from becoming homeless, shorten the time they spend homeless, and keep them from ending up homeless again.

Hampton's Department of Human Services leads the region's homelessness group and manages the money for the Virginia Peninsula Collaborative. That collaborative serves Hampton, Newport News, Poquoson, Williamsburg, James City County and York County. Partners include Newport News Social Services, the Hampton-Newport News Community Services Board, The Salvation Army Peninsula, Menchville House, Link, Transitions and LGBT Life Center. People get help through these local agencies.

Of the $1,060,922, $931,116 came from Virginia's general fund and $129,806 came from federal money passed through the state. Hampton didn't have to add any local money. Hampton has won this grant every year since 2014–15. The City Council, led by Mayor Donnie Tuck, approved it on its consent agenda on Sept. 13, 2023.
