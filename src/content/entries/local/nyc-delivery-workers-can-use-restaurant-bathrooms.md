If you deliver food for an app in New York City, the restaurant where you pick up an order has to let you use its bathroom. The law works through the apps: any contract a delivery app signs with a restaurant on or after Jan. 24, 2022 must require the restaurant to give couriers bathroom access. A restaurant can say no only when the route would take you through its kitchen, food prep, storage or dishwashing area, when your access would create an obvious health or safety risk, or in other cases the city's consumer and worker protection agency sets by rule.

You don't have to sign up for anything. The city offers a bathroom access card, in several languages, that you can show to restaurant staff. If you're turned away, you can file a complaint with the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection online or through 311.

Council Member Carlina Rivera sponsored the bill after news reports showed couriers sometimes waiting hours for a bathroom. She worked on it with Los Deliveristas Unidos, a campaign of immigrant delivery workers run by the Worker's Justice Project. The Council passed it on Sept. 23, 2021 as part of a package of delivery worker protections. Mayor Bill de Blasio backed the package but didn't sign this bill, so it became law without his signature.
