If you work in New York City, you probably earn paid time off you can use when you are sick, to care for a family member, or for medical appointments. You earn 1 hour for every 30 hours you work. At employers with 5 to 99 workers, you can earn up to 40 paid hours a year; at employers with 100 or more, up to 56. Domestic workers are covered too. You do not have to apply, and part-time, temporary, and undocumented workers are covered.

The 56-hour cap began January 1, 2021, under a 2020 amendment sponsored by Council Member Andrew Cohen. Since February 22, 2026, a law sponsored by Council Member Sandy Nurse also gives workers 32 hours of unpaid protected time immediately on their first day and at the start of each employer's calendar year. It became law without Mayor Eric Adams's signature.

The City Council first passed paid sick leave in 2013, with Council Member Gale Brewer as lead sponsor. Mayor Michael Bloomberg vetoed it, and the Council overrode his veto on June 26, 2013. In 2014, Mayor Bill de Blasio and Council Speaker Melissa Mark-Viverito proposed a broader version, carried by Council Member Margaret Chin, that extended paid sick time to businesses with as few as 5 employees, down from 15. De Blasio signed it on March 20, 2014, and it took effect April 1.
