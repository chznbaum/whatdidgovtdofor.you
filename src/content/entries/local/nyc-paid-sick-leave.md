If you work in New York City, you probably earn paid time off you can use when you're sick, to care for a family member, or for medical appointments. You earn 1 hour for every 30 hours you work, paid at your regular rate. At employers with 5 to 99 workers, you can earn up to 40 paid hours a year; at employers with 100 or more, up to 56. Domestic workers get up to 40 paid hours at employers with 1 to 99 workers and up to 56 at employers with 100 or more. You don't have to apply, and part-time, temporary, and undocumented workers are covered.

Since February 22, 2026, the law, now called the Protected Time Off Law, also gives workers 32 hours of unpaid protected time off starting on their first day.

The City Council first passed paid sick leave in 2013, with Council Member Gale Brewer as lead sponsor. Mayor Michael Bloomberg vetoed it, and the Council overrode his veto on June 26, 2013. Early in 2014, Council Member Margaret Chin sponsored a broader version that extended paid sick time to businesses with as few as 5 employees, down from 15, and widened the family members workers could care for. Mayor Bill de Blasio signed it on March 20, 2014, and it took effect April 1, 2014.
