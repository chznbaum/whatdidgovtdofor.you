Miami-Dade can receive up to $11.9 million in federal money for outreach to homeless people who live on the street and are dealing with serious mental illness, addiction, or both. Over four years, outreach teams will work downtown and in Overtown, Little Havana, Allapattah and Wynwood/Edgewater. They will connect people to treatment, recovery support and housing. A January 2026 count found more than half of the county's unsheltered homeless people in the City of Miami.

The county's Homeless Trust runs the effort, called the Miami Safe Streets Collaborative. Local nonprofits do the work, including Camillus Health Concern, New Hope C.O.R.P.S and The Advocate Program. They partner with hospitals, courts, police and shelters, and the effort ties into the new Miami Center for Mental Health and Recovery. People do not have to visit an office to start: outreach teams use hotspot mapping to reach them where they are.

The money comes from a new federal grant program, STREETS, that chose eight communities nationwide for up to $3 million a year for up to four years. Mayor Daniella Levine Cava's administration applied for the grant and won it in a competition. Commissioner Marleine Bastien sponsored the resolution accepting it. Commissioner Vicki Lopez co-sponsored it and moved its adoption; Vice Chairman Kionne McGhee and Commissioner Micky Steinberg also co-sponsored it. The commission approved it 9–0 on Oct. 20, 2026. The county doesn't have to put in any money of its own.
