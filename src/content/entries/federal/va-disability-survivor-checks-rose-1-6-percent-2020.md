If you're a veteran with a service-connected disability, your monthly VA check went up 1.6% starting December 1, 2019. The same raise applied to the extra money paid for a spouse, children or dependent parents, to the yearly clothing allowance for veterans whose gear wears out their clothes, and to dependency and indemnity compensation for surviving spouses and children. A veteran rated 100% disabled with no dependents went to $3,106.04 a month. A veteran at that rating with a spouse but no children or dependent parents got $51.64 more a month.

You didn't have to apply. VA raised the payments on its own, and the new rates stayed in place as the base for later raises. Congress has to pass a law like this every year, or these payments don't keep up with prices. The 1.6% matched the 2020 Social Security increase.

Rep. Elaine Luria, a 20-year Navy veteran from Virginia, introduced the bill in February 2019 with Rep. Mike Bost, the disability subcommittee's top Republican, as its original cosponsor. It was the first bill she got through the full House. Committee chair Mark Takano and top Republican Phil Roe managed it on the House floor, where it passed by voice vote on May 21, 2019. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent that September. President Donald Trump signed it on September 26, 2019.
