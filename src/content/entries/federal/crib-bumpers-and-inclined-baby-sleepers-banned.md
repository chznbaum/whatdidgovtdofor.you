Two baby products that have been tied to suffocation deaths can no longer be sold in the United States: padded crib bumpers, and sleepers for babies under a year old whose sleep surface tilts more than 10 degrees. The ban covers manufacturing for sale, selling, distributing, and importing them, no matter when a product was made. Vinyl bumper guards and slat covers count as bumpers too. Unpadded mesh crib liners are still allowed.

You don't have to do anything to get this protection. It applies to the stores and companies that sell baby gear, and the Consumer Product Safety Commission enforces it. The CPSC's advice is to put a baby to sleep on a firm, flat surface in a crib, bassinet, or play yard.

Rep. Tony Cárdenas introduced the bill, Rep. Jan Schakowsky helped lead it through the House, and Rep. Mondaire Jones cosponsored it. It passed the House in June 2021 as part of a bundle of bills, 325–103, with votes from both parties. In the Senate, Sens. Tammy Duckworth, Rob Portman, Richard Blumenthal, and Sherrod Brown had pushed their own crib bumper ban, and the Senate passed Cárdenas's bill unanimously in May 2022. Before the federal law, Chicago's crib-bumper ban took effect in 2012, followed by Maryland in 2013, Watchung, New Jersey, in 2016, and Ohio in 2017. President Joe Biden signed the law on May 16, 2022, and the ban started that November. The CPSC voted 4–0 in 2023 to write both bans into its rules.
