If you worked for a company doing work under a federal contract, such as cleaning a federal building, cooking in a base cafeteria, or maintaining federal property, this order set your pay floor at $15 an hour. It applied to new contracts and to old contracts that were renewed or extended starting January 30, 2022. You didn't have to apply. Your employer had to pay it.

The floor went up each January to keep pace with prices, reaching $17.75 an hour in 2025. Tipped workers used to have a lower minimum on these contracts. The order phased that out, so by 2024 they got the full minimum with tips on top. It also covered workers with disabilities who had been paid below the minimum under special federal certificates. The Labor Department expected about 327,300 workers to get a raise.

President Joe Biden signed the order in April 2021. Labor Secretary Marty Walsh's department issued the rule that put it into effect that November. The order built on a 2014 order from President Barack Obama, which had set a lower minimum for contract workers.

President Donald Trump revoked the order on March 14, 2025. The Labor Department stopped enforcing the $17.75 minimum. Only the lower Obama-era minimum is left, and it covers just older contracts.
