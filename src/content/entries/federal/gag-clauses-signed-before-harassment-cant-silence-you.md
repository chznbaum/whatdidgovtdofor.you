Many job offers, contractor deals and customer contracts include fine print that bars you from talking about what happens to you, or from saying anything negative about the company. Under the Speak Out Act, a court can't enforce that kind of clause against you in a sexual harassment or sexual assault dispute if you agreed to it before the dispute began. You don't have to apply for this. If an employer or business tries to use an old gag clause to silence you, the law makes that clause unenforceable.

The law covers current, former and prospective employees, independent contractors and customers. It applies to claims filed on or after Dec. 7, 2022. It doesn't touch confidentiality terms you agree to after a dispute starts, such as in a settlement. It also doesn't cover other kinds of harassment, and companies can still protect trade secrets. States can pass stronger protections.

Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand introduced the bill and co-led it with Sens. Mazie Hirono, Lindsey Graham and Marsha Blackburn. Reps. Lois Frankel and Ken Buck led the House version. Former Fox News anchor Gretchen Carlson and former contributor Julie Roginsky pushed for it too. Carlson's own settlement NDA isn't covered. The Senate passed it unanimously in September 2022, and the House followed 315–109, with every Democrat and 100 Republicans voting yes. President Joe Biden signed it on Dec. 7, 2022.
