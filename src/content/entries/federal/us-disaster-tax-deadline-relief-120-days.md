If FEMA approves Individual Assistance for your federally declared disaster area, certain federal tax deadlines now pause automatically for at least 120 days, double the old 60-day minimum. That covers things like filing your return and paying what you owe. If the IRS has your address in the covered area, you don't have to apply. The pause starts on the day the disaster begins and ends 120 days after the disaster begins or is declared, whichever is later.

Before this law, the IRS could postpone tax deadlines only after a federal disaster declaration. Now a governor, or the mayor of D.C., can ask in writing for relief after a disaster that only the state has declared. That covers storms, floods, fires and explosions that cause serious damage. The Treasury decides, after consulting FEMA. This part isn't automatic, but it gives people hit by smaller disasters a way to get relief. It applies to disasters declared after July 24, 2025.

Rep. Judy Chu first introduced the idea in 2019. In 2025 Rep. David Kustoff carried it in the House with Chu, and Sens. Catherine Cortez Masto and Marsha Blackburn led it in the Senate. The House passed it 388–0 with support from both parties, and the Senate passed it without objection. President Donald Trump signed it on July 24, 2025.
