The lowest-paid troops in the U.S. military got a raise well beyond the usual yearly bump. Every service member got a 4.5% raise in January 2025. Then, on April 1, active-duty troops in pay grades E-1 through E-4 got another 10% on top of that, about 14.5% for the year. E-5s with fewer than 10 years in got smaller targeted raises. Nobody had to apply. The new rates went into the military pay tables and showed up in mid-April paychecks. For a private with four months to two years of service, the April raise alone added $211 a month in base pay.

The idea came from the House Armed Services Committee's Quality of Life Panel, chaired by Rep. Don Bacon, a Republican, with Rep. Chrissy Houlahan, a Democrat, as ranking member. The panel's 2024 report said base pay for junior enlisted troops had fallen behind and called for a 15% raise. The House passed another 15% on top of the servicewide 4.5% raise, while the Senate committee proposed another 1%. The final defense bill settled on another 10%, for a reported 14.5% total, and the full-year spending law Congress passed in March 2025 paid for it.

The bill passed the House 281–140 and the Senate 85–14. President Joe Biden signed it on December 23, 2024. Most House Democrats voted no. They objected to a last-minute provision that stops military health insurance from covering some gender-transition treatments for service members' children.
