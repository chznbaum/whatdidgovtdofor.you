Federal law lets people who were sexually abused or trafficked as children sue the people responsible in federal court. This law took away the filing deadline. Before it, a survivor had to sue by age 28 or within 10 years of discovering the harm. Many survivors don't talk about abuse until much later, so that cutoff shut out a lot of them. The law covers 14 federal crimes, including child sexual abuse, child sex trafficking, forced labor, and making or sharing child sexual abuse images. A survivor who wins can recover actual damages or $150,000, plus legal fees, and a court can add punitive damages.

There's nothing to apply for. A survivor files a lawsuit. The law doesn't bring back claims whose deadline had already run out by Sept. 16, 2022. It also covers only federal crimes; state law decides claims over other abuse.

Sen. Dick Durbin, a Democrat, and Sen. Marsha Blackburn, a Republican, led the bill, and the Senate passed it without objection in March 2022. In the House, Rep. Deborah Ross carried a companion bill with Reps. Eric Swalwell, Guy Reschenthaler, and María Elvira Salazar. The House passed the Senate bill by voice vote on Sept. 13, 2022, with support from both parties. President Joe Biden signed it on Sept. 16, 2022.
