Money Follows the Person helps people on Medicaid who live in nursing homes and other institutions move back into their own homes or community settings and get their care there. States use the money to help with moving, housing searches, and support once people are home. By 2019, states were running short of funds. This law raised the program's federal money for fiscal 2019 from $132 million to $254.5 million, an extra $122.5 million. You don't apply to Congress for it. You go through your state's Medicaid program.

The law also kept a protection for married couples. When one spouse qualifies for certain Medicaid home- and community-based services, states must apply rules that let the other spouse keep a protected amount of income and assets. This law extended that requirement through the end of 2019. It also renewed grants to family-run centers that help parents of children with special health needs, and two months of funding for community mental health clinics.

Sen. Roy Blunt sponsored the shorter Senate substitute that became the final law. It replaced a broader House bill sponsored by Rep. Debbie Dingell, with Rep. Brett Guthrie as an original cosponsor and Republican partner on Money Follows the Person. The House passed that broader bill 371–46 in June 2019. The Senate passed Blunt's substitute by voice vote, the House accepted it without objection, and President Donald Trump signed it on August 6, 2019. Later laws extended both Money Follows the Person and the expanded spousal protections through September 2027.
