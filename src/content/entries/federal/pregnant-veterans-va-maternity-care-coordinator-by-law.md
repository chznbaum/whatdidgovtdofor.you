If you're a veteran who's pregnant and enrolled in VA health care, you can have a maternity care coordinator help you through pregnancy and the year after delivery. VA's current program calls for your coordinator to contact you once each trimester, after delivery and quarterly during the following year. Coordinators screen for health and mental health risks and connect veterans to classes, supplies and community help. Most VA maternity care comes from outside doctors and hospitals, and the coordinator helps keep that care connected with VA. To connect with a coordinator, contact your VA provider or your facility's Women Veterans Program Manager.

The coordinators started as VA policy. This law made running the program a legal duty for VA. It authorized an extra $15 million for the program in 2022 and required VA to train community maternity providers on veterans' needs, including mental health conditions linked to military service. VA separately expanded postpartum coordination from eight weeks to one year in October 2023. The law also required the first federal study of maternal deaths and severe complications among veterans. That study, released in 2024, found that the severe-complication rate nearly doubled from 2011 to 2020.

Sen. Tammy Duckworth introduced the Senate bill with Sen. Susan Collins, a Republican. Rep. Lauren Underwood sponsored the House companion with Reps. Gus Bilirakis and Brian Fitzpatrick, both Republicans, and Rep. Julia Brownley. It was the first bill from the Black Maternal Health Momnibus to become law. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent, and the House passed it 414–9. President Joe Biden signed it on November 30, 2021.
