If you got sick from the dust and smoke after the September 11 attacks, or lost a family member to a 9/11-related illness, you may be able to seek compensation from the federal September 11th Victim Compensation Fund. It covers first responders, construction and cleanup workers, people who lived, worked, or went to school in the fund's lower-Manhattan exposure zone, and people who were at the Pentagon or Shanksville crash sites during the covered periods. This law moved the claim-filing deadline from December 2020 to October 1, 2090, and funds the program through 2092. You must register with the fund by the deadline for your circumstances, then file a complete claim after your condition is certified.

By early 2019, the fund was running low on money and had started cutting new awards by up to 70%. The law required the fund to repay the difference, and nearly 1,700 people whose awards had been cut were restored to the full amount. It also lets the income limit used to calculate lost-wage awards rise with inflation.

Rep. Carolyn Maloney wrote the bill with Reps. Jerry Nadler and Peter King. Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand carried it in the Senate with Sen. Cory Gardner. Jon Stewart and sick responders lobbied for it, including retired NYPD detective Luis Alvarez, who testified weeks before he died. The bill passed the House 402–12 and the Senate 97–2, and President Donald Trump signed it on July 29, 2019. Congress exempted its cost from budget-offset rules, so no services were cut to pay for it.
