Before 988, the national suicide prevention line was reached through a toll-free number, 1-800-273-8255. A 2020 law made 988, a simple three-digit code, the nationwide number. The Federal Communications Commission required phone companies to connect it by July 16, 2022, and the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline has answered on 988 ever since.

You can call, text or chat with 988 if you're thinking about suicide or are in a mental health or substance use crisis. The law also lets states and tribes add a fee to phone bills to pay for local crisis services, as long as the money goes only to 988 services. By March 2026, the line had taken 23.3 million contacts: 15.8 million calls, 4.2 million texts and 3.4 million chats.

The push took three years. Republican Rep. Chris Stewart of Utah started it in 2017, and his 2018 law ordered the FCC to study a three-digit number. Republican Sen. Cory Gardner of Colorado sponsored the 2020 law, with Democrats Tammy Baldwin and Jack Reed and Republican Jerry Moran. Stewart and Democratic Rep. Seth Moulton led the House version. It passed both chambers by voice vote, and President Donald Trump signed it on October 17, 2020.

In July 2025, the federal agency that runs the line ended a specialized option for LGBTQ+ youth. Congress funded it again in early 2026, and it returned that September.
