Farms bring in hundreds of thousands of seasonal workers each year on H-2A visas. Many depend on the employer for their job, housing, and ride to the fields, which can make abuse hard to escape. This rule set new ground rules. Employers had to keep seat belts working in vehicles manufactured with them and make workers wear them. They generally could not hold a worker's passport or visa unless the worker voluntarily requested safekeeping in writing. Employers had to share their agreements with foreign recruiters, helping the government enforce the ban on recruitment fees. Firing a worker "for cause" had to meet set standards, workers could invite guests to employer housing, and they were protected from retaliation for joining together to push for better conditions.

Workers did not have to apply. The Labor Department planned to apply the new terms to H-2A applications filed on or after August 29, 2024, but a court order had already stopped the rule in 17 states.

Two Labor Department officials issued the rule under President Joe Biden: José Javier Rodríguez, who ran the Employment and Training Administration, and Jessica Looman, who ran the Wage and Hour Division. Growers and 17 Republican-led states sued, and a Georgia judge blocked the entire rule in those states, including Virginia, in August 2024. A Kentucky court later blocked specified provisions in four more states and for certain groups, while a Mississippi court stayed two organizing provisions nationwide. In June 2025, the Trump administration's Labor Department stopped enforcing the rule anywhere, and that July it proposed repealing most of it.
