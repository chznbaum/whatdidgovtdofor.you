If you come to the U.S. on an H-2A visa to work on a farm, or on an H-2B visa for seasonal work like landscaping, seafood processing or hotels, your authorization to work is generally tied to a petitioning employer. If the job ends before the approved period does, whether you quit or are let go, you and your H-4 dependents can remain in status for up to 60 days, or until your authorized stay ends if that comes sooner. This grace period is available once during each authorized admission period. You may also be admitted up to 10 days before the approved job period begins and remain up to 30 days after the petition expires. Those extra admission days alone do not authorize work.

If you are eligible, you can start a new H-2 job after the new employer properly files a nonfrivolous petition in the same visa category, or on its requested start date if that is later, without waiting for approval. If an employer retaliates after you report a violation, USCIS may treat a resulting status problem as an extraordinary circumstance when credible evidence accompanies a request to extend your stay or change status.

Petitioners and recruiters cannot collect prohibited recruitment or placement fees. If a petition is denied or revoked because a prohibited fee was collected, USCIS denies new H-2 petitions from the same petitioner or successor for one year and for up to three more unless affected workers are fully repaid. USCIS may also deny petitions based on certain employment-related violations during the petition or the previous three years.

The Department of Homeland Security issued the rule under Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas after proposing it in 2023. The 60-day grace period applies without notifying USCIS, but changing employers requires the new employer to file a petition, and retaliation-related relief requires a supported request.
