Steel mills that make iron in blast furnaces give off toxic dust, mostly manganese and lead. Much of it never goes up a smokestack. It leaks when furnace vents open, when hot iron is poured on the ground, and when slag is broken up and moved. This 2024 EPA rule limited those leaks for the first time. It also set new limits on mercury, dioxins and acid gases from these mills, and it requires mills to monitor the air at their fence lines.

You don't have to do anything to get the benefit. The rule covers the eight integrated steel mills operating in the U.S. About 478,761 people live within 5 kilometers of these mills and one idle mill. EPA found that those neighborhoods are poorer and more often Black than the country as a whole. At full implementation, EPA estimated the rule would cut about 64 tons of toxic metal air pollution and 473 tons of fine soot each year.

EPA Administrator Michael Regan signed the rule. It was set to phase in from 2025 to 2027. In 2025, after Cleveland-Cliffs and U.S. Steel petitioned for reconsideration, Administrator Lee Zeldin moved the 2025 and 2026 deadlines to April 2027. Fenceline monitoring may begin later because EPA must first approve a test method. A federal appeals court upheld the extensions in July 2026. EPA is still reconsidering several requirements, so protections could be weakened before compliance begins.
