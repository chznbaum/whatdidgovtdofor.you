Henrico County will extend public water lines to 41 homes in the Cedar Ridge neighborhood off Portugee Road in eastern Henrico. Today these homes rely on private wells. Elevated PFAS levels were found in the nearby Chickahominy River Basin in 2021, raising concerns about groundwater quality. Once the line is built, each household can choose to connect or keep its well, and the county will cover the connection fee. County officials said in October 2026 that the connection could take 18 months to two years.

Since 2021 the county has tested private wells for free and installed and maintained filters at no cost wherever PFAS turned up. It used more than $64 million in federal pandemic relief to give about 400 well users the option of public water. That money is now spent, and some homes still can't connect.

U.S. Rep. Jennifer McClellan asked for this project in the 2026 federal budget. She won $1,092,000 for it in the spending law President Donald Trump signed on January 23, 2026. Varina District Supervisor Tyrone Nelson said the county will keep looking for money until every resident has the option of public water. McClellan's request page says the full White Oak extension plan—not just the Cedar Ridge segment—would require about 35 miles of new pipe.
