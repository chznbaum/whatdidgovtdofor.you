Normally you can buy a plan on the Affordable Care Act marketplace only during open enrollment in the fall, unless something like losing a job or having a baby opens a special window. In 2021, with the pandemic still raging and more than 30 million Americans uninsured, the federal marketplace reopened to all Marketplace-eligible consumers. From Feb. 15 to Aug. 15, 2021, people could sign up or switch plans on HealthCare.gov without showing proof of a life change. Coverage started the first of the month after you picked a plan.

About 2.1 million people signed up for new coverage through HealthCare.gov. State-run marketplaces opened their own windows, and another 738,000 people signed up through those separate state windows. Many found low-cost plans: 48% of new HealthCare.gov enrollees selected plans with monthly premiums of $10 or less after tax credits.

President Joe Biden issued the executive order on Jan. 28, 2021, directing the Department of Health and Human Services to consider reopening enrollment. HHS announced the window that same day and set aside $50 million for outreach. The window first ran to May 15. In March, after the American Rescue Plan made marketplace tax credits bigger, CMS under HHS Secretary Xavier Becerra extended it to Aug. 15 so more people could get the new savings. The window closed on schedule. President Donald Trump revoked the order in January 2025.
