The TAKE IT DOWN Act gives people whose intimate photos or videos are posted online without their consent, including AI-generated fakes, two new protections.

First, it created federal crimes for knowingly publishing authentic intimate images or realistic computer-generated “digital forgeries” in specified circumstances. Adult cases require harm or intended harm plus privacy-related conditions. Cases involving minors require a specified harmful or sexual intent. Some good-faith disclosures, such as reports to law enforcement, are exempt. Maximum prison terms for publishing are two years for adult cases and three years for cases involving minors.

Second, covered websites and apps that host user content had to set up a way for victims or their authorized representatives to request removal by May 19, 2026. After a valid request, the platform must remove the reported image within 48 hours and make reasonable efforts by then to identify and remove known identical copies. The Federal Trade Commission enforces that rule and may pursue civil penalties against platforms that do not comply.

Republican Sen. Ted Cruz and Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar introduced the bill, and Republican Rep. María Elvira Salazar and Democratic Rep. Madeleine Dean led it in the House. First Lady Melania Trump made it a priority. It passed the Senate by unanimous consent and the House 409–2, and President Donald Trump signed it on May 19, 2025.
