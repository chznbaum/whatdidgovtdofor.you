If you work construction, your employer must now choose protective gear that properly fits you. The rule covers hard hats, gloves, safety glasses, hearing protection, respirators, coveralls, vests and fall-protection harnesses. Gear that's too big or too small can fail to protect you. It can also create new dangers, like a loose glove or vest catching in a machine, and gear that hurts to wear often gets left off.

You don't have to apply. The requirement covers every construction employer that has to provide protective gear. OSHA counts about 5.9 million construction workers who use protective gear and estimates that 184,935 of them may need nonstandard sizes. That group includes about 56,000 women under 5 feet tall. In one informal survey of 174 tradeswomen, 77% said gear that didn't fit had exposed them to a hazard they shouldn't have faced.

OSHA, led by Assistant Secretary Doug Parker, proposed the change in July 2023 and finalized it in December 2024. It puts the construction rule in line with the rule other industries already followed. OSHA expects it to cost employers about $1 million a year in total. In July 2025, OSHA proposed rolling back more than two dozen safety rules, but this one wasn't among them.
