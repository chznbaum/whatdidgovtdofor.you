When a hurricane, flood or fire hits and the president or SBA declares a disaster, the U.S. Small Business Administration makes low-interest, long-term loans to people who need to rebuild, not just to businesses. Since this rule, a homeowner can borrow up to $500,000 to repair or replace a primary home. Before, the limit was $200,000. Homeowners and renters can also borrow up to $100,000 to replace things like furniture, clothing, appliances and cars, up from $40,000. Those caps hadn't changed since 1994, while building costs and home values kept climbing.

You have to apply to SBA after a declared disaster and qualify for the loan. The rule also gives you 12 months, not 5, before your first payment is due. It drops the old $5,000 limit on fixing landscaping and lets you use loan money to protect your home against any kind of future disaster, not just the kind that hit. Recent SBA disaster notices list rates for homeowners as low as 2.875%, with up to 30 years to repay.

SBA Administrator Isabella Casillas Guzman issued the rule under President Joe Biden. It applies to disasters declared on or after July 31, 2023, and it also lets SBA raise the home limits further for a particular disaster.
