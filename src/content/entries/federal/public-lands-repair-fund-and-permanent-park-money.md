From fiscal 2021 through 2025, this law deposited half of otherwise unallocated federal receipts from oil, gas, coal and renewable-energy development on federal lands and waters, capped at $1.9 billion a year. National parks received 70%, the Forest Service 15%, and the Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Land Management and Bureau of Indian Education 5% each. The money repaired roads, trails, campgrounds, buildings and water systems. Advocates count more than 1,500 projects in all 50 states and D.C.

The law also made $900 million a year for the Land and Water Conservation Fund permanently available without yearly appropriations. That fund buys land and easements for public use and gives state and local governments grants for parks and recreation. Nearly all of its revenue comes from offshore oil and gas leasing. The repair-fund deposits did not reduce energy revenues already directed to states or other named funds.

Cory Gardner formally sponsored the Senate bill; Joe Manchin was its lead Democrat, and Steve Daines helped secure presidential support. The repair fund grew from work led by Mark Warner, Rob Portman, Angus King and Lamar Alexander. Maria Cantwell was a key Land and Water Conservation Fund champion. In the House, Joe Cunningham, Mike Simpson, Mikie Sherrill and Brian Fitzpatrick led an identical companion. The final bill passed the Senate 73–25 and House 310–107; Donald Trump signed it Aug. 4, 2020. Repair-fund deposits ended after fiscal 2025. A renewal bill has cleared committees in both chambers but not either full chamber.
