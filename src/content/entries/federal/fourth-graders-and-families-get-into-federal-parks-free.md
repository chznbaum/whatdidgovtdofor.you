If your child is in fourth grade, the family can visit federal parks and public lands free for the school year. The pass works at more than 2,000 sites run by the National Park Service, Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management, Fish and Wildlife Service and other federal agencies. Where a site charges by the car, everyone in one passenger vehicle gets in. Where it charges per person, up to three adults get in with the student. Kids under 16 are always covered. The pass doesn't cover parking, camping or special tours, and the student has to be there.

To get one, your child does a short activity on everykidoutdoors.gov and prints a voucher, or you can request a digital pass on Recreation.gov. Home-schooled 10-year-olds qualify too. The current pass runs through Aug. 31, 2027.

The Interior Department started offering the pass in 2015, but only as a policy. Sens. Martin Heinrich, a Democrat, and Lamar Alexander, a Republican, wrote it into law with the Every Kid Outdoors Act. It passed as part of a big public lands package led by Sens. Lisa Murkowski and Maria Cantwell, which also permanently renewed the Land and Water Conservation Fund. Large majorities of both parties voted for the package. President Donald Trump signed it on March 12, 2019. The program was set to end in 2026, but the EXPLORE Act, signed by President Joe Biden in January 2025, extended it through Sept. 30, 2031.
