If you live in public housing or a HUD-inspected multifamily property, NSPIRE sets the basic health and safety conditions your home has to meet. When an inspection finds a life-threatening problem, the housing agency or owner has 24 hours from receiving notice to make the hazard safe. At public and multifamily properties, severe problems have the same deadline. Blocking the hazard can count while a permanent repair is completed on a HUD-approved schedule. The property must certify the correction and provide evidence within two business days after the deadline. In voucher homes using NSPIRE, life-threatening problems get 24 hours, but severe and moderate problems generally get 30 days.

Public housing agencies and multifamily owners with HUD assistance contracts must inspect every unit themselves at least once a year and correct what they find. The standards require a working kitchen and toilet, safe drinking water, GFCI protection for outlets within six feet of water, permanent heat in designated climate zones, and smoke alarms. Federal law also requires carbon monoxide alarms in many assisted-housing programs. HUD inspections focus on the units where people live, and resident groups can recommend additional units for inspection. You do not need to apply; the standards govern the property.

HUD Secretary Marcia Fudge issued the final rule in May 2023, after a 2021 proposal and listening sessions in 15 states. Public housing switched to NSPIRE in July 2023 and HUD multifamily housing that October. Since Oct. 1, 2026, specified requirements such as GFCI protection and guardrails also affect those properties' inspection scores. HUD repeatedly delayed the voucher transition; housing agencies must switch by Feb. 1, 2027.
