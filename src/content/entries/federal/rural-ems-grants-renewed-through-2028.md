In rural areas, the ambulance that answers a 911 call often belongs to a small fire department or a volunteer squad. More than half of rural EMS agencies are staffed by volunteers, who often have to raise their own money. The SIREN grants help these agencies train and recruit crews, run certification courses and buy equipment. The 2024 law kept the grants going through 2028. It also lets agencies use the money for overdose-reversal drugs like naloxone, and it requires training crews to care for people in mental health or addiction emergencies.

Agencies apply to SAMHSA, the federal mental health and addiction agency. Ambulance services run by local or tribal governments can apply, and so can nonprofit ones. In June 2026, SAMHSA opened a $13.5 million round for about 45 two-year grants of up to $300,000 a year, with no local match required. Awards started going out that September.

Congress created the program in the 2018 Farm Bill. Sens. Dick Durbin and Susan Collins wrote the renewal and earlier won bigger budgets for the grants, raising them from $5 million to $11.5 million a year. Reps. Dave Joyce and Debbie Dingell carried it in the House. Lawmakers from both parties backed it. The Senate passed it in December 2023 and the House in September 2024, and President Joe Biden signed it on September 26, 2024.
