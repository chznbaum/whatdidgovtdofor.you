This law created a VA grant program that pays local groups to reach veterans at risk of suicide before a crisis. Nonprofits, tribes, and state and local agencies that win grants offer outreach, mental health screening, suicide prevention education, case management, peer support, and help getting VA and other benefits. They also connect people to mental health treatment. The program is named for Staff Sgt. Parker Gordon Fox.

Veterans don't apply to the VA for this. You go to a funded group near you. At-risk veterans who weren't dishonorably discharged can get help, and so can some former service members with other-than-honorable discharges, along with their families. Each group can receive up to $750,000 a year. The first grants were awarded in September 2022, and for fiscal 2027 the VA awarded $111.9 million to 191 groups.

Sens. John Boozman and Mark Warner led the IMPROVE Well-Being for Veterans Act grant proposal, and Rep. Jack Bergman introduced its House companion with Rep. Chrissy Houlahan as an original cosponsor. Their work helped shape the community-grant provision in a broader veterans mental health bill introduced by Sens. Jon Tester and Jerry Moran. The bill is named for John Scott Hannon, a Navy SEAL who died by suicide in 2018. The Senate passed it unanimously in August 2020, the House cleared it that September, and President Donald Trump signed it on Oct. 17, 2020. The current extension runs through Dec. 11, 2026. The House passed a bill on Nov. 11, 2026, that would extend the program through Sept. 30, 2029, but the Senate has not yet approved it.
