If you rented in an apartment complex financed by USDA Rural Development, including farmworker housing, this rule gave you time and a way out when you fell behind on rent. Your landlord had to give you written notice at least 30 days before an eviction for unpaid rent could take effect. The notice also had to tell you how to pay what you owed to stop the eviction, and how to recertify your income if it had dropped, which can lower your rent. You didn't have to apply. The protection came with your lease, and leases had to spell out the 30-day rule by September 2025.

The 2020 CARES Act already required 30 days' notice at these properties. In March 2024, the Rural Housing Service, led by Administrator Joaquin Altoro, wrote that requirement into USDA's own rules and added the instructions on catching up and recertifying. These tenants are primarily low income: the agency put the average for Section 515 households at $14,941 a year.

In February 2026, the Rural Housing Service under Administrator George Kelly removed the rule, calling it unnecessary. The 30-day notice in the CARES Act still applies, but landlords no longer have to explain how to catch up or recertify your income.
