If you're struggling with opioid addiction, federal rules let you start treatment with qualifying buprenorphine medicines without first going to a doctor's office. A DEA-registered clinician can prescribe after telehealth, and an audio-only phone call can qualify. That matters if you don't have a camera, reliable internet, or a clinic nearby. Federal and state prescribing rules still apply, so ask a provider who offers telehealth addiction care.

The permanent rule creates one path with extra safeguards. Before prescribing under that rule, the clinician must check the prescription database for the state where you are located. Prescriptions under that path can cover no more than the first six calendar months; after that, the prescribing clinician must examine you in person or use another telemedicine route authorized by federal law. A pharmacist filling a prescription under this path must verify your identity. You do not apply to the government.

A 2008 federal law generally requires an in-person exam before a controlled drug may be prescribed online. The government relaxed that rule during COVID. DEA Administrator Anne Milgram and HHS official Miriam Delphin-Rittmon issued this permanent exception in January 2025; the incoming administration delayed its effective date twice, and it took effect Dec. 31, 2025. Through Dec. 31, 2026, clinicians may instead use broader temporary flexibilities. Under that temporary authority, this permanent rule's six-month, database-check, and pharmacy-ID requirements do not apply. The permanent rule itself has no end date.
