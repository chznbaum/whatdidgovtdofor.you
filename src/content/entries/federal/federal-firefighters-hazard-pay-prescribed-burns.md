Federal wildland firefighters already earned 25% hazard pay on top of their base salary for qualifying fireline work on an unplanned wildfire. Prescribed burns are different: crews set those fires on purpose to clear brush and lower the risk of bigger fires later. Until this rule, prescribed-fire work itself did not trigger that differential, even though the smoke, flames, heat and falling trees are much the same.

The rule covers General Schedule employees—including workers on the GW wildland-firefighter pay plan—and Federal Wage System employees serving as firefighting-crew members on the fireline. Eligible work includes ignition, holding, patrol, mop-up, fireline construction and snag felling. General Schedule workers receive hazardous-duty pay, while wage-system workers receive environmental-differential pay. Employees do not apply for it as a benefit; agencies determine eligibility from the covered duties performed. Pre-ignition preparation does not count. OPM estimates about 10,000 General Schedule and 2,500 wage-system employees at Agriculture and Interior could be affected.

The Forest Service and Interior Department asked OPM for the change. The National Federation of Federal Employees, a union representing federal workers, approached OPM about prescribed-fire hazard pay in 2022 and pursued the change through bargaining, legislation and rulemaking. The Office of Personnel Management, led by Director Scott Kupor, proposed the rule in April 2026 and finalized it in August. Pay eligibility began with the first pay period on or after September 14, 2026.
