The Violence Against Women Act pays for much of the help survivors of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault and stalking get, through federal grants to local programs. The 2022 renewal, the first since 2013, keeps those grant programs authorized through 2027, many at higher levels. It adds a grant program for LGBTQ survivors, more support for culturally specific and rural services, better training for sexual assault exam providers, and more help for survivors 50 and older and survivors with disabilities.

It also restored tribal courts' power to prosecute non-Native people who commit sexual assault, child abuse, stalking or sex trafficking on tribal lands, and started a pilot for Alaska Native villages. And it created a new federal right to sue: if someone shares your intimate images without your consent, you can sue for your actual losses or $150,000, plus legal fees. VAWA grants go to state, tribal and local programs, not directly to survivors. Using the civil remedy requires filing a federal lawsuit.

Sen. Dianne Feinstein introduced the bipartisan Senate bill in February 2022 with Sens. Joni Ernst, Dick Durbin and Lisa Murkowski. Ernst had worked on it for more than three years. Murkowski and Sen. Brian Schatz wrote its tribal provisions. Rep. Sheila Jackson Lee introduced the House version with Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick and Jerry Nadler; it passed in March 2021. President Joe Biden, who wrote the original 1994 law as a senator, signed the final version as part of the 2022 government funding law on March 15, 2022.
