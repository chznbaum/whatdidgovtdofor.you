The GI Bill pays student veterans a monthly housing stipend. Students in online-only classes normally get about half of what in-person students get. When colleges closed their campuses and moved classes online in March 2020, many student veterans who had already signed leases faced losing hundreds of dollars a month, and in some places close to $2,000.

This law let VA treat a program that switched to distance learning because of the pandemic as if it were still in person. The Post-9/11 GI Bill housing stipend and the allowances paid under other VA education programs stayed at their normal rates, backdated to March 1, 2020. Students didn't have to apply; the payments just continued.

Sen. Jerry Moran, a Republican who chaired the Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee, introduced the bill with Sen. Jon Tester, the committee's top Democrat, and 28 senators from both parties signed on. Rep. Phil Roe introduced a House companion. The Senate passed it without objection on March 16, the House followed on March 19, and President Donald Trump signed it on March 21, 2020. A second law that April covered schools that closed outright. Congress extended the online protection past its first end date of December 2020, and it finally ended on June 1, 2022.
