Supplemental Security Income (SSI) pays monthly benefits to adults and children who are blind or disabled and to people 65 or older with very low incomes. In rental subsidy cases—when someone in the household is a parent or child of the landlord or the landlord's spouse—Social Security can treat below-market rent as income. Before this rule, that could cut a recipient's check by up to about one-third. Now, if the required rent is at least the presumed maximum value, SSA does not count the discount. For an individual, that threshold is $351.33 a month in 2026 and rises with the federal benefit rate.

SSA's own 2024 example assumes a person has no other countable income and pays $400 for an $800 apartment. Outside seven states, the old rule would have produced a $628.67 monthly payment; the nationwide rule would produce the full $943 federal rate for 2024. Tell SSA what rent you are required to pay; the agency may verify the amount and market value with the landlord.

Acting Commissioner Kilolo Kijakazi proposed the nationwide expansion in August 2023. Commissioner Martin O'Malley approved the final rule in April 2024, and it took effect nationwide on Sept. 30. The change grew out of court cases that had already provided the protection in Connecticut, New York, Vermont, Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin and Texas. SSA's actuaries estimated it would add $837 million in SSI payments over fiscal years 2024 through 2033. SSA's 2026 guidance confirms that the nationwide policy remains in effect.
