If someone swallows a pill, cleaning product or anything else that might be poison, you can call 1-800-222-1222 at any hour. Your call goes to one of 53 poison control centers, where nurses, pharmacists and toxicologists tell you what to do. Often they can tell you it's safe to stay home and skip the emergency room. The call is free and confidential, and you don't have to sign up for anything.

The previous authorization covered the national number, an outreach campaign and grants to the centers through fiscal 2024. This law renewed all three through 2029. It authorizes $700,000 a year to keep the help line running, $800,000 a year to let people know it exists and $28.6 million a year in grants to the centers. The centers handled more than 2 million human exposures in 2022, about one every 15 seconds.

Sen. Patty Murray, a Washington Democrat, introduced the Senate bill in May 2024. Sen. Tommy Tuberville, an Alabama Republican, was the lead Republican, and Sens. Ben Ray Luján and Mitt Romney joined them. Rep. Lori Chavez-DeRemer, an Oregon Republican, carried the House version, which cleared committee 47–0. The Senate passed the bill without objection in July, and the House passed it by voice vote in September. President Joe Biden signed it on September 26, 2024.
