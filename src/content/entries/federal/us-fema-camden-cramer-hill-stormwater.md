Cramer Hill, a Camden neighborhood along the Delaware River, floods in ordinary heavy rain, not just in big storms. In 2022, about 9,400 people lived there, and at least 13 streets flooded regularly. Residents described water coming into their homes every time it rained, and buses and school routes getting cut off.

On Nov. 23, 2026, FEMA approved more than $16 million from its Hazard Mitigation Grant Program for the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority to build new stormwater systems and other upgrades in Cramer Hill. This program pays for work that prevents disaster damage before it happens, rather than for repairs afterward. Residents don't need to apply for anything: the authority builds the system.

City and county officials asked FEMA for flood money for Cramer Hill in 2022. In 2024, the utilities authority, led by executive director Scott Schreiber, laid out plans centered on a nearly 6-foot drainage pipe under Harrison Street, one of the worst-flooded spots, that would carry stormwater to the river. FEMA's announcement doesn't spell out which parts of that plan the money covers. Cameron Hamilton was FEMA's administrator when the agency approved the grant.
