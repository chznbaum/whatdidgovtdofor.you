Each state's WIC program covers most of its baby formula through a contract with one manufacturer. When that company's formula is recalled, families can be left with benefits for products stores cannot sell. This rule sets minimum remedies for new and renewed formula contracts. They must allow formula in all unit sizes and noncontract brands without medical documentation, except for participants receiving Food Package III for special medical needs. If the contracted manufacturer's formula is recalled, the company must give the state a supply plan and pay rebates on eligible noncontract formula. The state decides when those remedies start and stop, and some options require a USDA waiver.

The rule also gives the Secretary of Agriculture permanent authority to waive or modify some WIC requirements during qualifying emergencies, disasters and supply shortages. Each state WIC program must keep an alternate operating plan for emergencies, recalls and other supply disruptions. The protections work through state WIC programs and USDA rather than a separate application from families.

Rep. Jahana Hayes sponsored the Access to Baby Formula Act during the 2022 shortage. Reps. Bobby Scott and Michelle Steel were original cosponsors, and Scott led House passage with Hayes. The House passed it 414–9 on May 18, 2022. All 220 Democrats who voted supported it; 194 Republicans voted yes and nine voted no. Six members did not vote. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent the next day after Sens. Debbie Stabenow and John Boozman led a companion bill. President Joe Biden signed it on May 21, 2022. Food and Nutrition Service Administrator Cynthia Long issued the final rule, which took effect February 12, 2024.
