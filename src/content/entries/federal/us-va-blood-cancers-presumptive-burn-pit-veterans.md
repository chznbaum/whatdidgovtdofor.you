If you served in the Gulf War or after 9/11 in certain countries and now have acute or chronic leukemia, multiple myeloma, myelodysplastic syndrome or myelofibrosis, VA assumes your service caused it. You don't have to prove that burn pit smoke or other fine particle pollution made you sick. That makes it much easier to get disability pay and VA health care.

The rule covers veterans who served in the Southwest Asia theater, which includes Iraq, or in Somalia on or after Aug. 2, 1990. It also covers anyone who served in Afghanistan, Syria, Djibouti, Uzbekistan, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon or Yemen on or after Sept. 11, 2001. You still have to file a claim, online at VA.gov or by calling 1-800-MYVA411. Survivors can file too. VA encouraged veterans whose claims were turned down before to apply again.

VA found enough evidence to link fine particle pollution to acute and chronic leukemias and multiple myelomas. It also covered myelodysplastic syndromes and myelofibrosis because they can progress to acute myeloid leukemia and often have poor outcomes. It used powers the 2022 PACT Act gave it to add conditions to the list. Secretary Denis McDonough signed the rule, and it took effect right away on Jan. 10, 2025, before the usual public comment period. VA estimated its budget impact at $1.7 billion over 10 years.
