If you deployed to a covered burn pit or toxic-exposure location, you can apply for a VA breast cancer risk assessment before 40. A mammogram is not automatic: a VA clinician must find it clinically appropriate after reviewing your exposure, family history and other risks. VA generally limits screening mammograms to birth-sex female veterans and transgender women after five years of hormone therapy. Birth-sex male veterans without symptoms are not screened; those with breast symptoms may receive a diagnostic mammogram. Screening mammograms are not routinely recommended at ages 18–29.

The law names Iraq and parts of Southwest Asia beginning in August 1990, and Afghanistan, Djibouti, Syria, Jordan, Egypt, Lebanon and Yemen beginning Sept. 11, 2001. It also covers locations listed by the Airborne Hazards and Open Burn Pit Registry; water and airspace do not count. You must apply by enrolling in VA health care or requesting SERVICE Act care only on VA Form 10-10EZ. Screening is free even if you are not enrolled, but this law does not cover follow-up treatment.

Dr. Kate Hendricks Thomas testified for the bill and fought for its passage after being diagnosed at 38. Sen. John Boozman (R) sponsored S. 2102 with original cosponsor Sen. Ron Wyden (D). Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks (R) sponsored the House companion; Rep. Julia Brownley (D) was its original cosponsor. The Senate passed S. 2102 by voice vote through unanimous consent, the House passed it 418–0, and President Joe Biden signed it on June 7, 2022.
