If you have Medicare and use insulin, you pay no more than $35 for a month's supply of each insulin your plan covers. That's true whether it comes through a Part D drug plan or, for people who use an insulin pump, through Part B. You don't have to apply: the cap is built into every plan, and the deductible doesn't apply to insulin.

Before the cap, insulin costs varied widely. In 2020, some Part D enrollees without low-income subsidies spent more than $2,300 for insulin over the year.

The cap was part of the Inflation Reduction Act, which Democrats passed without Republican votes. The Senate split 50–50 and Vice President Kamala Harris broke the tie. President Joe Biden signed it on August 16, 2022. The Part D cap started January 1, 2023, and the Part B cap followed that July.
