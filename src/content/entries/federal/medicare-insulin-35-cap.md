If you have Medicare and use insulin, you pay no more than $35 for a month's supply of each insulin your plan covers. That's true whether it comes through a Part D drug plan or, for people who use an insulin pump, through Part B. You don't have to apply: the cap is built into every plan, and the deductible doesn't apply to insulin.

Before the cap, insulin costs varied widely. In 2020, some Part D enrollees without low-income subsidies spent more than $2,300 for insulin over the year. Starting in 2021, a voluntary pilot under the Trump administration let drug plans that opted in charge $35 a month; the new law made the cap apply to every plan.

Sen. Raphael Warnock and Rep. Angie Craig led the push in 2022, and Craig's bill passed the House 232–193 that March. The cap became part of the Inflation Reduction Act, which Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer and Sen. Joe Manchin negotiated and Democrats passed without Republican votes. A cap for private insurance fell short in the Senate. The Senate split 50–50 and Vice President Kamala Harris broke the tie. President Joe Biden signed it on August 16, 2022. The Part D cap started January 1, 2023, and the Part B cap followed that July.
