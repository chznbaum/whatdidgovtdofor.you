Most workers who become disabled must wait five full months after their disability begins before Social Security Disability Insurance checks start. This law ends that wait for people with ALS, a nerve disease with no cure that usually kills within two to five years. Checks start right away instead, and so does Medicare, because people with ALS already skip Medicare's usual two-year wait once their disability benefits begin.

You don't apply for the exemption separately. If Social Security finds you disabled by ALS on a disability claim, the wait is dropped. The law first covered claims filed from Dec. 23, 2020. A fix Congress passed in March 2021 extended it to anyone approved on or after July 23, 2020.

Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI) and Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) first introduced the bill in 2016. Rep. Seth Moulton (D-MA) took it up in the House in 2017 and later carried it with Rep. Peter King (R-NY). ALS patients and their families lobbied for about a decade. The Senate passed the bill 96–1 on Dec. 2, 2020, with Sen. Mike Lee the only no vote. The House passed it on Dec. 8 without a recorded vote, and President Donald Trump signed it on Dec. 22, 2020.
