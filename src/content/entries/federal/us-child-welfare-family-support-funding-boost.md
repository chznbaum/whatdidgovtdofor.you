This law renews the Title IV-B child welfare programs through 2029 and raises annual mandatory funding for the Promoting Safe and Stable Families program from $345 million to $420 million. It is the first permanent increase in nearly two decades. Most of the added money goes to grants for states, territories and tribes to help children remain safely at home and support families who take in a child or reunite with one.

The law explicitly allows this money to cover one-time, short-term help when a family hits a crisis, including housing, utilities, transportation or food. State and tribal plans must describe policies to prevent separating a child from a parent solely because of poverty. Some family-support and preservation services can now include young people under 26, and $10 million a year through 2029 is reserved from discretionary funding for competitive kinship navigator grants. Families do not apply to Washington; services come through child welfare agencies and their grantees. In covered court cases, agencies must tell parents and children about any independent legal representation available.

Reps. Darin LaHood (R-Ill.) and Danny Davis (D-Ill.) introduced the child-welfare bill, drawing on 16 bipartisan bills. Sens. John Cornyn (R-Texas) and Michael Bennet (D-Colo.) introduced identical child-welfare legislation. The Senate passed the House bill by unanimous consent in December 2024; President Joe Biden signed it on Jan. 4, 2025. CBO estimated that savings from the law's child-support administration changes more than offset the added child-welfare spending.
