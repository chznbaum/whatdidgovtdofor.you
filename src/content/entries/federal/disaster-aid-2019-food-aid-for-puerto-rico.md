This 2019 law provided $19.1 billion to communities still recovering from hurricanes, Midwest floods, California wildfires and other disasters. It included $2.43 billion in HUD grants to rebuild housing and infrastructure in the hardest-hit places, $50 million to retrain and employ workers who lost jobs to the disasters, $55 million for Head Start programs, and about $3 billion for farmers' crop losses. It also kept federal flood insurance from lapsing through September 2019. Some of the long-term HUD grants were still being spent in 2026.

For Puerto Rico, it added $600 million to the island's food assistance program, which served 1.3 million people as of June 2019. USDA approved the grant that July, and the first extra benefits went out July 29. The supplement went to every household in the program over 12 months, so families didn't have to apply separately.

House Appropriations Chair Nita Lowey sponsored the bill. In the Senate, Appropriations Chairman Richard Shelby and Vice Chairman Patrick Leahy negotiated the final deal. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer pushed to separate the agreed disaster package from a fight over border funding. Sens. Rick Scott and David Perdue pressed for the bill; Scott had proposed the Puerto Rico nutrition funding, and Perdue called Trump and got him to back the deal. The fight lasted months because President Donald Trump opposed more aid for Puerto Rico. It passed the Senate 85–8 and the House 354–58. Most Republican votes and every Democratic vote were yes. Trump signed it on June 6, 2019. Congress designated the aid as emergency spending without cutting other programs to pay for it.
