Haul trucks, front-end loaders, dozers and other heavy machines are among the deadliest hazards in mining. From 2011 to 2020, accidents with this equipment at surface mines and the surface areas of underground mines killed 113 people. Federal mine regulators found that more than half of those deaths involved gaps in training, spotting hazards, or maintenance.

This rule requires every mine operator, and every contractor with a Part 45 identification number, to have a written safety program for this equipment. A contractor's program can be folded into the mine operator's. The program must cover finding and fixing hazards, maintenance and repairs, available safety technology, and training for people who work around the equipment. Operators must seek input from miners and their representatives when writing and updating the plan and give them a free copy on request. A designated person must review it at least yearly, and after an accident or major change at the mine. Miners don't need to apply.

The Mine Safety and Health Administration first proposed the rule in 2021. Its leader at the time, Assistant Secretary of Labor Christopher Williamson, issued the final rule in December 2023. The final version covers small mines with five or fewer miners, which the proposal had left out. Enforcement began July 17, 2024, and inspectors have written hundreds of citations under it since then. MSHA expects the rule to prevent about 43 deaths and 5,200 injuries over 10 years.
