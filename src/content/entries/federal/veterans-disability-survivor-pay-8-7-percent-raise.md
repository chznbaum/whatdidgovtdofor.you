If you get VA disability compensation, your monthly check went up 8.7% starting December 1, 2022, with the higher payments arriving in January 2023. The same raise applied to the extra pay for dependents, the yearly clothing allowance for some disabled veterans, and Dependency and Indemnity Compensation for surviving spouses and children. A veteran rated 100% disabled with no dependents went from $3,332.06 to $3,621.95 a month, which is $289.89 more. You didn't have to apply, because VA raised every payment automatically.

Congress has to pass a law each year to give veterans the same cost-of-living raise that Social Security gets. With prices climbing fast in 2022, the raise came to 8.7%, the largest since 1981. The higher rates stay built into later years' payments, and each year's new raise is added on top of them.

Rep. Elaine Luria of Virginia, who chaired the House Veterans' Affairs subcommittee on disability benefits, introduced the bill with Rep. Troy Nehls, a Texas Republican. Luria said House Veterans' Affairs ranking member Mike Bost had worked with her on the legislation for two years. Committee Chair Mark Takano brought it to the floor, and the House passed it without a recorded vote in September 2022. In the Senate, Veterans' Affairs Chair Jon Tester and the committee's top Republican, Jerry Moran, led its unanimous passage; the White House also credited Senators Patty Murray and John Boozman with leadership. President Joe Biden signed it on October 10, 2022.
