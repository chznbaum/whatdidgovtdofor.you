VA's Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers pays a monthly stipend to a family member who cares for a seriously injured veteran. It also covers the caregiver's health care, travel costs, and respite breaks. When VA rewrote the program's eligibility rules in 2020, families already in it became a “legacy” group with a transition deadline. Earlier extensions moved that deadline to Sept. 30, 2025. This rule moves it to Sept. 30, 2028.

The rule covers veterans and service members whose caregivers were approved by Sept. 30, 2020. It also covers those whose applications arrived before Oct. 1, 2020 and were accepted later, as long as the same primary caregiver remains designated. VA says the legacy group has nearly 15,000 participants. Until the new deadline, they stay eligible, and a reassessment can't lower their stipend except in limited cases. They don't need to apply for this extension.

Senators Richard Blumenthal, Angus King, and Patty Murray asked VA in May 2025 to extend the protections. The Elizabeth Dole Foundation and six other caregiver and veterans organizations also coordinated months of advocacy. VA Secretary Doug Collins approved the final rule on Sept. 12, saying the extension would provide consistency and stability while VA considers broader program changes. Caregivers with questions can call VA's Caregiver Support Line at 1-855-260-3274.
