If someone steals your Social Security number and uses it, you can end up with problems at the Social Security Administration: bogus benefit claims, wrong records, or the need for a new number. Fixing that has often meant dealing with multiple SSA employees and getting conflicting advice. This law requires Social Security to give you a single point of contact that stays with your case until it's resolved. The same goes if your new card gets lost in the mail on its way to you.

The contact is a team of specially trained staff, not just one person. That team tracks your case from start to finish and works with other parts of the agency to fix it as quickly as possible. If the staff change, the agency must keep your records continuous and tell you when appropriate. Starting April 5, 2027, this is how Social Security must handle covered cases, 180 days after the law was signed.

Rep. David Kustoff, a Tennessee Republican, introduced the bill in September 2025, with Rep. Nicole Malliotakis of New York as a cosponsor. The House passed it by voice vote that December. The Senate passed it in September 2026, and President Donald Trump signed it on October 7, 2026.
