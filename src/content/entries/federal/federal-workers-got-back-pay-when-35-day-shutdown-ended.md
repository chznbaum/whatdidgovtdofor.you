A partial federal shutdown began on Dec. 22, 2018, and lasted 35 days, the longest in U.S. history. About 800,000 federal employees were sent home or kept working without pay, and many missed two paychecks. This law reopened the closed agencies through Feb. 15, 2019, and made money available to pay those workers their standard pay for the weeks they had lost.

Workers didn't have to apply. Agencies paid them through regular payroll, and the administration said the vast majority got their back pay on or before Jan. 31. Some Homeland Security employees waited until mid-February because of a payroll problem. The law also repaid states and other grantees, with interest, if they had used their own money to keep a federal program running or to pay furloughed state workers.

Rep. Nita Lowey, who chaired House Appropriations, sponsored the resolution and moved the House's agreement to the final version. House Speaker Nancy Pelosi and Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer refused to fund a border wall as the price of reopening the government. Senate Appropriations Chair Richard Shelby sponsored the final substitute, which Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell brought to the floor. The House had voted 11 times to end the shutdown before President Donald Trump agreed to a deal without wall money. The Senate passed the deal by voice vote, the House agreed without objection, and Trump signed it that night.
