Most car seats click into metal anchors in the back seat, two low ones buried in the crack of the seat and a tether anchor for the top strap. When those anchors sit deep in the cushion or in odd places, parents often install the seat wrong. A University of Michigan study cited by federal regulators found about 86% of seats were installed correctly when the anchors sat within 2 centimeters of the surface. When they were 4 to 6 centimeters deep, that fell to about 43%.

This federal rule makes automakers fix that. Lower anchors can sit no more than 25 millimeters deep and must leave room to reach them at an angle. Tether anchors can't be tucked far under the seat or in hard-to-reach spots. Anchors in vehicles and the matching hooks on car seats must carry the same standard symbols, so you can see what goes where. You don't need to do anything: the changes come built into new vehicles and car seats.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration wrote the rule to carry out a 2012 highway law that ordered it to make anchors easier to use in every rear seat. It was issued in January 2025. Starting Sept. 1, 2028, 20% of each automaker's new vehicles must comply, and all of them by 2030. In 2026, after automakers asked for more time, NHTSA proposed giving them an extra year.
