Many car seats connect to metal anchors in the back seat: two lower anchors in the seat crack and, for forward-facing seats, a tether anchor for the top strap. When those anchors sit deep in the cushion or in odd places, correct installation becomes less likely. A University of Michigan study cited by federal regulators found about 86% of seats were installed correctly when the anchors sat within 2 centimeters of the surface. When they were 4 to 6 centimeters deep, that fell to about 43%.

This federal rule makes automakers fix that. Lower anchors must sit no more than 25 millimeters deep and leave room to reach them at an angle. Tether anchors can't be tucked deep under the seat or in other hard-to-reach spots. Vehicles must be marked near each anchor, and the matching connectors on car seats must carry the same standard symbols, so you can see what goes where. You don't need to do anything: the changes come built into new vehicles and car seats.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration developed the rule to carry out a 2012 highway law that ordered it to improve lower-anchor and tether ease of use in rear seats where feasible. Acting Administrator Sophie Shulman led NHTSA when the agency issued the rule, which Chief Counsel Adam Raviv signed under delegated authority. Starting Sept. 1, 2028, 20% of each automaker's applicable new vehicles must comply, and 100% by 2030. In 2026, after automakers asked for more time, NHTSA proposed giving them an extra year.
