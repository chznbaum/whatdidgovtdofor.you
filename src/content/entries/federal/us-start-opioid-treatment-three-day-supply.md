If you're in opioid withdrawal and need addiction treatment, a doctor or other clinician can now hand you up to three days of medicine at one time. It eases withdrawal while your referral to longer-term care is being arranged. The supply can't be renewed or extended, and clinicians still have to follow state and local drug laws.

Under the old "three-day rule," a clinician could give you only one day's dose at a time. You had to come back every day, often to an emergency room, for the next one. That's a hard ask for someone who is sick, working, or without a ride, and it's a dangerous time to lose touch: about 1 in 20 people treated in an ER for a nonfatal overdose die within a year. There is no federal application, but the rule does not require every clinician or facility to offer this treatment.

Rep. Raul Ruiz (D-CA), an emergency physician, worked with the American College of Emergency Physicians on the fix starting in 2018. Rep. Greg Walden (R-OR) joined Ruiz's 2019 bill as its sole cosponsor. The House passed it by voice vote in November 2020. It became law weeks later inside a funding bill that President Donald Trump signed on Dec. 11, 2020. The law told the Drug Enforcement Administration to rewrite its rule. DEA Administrator Anne Milgram signed the new rule on Aug. 2, 2023, and it took effect when it was published on Aug. 8.
