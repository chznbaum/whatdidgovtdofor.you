If you appeal a Social Security or SSI decision to a judge, SSA now has four standard ways to schedule your appearance: by phone, by online video on your own phone or computer, by video at a Social Security office, or in person. Phone and online video can let people with disabilities, long drives or limited transportation take part from home. Before this rule, the regulations allowed phone hearings only in limited situations, although SSA had offered phone and online video more broadly under temporary pandemic procedures.

SSA—not you—ultimately selects the hearing method from the options available after your agreement or objections, based on efficiency and the facts of your case. Before scheduling, it sends a “Notice of Ways to Attend a Hearing.” You normally have 30 days after receiving that notice to object in writing to phone, agency video or both; SSA can accept a late objection for good cause. SSA may use online video only if you agree in writing, and you can withdraw that agreement before the hearing starts. If you object to both phone and agency video, do not agree to online video and do not change residence while your appeal is pending, SSA will schedule you in person. In limited cases—including some involving incarceration, safety, a move or extraordinary circumstances—SSA may still override an objection.

Remote appearances were already common when SSA wrote the rule: it reported that about 70.5% of hearing appearances since offices began reopening in March 2022 were by phone. The rule put all four methods into the regulations as standard options. Commissioner Martin O'Malley approved it in August 2024. As of November 2026, SSA's current guidance and the regulation still list all four.
