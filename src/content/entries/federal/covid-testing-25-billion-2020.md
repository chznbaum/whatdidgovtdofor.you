In spring 2020, when COVID-19 tests were hard to find, this law set aside $25 billion for tests, laboratories, research, supplies, surveillance and contact tracing. HHS distributed the law's $11 billion state, local and tribal allocation in May: $10.25 billion through the CDC and $750 million through Indian Health Service programs. Other amounts were available for new-test research, health centers, rural clinics and as much as $1 billion to cover testing for uninsured people.

You did not apply for this funding. It went to public health departments, clinics, researchers and other organizations that ran testing or expanded capacity. Recipients of the $11 billion had to submit testing plans. The law also required HHS to write a national testing plan and report demographic test data when available.

Sen. Patty Murray said she secured the enacted $25 billion. Democratic leaders Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi negotiated the broader package with Treasury Secretary Steven Mnuchin and White House chief of staff Mark Meadows after Republicans proposed adding about $250 billion only to the small-business loan program. The Senate passed the package by voice vote, the House passed it 388–5, and President Donald Trump signed it April 24, 2020. Congress designated it emergency spending, with no service cuts in the law to offset it. In 2023, a later law cancelled the remaining unobligated testing money except $243 million and inspector-general funds.
