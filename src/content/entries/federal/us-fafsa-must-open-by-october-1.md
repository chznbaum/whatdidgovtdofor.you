The FAFSA is the form that unlocks Pell Grants, federal student loans, and much state and college aid. This law requires the U.S. Department of Education to open it by October 1 every year. The old law let the department wait until January 1. If the form won't be ready in time, the department now has to tell Congress by September 1, and the Secretary of Education has to testify about the delay and what it will cost students and families.

You don't have to do anything to get the benefit. The form opens on schedule, which gives you and your family more time to apply before state and college deadlines and gives schools time to send aid offers before you have to choose. The 2027–28 form opened in September 2026, before the deadline.

The law came after the redesigned 2024–25 FAFSA opened in late December 2023, about three months later than usual, and then had errors that held up aid offers for months. Rep. Erin Houchin (R-Ind.) wrote the bill, and Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.) introduced the Senate version. Rep. Bob Good (R-Va.) added the September certification and testimony requirements in committee. The House passed the bill 381–1 in November 2024, with Republicans and Democrats both voting for it. The Senate approved it by unanimous consent a week later, and President Joe Biden signed it on December 11, 2024.
