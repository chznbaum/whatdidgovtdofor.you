CHAMPVA is VA's health plan for certain family members of veterans. It covers spouses and children of veterans permanently and totally disabled by service-connected conditions, survivors of certain veterans, and primary family caregivers. Before this rule, it capped how much mental health and addiction care it would cover. You needed preapproval for more than 23 outpatient therapy visits a year or more than two sessions a week. Those caps and that extra outpatient preapproval trigger are gone, though care still has to be medically necessary.

The rule also covers phone-only telehealth visits when the service itself is covered and medically necessary. That coverage reached back to May 12, 2020, and people had until Nov. 26, 2024, to file claims for past phone visits. And CHAMPVA no longer charges a deductible or cost share for the contraceptives covered by the rule: IUDs, implants, shots, diaphragms, prescription birth control pills, emergency contraception and sterilization.

You don't have to apply. If you're on CHAMPVA, the coverage applies automatically.

VA proposed the changes in October 2022, and Veterans Affairs Secretary Denis McDonough approved the final rule in April 2024. It took effect May 30, 2024. As of November 2026, the federal rules still carry all three changes.
