If you apply for a federal job, the agency generally can't ask about your criminal record until it has made you a conditional job offer. That covers questions on the application, on USAJOBS, on hiring forms, and in interviews. The rule reaches competitive, excepted, and senior executive jobs across the executive branch. Some jobs are exceptions, including law enforcement roles and jobs that need a security clearance or involve sensitive national security work. The rule is about when an agency can ask, not whether your record can ever be considered.

You don't need to apply for this protection. If an agency asks too early, you can file a complaint with that agency within 30 days. The agency investigates and reports to the Office of Personnel Management (OPM), which can discipline the employees responsible.

The protection comes from the Fair Chance to Compete for Jobs Act. Its leads were Sen. Cory Booker, a Democrat, and Sen. Ron Johnson, a Republican, in the Senate, and Rep. Elijah Cummings, a Democrat, and Rep. Doug Collins, a Republican, in the House. Cummings died weeks before the bill passed. It became law inside the 2020 defense bill, which President Donald Trump signed on Dec. 20, 2019. OPM, led by Director Kiran Ahuja, issued the final rule in 2023. The rule took effect Oct. 2, 2023, and federal agencies still apply it today.
