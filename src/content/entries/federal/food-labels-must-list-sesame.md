If you or your child is allergic to sesame, the label on packaged food now has to tell you when it's there. Since January 1, 2023, sesame has the same labeling rules as the other eight major allergens, such as peanuts and milk. A food or dietary supplement with sesame must name it in the ingredient list, in a "Contains" line, or in parentheses after another ingredient. Before this, sesame could hide inside words like "spices" or "natural flavors."

You don't have to do anything to get this. Food already on its way to stores or on store shelves before 2023 didn't have to be pulled or relabeled. The law also told the Department of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on food allergy research and data.

There has been a catch. The FDA has seen some companies, mostly bakers, add sesame on purpose to foods that didn't have it, then label it, instead of keeping their lines sesame-free. The FDA has said it doesn't support this, because it leaves people with allergies fewer safe choices.

Sens. Tim Scott, a Republican, and Chris Murphy, a Democrat, wrote the bill in 2020, and Reps. Doris Matsui and Patrick McHenry carried it in the House. The Senate passed it without objection on March 3, 2021, and the House passed it 415–11 that April. President Joe Biden signed it on April 23, 2021.
