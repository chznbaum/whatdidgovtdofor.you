When one spouse needs nursing-home-level care, Medicaid lets the other spouse keep some of the couple’s income and assets so they can remain at home. Since 2014, that protection has also covered couples when the spouse needing care receives it at home or in the community instead of in a nursing home. This law extended the protection from March 31 to Sept. 30, 2019. In 2019, the community-spouse resource allowance ranged from $25,284 to $126,420, and the minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance was $2,057.50. A person applied through the state Medicaid program; the state then had to use the rules when deciding eligibility and, when applicable, how much income the recipient contributed toward care.

Without the protection, families could face a hard choice: move a spouse into a nursing home or spend down more of their assets. Rep. Debbie Dingell and Rep. Fred Upton had introduced a bill to make the protection permanent and pushed for its extension. Rep. Raul Ruiz sponsored the Medicaid package and credited Dingell and Upton on the House floor. Both chambers passed it by voice vote, and President Donald Trump signed it on April 18, 2019. Because the previous protection had lapsed after March 31, CMS directed states to reverse adverse eligibility or income-contribution calculations made from April 1 through April 18. Later laws kept the protection going through Sept. 30, 2027.
