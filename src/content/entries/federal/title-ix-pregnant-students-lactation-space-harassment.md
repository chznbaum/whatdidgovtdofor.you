The 2024 rule required schools and colleges receiving federal education money, where the rule was allowed to take effect, to make reasonable changes for pregnant students. Those changes could include breaks, schedule changes, time for medical appointments, and a clean, private lactation space. The rule also required voluntary pregnancy leave and gave employees lactation time and space.

A student did not have to file a discrimination complaint to get pregnancy-related help. But the student, or someone acting for them, had to tell the school’s Title IX coordinator about the pregnancy or related condition. The coordinator then had to arrange the needed steps. Schools could ask for documentation only when it was necessary and reasonable.

The rule also broadened which hostile-environment harassment schools had to address. Conduct could qualify if it was objectively and subjectively offensive and severe or pervasive enough to limit or deny access to education. The rule also said sex discrimination included discrimination tied to sex stereotypes, pregnancy, sexual orientation or gender identity.

Catherine Lhamon led the Education Department’s Office for Civil Rights, which issued and enforced the rule, and Education Secretary Miguel Cardona signed it. Courts kept it from ever taking effect in 22 states; by late August 2024 it was blocked in 26 states and some schools elsewhere. A federal judge vacated the whole rule nationwide on Jan. 9, 2025. The department formally repealed it and restored the pre-2024 rules on Sept. 29, 2026.
