VA's Chapter 18 programs cover eligible children with spina bifida whose parent is a Vietnam veteran or a veteran with certain service in Korea or Thailand. They also cover eligible children with certain other birth defects if their mother served in Vietnam. The spina bifida program pays for medically necessary care with no copays or deductibles. This 2023 law makes clear that covered health care and benefits last for the child's whole life and continue even if a parent dies.

The law also requires VA care and coordination teams to try to contact each covered child at least once every 180 days and to reach out quickly when a condition on VA's trigger list is identified. VA must also maintain an advisory council to hear from covered children and veterans and report to Congress every two years on enrollment, social-worker support, and outreach. People seeking these benefits still apply through the existing VA programs; there is no separate application for lifetime coverage.

A 2021 VA inspector general report reinforced the case for changes. It found that the two VA offices running the spina bifida program were not sharing data well enough, contributing to delayed health care enrollment. VA also provided inconsistent outreach and ineffective case management for some beneficiaries.

Sen. Mike Braun, an Indiana Republican, first proposed lifetime benefits and care coordination in 2020. He introduced S. 112 with Sen. Maggie Hassan, a New Hampshire Democrat, and Rep. Jim Baird led the identical House bill. The Senate passed S. 112 by unanimous consent, and the House passed it 422–0. President Joe Biden signed it on October 6, 2023.
