If you're buying a home or opening a business on tribal trust or restricted land, your mortgage may need BIA approval. Federal rules already gave BIA 20 days to decide complete residential and business leasehold-mortgage applications. The GAO found that about a quarter of the applications it could analyze missed that deadline in 2021–22. Sen. Mike Rounds said some approvals took more than a year.

This law puts the deadlines into federal law. BIA must check a mortgage package within 10 days and notify the lender within 2 days of finding a gap. For covered packages, it then has 20 days to decide a complete home or business leasehold mortgage, and 30 days for a land mortgage or right-of-way. Denials must be written and explained, and BIA must give notice immediately if it misses a deadline. Title reports also have deadlines. The law directs BIA to establish a Realty Ombudsman to monitor compliance and take complaints. These protections need no separate application. The leasehold rule excludes packages submitted by tribes approved to issue their own leases under federal law.

Sens. John Thune and Tina Smith introduced the bill in February 2025, with Mike Rounds as an original cosponsor. Rep. Dusty Johnson led it in the House. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent that December. The House approved it 384–40 in March 2026, with most members of both parties voting yes. President Donald Trump signed it on May 4. CBO estimated implementation would cost about $2 million over 2025–2030.
