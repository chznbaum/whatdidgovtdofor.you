If you're a veteran using the Post-9/11 GI Bill, the Montgomery GI Bill-Active Duty, or VA Veteran Readiness and Employment, public colleges in the state where you live must charge you no more than in-state tuition and fees. It doesn't matter how long ago you got out. Before this law, the discount only applied if you enrolled within three years of discharge. Waiting longer could mean paying out-of-state rates, which the GI Bill doesn't always fully cover.

Ask your school what proof it needs. Some schools require you to show that you live in the state or intend to become a resident. VA must disapprove courses at public schools that charge covered students above the resident rate. For the two GI Bill programs, this protection requires discharge after at least 90 days of active service; veterans entitled to VR&E qualify under separate rules. The change applies to terms starting on or after August 1, 2021. The law also tells VA to post each public school's in-state tuition rules online.

Rep. Phil Roe, a Republican, and Rep. Elaine Luria, a Democrat, introduced the House fix in July 2019. Sen. Marsha Blackburn, a Republican, and Sen. Joe Manchin, a Democrat, brought the same idea to the Senate that month. It became part of a large veterans package that the leaders of the House and Senate Veterans' Affairs committees, Mark Takano, Jerry Moran and Jon Tester, moved through Congress. The Senate passed the package unanimously. President Donald Trump signed it on January 5, 2021.
