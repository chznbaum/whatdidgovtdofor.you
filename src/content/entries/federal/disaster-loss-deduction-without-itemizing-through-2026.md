When a hurricane, flood, or wildfire wrecks your home or belongings, this law lets you deduct the uninsured loss even if you take the standard deduction. It covers federally declared disasters whose incident period began between Dec. 28, 2019 and the end of 2026. After a $500 reduction for each casualty, the rest is deductible without the usual 10%-of-income floor. Without this law, disasters beginning after July 4, 2025 would fall under those stricter rules.

The law also excludes qualified wildfire relief payments from taxable income. Together with the 2024 law, it covers payments received from 2020 onward for losses not covered by insurance or another reimbursement, if the fire was federally declared from 2015 through 2026. Covered payments include extra living expenses, non-employer lost wages, injuries, death, and emotional distress. You claim the casualty deduction on your tax return; qualified wildfire payments are left out of gross income.

Rep. Greg Steube (R-Fla.) introduced the bill with Reps. Mike Thompson (D-Calif.), Jimmy Panetta (D-Calif.), and Doug LaMalfa (R-Calif.). Thompson and LaMalfa developed the wildfire-payment relief for California fire victims; Steube and Thompson co-led the 2024 discharge petition that forced an earlier version to a vote. The bill was renamed after LaMalfa died in January 2026. Sens. Rick Scott (R-Fla.) and Adam Schiff (D-Calif.) led the Senate companion. President Donald Trump signed the bill on Sept. 11, 2026. JCT estimated $408 million in reduced revenue from 2026 through 2036; the law did not pair the relief with service cuts.
