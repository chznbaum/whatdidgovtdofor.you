Tidewater Gardens was a 618-unit public housing complex built in 1955 on top of a filled-in creek next to downtown Norfolk. More than half the site sat in the 100-year floodplain, and streets often flooded at high tide. In 2019 the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development gave Norfolk and its housing authority $30 million to replace it and rebuild the surrounding St. Paul's area.

The current plan calls for 714 new mixed-income homes, a restored street grid, and a buried stretch of Newton Creek brought back to the surface as a "blueway" with places for recreation. New homes are being built on the old site and nearby, and Tidewater Gardens households could also take a housing choice voucher and move to a place of their choosing. The rebuilt neighborhood is now called Kindred. The city committed $3 million a year for five years to People First, a program of case management and job, health and education help for residents.

Several Kindred buildings have opened, and more than 100 former residents have moved back. Unity Place, with 140 apartments and a McDonald's, began leasing in 2025 and held its grand opening in May 2026. The next building, Kinship at Kindred, reserves 73 of its 191 apartments for former Tidewater Gardens residents.

The grant was awarded under HUD Secretary Ben Carson. Plans to redevelop the neighboring Young Terrace and Calvert Square communities are expected to begin in 2027.
