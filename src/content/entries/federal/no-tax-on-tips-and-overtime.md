The One Big Beautiful Bill Act created two new federal income tax deductions for working people, starting with 2025 returns and running through 2028.

The tips deduction lets you subtract up to $25,000 a year in qualified tips from your taxable income. Employees and self-employed people in jobs that customarily and regularly received tips on or before December 31, 2024, may qualify under a list the Treasury published. A separate tax-code rule excludes workers in specified service trades or businesses. The overtime deduction covers the extra "half" of time-and-a-half pay that federal wage law requires, up to $12,500 a year, or $25,000 for married couples filing jointly.

Both deductions work whether you take the standard deduction or itemize. You claim them on your tax return. You need a valid Social Security number, and married people must file jointly. The deductions shrink for people with modified adjusted gross income above $150,000, or $300,000 for joint filers. In the first filing season, tips filers averaged deductions of over $7,000 and overtime filers over $3,100.

Republicans passed the law without Democratic votes. Vice President JD Vance broke a 50–50 tie in the Senate, the House approved it 218–214, and President Donald Trump signed it on July 4, 2025.
