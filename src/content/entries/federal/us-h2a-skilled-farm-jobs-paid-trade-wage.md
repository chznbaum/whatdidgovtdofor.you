Farms that bring in H-2A guest workers must pay at least a minimum hourly rate the Labor Department sets. That rate keeps foreign hiring from dragging down pay for workers already here doing the same jobs. Until 2023, that rate came from a survey of field and livestock workers, even for workers who drove semis, built barns or supervised crews.

This rule changed that. Field and livestock jobs, which are about 98% of H-2A positions, kept the farm survey rate. Other jobs, like construction laborers, heavy truck drivers, farm supervisors and equipment mechanics, had to pay at least the average wage for that trade in the state, taken from a Labor Department wage survey. Workers didn't need to apply. Employers had to promise the rate when they asked for guest workers. The department estimated the change would move about $37.8 million a year from employers to workers.

Labor Secretary Marty Walsh's department issued the rule, and Brent Parton, acting head of its Employment and Training Administration, signed it. It took effect March 30, 2023. Louisiana sugarcane growers sued over the truck-driver rate. In August 2025 a federal judge threw out the whole rule, and the Labor Department under the Trump administration didn't oppose the final judgment. The department then set a new method that pays less to some workers. Another judge found that replacement unlawful in August 2026 but left it in place temporarily and later scheduled it for replacement and vacatur on Dec. 30, 2026.
