If you have a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration and fall behind, your servicer can modify the loan by rolling what you owe into a new loan term. Before this rule, FHA's standard regulation capped that term at 30 years, although a temporary COVID option already allowed a 40-year term when paired with an interest-free second lien called a partial claim. Since May 8, 2023, the standard term can run up to 40 years. Spreading the balance over 10 more years lowers the payment compared with a 30-year modification, so a family that could not afford the shorter option may be able to keep its home. The tradeoff is more interest over time and slower growth in home equity.

You do not apply separately for the 40-year option. Contact your servicer and ask to be reviewed for FHA loss mitigation. You must provide current information, and you may have to complete a trial payment plan before receiving a permanent modification. The servicer reviews FHA's options to determine what help you qualify for. Since October 2025, FHA has generally limited borrowers to one permanent loss mitigation option every 24 months.

Making the 40-year term a standard option also brought FHA in line with Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans, which already offered it. HUD proposed the change in April 2022 under Secretary Marcia Fudge, and public comments were overwhelmingly supportive. The department finalized it without changes in March 2023. FHA Commissioner Julia Gordon signed the rule.
