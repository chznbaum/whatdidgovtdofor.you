A TEACH Grant helps pay for college if you promise to teach four years in a high-need field at a low-income school. If you do not finish, it becomes a loan with interest. For years, some teachers lost grants over paperwork errors. The bill’s sponsors, citing the Office of Management and Budget, said 94,000 recipients had seen grants converted.

The law lets anyone whose grant became a loan ask the Education Department to reconsider, including older or paid-off loans. To win reinstatement, show you met or are meeting your service promise and the conversion had a valid reason, such as late paperwork, department error or delay, or a past voluntary conversion. The department has 90 days to decide. If approved, the loan, interest and fees are discharged. Payments shift to other Direct Loans or are refunded. The department asks credit bureaus to remove negative marks, and unfinished teaching service remains due. Recipients whose eight-year service window began during or included the qualifying COVID emergency got up to three extra years.

The department began accepting reconsideration requests in February 2019 and changed its rules in July 2021. The law made reconsideration a legal right with a deadline. Sen. Mike Braun and Sen. Kyrsten Sinema led the Senate bill; Del. Eleanor Holmes Norton and Rep. Victoria Spartz led the House companion. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent in April 2021. The House passed it 406–16 that September with support from both parties. President Joe Biden signed it on October 13, 2021.
