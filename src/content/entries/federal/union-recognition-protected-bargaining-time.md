If most of your coworkers sign up and your employer agrees to recognize your union without an election, the union gets a fair chance to bargain your first contract. Under this rule, the NLRB generally will not process an election petition challenging the union for at least 6 months after the first bargaining session, and for up to a year depending on how talks are going. A 2020 rule had required a posted notice and a 45-day window in which workers or a competing union could file a petition backed by at least 30% of the bargaining unit. The Board's compiled data found 413 notice requests and at most two changes in representative status.

The rule also lets regional labor officials pause an election when a party files an unfair labor practice charge, requests a pause and supplies an offer of proof describing conduct that, if proven, would interfere with workers' free choice. It gives building-trades unions a clearer route to the same protections other unions have. Workers don't have to apply. The recognition bar applies to unions recognized on or after September 30, 2024; the other changes apply to cases filed after that date.

NLRB Chairman Lauren McFerran and Members Gwynne Wilcox and David Prouty issued the rule, reversing the 2020 changes and restoring decades of earlier Board law. Member Marvin Kaplan dissented. As of November 2026, the rule remains in the federal regulations, but a pending rulemaking petition asks the Board to repeal its recognition-bar and blocking-charge protections.
