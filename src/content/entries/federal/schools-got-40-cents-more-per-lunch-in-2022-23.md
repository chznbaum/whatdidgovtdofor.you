Food prices and supply problems squeezed school cafeterias as pandemic meal waivers neared their June 30 end. The law paid schools more for meals in 2022–23: 40 cents more per lunch and 15 cents more per breakfast, whether it was free, reduced-price or paid. Child and adult day care, family child care and afterschool programs got 10 cents more per meal or snack. Family child care homes were paid at the higher Tier I rate for that year.

Schools and care providers received the higher payments without families applying. The law let USDA offer summer meal flexibilities, such as grab-and-go meals and parent pickup, through September 2022. It didn't extend free meals for every student, so families again applied for free or reduced-price meals that fall. The extra payments ended June 30, 2023.

The bipartisan deal was written by Sen. Debbie Stabenow and Sen. John Boozman, who led the Senate Agriculture Committee, and Rep. Bobby Scott and Rep. Virginia Foxx, who led the House Education and Labor Committee. A House-passed version would also have made reduced-price meals free, but that provision was dropped to clear the Senate. The Senate then passed the bill unanimously, the House agreed on June 24, and President Joe Biden signed it on June 25, 2022. It added nothing to the deficit. Congress offset its cost by rescinding $3.6 billion in unobligated pandemic-relief balances at USDA, the Education Department and the Small Business Administration and ending $20 million a year for USDA nutrition research after 2022.
