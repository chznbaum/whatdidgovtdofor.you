Fort Monroe sits on a peninsula at Old Point Comfort in Hampton. It served as a military post until 2011. When the Army moved out, President Barack Obama signed a proclamation on November 1, 2011, setting aside about 325 acres as the Fort Monroe National Monument. It is now a national park.

The site holds two turning points in American history. In 1619, the first enslaved Africans brought to England's colonies in America arrived on this peninsula. In 1861, early in the Civil War, Gen. Benjamin Butler's "Contraband Decision" at the fort gave thousands of enslaved people a path to freedom.

Today the park is the largest stone fort ever built in the United States, ringed by a moat and shaded by more than 300 live oaks, including the Algernourne Oak, which is about 500 years old. Visitors can explore the fort's history, watch wildlife such as ospreys, and attend events like the annual August ceremony marking the 1619 arrival. The proclamation called for a management plan to preserve the site and explain its history to the public.
