If you've been treated for addiction at a federally assisted program, Part 2 gives your records extra protection. Without your written consent or a qualifying court order and legal demand, they generally cannot be used to investigate or prosecute you or used against you in civil, criminal, administrative or legislative proceedings. That protection follows covered records when they reach other doctors, hospitals, health plans and business associates. If a clinician separately keeps SUD counseling notes for their own use, those notes need separate consent before they can be shared.

You do not have to apply. If a Part 2 program has a breach of unsecured records, it must notify you and HHS under rules modeled on HIPAA. You can file a complaint with HHS's Office for Civil Rights, and a regulated organization cannot retaliate. The office began accepting complaints and enforcing the rule on Feb. 16, 2026; remedies can include civil money penalties.

The rule also makes sharing easier. You may sign one consent allowing future use and disclosure for treatment, payment and health care operations until you revoke it in writing. Some commenters warned that this broader consent could weaken privacy, while HHS said it would improve care coordination.

Sen. Joe Manchin introduced S. 3374, the Protecting Jessica Grubb's Legacy Act, with Sen. Shelley Moore Capito as an original cosponsor. Congress enacted its provisions as Section 3221 of the CARES Act, which President Donald Trump signed on March 27, 2020. HHS Secretary Xavier Becerra issued the final rule in February 2024.
