Sometimes VA pays you too much in disability, pension or education benefits, often because of its own mistake or a change it was slow to process, and then sends a letter saying you owe the money back. You can ask VA to waive the debt if repaying it would be unfair. You used to have only 180 days from the debt letter to ask. Now you have a full year.

The longer window applies to debt notices VA has sent since Dec. 29, 2024. To ask for a waiver, send VA a financial status report (VA Form 5655) and a statement explaining why you shouldn't have to repay. If you can show that the letter reached you late because of a mail or VA error, or other circumstances beyond your control, VA can count the year from when you actually got it. Notices sent before that date still had the old 180-day limit.

Rep. Chris Pappas, a New Hampshire Democrat, wrote the change into his VA Beneficiary Debt Collection Improvement Act in 2021, working with Rep. Troy Nehls, a Texas Republican. Congress folded it into a large veterans package, the Cleland-Dole Act, which passed as part of the year-end 2022 spending law with votes from both parties. President Joe Biden signed it on Dec. 29, 2022. The law gave VA two years to start, and VA published a final rule in November 2025 that took effect Jan. 26, 2026.
