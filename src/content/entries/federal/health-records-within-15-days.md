When you ask a covered doctor, hospital or health plan for access to your protected health information, it will have to act on the request as soon as practical and within 15 calendar days, down from 30. If it grants the request, it must provide access within that period. It may take one extension of no more than 30 additional days, but must tell you in writing why and give you a completion date. A shorter federal or state deadline still controls.

In-person inspection is free, as is automated access to electronic records through a patient portal or personal health app. A portal or app request that requires staff to prepare a copy manually may still carry a reasonable, cost-based fee. Covered entities with websites must post estimated fee schedules. If electronic records are readily producible in an app's format, they must be provided that way when requested. You may also direct electronic records in an electronic health record to another person. Providers cannot impose unreasonable barriers such as requiring notarization when a less burdensome verification method is practical.

The rule also expressly permits covered entities to share protected information with social-service agencies and community groups for individual care coordination or case management without prior authorization. HIPAA's minimum-necessary limit still applies where required, including disclosures for health care operations.

OCR developed the proposal under Director Roger Severino as part of Deputy Secretary Eric Hargan's Regulatory Sprint under Secretary Alex Azar. Nearly six years later, OCR under Paula Stannard finalized many of those ideas, and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. signed the rule. HHS dropped other proposals, including looser standards for disclosures to family members and caregivers. Compliance begins July 17, 2027.
