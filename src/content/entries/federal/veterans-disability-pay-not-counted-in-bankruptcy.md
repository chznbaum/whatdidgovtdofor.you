For many people who file under Chapter 7, a "means test" uses recent income and allowed expenses to check whether the case is presumed abusive. That presumption can lead to dismissal or conversion to Chapter 13, a repayment plan that usually lasts three to five years. Chapter 7, when available, clears most unsecured debts. The HAVEN Act keeps certain VA and Defense Department payments tied to a service member's disability or death out of the current-monthly-income calculation used in the test. Social Security benefits were already left out.

The change does not require a separate application. If you or your family gets covered payments, you leave them out of current monthly income when completing the means-test forms. More disabled veterans may qualify for Chapter 7, and covered payments are also left out of Chapter 13's disposable-income calculation. One limit: military disability retired pay is excluded only to the extent that it is more than the regular retired pay the service member would otherwise get.

Sen. Tammy Baldwin, a Wisconsin Democrat, introduced the Senate version with Texas Republican John Cornyn. Rep. Lucy McBath, a Georgia Democrat, introduced the House bill with Florida Republican Greg Steube and led it through the House. It passed the House by voice vote on July 23, 2019, and the Senate by voice vote on Aug. 1. President Donald Trump signed it on Aug. 23, 2019. The law has no end date, and the exclusion remains in the bankruptcy code.
