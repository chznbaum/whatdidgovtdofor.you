If you work on a road, bridge, school, or other construction project covered by the Davis-Bacon laws, your employer must pay at least the local prevailing wage for your trade. This rule changed how that wage is set. When no single rate is earned by most workers in a trade, the most common rate now counts as prevailing if at least 30% earn it. Before, the Labor Department used a weighted average. The government used this 30% test from 1935 until 1983.

The rule also lets the Labor Department adjust certain rates not set by collective bargaining between surveys, using a federal measure of compensation changes, so they are less likely to sit unchanged for years. It protects workers from being fired or punished for raising pay concerns or helping an investigation. It also clarifies which companies count as prime contractors and when upper-tier subcontractors can share responsibility for underpayments. You do not apply: the protections are built into covered contracts.

Acting Wage and Hour Administrator Jessica Looman signed the proposal in March 2022, and Labor Secretary Marty Walsh announced and backed it. Acting Secretary Julie Su issued the final rule in August 2023 after the first comprehensive review of these regulations in nearly 40 years. In 2026, a federal court vacated three parts covering some material suppliers, some delivery drivers, and contracts missing required wage clauses. Associated Builders and Contractors is still suing to overturn the 30% test.
