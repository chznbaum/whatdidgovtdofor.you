The Crime Victims Fund uses fines and penalties from federal criminal cases, not taxes. It supports state programs that reimburse medical bills, counseling, lost wages, and funeral costs, and local services including domestic violence shelters, rape crisis centers, and child abuse programs. As prosecutors increasingly used deferred- and non-prosecution agreements, those penalties went to the general Treasury, fund deposits fell, and grants shrank. The law redirects those penalties to the fund. By September 30, 2026, the agreements had sent it about $3 billion.

It also raised federal reimbursements for state compensation payouts from 60% to 75%, let states waive local programs' matching costs, and allowed compensation when victims had good reason not to cooperate with police. Victims apply to the program in the state where the crime happened.

Rep. Jerrold Nadler introduced the House bill. Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick, Sheila Jackson Lee, Ann Wagner, Mary Gay Scanlon, Cathy McMorris Rodgers, Debbie Dingell, and John Moolenaar co-led it. Sens. Dick Durbin, Lindsey Graham, Tammy Baldwin, Chuck Grassley, Dianne Feinstein, John Cornyn, Amy Klobuchar, and Lisa Murkowski led the Senate effort. The House passed it 384–38, the Senate 100–0, and President Joe Biden signed it July 22, 2021. It redirects existing penalties from the general Treasury rather than using taxes. Fund deposits still fluctuated, and state victim assistance grants fell an average 40% from fiscal 2023 to 2024.
