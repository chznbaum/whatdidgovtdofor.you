If you get VA disability compensation, your monthly payment went up 5.9% on Dec. 1, 2021. The same raise went to dependency and indemnity compensation for eligible surviving spouses and children, and to the yearly clothing allowance for veterans whose prosthetics, orthopedic devices, or skin medicines damage their clothes. A veteran rated 10% got $152.64 a month, $8.50 more than before. A veteran rated 100% with no dependents got $3,332.06, $185.64 more. The clothing allowance rose to $891 a year.

If you were already receiving one of these benefits, you did not have to apply for the higher rate. VA matched the 5.9% increase Social Security recipients got for 2022. Benefits groups called it the largest increase in nearly 40 years, as prices were climbing fast.

VA disability pay does not rise with prices on its own: Congress has to pass a law each year. Sen. John Thune, a South Dakota Republican, introduced this bill with Sen. Brian Schatz, a Hawaii Democrat, as original cosponsor. Rep. Elaine Luria, a Virginia Democrat, sponsored its House companion with Rep. Troy Nehls, a Texas Republican, as original cosponsor. Democratic Sen. Jon Tester's Veterans' Affairs Committee reported an amended version to the Senate, which passed it by unanimous consent. Democratic Rep. Mark Takano moved the bill's House passage, and Republican Rep. Mike Bost led his party's side of the floor debate. The House passed it 423–0, with every Democrat and Republican who voted backing it. President Joe Biden signed it on Oct. 8, 2021.
