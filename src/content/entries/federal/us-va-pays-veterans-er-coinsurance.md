Say you're a veteran who has an emergency, goes to the nearest non-VA hospital, and also has private insurance or another health plan. Your plan may pay most of the bill and leave you with coinsurance, your percentage share of the cost. Since April 24, 2023, VA can pay that share. VA pays after your other plan does, up to whatever you still owe or 70% of what Medicare would pay, whichever is less. If the provider accepts VA's payment, you no longer owe that coinsurance. VA still won't cover your plan's deductibles or flat copays.

You file a claim with VA for emergency care at a non-VA hospital, the same way you would for any other outside emergency. The rule also opened a one-year window for claims involving qualifying visits on or after Feb. 1, 2010 that had occurred more than 90 days before Feb. 22, 2023. Veterans could file until Feb. 22, 2024, whether or not they had filed before.

Veterans had to fight for this in court. Coast Guard veteran Amanda Wolfe was left with about $2,500 after her insurer paid most of a bill of more than $20,000. Represented by the National Veterans Legal Services Program, she brought a class action. In March 2022, the Federal Circuit held that coinsurance is not like a copay. That October, Wolfe and fellow veteran Joshua Kimmel won an order requiring VA to rewrite its rule within 120 days. Veterans Affairs Secretary Denis McDonough approved the final rule.
