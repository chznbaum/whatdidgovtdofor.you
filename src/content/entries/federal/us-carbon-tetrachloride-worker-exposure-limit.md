Carbon tetrachloride is a chemical that can damage the liver and cause liver cancer and other tumors. Factories still use it to make other chemicals, including newer refrigerants that do less to warm the planet. This EPA rule requires employers whose workers handle it to keep the air at or below 0.03 parts per million averaged over an 8-hour shift. The old OSHA limit is 10 ppm. Employers have to test the air, mark off and restrict access to high-exposure areas, train workers, protect their skin, and provide respirators when other controls aren't enough. The rule also bans uses that had already stopped, such as adding the chemical to car fuel or plastic parts, so they can't come back.

Workers don't need to apply for anything. EPA estimated that 852 to 9,554 workers, plus 500 to 4,144 people who work nearby without handling it, are exposed each year at roughly 30 to 71 facilities.

EPA's chemical safety office under Michal Freedhoff wrote the rule, and Administrator Michael Regan signed it in December 2024. It gave companies 36 months to put the full program in place, up from the 12 months first proposed. In September 2025, EPA under Lee Zeldin said it would reconsider the rule, including the limit itself. In July 2026 it pushed the deadline for non-federal employers to meet the limit to September 2027.
