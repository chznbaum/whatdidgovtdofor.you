New passenger cars, SUVs, pickups, vans and most small buses will have to tell the driver when rear seat belts are not in use. When the car starts, a dashboard light lasting at least 60 seconds shows how many or which rear belts are buckled or unbuckled. If a rear rider unbuckles while the car is moving, a sound and a light go off for at least 30 seconds. The front warning also gets stronger and now covers the front outboard passenger as well as the driver. The chime continues while an unbuckled driver or front passenger's vehicle is traveling at 6.2 mph or faster.

You do not need to apply; the warnings come built into new vehicles. Rear-seat riders buckle up less than people in front, and about half of passenger-vehicle occupants who died in crashes in 2022 were unbelted. NHTSA estimates the rule will save about 50 lives and prevent more than 500 injuries a year once fully in place. It estimated a $19.59 minimum cost in 2020 dollars for each vehicle needing new rear buckle sensors, about 53% of affected new vehicles.

Public Citizen and Advocates for Highway and Auto Safety petitioned for the rule in 2007. Congress ordered it in 2012 and set an October 2015 deadline. Kids and Car Safety and the Center for Auto Safety sued in 2017 after NHTSA missed it. NHTSA finalized the rule in December 2024 and published it in January 2025. In April 2026, after automakers sought more time, NHTSA moved the main compliance date to Sept. 1, 2028; multi-stage manufacturers and alterers have until Sept. 1, 2029.
