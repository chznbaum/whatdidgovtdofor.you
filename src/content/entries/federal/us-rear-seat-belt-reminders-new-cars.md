New cars, SUVs, pickups, vans and most small buses will have to tell the driver when rear belts are not in use. At startup, a dashboard light lasting at least 60 seconds shows how many or which rear belts are buckled or unbuckled. If a rear rider unbuckles in drive or reverse, a sound and light last at least 30 seconds. Front warnings also get stronger and cover the driver and front outboard passenger. The chime stays on while an unbuckled driver or front passenger is traveling at least 6.2 mph.

No application is needed; the warnings come built into new vehicles. Rear-seat use is lower than front-seat use, and about half of passenger-vehicle occupants killed in crashes in 2022 were unbelted. NHTSA estimates the rule will save about 50 lives and prevent more than 500 injuries a year once fully in place. It estimated the minimum cost at $19.59 in 2020 dollars for roughly 53% of affected new vehicles needing rear sensors and wiring.

Public Citizen and Advocates for Highway and Auto Safety petitioned in 2007. Congress ordered the rule in 2012, with an October 2015 deadline. Kids and Car Safety and the Center for Auto Safety sued in 2017 after NHTSA missed it. Under Acting Administrator Sophie Shulman, NHTSA finalized it in December 2024; Chief Counsel Adam Raviv signed it. In April 2026, after automakers sought more time, NHTSA moved the main compliance date to Sept. 1, 2028; multi-stage manufacturers and alterers have until Sept. 1, 2029.
