Starting October 20, 2027, if a dating site or app ends or suspends an account because it sees a significant risk the user will try to get money by fraud, it must warn everyone who received a message from that account. The notice identifies the profile and latest contact, says the user may have used a false identity or attempted fraud, warns against sending money or financial details, and gives safety tips and customer-service information.

You do not need to apply. The warning goes by email, text, or another accepted method, normally within 24 hours. Circumstances can extend the deadline to three days after the ban. After a law-enforcement delay, it is due within three days. The FTC and state officials can enforce the law. Apps do not have to find or ban scammers; the rule starts only after a fraud-risk ban. It blocks different state and local notification rules but preserves state contract and tort law.

Rep. David Valadao (R-Calif.) first introduced a broader version in 2022. Rep. Brittany Pettersen (D-Colo.) joined him on a 2023 version that passed the House in 2024 but not the Senate. In 2025, Valadao introduced the bill with Pettersen and Reps. Tom Suozzi (D-N.Y.) and Craig Goldman (R-Texas). Sens. Marsha Blackburn (R-Tenn.) and John Hickenlooper (D-Colo.) introduced the Senate companion; Blackburn brought the House bill up for Senate passage. The House passed it by voice vote in 2025 and the Senate by unanimous consent in 2026. Donald Trump signed it Oct. 20, 2026.
