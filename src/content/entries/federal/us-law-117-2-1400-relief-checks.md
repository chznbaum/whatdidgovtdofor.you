In spring 2021, a year into the pandemic, the federal government sent most households a third relief payment. The full payment was $1,400 for each eligible adult, with another $1,400 for every qualifying dependent, including college students and older relatives. A family of four could get $5,600.

Most people didn't have to apply. The IRS used tax returns and Social Security records to send payments by direct deposit, paper check, or debit card. The first payments went out March 12, 2021, the day after the law was signed. The full amount went to most filers with adjusted gross income up to $75,000, heads of household up to $112,500, and joint filers or qualifying widows and widowers up to $150,000. Payments shrank above those amounts and stopped at $80,000, $120,000, and $160,000, respectively. People who were missed, or whose families grew in 2021, could claim the money on their 2021 tax return. By the end of 2021, the IRS had sent more than 175 million payments worth over $400 billion.

Ways and Means Chair Richard Neal's committee wrote the payment provision, and Budget Chair John Yarmuth sponsored the full American Rescue Plan Act. Joe Biden had proposed the $1,400 checks in January 2021 to bring December's $600 payment up to $2,000. The bill passed without Republican votes: 50–49 in the Senate and 220–211 in the House. Biden signed it on March 11, 2021.
