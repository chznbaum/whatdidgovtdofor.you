When a veteran needs daily help but wants to stay home, VA can pay for a home health aide, respite care for family caregivers, adult day care, skilled home health care, or Veteran Directed Care, where veterans hire their own helpers. Before this law, VA could spend only up to 65% of what nursing home care would cost. When veterans reached that cap, they had to pay the remaining costs themselves or enter nursing home care. Now VA can spend up to the full nursing home rate. For veterans with ALS, a spinal cord injury, or a similar condition, VA can approve even more after a clinical review.

VA began using the higher limit on Sept. 11, 2025. Ask your VA social worker whether it could cover your care.

Sen. Jerry Moran introduced the home care bill with Sens. Jon Tester and Maggie Hassan in 2022 and again in 2023. Reps. Julia Brownley and Jack Bergman carried the House version. It became Section 120 of a broader veterans package named for former Sen. Elizabeth Dole, a longtime military-caregiver advocate. Committee leaders Mike Bost, Mark Takano, Tester, and Moran moved the package through Congress. The law delayed a scheduled cut in VA home-loan funding fees until June 9, 2034; CBO estimated that would collect $1.523 billion from VA borrowers over 2025–2034 to offset the package's expanded benefits. The Senate passed it without objection, the House passed it 382–12 with support from both parties, and President Joe Biden signed it on January 2, 2025.
