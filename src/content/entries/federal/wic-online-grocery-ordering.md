WIC helps pregnant women, new mothers, babies, and young children buy healthy food. Federal rules generally required you to pay in person at the register and shop at a WIC vendor with a fixed location, so states needed special waivers for online transactions. The new USDA rule removes those barriers on Dec. 7. It lets states approve internet vendors, and participating shoppers can use WIC benefits for pickup or delivery orders.

Online shopping remains a state choice, and availability also depends on whether an authorized internet vendor serves your area. For its paperwork and cost estimates, USDA assumes 44 of the 88 WIC state agencies will implement online shopping. You cannot use WIC benefits for delivery, service, convenience, or bag fees. If a store charges other customers those fees, it must let you pay them another way.

Return rules phase in later. By Nov. 6, 2028, states that authorize internet vendors must support returns to your benefit balance for all vendor types. Benefits from in-person returns must be restored immediately. For post-delivery returns, vendors have two business days after receiving the item or learning that an order was unfulfilled.

USDA first proposed the change in February 2023. Acting Administrator Shiela Corley signed the final rule, which was published Nov. 6, 2026 and takes effect Dec. 7, 2026. The National WIC Association called it long-awaited.
