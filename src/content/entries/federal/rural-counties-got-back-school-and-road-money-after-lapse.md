Many rural counties contain large areas of national forest, and federal land is not subject to local property tax. Since 2000, Secure Rural Schools has supplemented older timber-revenue payments to help cover schools, roads, bridges and emergency services. The program lapsed after fiscal 2023, leaving counties with much smaller payments under the older formula. In Skamania County, Washington, schools laid off staff and a middle school closed.

This law restored the program. The Forest Service began issuing $182 million in retroactive fiscal 2024 payments in February 2026 and announced $230.6 million in Title I and Title III funding for fiscal 2025 that April. A final fiscal 2026 round is due in spring 2027. Residents do not apply. The money goes to eligible states and counties for local schools and services.

Sen. Ron Wyden, an Oregon Democrat, co-authored the original law in 2000. Sen. Mike Crapo, an Idaho Republican, sponsored this renewal; Wyden, Sen. Jim Risch and Sen. Jeff Merkley cosponsored it. Rep. Doug LaMalfa sponsored the House version, with Rep. Joe Neguse as an original cosponsor. The House moved after Crapo, Wyden, LaMalfa and Neguse led an 89-lawmaker letter asking for a vote; Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez led the House passage push. The Senate passed the bill unanimously, and the House passed it 399–5. President Donald Trump signed it on December 18, 2025. The authorization expired September 30, 2026; the senators have introduced S. 5608 to extend it through fiscal 2029, but it remains in committee.
