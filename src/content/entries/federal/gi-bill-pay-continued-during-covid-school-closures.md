When COVID-19 shut down campuses in spring 2020, many student veterans stood to lose their housing money, their VA work-study jobs, or months of GI Bill time they had earned. This law kept that from happening. Veterans who couldn't get to their work-study jobs kept getting paid, or could push back their contract's end date instead. If a school or program closed, VA could keep paying GI Bill or vocational rehabilitation benefits for up to four weeks. Veterans in VA job-placement services could get two more months of subsistence pay.

The law also kept the shutdowns from using up benefits. Classes a student lost credit for because of a closure didn't count against their GI Bill. The deadlines to use the Montgomery GI Bill, older Post-9/11 benefits, and vocational rehabilitation were paused for as long as a closure lasted. Children using benefits passed down from a parent got the same pause. VA made these changes automatically, so students didn't have to apply.

Rep. Mark Takano, who chaired the House Veterans' Affairs Committee, wrote the bill with Ranking Member Phil Roe. Senate committee leaders Jerry Moran and Jon Tester carried it in the Senate. It was the second COVID fix to the GI Bill, passing the House on March 31 and the Senate on April 21. President Donald Trump signed it on April 28, 2020.
