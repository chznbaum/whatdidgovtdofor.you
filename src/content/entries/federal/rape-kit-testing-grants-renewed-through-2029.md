Many rape kits have sat untested in police evidence rooms for years. The Debbie Smith grant program pays state and local crime labs to test that evidence, especially rape kits and cases with no known suspect. It also pays labs to add staff and equipment so new backlogs don't form. Labs upload the DNA profiles to the FBI's national database, where they can match a known offender or link crimes together. Since 2004, the Justice Department says, the program has helped analyze more than 1.6 million DNA cases.

Survivors don't need to apply for anything. The money goes to crime labs and police. This law reauthorizes the program through fiscal 2029 and authorizes $151 million a year. Congress decides the actual amount each year, and in the House debate Rep. Jerry Nadler noted that yearly funding had never reached the full $151 million. Grants are still going out: in April 2026, labs in the Houston area won more than $2.7 million.

Rep. Ann Wagner, a Republican, wrote the bill, and Rep. Steve Cohen, a Democrat, was its lead cosponsor. Reps. Wesley Hunt, Sheila Jackson Lee, and Ben Cline were original House cosponsors later recognized for their leadership. Sens. John Cornyn and Amy Klobuchar led the Senate effort with Dianne Feinstein and Lindsey Graham, while Sen. Rand Paul contributed the audit-grant extension. The House passed the bill 405–0 in November 2023, and the Senate passed it without objection in July 2024. President Joe Biden signed it on July 30, 2024.
