If you get a mammogram at a VA facility, it now uses 3D breast imaging, also called tomosynthesis. A 3D mammogram finds breast cancer better than the older flat image, and fewer patients get called back for more scans. The law gave every VA facility that does mammograms two years to make the switch. In 2024 the head of the VA's radiology program said every in-house program had done it. You don't need to apply: the 3D scan is the standard VA mammogram.

The law also started a pilot for veterans who live far from a VA mammogram site. They got screened closer to home, and the images went to a VA center where radiologists read them. It opened in 2023 at five sites and had done 7,416 mammograms by June 2025. The law authorized the pilot for three years, through May 2026. S. 3395, a bill to make it permanent, remained in committee after an April 2026 hearing. The law also made the VA study genetic testing for veterans with breast cancer and check whether imaging sites work for veterans with paralysis.

Sen. Jon Tester introduced the bill with Sens. Susan Collins, John Boozman, and Mazie Hirono. Reps. Julia Brownley and Mariannette Miller-Meeks led its House version. The Senate passed it unanimously, and the House voted 419–0. President Joe Biden signed it on June 7, 2022.
