The VA offers two grants that help veterans and service members with certain service-connected disabilities buy, build or change a permanent home with ramps, wider doors and other accessibility features. The law doubled how many times they can use the grants from three to six and raised the total available. For fiscal 2027, the lifetime maximums are $133,042 for SAH and $26,654 for SHA, and VA adjusts them each year with construction costs. VA can also approve 120 post-9/11 veterans with loss or loss of use of one lower limb each year, up from 30.

The law also made veterans with qualifying blindness in both eyes eligible for the larger grant. Veterans apply to VA. Starting in October 2030, veterans whose last grant was more than 10 years earlier can get extra money if their home lacks adaptations reasonably necessary for their disability. Congress paid for changes in this law by extending higher funding fees on some VA home loans.

Reps. Gus Bilirakis (R) and Mike Levin (D) carried the House bill, with Phil Roe (R) as an original cosponsor who pressed leaders to schedule final passage. Sens. Jerry Moran (R) and Kyrsten Sinema (D) introduced the Senate version. Veterans Ryan Kules and Paul Benne were tireless advocates, and Paul and Christine Benne's case helped inspire the Senate bill. It passed both chambers with support from both parties, and President Donald Trump signed it on August 8, 2020.
