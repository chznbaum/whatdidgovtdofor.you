Ridley Place was an aging 259-unit public housing complex in Newport News's Southeast Community. In 2019, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development awarded the city and its housing authority $30 million to help replace the development and revitalize the surrounding Marshall-Ridley neighborhood.

HUD's original approved plan called for 487 mixed-income homes on several sites in the Southeast Community and downtown. It included one-for-one replacement of all 259 Ridley Place public housing units, along with moderate-income and market-rate housing, parks and a restored street grid.

The apartments serve residents at several income levels. Former Ridley Place households receive priority during lease-up for assisted replacement units, but they still submit applications through property management. Urban Strategies now provides case management and connects families with health, education, workforce and childcare services.

Construction has proceeded in five phases with developer Pennrose. Earlier phases have opened, and Pennrose reports more than $120 million in housing investment across the five phases. The final phase will add 73 apartments and ground-floor commercial space at 2815 Washington Avenue; it broke ground June 11, 2026, and is scheduled for completion in August 2027. HUD Secretary Ben Carson announced the federal award, while Newport News and its housing authority have led local implementation with funding appropriated by the City Council.
