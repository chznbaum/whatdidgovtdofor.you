This rule made refunds to your original form of payment the default instead of something you have to chase. If an airline cancels your flight or significantly changes it, and you don't accept the rebooking or other offer, the airline owes you a refund. That includes taxes and fees.

A significant change includes a departure or arrival time moved by 3 hours or more on a domestic flight, or 6 hours or more on an international one. If your checked bag arrives more than 12 hours late on a domestic flight, file a mishandled-baggage report; barring limited exceptions, the bag fee comes back to you. International flights have longer limits. If you paid for an extra service and the airline didn't provide it, that fee comes back too. Airlines must pay within 7 business days for credit card purchases and 20 calendar days for other payment methods.

The Department of Transportation under Secretary Pete Buttigieg issued the rule in April 2024. A few weeks later, Congress wrote refund rights into federal law in the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024, which passed with large bipartisan majorities, and President Joe Biden signed it in May 2024.
