As COVID-19 shut down much of the country in March 2020, Congress passed the CARES Act. One of its most direct pieces was a cash payment to households, sent automatically to most eligible people.

Eligible adults got up to $1,200, married couples filing jointly up to $2,400, and families another $500 for each qualifying child under 17. The full amount went to single filers with incomes up to $75,000, heads of household up to $112,500 and joint filers up to $150,000. Payments shrank by 5% of income above those levels. People who had filed a 2018 or 2019 tax return generally got their money automatically by direct deposit or mail. People receiving Social Security, SSI, railroad retirement or VA benefits also received automatic $1,200 payments, but some had to provide dependent information to receive the child amount promptly. Other people who did not normally file taxes had to submit basic information to the IRS. By May 31, 2020, the government had sent 160.4 million payments worth $269 billion.

The bill passed the Senate 96–0 and the House by voice vote, and President Donald Trump signed it on March 27, 2020. Eligible people who missed the first round could claim it on a 2020 tax return until May 17, 2024.
