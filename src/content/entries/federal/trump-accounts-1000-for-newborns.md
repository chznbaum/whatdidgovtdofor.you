The One Big Beautiful Bill Act created Trump Accounts, a new kind of savings account for children, along with a $1,000 pilot program for newborns. For every U.S. citizen child with a Social Security number born from January 1, 2025, through December 31, 2028, the Treasury puts $1,000 into the child's account.

The money must be invested in mutual funds or exchange-traded funds that track a U.S. stock index such as the S&P 500. Starting July 4, 2026, parents, relatives and others can add up to $5,000 a year in total, and employers can chip in up to $2,500 a year for a worker or a worker's child. The money generally stays put until the year the child turns 18.

You do have to act to get the $1,000. The Treasury automatically opened accounts for eligible children under 18, but a parent or guardian must claim the account, through the Trump Accounts app, the website or IRS Form 4547, before the seed money is deposited. By October 2026, the White House reported nearly 70 million accounts and $1.3 billion in $1,000 seed deposits.

Republicans passed the law without Democratic votes. Vice President JD Vance broke a 50–50 Senate tie, and President Donald Trump signed it on July 4, 2025.
