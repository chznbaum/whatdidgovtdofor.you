If your school shuts down or loses VA approval while you're on the GI Bill, you can get back the benefit time you used there when fewer than 12 of your credits carry over to a new school. Before this law, VA made you enroll at a new school first and wait for it to decide which credits it would accept. Many students gave up. Now you sign a statement saying you're transferring fewer than 12 credits. VA has to accept it, restore your benefit time, and give you a certificate of eligibility to show your next school. VA also has to tell students who may qualify. If you later transfer 12 or more credits, VA takes the certificate back.

The law also removed a rule that made service members pick an end date when they transferred Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits to a spouse or child. A wrong date, like a child's 18th birthday entered by mistake, could cut off benefits a family was counting on.

Rep. Vern Buchanan wrote the bill, and it took in Rep. Greg Murphy's fix for the end dates. The House passed it 412–1 in May 2022, with support from both parties. The Senate passed it without objection that December, and President Joe Biden signed it on December 27, 2022.

The full-restoration rule is temporary. It covers closures from August 2021 through December 11, 2026, a deadline Congress has pushed back several times in stopgap spending laws.
