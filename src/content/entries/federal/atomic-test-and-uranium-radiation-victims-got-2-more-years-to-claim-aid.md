The Radiation Exposure Compensation Act pays a one-time lump sum to people who got certain cancers and other diseases after radiation exposure from U.S. atomic weapons tests or from mining, milling, or hauling uranium. Survivors can file for a family member who died. The program was set to stop taking claims in July 2022. This law kept it open two more years, until June 2024.

During that window, the Justice Department paid $50,000 to downwinders who lived near the tests, $75,000 to people who took part in them, and $100,000 to uranium miners, millers and ore transporters. You had to file a claim and document a covered illness and qualifying residence, work or onsite participation. Between May 2022 and July 2024 the department approved 2,598 more claims, worth about $154 million.

Sen. Mike Lee, a Utah Republican, sponsored the bill, which came out of talks over his broader downwinders bill. Sens. Ben Ray Luján and Mike Crapo, longtime leaders on the program from opposite parties, pushed for the extension. In the House, Reps. Teresa Leger Fernández and Burgess Owens led a letter from both parties asking for a vote. Rep. Greg Stanton’s earlier hearing and committee work helped pave the way. The Senate passed the bill unanimously on April 28, 2022, and the House passed it on May 11. President Joe Biden signed it on June 7, 2022.

The extension added no new people or places. The program lapsed in June 2024, and Congress reopened and expanded it in July 2025.
