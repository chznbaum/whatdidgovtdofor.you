If you work for the federal government or the D.C. government and a shutdown sends you home, this law says you get paid for the days you missed once the shutdown ends. If you're kept on the job without pay, you get your regular pay for that time too, and you can use your leave while it lasts. The money must come as soon as possible after funding returns, without waiting for a regular payday. You don't have to apply. The law covers every funding lapse that started on or after Dec. 22, 2018, not just the one that prompted it.

Sen. Ben Cardin of Maryland wrote the bill during the 35-day shutdown of 2018–19, with Sen. Susan Collins of Maine as his lead Republican partner. Sen. Chris Van Hollen pushed it through. The Senate passed it without objection, the House voted 411–7, and President Donald Trump signed it on January 16, 2019.

The guarantee is now being challenged. In 2025, OMB Director Russell Vought argued that furloughed workers aren't owed back pay unless Congress specifically provides it after each shutdown. OPM adopted that position in January 2026. Congress has nevertheless directed payments each time: the November 2025 law paid workers after the 43-day shutdown; the February 2026 law covered the four-day partial shutdown; and an April law directed back-pay payments after the long DHS funding lapse.
