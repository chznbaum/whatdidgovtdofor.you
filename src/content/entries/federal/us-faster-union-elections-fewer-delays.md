When workers file for a union election, the National Labor Relations Board now moves the case along faster. A hearing usually opens 8 calendar days after notice goes out. Under the 2019 rules it was 14 business days, nearly three weeks. Once the regional director orders an election, the vote is set for the earliest date practicable. A 20-business-day wait written into the 2019 rule never actually took effect: a court blocked it, and the Board stayed it until this rule repealed it. Employers must post the notice of the petition within 2 business days, not 5. Most disputes over who belongs in the voting group can wait until after the vote, continuing the practice that remained in place while a contrary 2019 provision was blocked.

The rule covers private-sector workers under the National Labor Relations Act. It applies to petitions filed on or after Dec. 26, 2023. Workers don't need to do anything extra: the faster timeline applies to every case. Long delays give employers more time to campaign against a union before people vote.

The Board restored the faster procedures it first adopted in 2014. Federal courts had upheld those rules, and a 2019 Board had rolled them back. Chair Lauren McFerran and members Gwynne Wilcox and David Prouty voted for the rule in August 2023. Member Marvin Kaplan dissented, arguing that fast elections leave voters less informed.
