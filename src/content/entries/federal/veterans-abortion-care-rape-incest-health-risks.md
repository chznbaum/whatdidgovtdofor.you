For decades, VA health care didn't include abortion or even counseling about it. In September 2022, a few months after the Supreme Court ended the federal right to abortion, VA changed that. Veterans could get abortion counseling, and VA would provide or pay for an abortion when a pregnancy endangered the patient's life or health or came from rape or incest. The same coverage reached CHAMPVA, the health plan for families of some disabled and deceased veterans. Veterans had to be eligible for the VA medical benefits package, and family members had to be enrolled in CHAMPVA. A health professional determined whether an abortion qualified.

VA said it acted because more than 100,000 veterans and CHAMPVA members lived in states that had banned or limited abortion or were expected to. Use stayed low. Between September 2022 and August 2025, VA covered or provided abortions for about 100 veterans and 40 CHAMPVA patients.

Then-VA Secretary Denis McDonough approved the 2022 interim rule and signed it into final form in February 2024, under President Joe Biden. VA took in nearly 58,000 public comments before making it final.

VA ended the policy on Dec. 22, 2025, through an immediate directive following a Justice Department opinion. VA Secretary Doug Collins approved the final repeal rule the next day, and it took regulatory effect Jan. 30, 2026. VA now bars abortion counseling and most abortion care while still covering treatment needed to save a patient's life. Minority Veterans of America asked the Federal Circuit to strike down the repeal; the case remained pending as of Nov. 12, 2026.
