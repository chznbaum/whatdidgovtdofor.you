If you use a wheelchair or have trouble standing or climbing, a checkup can go wrong before it starts. The exam table may not lower, the scale may not hold a wheelchair, or the mammogram machine may only work if you stand. This rule sets national standards for accessible medical equipment at health care run by state and local governments. That covers public hospitals, public clinics, public college health services, and contractors that run clinics for them.

Since October 8, 2024, any exam table, exam chair, scale, X-ray machine or mammography machine these providers buy or lease generally must be accessible until they hit the rule's quota. The quota is at least 10% of each type, or 20% in settings that specialize in conditions affecting mobility, including rehab and outpatient physical therapy, and never less than one unit. By August 9, 2026, every covered public entity that uses exam tables or scales generally had to acquire at least one accessible version of each type it uses. Staff must be able to operate the equipment and help you transfer onto it. A provider can't turn you away because it lacks accessible equipment, and it can't make you bring a companion to help if other patients are not required to.

You don't have to apply for anything. The protection comes with the care. Attorney General Merrick Garland issued the rule under the Americans with Disabilities Act. It adopts equipment standards the U.S. Access Board wrote in 2017.
