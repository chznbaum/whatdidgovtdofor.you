For two years during the pandemic, VA could give COVID-19 vaccines to many more people than the veterans enrolled in its health care. The law covered veterans who weren't eligible to enroll, veterans abroad who use VA's Foreign Medical Program, and CHAMPVA beneficiaries, who include spouses and children of veterans with permanent, total service-connected disabilities or who died of service-connected conditions. It also covered caregivers in specified VA caregiver-support and home-care programs and veterans' spouses. VA Secretary Denis McDonough said the law grew the group VA could vaccinate from about 9 million people to 33 million.

Enrolled veterans still came first, and VA offered shots to the others as supply allowed. The authority lasted only as long as the federal COVID-19 public health emergency. When that emergency ended on May 11, 2023, the extra eligibility under this law ended with it.

House Veterans' Affairs Chairman Mark Takano and the committee's top Republican, Mike Bost, introduced the bill. It had more than 50 cosponsors, and the House passed it without objection on March 9, 2021. In the Senate, Jon Tester and Jerry Moran wrote the substitute that added spouses and CHAMPVA families; John Boozman and Richard Blumenthal were original cosponsors of their Senate bill. The Senate passed that version by voice vote on March 17, the House agreed two days later, and President Joe Biden signed it on March 24, 2021.
