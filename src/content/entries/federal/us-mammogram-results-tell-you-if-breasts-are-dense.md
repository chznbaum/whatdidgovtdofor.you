When you get a mammogram, the facility must send you a summary of the results in plain language within 30 days. Since September 2024, that summary has to say whether your breast tissue is dense or not dense. If it is dense, the letter must also say that other imaging tests may help find cancer in some people with dense tissue. If a result looks suspicious or highly suggestive of cancer, you and your doctor must hear within 7 days. Your doctor's copy of the report rates your density in one of four standard categories.

This matters because dense tissue can hide tumors on a mammogram, and FDA says about half of U.S. women over 40 have it. Before this rule, whether you were told depended on your state. You don't need to do anything to get the notice: every FDA-certified mammography facility must include it. The rule doesn't require extra tests, so talk with your doctor about what fits you.

The push began with patient advocates such as Nancy Cappello of Connecticut, who was diagnosed with advanced cancer after a normal mammogram. Rep. Rosa DeLauro and Sen. Dianne Feinstein carried national density bills for years. In 2019 they got the requirement into a federal spending law, which President Donald Trump signed on February 15, 2019. FDA Commissioner Robert Califf issued the final rule in March 2023, and facilities had 18 months to comply.
