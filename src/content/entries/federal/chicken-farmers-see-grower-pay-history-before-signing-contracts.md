Most U.S. broilers, chickens raised for meat, are raised by farmers under contract to companies. The farmer runs chicken houses; the company owns the birds and feed and sets the pay. This rule requires the company to give a grower a disclosure document and contract before signing. When housing specifications stay the same, the review period is 14 calendar days, but the grower may waive seven. When a housing investment is planned, the documents must arrive with the housing specifications and a financing letter. The disclosure shows five years of average annual gross pay at the local complex, usually broken into fifths. At complexes with nine or fewer growers, it gives mean and standard deviation instead. It covers minimum flock placements, grower turnover, past lawsuits, sick flocks and disasters, and grower costs.

Many companies use tournaments to rank growers for pay. They must give growers details about the chicks placed on that farm within 24 hours of delivery. At settlement, they must show how each grower ranked and what inputs everyone received; the sheets need not name other growers. Growers do not apply. Smaller companies are exempt from the disclosure document in limited cases without housing investments.

Agriculture Secretary Tom Vilsack announced the rule in November 2023. Bruce Summers headed the Agricultural Marketing Service that issued it, and Associate Administrator Erin Morris signed the final text. It took effect in February 2024. In 2026, USDA under Secretary Brooke Rollins put a plan to rescind it on its regulatory agenda.
