When a highway, rail line or other project paid for with federal money takes your home, federal law says you get more than the price of the property. You also get help moving and help with the higher cost of a comparable new place. This rule raised those payments for the first time since Congress set them in 2012. The standard payment for an eligible homeowner who lived there at least 90 days can now provide up to $41,200 toward a replacement home. The standard rental-assistance payment can provide up to $9,570, up from $7,200. Payments to help a business set up again rose to as much as $33,200.

You must file a claim with supporting documents such as bills or appraisals, generally within 18 months. But the agency cannot leave you to navigate it alone: it must explain which payments you may qualify for and help you complete and file the claim. Under the rule, it must also ask what kind of housing you need, use comparable homes from your own neighborhood when feasible, and can reimburse eligible costs such as rental application and credit check fees. The rule also lets the government adjust the amounts again for inflation without rewriting the full regulation.

The Federal Highway Administration, led by Administrator Shailen Bhatt, wrote and issued the rule under authority delegated by the Transportation Department. It is the first comprehensive update of these regulations since 2005. FHWA administers the Uniform Act across 17 federal agencies. The rule was published in May 2024 and took effect June 3, 2024.
