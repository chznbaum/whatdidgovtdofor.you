If a qualifying service-related disability like the loss of use of your hands or feet, severe vision loss, severe burns, or ALS means you need an adapted vehicle, the VA pays a grant toward buying one. As of October 2026 it pays up to $27,908.90, sent straight to the seller. That grant used to be once in a lifetime. Adapted vehicles last about 11.5 years on average, and replacing one could cost $40,000 to $65,000 new, according to a 2020 estimate. This law lets the VA give you another grant once more than 30 years have passed since your last one. Starting January 5, 2033, the wait drops to 10 years. You can apply online or submit VA Form 21-4502, and you must get VA approval before buying the vehicle.

The law does more. Veterans can now get VA help with van lifts, raised doors and roofs, air conditioning, and wheelchair tie-downs as medical care. VA can also cover trailers built to haul a power wheelchair or scooter. Other sections protect GI Bill students when an emergency closes their school or moves classes online.

Sen. Susan Collins and Sen. Joe Manchin first introduced the AUTO for Veterans Act in 2020. Rep. Lizzie Fletcher and Rep. Mark Takano led a House version, while Rep. Dan Meuser and Rep. David Trone introduced a companion bill. Paralyzed Veterans of America pushed the change. The vehicle provisions entered Rep. Mike Levin's student-veteran bill through Sen. Jerry Moran's substitute, which the Senate passed without objection. The House agreed 380–35, with every Democrat and most Republicans voting yes. President Joe Biden signed it on January 5, 2023.
