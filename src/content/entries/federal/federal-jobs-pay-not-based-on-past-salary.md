If you take your first federal job in one of these covered pay systems, or come back to federal work after a break, the agency can't use your old private-sector or other non-federal pay to decide what it pays you. It also can't set your pay by matching another employer's job offer. This holds even if you volunteer the number. Agencies are told not to ask for it in the first place.

When an agency wants to start you above the bottom of a pay range, it has to point to other reasons. Those can include your skills, its recent luck recruiting for the job, or how far federal pay trails the market for that work. It also has to look at what it recently paid people with similar qualifications in similar jobs. People who already have federal service can still have their past federal pay counted, under policies each agency must now write down. You don't need to apply for any of this. It's simply how hiring works now.

The idea is that pay history carries old gaps forward. OPM found that among nonseasonal, full-time, permanent executive-branch employees, women were paid 94 cents for every dollar paid to men in September 2021. President Joe Biden's 2021 executive order on the federal workforce told the Office of Personnel Management to consider a ban. OPM, led by Director Kiran Ahuja, proposed it in May 2023 and finalized it in January 2024.
