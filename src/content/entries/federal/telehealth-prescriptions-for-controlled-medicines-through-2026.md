Federal drug law normally requires a clinician to see you in person before prescribing a controlled medication by telehealth. That covers many common prescriptions, including ADHD stimulants, some anxiety and sleep medicines, and drugs used to treat opioid addiction. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the government waived that rule so patients could start and continue these medications over video. This rule keeps the waiver going for all of 2026.

There is no patient application. A clinician registered with the DEA can prescribe Schedule II through V medications by telehealth to a patient they have never met in person, as long as the prescription is for a legitimate medical purpose and meets federal prescribing rules and applicable state law. An estimate cited in the rule, based on unpublished data covering 258 organizations and reviewed by Epic, Johns Hopkins, and Stanford, found that more than 7 million controlled-substance prescriptions in 2024, about 16% of the covered prescriptions, were written without an in-person exam first.

DEA Administrator Terrance Cole signed the rule on Dec. 15, 2025, and Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. issued it jointly with him. It was the fourth one-year extension. The agencies said letting the waiver lapse would abruptly cut off patients' care and swamp clinics with requests for in-person visits while DEA finished its permanent rules. The waiver now ends Dec. 31, 2026. As of early November 2026, DEA had not issued another extension or a final permanent rule, and patient advocates were pressing it to act.
