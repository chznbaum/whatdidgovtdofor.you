# Portrait review

Independent side-by-side review of every published ink portrait against the photo it was drawn from (`review-sheet.py` sheets, plus full-size photo and drawing where needed). Judged on likeness, skin tone (hatching density vs. photo value), flattery parity across parties, style (`style.txt`, `docs/design.md` "Portraits"), and framing.

Verdicts: `ok` ships as is; `minor` ships but is worth a redraw when convenient; `redraw` should not ship as is.

## Parity across the set

**Party.** No Republican is drawn less flatteringly than his photo. All 13 R portraits are men drawn from posed government portraits, and 12 are clean. Trump's is, if anything, gentler than his photo: paler, softer line, creases no deeper than the photo shows. Every harsh drawing and every unflattering source is on the Democratic side. None of that is drawing bias against Democrats; it follows from where the photos come from. Federal officials (most of the Republicans) have bioguide portraits, while many NY and VA Democrats only had candids or TV frames. But the effect on the page is that Republicans as a group come off at least as well as Democrats:
- Five Democrats (Bobby Scott, Gale Brewer, Louise Lucas, Mark Takano, Joseph Addabbo) are drawn older, rougher or with altered skin. No Republican is.
- Seven Democrats (Cuomo, de Blasio, Nolan, Salazar, Hochul, Chin, Hunter) are shown mid-word or unsmiling because their only source is a candid or TV frame. Every Republican is posed. Trump's stern look is his own official portrait's.

Fixing the five redraws and re-sourcing the seven candids would level this.

**Race.** This is the larger concern. Three of the five redraws are people of color (Scott, Lucas, Takano), out of about two dozen people of color in the set (counting Black, Latino, South Asian and East Asian officials).
- In all three, the model switched to a heavier hand (woodcut or scratchboard blacks, stipple or flecks, added creases), which reads harsher and older.
- Two have skin pushed off: Scott lighter, so his skin reads lighter than his gray hair; Lucas darker than her photo.

Most people of color are drawn well, with dense, even hatching where every feature stays readable and values close to the photo in grayscale: Stewart-Cousins, Sewell, Ossé, Ward, Underwood, Warnock, Gibson, Hanif, Carroll Foy, McClure, Joyner, Nurse, Mamdani, Hunter, Myrie. Herring runs a shade light and Ramos a shade dark (both minor).

**Skin method.** I put every source photo in grayscale beside its drawing. As a rough cross-check, I also measured the cheek value of every photo (face-detector box) and drawing (eyes.json). The drawings lift everyone lighter than the photo, roughly drawing = 0.56 × photo + 138 on a 0–255 scale, so light skin goes to near-white paper, as the style asks. People of color mostly sit on or slightly darker than that line, which is right. Photo exposure varies too much for the numbers to decide anything alone, so every verdict above rests on the side-by-side. The same lift turns tanned or ruddy white men near-white (Trump, Saslaw, Hatch, Ebbin), which is acceptable under the light-skin rule; only Trump is flagged, because his dark suit was lightened too.

**Likeness drift.** The model's commonest error is slimming and lengthening round faces, almost always women's: Cruz, Herring, Carroll Foy, González-Rojas, Delaney, Mayer, Ramos (the other way: broader). It also tends to lighten mid-brown hair (Paulin, González-Rojas, Cruz) and gray golden-blonde hair (Mayer). It's flattering in direction but still not their face. There are no R women to compare against, so this is not a party skew. Redraw notes should name the face shape outright.

**Style and framing.** Most of the set is one consistent fine-line dip-pen hand.
- Off-hand outliers: Brewer (scratchy, cutout debris), Boysko (woodcut blacks), Takano (scratchboard flecks), Lucas (woodcut, stipple), Rivera (smooth cartoon).
- A softer, pencil-like group is acceptable but could be crisper: Biden, Trump, Surovell, Saslaw, Paulin.
- Framing is consistent except Brewer and Boysko, framed about 15% large with no shoulders; their eyes.json entries look under-measured. Nurse, Hunter and Rivera sit lower because big hair or a hat pushes the eye line down, which is how `normalize.py` is designed to work.

**Not yet published:** brad-lander. There is a raw drawing in `raw/`, but no cutout, eyes entry, webp or `portrait:` field as of the final re-run of `review-sheet.py`. zellnor-myrie was published during the review and is reviewed above (ok).
