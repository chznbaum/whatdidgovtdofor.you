### What the run learned about drawing

- A heavy woodcut or scratchboard hand still shows up on first tries, more often on darker-skinned people. Adding "delicate, refined hedcut with thin black lines; no high-contrast engraving, no thick or jagged strokes, no white scratches, no black blotches" to the notes fixed it every time.
- Measure smiles; don't eyeball them. Read the corners at the half-value point of the lip line on photo and drawing alike. Across the set, mouths run about +3% wide.
- Dark suits drift to mid-gray, mostly on light-skinned men; name the suit "near-black" in the notes.
- For a hair value the model keeps missing, a grayscale copy of the photo as input holds it.
- Small sources (faces under ~250px) draw better from a 2-3x LANCZOS upscale; very wide or tight crops draw better from a head-and-shoulders crop.
- `skin-check.py` used to measure the largest "face" its detector found, which could be a blouse or a chest; it now prefers boxes with eyes in them. Beards and dark backgrounds still pull its number darker.

### What the run learned about drawing

- **Smiles are still the commonest failure,** drawn 6–13% wide on most of the drawing round's left-outs. Putting the measured ratio in the notes ("the mouth is exactly 1.07 times the distance between the pupils, corner to corner: keep it that narrow; do not widen or open the smile") worked on most fix tries. But many "wide" readings were wrong at the photo's end: corners read at the darkest pixel instead of the half-value point, moustaches hiding the corners (Zaccaro's photo reads 1.05–1.10 ipd at the dark core and 1.275 at the lip junction), and pupils misread on small or squinting photos. The Nov. 15 reviewer's reads differed from the drawers' by up to 5 points for exactly those faces. Read the pupils by hand at high zoom and re-measure the photo before blaming the drawing.
- **Pupil misreads also faked shape failures.** Avula's jaw, Bynoe's width and Grace Lee's lower face all came back on the photo's outline once the pupils were re-read. Baynard's jaw, 7–10% wide on six tries, was real.
- **Medium and deeper skin gets lifted toward paper on first tries** (New York 8 of its 12 people of color; Kozlowski, Johnson, Jost, Hawj; Williams-Graves, Srinivasan; also many light faces). The fix that works is the color photo plus the face's value described against its own clothing: Clark, Hylton and Torian landed at −11 to −16, beside Warnock, and Bynoe's Nov. 15 redraw at −2.4. Grayscale input with the photo's absolute values stated overshot: −42 to −50 on tries for Torian, Clark, Hylton, Bagby and Avula, −61.8 on a Nov. 15 Bynoe try, and Bynoe's published −40 in a tonal graphite hand; it also widened Bagby's mouth 12–14%. A grayscale copy with a target on the drawing's scale (Avula's "cheeks about 180") did work.
- **Darker hatching brings out lines.** Once notes push a tan or deep skin darker, weathered faces get a coarse engraving with white flecks (Bacon, Peake, Mayorkas, LaHood, Deeds, McDougle) and soft folds become firm lines (Anthony, Brooks-Powers, Rivera). "Smooth, fine parallel hatching on the cheeks, no white flecks, no stipple; soft smile folds; under-eye lines no deeper or more numerous than the photo shows" prevented it on first tries (Pinto, Champion, Baker, McEwen) and fixed it on refix (Anthony, Peake, Rivera, Bacon).
- **The soft gray-pencil hand** shipped on fair women (Wagner, Houchin, Houlahan, Hemmingsen-Jaeger, New Craig) and was the commonest way a fix try failed when asked for darker hair or skin (Hicks, Franson, Lynch, Reid, Krishnan, Peake). "Crisp black ruled hatching, never gray pencil, graphite or grain" in the notes held it off.
- **Hair a shade light:** blonde goes platinum, red goes ash, black goes salt-and-pepper, mid-brown gets white highlight strands. A grayscale copy as input held near-black hair every time it was used (Zhuang, Valdivia, Rivera, Abubaker, Vernikov, Robertson, Shin, New Craig, Dougherty) but not light hair (Trammell's grayscale try went platinum). For blonde, red and gray, stating the hair's grayscale value beside the face and "few highlight strands, never silver or white" fixed Seibold, Reid, Lynch and Trammell; Hicks needed a mid-dark target from grayscale input.
- **Crops:** a head-and-shoulders crop with clear space above the hair fixed cut crowns (Farías, Grace Lee). A crop much narrower than five pupil distances comes back with a big head and shoulders that stop inside the frame (McDonough fix 2); one drawn small in its frame leaves empty strips at the lower corners (Reid).
- **Keep eyes.json in step with the published drawing.** Detrick's ipd was 8% short, Bacon's v1 entry overstated his (135 vs 126), and Krishnan's held an unpublished try's numbers under the live v1, which any re-run of `normalize.py` would have mis-framed by ~13%. Re-measure after every redraw and whenever a version is restored. Turned heads foreshorten the pupil distance and frame large (O'Donnell).
- **A drawing follows the photo it links to.** Sturtevant's two-era composite was redrawn to the linked photo on the owner's rule. Expression notes in `sources.json` count too: McDonald's fix dropped the gentle smile his notes asked for, and it had to be put back.
- **Nov. 15: edit, don't redraw, when one feature keeps failing.** After fresh draws widened Trammell's mouth three times and drifted McDonald's face, editing only the mouth patch of the published drawing fixed each and left everything else byte-for-byte. Anderson's value was fixed by regenerating from the run-3 drawing.
- Mid-tone garments still go near-white (Linda Lee's periwinkle blazer, Eachus's gray suit), and eyes come out more open than a smiling squint on every try for some faces (Martinez); both are worth a note on the first try.

### What the run learned about drawing

- **Never write the photo's own gray values into the notes.** This was the run's main failure. Notes like "cheeks about 100–115 of 255 ... keep the face at that value" were drawn literally, with none of the lift every face in the set gets, worst from grayscale input. Givens (forehead 133 → 144), Naya Young (cheeks 88 → 104), Onyema (cheeks 130 → 125, from color input) and the light-olive Vitale (shadow cheek 120 → 121) came out darker than any peer, and Reynolds-Jackson and Baptiste-Alsbrook were about half-lifted. It landed fine on bright, high-key photos (McKnight, Baraka) and was ignored once (Carter), so it was unpredictable. On light hair it did the opposite harm: Deegan's blonde stayed at its photo value under a lifted face and read gray. The run's own briefs offered it: run 4's fix brief and drawing addendum, inherited from run 3's fix brief ("draw from a grayscale copy and state the face's grayscale value from the photo"), kept it as a second option until about 07:40, though the repo's DRAWING-BRIEF.md had dropped it earlier in the night. What fixed each face was color input with the value described against the person's own clothes and hair (Onyema, Vitale, Givens), or a value edit of the drawing itself (Naya Young, Baptiste-Alsbrook, Gray, Hayes, Bastien, Manchin, Demings), with any number given on the drawing's own scale and the tile checked on orange beside peers whose photos are as dark. Measure those peers' photos in the same boxes: Naya Young's fix5-1 met the reviewer's box targets and still sat lighter than three peers with equally dark photos.
- **Grayscale input** is still the fix for light and tanned faces that color input lifts to paper (Jugovich, Jordon Johnson, Hill, Seeberger, Weprin; the values on Manchin's try 3), with the value described relative to the shirt and hair. But it pushed deeper skin dark (above), turned older tanned faces coarse (Weprin, Ortega) and gave tonal, filtered-photo faces (Viera, Vitale, Hanewicz's try 2, Manchin's try 3, Duman's try 3).
- **Edit, don't redraw** (method above). Local edits stayed byte-identical outside small boxes; whole-face changes regenerate and must be checked as new. The side effects: a paler, softer spot just past an edited mouth corner (Glassman, Kijakazi, Floyd, Kaine, Gill), invisible on tiles; a pasted mouth that came back pale and tonal, with a dotted moustache (Givens); and a whole-face value edit that regenerated as soft pencil (Hayes), fixed by a re-ink edit whose notes said "change ONLY how the skin is inked". "One step darker" overshot on Zwicker, Onyema's fix 2 and Bynoe's fix4-4, and "two steps lighter" on Baptiste-Alsbrook's fix 2. A value edit can't move contours (Baptiste-Alsbrook's jaw), and no local edit rescues a try that fails on several properties (Christy, Duman, Pitts). Name both mouth corners against features and give the ratio; "narrower" in words did nothing in fresh draws.
- **Re-measure the photo before blaming the drawing.** Four left-outs (Cochran-Johnson, Cupid, Naya Young, Ruiz) passed as drawn once the photo was re-read: darkest-pixel corners, a patch of eye white read as a catchlight, off-center catchlights, and for Turner a catchlight on an iris edge. Readers still disagree by up to ~8% on soft corners (Naya Young's photo read 1.14, 1.175 and 1.22–1.23). The automatic pupil finder misread 7 of 18 federal faces. Pupil misreads also faked shape flags (Meeks's head; Bhatt's, on the fixer's re-measure; Glover's width). A short photo read became a narrow mouth: Miller's note pinned each corner "slightly inside" its pupil, and the smile came out −7 to −10%. A single trailing line or tick past one corner reads as width (Bailey, Turner, Kaine); one-corner edits fixed them.
- **Gray and silver hair:** the face gets the set's lift and gray hair keeps its photo value, so a face that matched its hair in the photo ends up lighter than it (Ahuja, Garland; run 2's Hoylman-Sigal, run 3's McDonald). Lift the hair with the face. Hair-only mask edits fixed both, and fixed the light-hair drift on Camp, Murray, Deegan, Hutcheson and Brennan without touching their faces. Light hair on women still runs a shade light on first tries (Quijano and Ruiz ship with it).
- **Teeth:** a bright, even band of teeth wider than the photo's, where its outer teeth are shadowed, reads as a broader grin with the corners in tolerance (Reynolds-Jackson +8%, Freiman +30% lit width, Mukherji +10%). Now in the brief.
- **Tight crops:** a photo that ends at the shoulders, or is narrower than about five pupil distances, leaves suit edges or orange bands in the tile (Alexander's 400x500, Tuck's 600x600, McDonough's alt at ~4.1 ipd, Altoro's 360px try 2). Padding the input with the suit roughed in fixed Altoro (try 3; the reviewer saw a plain dark suit, nothing misleading), but the permission system blocked the same move for McDonough, Alexander and Tuck, and even Tuck's framing-only change. Whether a drawing may extend clothing past the photo's edge is the owner's call. Narrow drawings need a tighter frame (Bastien `[4.58]`, +9%; Ortega `[4.6]`, ~9%), and turned heads foreshorten the pupil distance (Cupid `[5.5]`).
- **Small sources:** faces of ~95–150px (Christy, Duman, Glover, Pitts) and Gray's 300px photo failed or took the most work, and New Jersey's 250x364 photos gave five of that group's eight first-pass non-`ok` verdicts. No small-photo drawing invented detail; the compensating notes failed. Faces of 60–75px stay held (Shulman, Barber, Bowles).
- **Darker hatching still brings out lines** on lined faces (Weprin, Zwicker, Vitale's fix; Ortega mildly), and a soft photo can still come back with coarse, flecked skin (Turner's own try). The smooth-hatching sentence fixed Weprin and Zwicker; Vitale and Turner wait for texture edits.
